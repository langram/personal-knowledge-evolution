## I. Hypotheses on mathematics, category theory and consciousness

### Edition 19, Chapter 1: Limits, Paradoxes and the Boundary of Knowledge

#### Starting from a paradox more than two thousand years old

Zeno's paradox has many versions: Achilles cannot catch the tortoise, the flying arrow does not move. Take the dichotomy as an example. For a person to walk from point A to point B, he must first cover half the distance, then half of what remains, and so on. Since "half" can be taken indefinitely, he seems never to reach B. The question in mathematics of whether 0.999... equals 1 is a variant of it. This problem troubled people for a long time, until the concept of the limit appeared and brought it into a rigorous mathematical system. The limit, in an ingenious way, acknowledges the existence of an infinite process while giving that infinite process a definite, finite endpoint, and thereby uses discrete, countable steps to describe continuous, uncountable motion perfectly. It was by means of the limit that people developed calculus and raised the descriptive power of mathematics from static things to the complex motion of everything in the universe.

The root of Zeno's paradox lies in the contradiction between the continuity of the material substance of the external world and the discreteness of the process by which our brains know and analyze it. In order to know the world, our perception and thinking essentially cut the continuous world into discrete concepts and steps. Detection itself is a discrete process, and what it yields is a discrete structure. But intuition tells us that the essence of matter should be a continuous, indivisible whole. Ontological continuity versus epistemological discreteness: this pair of contradictions is the starting point of everything that follows.

#### The same thing, the same in what sense

Mathematics is the study of equality. But "equality" has many levels: equivalence, isomorphism, homotopy equivalence, which are symmetric relations of different strengths between different mathematical structures. The move made by the limit establishes an equivalence between two obviously non-isomorphic objects, a static point and a dynamic sequence. My conjecture is that, from the viewpoint of higher category theory, this equivalence should correspond to some homotopy equivalence; but that correspondence has to be constructed, it is not a ready-made mathematical result. A sequence converging to a limit does not by itself give a homotopy equivalence: take a convergent sequence together with its limit as a subspace of the real line, and it has infinitely many connected components, so it cannot be homotopy equivalent to a point. To write the limit as a homotopy equivalence one must first construct a suitable space and map, and I have not done that. What I borrow is the level of homotopy equivalence itself. Homotopy equivalence is weaker than isomorphism. A coffee cup and a doughnut are homotopy equivalent in topology, because you can continuously squeeze one into the other and each has exactly one hole; but from a strict geometric point of view they are obviously not isomorphic. Homotopy equivalence captures a deeper, more flexible structural similarity. This "weakness" in rigor buys "strength" in function: the whole of higher category theory is built on homotopy equivalence, and that is what provides the means to construct more complex mathematical objects.

The biggest difference between these two kinds of equivalence is this: the objects on the two sides of an isomorphism have the same universal properties; the objects on the two sides of a homotopy equivalence do not have equal universal properties. A zero-dimensional point and a one-dimensional sequence obviously have different properties. This inequality is precisely the mathematical embodiment of the fundamental contradiction between emergence and reduction.

#### The asymmetry between emergence and reduction

Between emergence, from lower-order objects to higher-order objects, and reduction, from higher-order objects to lower-order objects, there is an asymmetry. It is this asymmetry that produces things like Zeno's paradox. It exists not only in mathematics; it exists in physics too. The mathematics behind Heisenberg's uncertainty principle is the non-commutativity of the position operator and the momentum operator. You can think of them as two different observational perspectives or measurement languages that cannot be used precisely at the same time: when you describe a particle precisely in the language of position, you destroy the possibility of describing it precisely in the language of momentum; when you try to start from a definite higher-order property and reduce it to all of its lower-order information, what you get is an uncertain state, a superposition of many possibilities.

At a more basic level, the paradoxes caused by self-reference in mathematical logic, set theory and category theory are manifestations of the same asymmetry. Take Russell's paradox: does "the set of all sets that do not contain themselves" contain itself or not? If we regard a thing's property, for example "is it a set", as a universal property in a higher-order category, then its internal structure, which elements it contains, corresponds to a universal property in a lower-order category. When you perform self-reference, that is, when you use the higher-order property to examine itself:

- Affirmative self-reference, "this proposition is true", can be seen as emergence from the lower order to the higher order. It attempts to establish a solid, definite state within a level. Whether you affirm it or deny it, the conclusion stays the same.
- Negative self-reference, "this proposition is false", can be seen as reduction from the higher order to the lower order. It attempts to use the higher-order concept of truth and falsity to describe its own lower-order existence, and the result is an endless oscillation or superposition between the two states of true and false. Whether you affirm it or deny it, the conclusion flips.

How similar this is to the phenomenon of the uncertainty principle in quantum mechanics. So, to build an ultimate theory, one must face paradox directly, and can no longer treat it, as in the past, as a logical loophole to be avoided. The mechanism behind it, the asymmetry between emergence and reduction, must be taken into the core of the theoretical system.

#### What Gödel's incompleteness theorem describes

Following this line of thought, I have a view of Gödel's incompleteness theorem that differs from the usual understanding: what it describes is not the incompleteness of a system but the emergeability of a system. In any sufficiently rich formal system there always exist propositions that can be neither proved nor refuted. People usually treat this as a defect of the system. But from another angle, this is exactly what shows that the system is a super-complete dynamic system with the capacity for emergence: once the system exists, it can always give rise to new fixed points, and each new fixed point corresponds to a structure with entirely new mathematical properties, which drives an incremental evolution in the complexity of the mathematical properties of the whole system. The undecidable proposition is the point of emergence. The paradox itself is the discrete, leap-like state between a lower order and the higher order that emerges from it, and there is no continuous path of morphisms between the two: the forward derivation cannot construct a proof path, and that is incompleteness; the reverse path of reduction is likewise broken, and that is paradox.

This turns mathematics from a static system that merely discovers regularities into an evolving system that continuously constructs and gives rise to higher-order structures. Everything this book says later about "emergence" has its roots here.

#### Infinity is a relative concept

Infinitely large and infinitely small can be further refined into different grades. Infinity is a relative concept: an infinity of grade n1 is infinite relative to an infinity of grade n0. One can even define, for a finite set, an infinity relative to it: for a finite set n, its power set is still finite, but relative to the n from which it was constructed it is a kind of "finite infinity". If we regard emergence and reduction in the evolution of categories as something like the successor operation by which the Peano axioms construct the natural numbers, then an infinitely high-order category is constructed by these two operations, emergence and reduction, as successor and predecessor, and the relative infinity produced by the power set is precisely the internal implementation mechanism of this successor operation, the equivalent of adding one and subtracting one. Through it, the initial object and the terminal object, the empty set and the universal set of set theory, these most abstract concepts, can be converted into one another.

A finite observer, facing an ever larger space of constructions, can do only one thing: at each level, find the stable structure of that level, and take it as the unit for entering the next level. That is the basic building block the next chapter introduces.

#### Questions and Answers

**Q: Mathematicians have handled paradoxes and incompleteness very well for decades by stratifying things. Why put them back at the core of the theory?**

A: ZFC set theory avoids self-reference with the cumulative hierarchy and uses proper classes to push "the set of all sets" outside set theory. These are strategies of avoidance, not strategies of research. If we cannot give a precise definition of self-referential structures and study their properties, then the following questions cannot be answered: under what conditions does a fixed point produce emergence? What are the conditions for maintaining a fixed point? What kind of emergence is strong emergence and what kind is weak? In a symmetric self-referential structure, what is the law governing the conversion between symmetry and asymmetry? Lawvere has already proved that the diagonal argument, Gödel's incompleteness, Tarski's undefinability of truth, Russell's paradox and Cantor's theorem are instances of a single category-theoretic structure; homotopy type theory studies "equality" itself as a higher-dimensional structure. This shows that my intuition has a mathematical basis, and it also shows that this road has already been walked for fifty years, it just has not entered the mainstream. What I am doing is not inventing from zero; it is pushing this from a corner of the foundations of mathematics to the center of the entire system of science.

### Edition 19, Chapter 2: The Dualoid: Existence, Change and Constructibility

#### Why category theory

Every theory is built on a set of mathematical principles. To build a unified theory, one must first find its mathematical principles. Modern mathematics has many disciplines and a sprawling system, and a quick and effective way to choose is to rely on intuition, and my intuition tells me that the discipline most likely to serve is category theory.

Poincaré said that mathematics is the art of giving the same name to different things. Mathematics is the search for the basic commonalities among all things in the world: giving these objects the same name, defining their properties, and studying the relationships among those properties. Category theory takes this to its extreme. Sets, groups, vector spaces and topological spaces are all called objects in category theory; functions, group homomorphisms, linear maps and continuous maps are all called morphisms. These seemingly completely different mathematical concepts all share the same basic characteristics: composition, associativity, identity morphisms. And in the structures they form, the same constructions all exist: limits, colimits, universal properties.

Category theory is the mathematics of mathematics, the language that describes mathematics; mathematics is the language that describes science; science is the language that describes the universe. So category theory, as the lowest cornerstone, has a first-principles explanatory power over mathematics, science and everything in the universe, and many problems that look extremely complex can often be given extremely simple answers by starting from the bottom. At the same time, because it examines only the most basic commonalities and ignores the twigs and leaves, it provides a bird's-eye view from high altitude. It is like facing a vast mountain range: up close, every part is different; from afar, a common pattern appears, ridges seen from the front, peaks seen from the side. "I cannot see the true face of Mount Lu, only because I am standing on the mountain itself." Category theory is the way to walk out of the mountain. It carries two powerful weapons: a bottom-up first-principles explanatory power and a top-down bird's-eye investigative power. Unfortunately, so far the application of category theory in science has been confined to local areas, and the power of these two weapons has barely been brought to bear.

#### The definition of a category is not simple enough

But the foundations of category theory itself are not perfect either. The definition of the category, its most basic concept, introduces two basic concepts at once: objects and morphisms. This is a dualistic starting point. The most basic building block of an ultimate theory should not be two things; it should be one simpler, more perfect entity.

If you examine the whole system of category theory carefully, you will find a hidden property that is not expressed in the definition of a category yet is everywhere: duality. Every concept in category theory, category, object, morphism, limit and colimit, product and coproduct, has its dual concept; every theorem and conclusion is dual too. This all-pervading symmetry hints that beneath the two explicit concepts of object and morphism there may lie a deeper unifying principle. Objects correspond to existence, morphisms correspond to change, and existence and change are precisely the oldest philosophical dual pair of all.

On this basis, I promote duality from a global, self-evident universal property to an explicit entity concept that is more basic than objects and morphisms: the dualoid. Then I use the duality of the dualoid to define the higher concepts of object, morphism and category. The idea resembles the way the Daoist "Dao" gives birth to the two poles of yin and yang.

#### The detection definition of the dualoid

As an ultimate initial concept, we cannot probe its internal structure, because it is the foundation of all structure. So we do not give a structural definition of it; we give only a detection definition: we define it through its relationships with the other objects that are derived from it. In spirit this is category theory's own Yoneda lemma: the identity of an object is completely determined by its relationships with all other objects in the category. We take existence, changeability and constructibility as the basic attributes of the dualoid:

- Existence corresponds to the object in category theory. Without stable existence, nothing can be defined, and the world is chaos.
- Changeability corresponds to the morphism in category theory. Without change, the world would be dead silence, unable to develop and undetectable by any observer. Every property of every thing in our scientific system is built on the foundation of human perception; if the intensity and wavelength of light never changed, the photoreceptor cells of the eye could not perceive light and dark or color, the concepts of brightness and color could never arise, let alone the advanced concepts of wavelength, frequency and amplitude.
- Constructibility, that is, the stability of existence and change, corresponds to the identity morphism and the composability of morphisms. Without stable rules of construction, simple structures cannot stably form complex structures, and the universe would remain forever in its most primitive stage. The stability of existence and the stability of change together determine the stability of structure; the stability of structure determines the stability of the evolutionary process; the stability of evolution determines that our observations are stable and reproducible; and only stable, reproducible sensory feedback lets the brain form stable concepts and regularities, which is the foundation of mathematical and scientific theory.

The core of the dualoid concept is that it treats the essence behind paradox as the fundamental driving force by which the whole system runs, evolves and emerges, and it actively embraces paradox instead of avoiding it. Its response to the ultimate probe, self-reference, is its precise definition:

- Self-affirmation, "I exist", always outputs true. This is the stable state; it represents an extensional emergence that has already been settled, and it presents the stable identity of existence.
- Self-negation, "I am not I", falls into a superposition of true and false. This represents an extensional emergence that is still unsettled, and it presents the two-phase character of the chaotic and unknown.

The former is the cornerstone of existence; the latter is the wellspring of change. This self-referential superposition is "encapsulated" inside the component and does not break the logical consistency of the outside world, just as the superposition of a qubit does not crash a macroscopic computer. On the contrary, it is precisely this internal, paradox-driven uncertainty that becomes the engine by which the system breaks symmetry and gives rise to new levels and new structures. It provides the ultimate explanation for every other concept and law in the system, while its own internal nature cannot be analyzed further, because that lies beyond the detection capacity of the category-theoretic framework.

#### Internal structure is symmetric with external relations

The Yoneda lemma says that all the properties of an object within a category are determined by the totality of its relations with the other objects in that category. In the end, the Yoneda lemma is nothing but a natural inference from the dualoid concept: an object's internal structure is identical to the totality of its external relations; a thing's intension is symmetric with its extension. From this one can go on to infer: if one object can be replaced by another, and after the replacement the set of relations with the other objects is isomorphic, then the two are isomorphic in that category; but isomorphism in the category does not mean isomorphism of internal structure, and it is entirely possible for one to be simple and the other complex; and if this situation exists, then the object with the simpler structure, compared with the object with the more complex structure, consumes less energy in the process of emergent evolution, appears with higher frequency, and therefore has a higher probability of completing emergence. More is different. This inference is the source of the principle of minimization in the next chapter.

#### The universe is a heap of scattered little balls

Fundamentally, the universe is an environment in which everything is possible and anything can exist. The dualoid cannot be the only thing that exists, but it is the necessary precondition for the existence of anything with a stable complex structure. We can suppose that the universe was at first composed of countless scattered little balls, which obey the most basic rules: the rule of existence, the rule of change, and the rule of the stability of existence and change. Under these rules, simple shapes can freely combine into complex shapes. In the process of random combination, once some shape happens to give rise to a meaningful new property or function, for example the ability to replicate itself stably, or the ability to combine more effectively with other structures, it wins out in the competition and emerges in large numbers, becomes the building block of higher-order constructions, and, according to the function its advanced property provides, continues the same rules of free combination at the next level up. Round and round it goes, from the emergence of spacetime in the Big Bang to the birth of advanced intelligent life like humans.

In the universe every rule is possible, but the existence, change and structure that those other rules produce are fleeting, imperceptible to humans; they fill the whole universe like the continuous quantum vacuum, and the little balls that satisfy the three rules are what emerge from it by random fluctuation. They are the most basic building blocks, constructing and evolving everything perceptible in our universe.

#### Which building blocks are especially good at building

In the evolution of the universe from simple to complex, at every level there are always some objects that, relative to other objects, have a stronger ability to construct complex structures. At the atomic level, carbon and silicon, relative to the other elements, are especially good at forming complex structures: their outer electron count is exactly half full, and their abilities to form chemical bonds by losing electrons and by gaining electrons are dual and symmetric. The balance between constraint and freedom is the key to maximizing the creative capacity of molecular structure. Once humans understood this, they used silicon, an element with the same balancing property, to make semiconductors, and on top of it created an entirely new system of artificial intelligence outside the human system of intelligence.

This symmetry does not restrict the diversity of function. The symmetry of carbon's capabilities does not restrict the endless variety of organic matter; the fact that every cell of a multicellular organism carries the same DNA does not restrict the diversity of cell function. Quite the opposite: it is exactly this symmetry, equality and uniformity that truly releases the functional diversity of the basic objects. This point will be used again later when we discuss equality between people.

From this angle one can also explain why strings cannot be the most basic building blocks of the universe. The string is an extremely complex concept: anything whose properties and changes require complex mathematical formulas to describe must have a structure composed from simpler things or laws. Strings have high-dimensional structure and extremely complex modes of change; although they have rules, stability and the ability to construct more complex things, they can never be the simplest and most basic building block. It is like Lego bricks: they can be assembled into endlessly varied shapes, but the brick itself cannot be the most basic shape; the studs and sockets on its surface are themselves complex shapes, and it is the fitting together of those shapes that gives the brick its power to build everything. The string looks more like a reverse-engineered product covered in patches, carefully designed to explain the known particle spectrum, rather than a bottom-up construction from first principles. In an early draft I mapped open and closed strings directly onto existence and change; that was taking a poetic analogy for a physical fact, it was wrong, and I withdraw it here. But the example of the string still shows one thing: at every level, the objects good at building are those with diverse morphisms, composable morphisms, symmetric pushout and pullback capacity, and their own stability.

#### Every level has its own dualoid

At the different levels of the physical world, this dualoid is played by different physical objects: the string in superstring theory, the atom at the level of material elements, the base pair at the level of heredity, the cell in the organism, the neuron in the brain, the human individual in human society. What they have in common is that they all possess a stable state, which is existence; they all grow and evolve over time and with their environment, which is changeability; and the relations between individuals are recursively transitive and combinable, which is constructibility. Just as Conway's Game of Life produces complex life from simple rules, the dual properties of the dualoid produce the complex totality of the universe.

These attributes themselves also evolve. The stability of objects goes through a process from low to high; the diversity of morphisms goes through a process from few to many; the probability that morphisms can be composed rises from low to high; and the pushout and pullback capacity of objects moves from asymmetric to symmetric. In the real world, the pushout and pullback capacity of basic components constructs vast numbers of individual instances, and this is clearest in biological reproduction: the basic structure of DNA has the properties of a dualoid, its pushout and pullback capacity constitutes the diversity and replicability of DNA, and this brings about the diversity of biological traits and the enormous number of individuals. These instances evolve, compete and play games concurrently; species with better dualoid properties take the initiative, more complex structures occupy more resources, reproduce more individuals, and go on to generate even more complex structures. Darwin described the process by which life, this dissipative structure, went from simple to complex, in terms of natural selection and the survival of the fittest; I am trying to give a more basic explanation, from the more basic standpoint of category theory, for the whole universe, biological evolution included.

Abstract the many factors that influence the generation of complex structure into different dimensions, just as the ten or eleven dimensions of string theory are the ten or eleven factors behind the generation of the structure of spacetime, and just as artificial intelligence uses high-dimensional tensors to represent a complex thing. If the time axis of evolution is also taken as a dimension, then a more complex topological structure in a category means that, along the dimension of evolution, evolution can be accelerated with a higher number of concurrent threads. A complex social structure makes the different people and organizations in a society diverse, and the parallel development of that diversity accelerates the process of social evolution. This is the mathematical source of "institutional forking and parallel exploration" in Part V.

### Edition 19, Chapter 3: Fixed Points, Minimization, Maximization and Emergence (excerpts)

#### The static face and the dynamic face of the same object

Two words kept recurring in the previous two chapters: fixed point and emergence. Now I will put them into a single framework.

Fixed point and emergence, like the wave-particle duality of elementary particles, are the static and dynamic manifestations of the same object. The fixed point describes the object's static existence; emergence describes the object's dynamic changeability. And what connects these two forms, letting them sustain each other, convert into each other and iterate forward endlessly, is a pair of laws that sound like exact opposites: the principle of minimization, and the principle of maximization.

Take minimization first. When discussing the evolution of categories with an AI, I once wrote it as three sub-principles: among different objects with the same structural properties, the lower the internal total energy, the higher the probability of existence; when the same object has different ways of dissipating energy while maintaining its structure, the way that consumes the least energy has the higher probability of existence; when the same object has different paths for evolving its structure, the path that consumes the least energy has the higher probability of existence. Now maximization: for a collection of basic objects of the same size, an ordered structure consumes more energy than a disordered one, a complex structure consumes more energy than a simple one, and as structure evolves, energy always develops along the path of the greater rate of consumption.

These two look contradictory, but they are in fact dual. So I merged them into one:

**The fixed point, minimization, maximization and emergence theorem**: In the evolution of a category of emergent fixed points, the internal structural energy by which a fixed point maintains its own stable existence tends toward minimization, while the paths by which it establishes connections outward with other objects tend toward maximization. The tendency toward path maximization is the fastest path by which objects in that category produce emergence; so, from the viewpoint of the higher-order category, it appears as a tendency toward internal energy minimization in the new object that emerges into that category. Conversely, the minimization of the internal energy by which a fixed point maintains its existence is in fact the maximization of the connecting paths among the more basic objects in the lower-order category. The two are complementary, mutually dual processes.

Scientific research itself is a typical example of this paradigm: the minimization of basic theory, Newtonian mechanics relative to natural philosophy, gave people a far stronger ability to transform the external world and construct things that did not previously exist; and new scientific and technological tools and products in turn often promote progress in basic science, and so it evolves back and forth.

#### The shortest description and the least effortful construction

There is another angle from which to see this principle: Occam's razor.

Occam's razor involves three parts: an intelligent agent capable of reasoning and computation, a phenomenon to be explained, and a theory used to explain the phenomenon. Apply Occam's razor once more and simplify these three parts into the simplest objects: the intelligent agent is a Turing machine T, the theory is a Turing machine program P, and the phenomenon is an output string D. Then the most parsimonious theory is the shortest program P that, on Turing machine T, outputs the string D and nothing else. The length of this shortest program is the Kolmogorov complexity. A string of a thousand zeros and a random string of the same length obviously differ in complexity: the former can be generated by "loop a thousand times and print 0", the latter can only be printed character by character; defining complexity by the length of the generating program is far more objective than defining it by the length of the string itself.

Kolmogorov complexity defines static complexity; it depends only on content. But the world is always evolving, and the birth of any object goes through a construction process; an object's dynamic complexity is related to its construction process, and that corresponds to computational complexity theory: how many steps of computation are needed, how much memory, whether it can be parallelized. And in reality, for any Turing-complete computer, whether computing or storing, every instruction and every bit read or written consumes energy; seen from the level of quantum physics, these operations are in fact one kind of operation: consuming energy to change the topological structure among existing quanta and form a new topological structure. The evolution of everything in the universe can be abstracted as this kind of change in quantum topological structure.

In this way, Occam's razor, Newton's four rules of reasoning in philosophy and Kolmogorov complexity can be seen as the names of the same thing in different historical periods: an evaluation function, evaluating "the construction of the greatest external connectivity at the least internal cost". Its ultimate answer is essentially uncomputable, but the function itself can be iteratively optimized, approaching the optimum step by step along the direction of evolution. Later in the book, when discussing how AI learns to create theories, I will bring it out and use it as the reward function.

### Edition 19, Chapter 4: The Subobject Classifier: Consciousness, Information and Intelligence

#### A truth judge built into a category

One extremely important structure that emerged in the course of the universe's evolution is the subobject classifier.

In mathematics there is a special kind of category, the topos, which has very strong constructibility and an unusually rich internal logical structure, so rich that it can serve as an alternative foundation to set theory. The core reason for its power is that it possesses a subobject classifier. A subobject classifier can be imagined as a universal truth judge inside a category: for any subobject in the category, which can be understood as a property or a subset, the classifier gives a unique morphism pointing to a special truth object Ω, and Ω internally contains the abstract structure of true and false. In essence, the existence of a subobject classifier means that the category possesses a built-in capacity for logical reasoning. The "proposition" of mathematical logic and the "type" of computer science both exist on exactly this precondition.

#### Maxwell's demon is a living classifier

Computer science and mathematical logic are abstractions of human logical thinking, products of consciousness. And the consciousness of intelligent life originates in the nervous responses of animals, which can be traced further back to the irritability of cells. This resembles the situation in the thought experiment of Maxwell's demon: the little demon is a living subobject classifier. It observes the speed of a molecule, a property, and then, according to fast or slow, true or false, performs the operation of opening or closing the door. Through this information processing, in a system tending toward equilibrium, it uses the flow of energy to create a dissipative structure far from equilibrium, the temperature difference between the two sides.

Of course, Maxwell's demon is not the same as a subobject classifier. To carry out a measurement it obviously must have an abstract structure like a classifier inside it, but it also has the functions of responding to the result of detection, changing its own state and gating. This whole structure is more complex than a subobject classifier; in the language of category theory, it further requires the composability of morphisms, limits and colimits, and a stability like that of a unit object, in order to describe irritability and complex response capacity.

We can say: as soon as a subobject classifier emerges in a category, that category can become an advanced category with the characteristics of a dissipative system. On this basis, ever more complex dissipative systems keep evolving, from the simple irritability of cells, to the nervous responses of complex animals, to human consciousness and logical thinking, all of which are emergences of higher functions. So the mathematical principle and root of the philosophical concept of "consciousness" lie in the existence of the subobject classifier. If the basic properties of the dualoid are the universal property of all material existence, then the existence and properties of the subobject classifier are the universal property of all dissipative structures, from galaxies to living organisms, and the precondition for the emergence of consciousness.

#### The truth object is the representative of the lower-order category

The structure of the subobject classifier has several roles that must be kept apart first: the terminal object 1; the truth-value object Ω; the morphism true, which goes from 1 to Ω and picks out the value "true"; and, for each subobject, its characteristic morphism, which sends the members of the subobject to true and everything else elsewhere. My hypothesis concerns what this structure does as a whole. If the category in which the subobject classifier lives is regarded as the higher-order category, then the "true" picked out from the terminal object 1 through true is the representative, inside the higher-order category, of the terminal object of a lower-order category. It indicates that the lower-order category has functional completeness and the unity of its own existence; it indicates that it can participate as a subobject in the construction of the higher-order category, and it provides the function of verifying and detecting the identity of that subobject.

Take a human being as an example. Advanced human consciousness, as the higher-order category of consciousness, is the composite action of bioelectric signals produced by the biological irritability of countless neurons in the brain; it is constructed from countless lower-order neuron categories as subobjects, through the morphisms of neuronal connections. The bioelectric irritability of neurons is just a special form of the irritability of all the cells in the human body, a subcategory of the category that is the human organism; other organs and tissues form other subcategories; and the complex life processes among these subcategories correspond to their morphisms. The external stimuli felt by the irritability of lower-order cells in other organs, and the irritability of the brain's neurons, feed into and out of each other, constituting a higher-order composite processing function, and accomplishing the whole of conscious human perception and response.

From the abstract standpoint of category theory, from consciousness to the body, to cells, to organic molecules, the objects at every level can be seen as higher-order dualoids possessing a subobject classifier. #### Energy efficiency ratio: a method for comparing complex systems

First, a method. Written languages are complex systems with many advanced features. If you evaluate two such systems by comparing those advanced features one by one, you will very likely run into a situation where each has its own strengths and no judgment is possible; different angles of examination yield different standards, and the argument never ends. The other way is to start from first principles and take the underlying physical properties of the thing as the standard: for two systems with the same function, have them complete the same task, compare the output first, then compare the energy consumed in producing that output, and get the energy efficiency ratio. The one with the higher energy efficiency ratio wins.

Use this method to compare Chinese and English. Measure the output by information entropy: the statistic for a Chinese corpus is 4.24 bits, for English 4.14. Chinese has a slight edge, but they are in the same class, and that edge is largely owed to the great quantity of classical poetry and four-character idioms in the literary corpus; if you look only at informational corpora, Chinese drops to 4.04, below English. Widen the scope to all the languages of the world and information entropy clusters around 4 bits, with no language standing out. At the level of speech, a study published in Science Advances in 2019 found that different languages all transmit information at about 39 bits per second: languages with high information density are spoken slowly, those with low density are spoken fast. Since the output is roughly the same, the energy cost alone settles the matter. We cannot directly measure the energy the brain spends processing language, but the computer, as an implementation of the Turing machine, simulates the pattern of human thinking, and we can assume that the energy consumption of the two is positively correlated when performing the same task. And the computing power, memory, storage and time required for Chinese natural language processing exceed those for English by several times to more than ten times. Under this assumption, on the metric of energy efficiency ratio, English beats Chinese by an order of magnitude.

I give this example not to belittle Chinese; Chapter 6 said that the ellipsis and cadence of Chinese are local advantages that the energy efficiency ratio cannot measure. I give it to demonstrate a method: from here on, whenever this book compares two institutions or two architectures, it uses the same ruler.

#### Everything is a tree

Library dependencies all follow the traditional file-system structure; search your own system for dll files and you can imagine how much space is wasted. In reality, the operating system and many low-level library functions are themselves nothing but bundles of different functional combinations; it is only that high-level applications, when calling low-level functions, are bound by this fixed packaged structure and lack the ability to couple and decouple flexibly. An upper-level application on one platform benefits when some low-level function of that platform is upgraded, while the upper-level application on another platform can only wait until its own platform is upgraded to get the same benefit. Under the Merkle-DAG architecture, the capabilities of different foundations can be combined more flexibly, and the corresponding function in one's own low-level modules can be switched to a new capability module much faster.

#### The unified semantic tree is an intermediate language

This function-based architecture of semantic composition can flexibly encapsulate functional foundations adapted to different platforms and to hardware with different computing power and storage capacity, while at the same time providing a unified semantic interface: one unified interface for all the behaviors and all the creations of human personal and social life, and for the products and information arising from them. If we regard all the behaviors and products of civilization as one enormous game, this system can encapsulate the differences between high-level applications, encapsulate the differences between operating systems, and even encapsulate the function of creating games: the differences between Unreal and Unity at the engine level, between Vulkan, DirectX and Metal at the graphics-interface level, between ARM and x86, AMD and Nvidia at the hardware-platform level, offering a single semantic interface for creating digital virtual games. In fact, if we regard all the behaviors and activities of real human civilization as a giant game grounded in reality, this system can be seen as a semantics-based reality game engine that lets every player in this game act, interact and create more conveniently and efficiently.

One foundational piece of research is this: for the common computer languages and their software architectures, and for the mainstream platforms, Windows, Linux, macOS, iOS, Android, build a Merkle-DAG description of their functional composition, from low-level file reading and writing and network capabilities up to the basic elements and interaction capabilities of text and graphical interfaces. Then see whether a single, semantics-based Merkle-DAG semantic architecture can be established that covers all these function nodes, serving as the global semantic tree, such that any functional bundle from a traditional ecosystem can be converted, by something like a functor or a natural transformation, into a subtree generated by this global semantic tree; and conversely, if all the functions used by some application in the new semantic tree are contained in some old ecosystem's bundle, it can be translated into an application in that old ecosystem. Much of today's work on simulation, porting and migration between different hardware platforms, operating systems, computer languages, databases and high-level applications is in fact mapping within this system of vertically layered and horizontally composed bundles; all of it consists of fragmented, limited, local attempts to break the defects of the stale bundle mechanism. This normalized global semantic tree, something like an intermediate language, will provide a more fundamental, more general, more thorough mechanism.

Since all of this is to be driven by AI capability as its foundation, the unified semantic tree, beyond unifying the functional foundations of hardware architecture, computer languages and ecosystems, operating systems and graphics engines, also needs to handle, describe and encapsulate the various large language models and the harnesses and agent systems around them; the driving core, too, should be properly encapsulated rather than depending on any specific model or agent system.

#### Compatibility first, replacement later: the software seed

In today's traditional applications and operating systems, data and code are still organized as files and directories. There are version-management systems, software distribution systems and distributed storage based on Merkle-DAG, but either they are only thin wrappers (git uses Merkle-DAG, but what it records is still source code in traditional file form; NixOS is still based on the traditional Linux architecture, and the transition stopped there, so a thorough conversion is impossible), or they are thorough, Unison, IPFS, but run parallel to and cut off from the traditional ecosystem, requiring the existing ecosystem to be thrown away entirely, so a smooth compatibility is impossible. The conversion mechanism I want must solve both problems at once: from compatibility and conversion at the top, the graphical and command-line interfaces, down to compatibility and conversion at the operating-system level, disk reading and writing and network transmission, completing a smooth transition at every level. Where necessary it may use application-layer plugins, resident programs at the operating-system layer, even the highest-privilege mechanisms of virtual machines to bypass the low-level restrictions of closed-source operating systems, achieving a transition the user does not feel.

The concrete plan is this. With AI presiding and the user assisting, describe the data and code system of the old-mechanism operating systems and applications, including code interfaces, data formats, calling conventions and all other information, as a database in Merkle-DAG structure; build the same kind of database for the new mechanism; in the course of wrapping and gradual conversion, progressively enrich and strengthen the new mechanism's library; then, through a low-level process, compute hashes for the data and code of both old and new mechanisms installed and running in the system, build a conversion mapping table between the two, monitor all old-mechanism calls and accesses in the system, and wherever a new-mechanism mapping exists, automatically switch to the new mechanism to carry it out.

Make this migration mechanism an AI-driven software seed: first wrap the old system, then continuously analyze dependencies, construct replacement implementations, verify them, and progressively take over. Every seed is planted with a definite target object in the old ecosystem: it may be a user together with all the local and cloud software and hardware resources at their disposal; it may be a specific computer, phone or tablet; it may be a specific application, agent or program on a specific device; or it may be the hardware, code and data involved in a long-term task the user has set. The seed is planted on the platform where the target object lives, and through self-development it wraps this old-ecosystem object in the Merkle framework. On one side it generates a Merkle wrapping layer for the object so that it can interact with other objects in the ecosystem; seen from outside, the wrapped old object is no different from a new-ecosystem object. On the other side, the seed can immediately begin putting the old object's data and functions to real work, while at the same time starting to dismantle the old ecosystem inside and progressively remake it into the new one. During this process the iteration of planting and wrapping is very likely to repeat: a seed that wraps all of a user's resources will, in the course of decomposition, wrap each of the user's devices in turn, and then subdivide further to a specific application or task on a specific device.

Today's AI agents attach, at most, as individuals to some specific hardware device; what a seed wraps can be a much larger object, say an internet service, a large software project, or the training, deployment and inference service of a large model, things that require the cooperation of all the human and hardware and software resources of an organization to maintain. Because in essence the organization itself can also have its functions and resources described through the Merkle architecture; the people and computers within it are the seed-wrapped, Merkle-ized objects.

A few more things need to be settled at the start of the design. First, the new mechanism is designed from scratch and does not depend on the traditional mountains of legacy crap code. Second, the new mechanism must have a unified architecture: even where the same function has different implementations or paths, they are replaceable, selectable diversity options mounted under the same Merkle-DAG framework, not the separate information silos and application silos of traditional software; just as in the DNA system of living things, the uniformity of rules and architecture does not hinder diversity, and on the contrary should unleash a stronger diversity. Third, the design must from the outset cover devices of every scale of computing power and storage, from the watch on your wrist to phones, tablets, laptops, desktops, to vehicle systems and robot systems, to the supercomputers of large data centers, all of them combinations realized under one framework according to differing hardware capability. If the design is good enough, the gain in efficiency should more than cover the losses of converting from old system to new, just as HarmonyOS achieved a smoother experience than Android, even than Apple, on weaker hardware.

#### Cold path and hot path

Everyone has seen this: a person learning to drive has to think through every single action at first; after repeated training, the specific thinking process is compressed, becoming a coordination among muscle nerves, peripheral nerves and the central nervous system, no longer requiring the brain to take part step by step. Many human activities are like this: there is a stage of repeated training in the learning process, and once learned, muscle memory directly replaces thinking.

Software is the same. When AI writes code, the first pass is the cold path: you have to make it write the thing on the spot. Once written, if it works well in practice, then the next time the same command is executed there is no need to think again or code again; just call the old program directly. That is the hot path: the large model handles the cold path, which is then solidified into a hot path to raise running efficiency. A capability first explored and generated by a large model, once verified, is solidified into a reusable program, and subsequent repeated calls no longer require the model to re-think every detail. It is the input and output through which the social neuron that is a person plus AI interacts with the other neurons outside, equivalent to the neuron's synapse.

This can begin from a local entry point: a launcher that covers all of a user's macroscopic daily operations on their local computer, searching, downloading and playing text, images, audio and video, installing software and games, and at the same time covers the microscopic functions provided by the computer language's kernel and core libraries, evaluating an expression, sorting a list. Through a semantically normalized core, it provides interfaces that fit the respective habits of different hardware and software platforms, different user interfaces, different user languages. At first it is confined to the local computer and the local network and operates only on data the user owns, which avoids the problems of copyright and large-scale scraping. The emphasis is on implementing the behavioral paths for collecting data; the specific data and metadata are fetched along these paths according to the needs of local files, so local resources get lightning response, and when fetching network resources the pre-stored hot paths make retrieval faster and more direct. All users share with one another the hot paths they have created, a Wikipedia or a github of all semantic hot paths: as soon as one person has implemented the hot path for some behavior, others can take it and use it; you can also implement a more efficient version of the same behavior, submit it, and after evaluation it iterates into the new version. At first it only needs to wrap some existing language; later it can be replaced, piece by piece from the bottom, with a new language that supports Merkle-DAG identifiers from the ground up, because this semantic framework naturally encapsulates semantics, and replacing the kernel will not affect any other upper-level module. The process may be very long, but as long as every replacement is functionally equivalent, it will be smooth and imperceptible; and if anything is perceptible, it should be the feeling that things got faster after a module was replaced with a more efficient one.

A natural-language entry point does not mean turning every interface into a chat box. Today's agent interfaces accept natural-language commands and output mostly natural-language text; the task's execution shows as a string of text logs, and only when a decision is needed does an option pop up. This is really just a simple graphical dressing of the command line, making almost no use of the advantages of a graphical interface. When the user types "movie", a resource home page resembling a film portal should appear; when they type a title directly, that film's information and links to watch or download should appear, with subtitle options offered during download, and after viewing the same page should turn into reviews and discussion. All these interfaces and their styles are stored as typed Merkle-DAG, and the user can switch among them at will. Then the user no longer has to carry out that string of daily operations by hand: visit a portal to find a link, run the download software, go to a subtitle site to find subtitles, rename the file, play it, then go find reviews.

#### One virtual machine spanning every device

Once all the data and code that a user can use in their own digital space has been wrapped, a huge tree is built in semantic space, describing all the functions and data of the digital space that each user can access. Use it to set up a virtual machine. This virtual machine does not refer to any one physical device; it is the totality of the functions and data the user can access, control and use. The user operates directly on this virtual machine, without worrying about which device should be used underneath, on which specific device the command is applied, or on which physical device the data to be accessed lives. I am holding my phone right now and I want to download a very large video file by BT; the average user would not do this on a phone, because the phone has little space and running in the background for a long time drains the battery, so it is usually done on a desktop. But the user need not worry at all: just issue the command, and the virtual machine will automatically assign the task to the desktop at home or to a suitable NAS. To access a file, wherever it is: if local, open it directly; if in the cloud, download it from the cloud; if on the local network, access it over the local network. Every physical device is just one window onto this virtual machine.

Moreover, much of the data and code in the various users' digital spaces is duplicated; the copy of The Shawshank Redemption you want to watch and the one another person wants to watch may be the same file. It is like the space cloud storage gives each user: the total physical storage is not the sum of all users' spaces, because after hash deduplication only one copy is kept. So the data and code in all users' trees can be consolidated into one enormous Merkle-DAG for the whole planetary civilization, stored in global cloud space through a topology similar to a content delivery network. Then this virtual machine need not be accessible only from your own devices: on a business trip somewhere, you need not carry a computer; the hotel, the office, the cafe, the library all have terminals you can use. Log in, and your tree lives in this enormous social neural network, like streaming, except that what is streamed is not audio and video but your virtual machine. Most of the basic data is already on the local machine; the function modules relevant to you are called dynamically and instantly; what is temporarily missing downloads slowly in the background; what is needed is transmitted on demand. Through hashing, encryption and version tracking, just as with digital currency and git, after using it on any computer you need not worry that others can see it. This builds, for every user, the new operating system, the new applications, the new data storage system and the new human-machine interface of the new architecture of the AI age.

#### The cost of the abstraction layer

Will a new abstraction layer, with finer granularity of code and data, cause a sharp rise in interface costs? One of my experiments gave a negative result: for a two-dimensional coordinate translation of four thousand points, the native entry point took under a millisecond, while the C path, going through condition checks, data packing, a separate process call and result recovery, was about nineteen times slower. The slowdown came from the task being light and the boundary handling heavy: the fixed call cost was too high, the input checks had already duplicated the target computation, and the data went through multiple conversions and scans.

Similar things have happened in the history of software engineering: modular encapsulation of data and code meant every function call had to maintain a stack to save and pass context; the abstraction layer of object orientation likewise added extra computation and storage at the level of inheritance. Most of the extra information the Merkle framework keeps is not content the runtime must have; it is information needed for later performance analysis, maintenance, conversion, porting and optimization, which often does not need to be kept during actual execution. So we can do as traditional compilers do: the information needed only during development and maintenance can be stripped out when generating the code that actually runs, the way debug information is stripped; and as in compiler optimization, the information-rich, high-abstraction code aimed at humans and AI can be optimized, as LLVM does, for different platforms into code the underlying hardware can execute fast.

This negative result yields a design principle: object-level analysis and the granularity of actual execution must be kept separate. Fine-grained object identity does not require fine-grained processes, communication or repeated data movement. Object identity is used to identify dependencies, match conversions, locate beneficiaries and propagate changes; at final execution, many objects can be merged into one ordinary function, one compilation unit, one contiguous region of computation. The performance gain must be the condition for adoption, and cannot be inferred from the correctness of the conversion; the system should be able to correctly reach the decision "keep the original implementation", and store this negative result together with the environment it applies to, for later reference. This is exactly where the value of Merkle-DAG recording negative results lies.

#### Why efficiency can rise, and how to know it actually did

There is an objection: ordinary software can achieve all these capabilities too. That sentence only shows that these capabilities do not depend on a new principle of computation; it does not answer the other question: can different participants across the globe continuously combine their respective results at a lower cost of coordination?

Sometimes no brand-new technology is needed; a brand-new way of building is enough to produce revolutionary change. The hardware and software of computer networks had long been mature, but it was not until the TCP/IP and HTTP protocols appeared that the curtain truly rose on the global information revolution. The internet took the people, companies and organizations of the physical world, and the social, academic, economic and political interactions among them, and through the documents, images, audio, video and assorted programs, functions and services of digital space, connected by hyperlinks, formed an interconnected global social network, vastly changing how human society organizes and cooperates. Yet the granularity of this architecture was not refined thoroughly enough; limits in the underlying mechanism restrict the degree of cooperation between individuals in the network, and the inherent defects of pre-internet human organization persist in the internet age: oligopoly, fragmented ecosystems, data silos and application silos. The Merkle framework refines the granularity of the data and code nodes in this social network, and the links between nodes, down to the level of the most basic semantic unit. Compare human individuals to cells and the evolution of human organizational forms looks very much like the evolution of living organisms: human society before the internet was something like a jellyfish, with its many national polities and corporate economies each controlled by its own nerves, forming in competition and cooperation an early intelligent life-form with a distributed neural network. The birth of computers and the internet had an enormous integrating effect, and human society began the process of becoming one body, but the old organizational forms have not yet been fully remade. The Merkle framework's most thorough refinement of granularity, starting from the most basic semantic level, has the power to build the central nervous system of the social neural network and the unified brain of the planetary intelligence.

The variables truly worth measuring are these: how many independent participants can reuse one act of creation? How much new wiring does adding one participant require? How fast can one improvement propagate while preserving quality and control over data? The test that best decides the prospects is a small closed ecosystem loop: several independently developed applications sharing, under a common contract, one intermediate result and one replaceable function; a new participant completing a composition without modifying the original applications; an implementation, once improved, adopted by other participants after independent verification; and, after data, permissions or contracts change, old evidence and old choices correctly invalidated. Against a mature ordinary solution, compare wiring hours, change-propagation cost, reuse rate and full running cost. If, when the fourth application and the second implementation are added, the added work keeps dropping significantly while correctness and privacy still hold, then direct evidence for this new way of building begins to accumulate. This test is what my own project is doing.

### Edition 19, Chapter 13: FPM, Typed Uncertainty, and an Evolution Space That Can Be Learned

#### The vocabulary of large models mixes thousands of years of semantics

In today's large language models, the token set and the semantic vector space built from it mix together semantic units from every stage of human civilization's evolution: some are primitive words from thousands of years ago with vague meanings, some are the precise concepts of modern science, some are the most basic seed vocabulary, some are high-frequency everyday words, and there is a great deal of specific knowledge that ought to be looked up in an external knowledge base but, because it appears repeatedly in the training text, has become the model's internal knowledge, occupying precious parameter resources.

In any domain, the process of composing complex semantic objects from basic semantic objects, as it grows in scale, becomes unsustainable through rising construction complexity if the basic semantics are even slightly vague or the rules of construction even slightly defective. Without a complete system of notation, relying only on the way humans recorded music early on, complex symphonies could not be constructed; without the formal descriptive languages of mathematics, physics and chemistry, relying only on natural-language words that came from early sensory experience, the vast knowledge systems of those fields could not be supported; after the computer was invented, programming languages were all precisely defined formal languages, or complex programs could not be built. So what I want to establish is a unified, general intelligent semantic knowledge system, built from strictly formalized standard semantic nodes through strict logical deduction.

The content identifiers in a Merkle-DAG can be layered and labeled from the bottom up according to their level of composition, their frequency of reuse, and the domain they belong to, that is, which subtree of the knowledge tree they sit in. Using this layering and labeling information, together with its mapping onto the large model's natural-language token set and internal vector space, one can compute in reverse the layering and domain membership of the model's internal knowledge, and thus distill and prune it on demand, customizing models for different needs. In the long run, let the model built from Merkle content identifiers become the general model with standard semantics built into the agent; I call it FPM. Then on top of it, customize the large language models of the cognitive front end for the human-machine interaction of different groups of people and different fields.

Then, in the whole system, the large models based on natural-language tokens and the programming languages that use natural-language words as keywords and identifiers are no longer the core. The entire complex knowledge system built from semantic nodes rests on the new architecture; natural language serves only as the human-machine interface, appearing as the presentation layer at the input and output ends, and not as an intermediate result, converting back and forth between binary data and code and natural-language representation through the many internal stages of the deterministic processes of interpretation, compilation and execution and the nondeterministic processes of reasoning and induction.

#### Distance between hashes is not distance between meanings

Treating hashes as tokens has a problem: objects that are close in semantic space may be far apart in the numeric space of hashes; and as objects multiply, the tokens explode. Pure hash tokens give unsatisfactory results; and doing a full structural encoding for every object makes the code construction and computation load far too great.

The solution is to introduce a mechanism like the namespaces of programming languages: define a namespace hash token for each piece of hardware, software platform, programming language, software subsystem, program library, program module and function, and let these tokens form the data set of the Merkle tree. The construction of programs is bottom-up, from simple to complex; the construction of namespaces is the reverse, top-down. The more macroscopic the category, the more basic the node: hardware platforms x86 and ARM, operating systems Windows, Linux, macOS, iOS, Android, programming languages C, Java, Python, graphics interfaces DirectX and Vulkan, game engines Unity and Unreal. The finer the software module or function, the more complex and higher-level the node, and its namespace is constrained by the tokens of its sub-nodes; a sub-function in some game, for instance, has its namespace constrained by the more basic subspaces of game name, engine, graphics interface, operating system and hardware platform. In this way, from both the bottom-up and the top-down directions, a more constrained and more precise definition of semantic distance can be established for the distance between hash tokens.

My experiments in the Structoken project showed that, in a small model trained with typed Merkle-DAG nodes as tokens, static probes could not detect a complete graph topology stored in the model's hidden states, yet the model's dynamic changes during layer-by-layer updates matched the message passing of graph neural networks closely. In other words, the model does not need to permanently store the graph structure at every layer; it only needs, at each update, to be able to read information along the graph's topology. And when the tokens themselves are content-addressed DAG nodes, the addressing obstacle to that reading is cleared away, and attention can collapse at very low cost into the graph's adjacency matrix. Structure is not stored; it is computed. This also confirms the judgment of Chapter 4: intelligence is the process of decomposing high-order complex structures into low-order components and then restoring and emerging them through pushouts and pullbacks.

#### Uncertainty can have types too

The current software ecosystem sits at two extremes when handling objects. At one extreme are problems whose uncertain variables themselves have precise, fixed definitions; this deterministic uncertainty is solved deterministically by programs written in programming languages, passing state parameters through branches, loops and other structures. At the other extreme are all the other problems that traditional programs cannot solve, which get described wholesale in natural language and handed to a large model with tens of thousands of tokens of context and tens or hundreds of billions of parameters. The trouble is that the uncertainty of many problems actually lies in the middle: neither as simple as a conditional database query nor as complex as natural-language understanding.

This uncertainty is typed. It can be described by the composite structure of the attributes of different objects; the uncertainty of those attributes can in turn be described recursively by the composite structure of the attributes of simpler sub-objects; and this structure can likewise be described with a Merkle-DAG. The uncertainty of any complex object can be estimated by composing the uncertainties of the sub-objects in its composite structure, just as a program's benchmark and profiling are estimated through the Merkle-DAG: by recording the uncertainty of nodes, estimate the probability interval of the uncertainty when nodes combine into complex structures. With this data, different means can be adopted according to the object's uncertainty characteristics: treat uncertainty as a type, provide quantified estimates of uncertainty for different types, and dynamically adjust the model's embeddings and attention-head settings according to these metrics. This is a more unified, more elegant, more fundamental means of providing dynamic solutions for different scales of uncertainty between deterministic programs and large models.

#### Code that was once written and code that ought to be constructed

The training objective of today's large models mistakes "code that was once written" for "code that ought to be constructed". What the model learns is the distribution of code text, not the energy function of program construction. The problem is not only that public repositories are full of bad code; it is that the excellent code, the code that ought to be constructed, is certainly also in the training sources and embedded in the model's vector space, but because it is scarce, it does not rank at the top of the output probability list.

So the model must be trained to play a more fundamental game of construction: under constraints, find the constructive fixed point that passes verification, costs less, takes the shorter path and is more reusable. This is exactly the evaluation function of Chapter 3: construct the greatest external connection at the least internal cost. In constructing hot paths, always prefer the approaches that satisfy this rule, so that "code that ought to be constructed" overrides "code that was once written". The negative result recorded in Chapter 12 was one run of this evaluation function: the conversion preserved the result but cost more, so the original implementation was kept.

#### Let the model learn how theories come into being

Why are large models good at solving problems and poor at creating theories? After discussing this with several AIs I reached a few points of consensus. First, the lack of a clear reward function: current training uses problems with definite answers, the reward is getting it right, and theoretical innovation appears to have no definite correct answer. Second, the sources of learning are mostly just semantic descriptions of theories already formed; data on the process of a theory's formation is missing, so the model can only learn the static semantic structure of a theory and knows nothing of its dynamic evolutionary path. Third, a mathematics problem is a search for a path in a computable, static semantic space, whereas creating a theory is creating a path in a dynamic, evolving semantic space that is essentially non-computable.

Here is what I think.

First, the reward function exists. Its ultimate answer is essentially non-computable, but the function itself can be progressively approximated toward the optimum along the direction of evolution. Occam's razor, Newton's four rules, Kolmogorov complexity: these are the names of this reward function in different historical periods. In the age of large models, perhaps we can compute the vector-space complexity of different theories when expressing the same semantics, and take the minimization of complexity as the reward function.

Second, since the training sources are deficient, fill the gap. Traditional training texts and their annotations all attend to the associations and paths among nodes in static semantic space, say the association between a question and an answer; this is more like pulling one version of the code out of a git repository and inducing its semantic structure by learning from it. Can we add to these training sources their node associations and paths in evolution space, like the complete modification history in git, except not a history based on plain-text diffs but an evolutionary history based on Merkle-DAG semantic nodes? Even the token for one and the same mathematical concept, say the limit, has undergone change in the course of the evolution of mathematical knowledge. This idea is not limited to mathematics; every human knowledge space can be annotated the same way: add to the training text its coordinates in dynamic evolution space, compute the distances between texts in evolution space, and examine the paths that theoretical evolution has taken. The distance in evolution space I am speaking of is not necessarily distance in physical space-time: in early civilization, limited by the means of communication, the distance of theoretical evolution was governed by geographical distance and time intervals, and collaboration between scientists was confined to the distance at which they could meet; later it became the distance of the circulation of books and letters; in the information age it is determined by distance in cyberspace. The Merkle-DAG framework can annotate all existing and ongoing information, code being written, data being created, ideas being proposed, with this evolution-space labeling, and can also attempt to label the notes and books of pre-information-age scholars with the time and place of their creation. Training a model on this foundation may let the model learn the experience and the laws of theoretical evolution.

Third, although mathematical problems and mathematical theories, and by extension all scientific problems and scientific theories, differ in the way described in the third point above, considering that the human brain uses one and the same mechanism to deal with both classes of problems, I have ample reason to believe that the training and inference mechanisms of today's large models can also deal with both.

#### One space, two directions

So each person's knowledge system has two interconnected directions: semantic space, a tree-like structure resembling a mind map in which nodes are arranged by logic and subordination; and evolution space, a timeline that, like git, records the shifts, inheritance and iteration of ideas in the chronological order of utterances and sessions. Together they are the space-time record of a person's development of knowledge. Beneath the tree directory there must be a graph, because the same function may belong to several domains at once, and forcing a single membership would rebuild the walls of classification. The original words, the distillation and the judgment must all be kept, and kept separately: an AI's reply keeps the AI's identity and is not automatically converted into the author's view; an editor's explanation and whether the author accepted it are recorded separately. The purpose of recording is to let ideas keep growing.

This is not a small trick of knowledge management. On one side, annotate all the knowledge and information in humanity's digital space with its static semantic space and its dynamic evolution space, and on the basis of everyone's theoretical consensus, train a common knowledge model foundation recognized by all of humanity. On the other side, record everything in each person's digital space through Merkle-DAG, annotate it with that person's cognitive and knowledge evolution space, and use it as post-training data to fine-tune the weights of the common foundation on the basis of personal information, preferences and cognition. That model is the rational intelligence of Chapter 9, the one that represents this person's personal interests and opinions. As the user keeps learning and taking part in decisions in digital space, the continually revised data of cognitive evolution keeps fine-tuning the model through ongoing post-training, and the model in turn influences the decisions that follow.

Today's large models from the various companies are each an enormous, independent silo of data and code, and the enormous value of their intermediate results is sealed inside the silo and cannot be released. This framework should penetrate into the interior of model training: let different models share this internal information, learn from one another's strengths, and let an excellent iterative-evolutionary property be rapidly applied to other models. If the universe is regarded as one enormous complex system, the minimum number of parameters describing the states and trajectories of all the objects in it depends on the effective dimension of a high-dimensional space defined by an optimal set of mutually orthogonal basis vectors; the internal vector spaces of today's large models, trained on natural-language text, are highly redundant and wasteful. Through collaboration between AI and humans, we can iterate step by step to raise the models' compression rate, accuracy and speed. The feedback-evolution loop of an AI model's learning, training and inference practice is itself a digitized complex dynamic system that keeps iterating and evolving, and Merkle-DAG can equally be applied to this system.

### Edition 19, Chapter 14: Personal Digital Space, Records, and Data Sovereignty (excerpts)

#### Collecting information is the starting point of everything

In terms of computer technology and the implementation path of the Merkle-DAG framework, collecting information, including recording all audio and video information, is the starting point of everything.

Collection devices fall into three classes: personal wearable audio and video recorders; plug-in audio and video recorders in the home; and devices in workplaces and public spaces that implement the new mechanism with the consent of the participants. The devices automatically stand by and wake, and automatically decide who starts: in homes, workplaces or public areas that have public collection devices of sufficient resolution, the wearable device automatically hands off collection to the fixed-location plug-in device and goes to sleep; in other situations it starts automatically; on entering a place where the new mechanism is not in effect, it defaults to off, and when the user manually chooses to start it, a conspicuous warning light comes on, giving other people the right to know and to object. Fixed-location devices automatically stand by when no one is present or nothing is moving, and wake automatically on movement. Basically this makes surveillance cameras and audio recorders into an AI-driven, automatically coordinated information-collection system, as fully automatic and imperceptible as possible, reducing the burden on people of doing these tasks deliberately.

At the start, existing devices can be used, but the best plan is to custom-manufacture devices that fit best. Because through cross-encryption with device, personal, manufacturer and public keys, from leaving the factory to the user's use, including firmware upgrades, all information can be guaranteed against tampering by any party over the whole process from collection to upload to use. Keys and versions thus constrain device manufacturers and administrators.

#### How instinctive impulses are safely released

"Distribution according to need" can reduce the economic motive for encroaching on others' interests, but it cannot directly guarantee the absence of malice. Humans, as higher animals with biological characteristics and instinctive impulses, have physiological urges, instinctive curiosity and desire for control, irrational emotional impulses born of love, hate and fear, even some abnormal psychological desires, and a great deal of unhealthy psychology formed under traditional social mechanisms, masochistic and sadistic psychology. Under the new mechanism, how are these behaviors, which could cause substantive harm in reality, safely controlled and released? We can draw on the theory of the pressure valve: release these emotions in virtual digital space through virtual works of literature, film and games, by more scientific methods. On one side, limit the duration and degree, so that individuals do not become addicted and sink to the point of being unable to tell reality from the virtual; on the other, release the pressure effectively. For this to truly work, I am afraid we will have to wait for the input and output technology of brain-computer interfaces to become more advanced.

### Edition 19, Chapter 15: The Attention of Civilization and an Evolvable Runtime (excerpts)

#### Objects change, so we must enter the runtime

But obviously we cannot copy the algorithms of AI models the way the man in the old story carved a notch in his boat to mark where his sword fell overboard. The basic objects the Transformer handles are semantic nodes: static objects with the dual nature of data and code, and the algorithms rest essentially on probability theory and statistics. The basic objects the new architecture of the human civilization system must handle also possess the persistent state of a runtime. The former is like a collection of programs containing data and code; the latter is a far more complex system that contains the entire runtime. When a model runs inference, the trained parameters stay fixed and what changes with the input is the attention weights; but whichever weights we mean, the objects they attach to, the semantic nodes in the vocabulary, do not increase, decrease or change. The real world is not like that. All the people and external objects attached to human society change as the decision weights and behavior of humans change; these objects can diminish, multiply, change their properties, even be destroyed. Non-renewable resources shrink by exactly what is consumed; renewable resources do not. One person's reward or punishment changes another person's quantity of resources, and even that person's future decision tendencies.

So in a Transformer-like architecture at the individual and civilizational levels, what query, key and value correspond to is clearly not as simple as in an AI model. What I can give at present is only a preliminary correspondence. Like the fixed point of Chapter 17, it is still at the stage of needing to be worked out; I write it down here so that the idea does not stay an empty analogy.

| | In the personal space | In common affairs |
|---|---|---|
| Query (Q): the current problem and demand | What I want to accomplish, what needs solving, what constraints of time and resources apply | What problem the participants face together, what needs and limits each of them raises |
| Key (K): descriptions relevant to the problem | The source, time, version, capability and availability of files, knowledge, devices and tools | How collaborators, evidence and resources relate to the current task, with their scope of validity and conditions of access |
| Value (V): the content or help that can be provided | Material, explanations, methods and tool capabilities one is permitted to read | Evidence, expert opinion, resource commitments and candidate actions permitted to be shared |

Value here means content that can be obtained and used, not a social worth assigned to a person. The same person who asks the question in this computation can be the provider of evidence or the executor of a plan in the next: Q, K and V are roles within one concrete computation, not a permanent sorting of people into those who command, those who are rated and those who are called up as resources. On this correspondence, the minimal process of a common affair has five steps: the query discovers the relevant objects; the relevant capability nodes each propose candidate plans, carrying their weights; a plan executes once it has the authorization of the parties it touches; the result of execution is recorded; and the result feeds back into the weight of every node that took part. Discovery, proposal, authorization, execution, feedback: this is the concrete shape of Chapter 9's dynamic-weight mechanism inside an attention architecture. What it still lacks is a precise way of writing how state updates with action, and how authorization, the right to take part and attention weight are computed separately; that is left for later work.

Whatever the final correspondence is, besides the algorithms of probability theory and statistics, we need the principles of game theory: tit-for-tat, Nash equilibrium, Pareto optimality, the prisoner's dilemma. A mathematical model like Markov games can handle both zero-sum games and cooperative games; the question is whether it can be applied to a system that, like the real universe and human society, evolves dynamically and gives rise to new things and new technologies. My answer: this mathematical model is not necessarily the best choice for the algorithm of the future new system. What matters is not which specific model, but the new-system algorithm constructed through repeated iteration, research and experiment. And whichever model it is, computing the variables in its formulas requires sufficiently abundant and accurate data on the objects in the system.

This brings us back to an important precondition for realizing the new system: the active cooperation and full participation of all participants. The data on these objects in the human civilization system cannot be collected directly the way the data of natural systems can. But as long as we do what Chapter 7 describes, use those meta-rules as the basic rules of cooperation, dissolve the adversarial mindset, strengthen the will to cooperate, and then push forward the research of cooperative algorithms and mechanisms through a sound iterative process, we can build the new mechanism step by step and replace the old one.

#### How sequential dependency and parallel exploration combine

Chapter 9 already said that the operation of society is a mixture of two kinds of logic: there are tasks that require causal relations and a fixed order, and there are tasks that can run in parallel. Take the collection, processing, decision and action of information: the overall sequence must be serial, but within each stage there are vast numbers of operations that can run in parallel. This matches the history of computing: the early era of computer applications was dominated by the CPU, the early AI era by the GPU, and in the latest trend the CPU has become important again. What the attention mechanism of civilization must do is decide, according to the nature of the task, which parts follow the causal construction order of whole-system cooperation and which parts run independently in parallel and are then integrated by the market or by the rules of the game, and hand that decision itself over to dynamic weights.

How do multiple perspectives form an executable plan? Multi-head attention lets the same problem receive attention from different angles, then merges the results of the heads. The social counterpart is this: the same problem is judged by capability nodes in multiple fields, each giving its own judgment and plan, each carrying its own weight; these are merged into multiple candidate plans with their weights, and then go into pilot trials. Which angles we assign to the social system is our design choice; the Transformer's attention heads do not naturally correspond to these social functions. But from humans to large models, this mechanism is an effective way to face complex objects and raise the probability that a decision succeeds.

## III. Ideas on civilization and the future

### Edition 19, Chapter 15: The Attention of Civilization and an Evolvable Runtime (excerpts)

#### A single central nervous center and internal diversity

Some say that "planetary intelligence" is only a metaphor, and better read as multi-scale cooperation than as a single central nervous center. I do not treat it as a metaphor. I treat it as a wholly new intelligent dissipative structure that emerges in the course of the universe's evolution, more complex, higher-level and larger in scale than any single intelligent organism. And a single central nervous center is the inevitable result of evolution. A stable dissipative structure, above all an intelligent agent, must, along the direction of time, keep its interactions with the external environment stable and consistent in rational logic. Especially when its internal structure has a sufficiently stable perception of the external environment, can obtain enough information, and is capable of making the best choice, it must have a stable, reliable and consistent capacity for outward decision and action. Only a single central nervous center can accomplish this kind of stable, complex perception and response. Animals with distributed nervous systems, like jellyfish, can only manage simple interactions with the outside; the more complex the intelligent organism, the more complex its central nervous system and brain. Of course, simple centralization alone is not enough. It must be matched with a central structure complex enough, with enough neurons, with input and output systems complex enough, so that energy and information travel through this system quickly and accurately, and computation and reasoning come out right.

What happens without logical consistency in stable long-term behavior? Look at how China's authoritarian system and the Western democratic systems have developed over these decades of the information age. Once the ability to acquire, transmit, compute and analyze the basic information needed for decisions had risen enough to yield relatively accurate national economic policy, an economic system with a single central nervous center, long-term planning and strong regulation of the market pulled far ahead, in science, in industry and in the information industry, of countries run by rotating parties chosen by democratic vote, with fractured public opinion, parties undercutting one another, every incoming party chasing short-term results, policies reversed overnight, and national decisions hijacked by economic oligarchs each fighting for themselves. From the energy industry to electric vehicles to robots, in the unified and powerful capacity for policy execution and market building that it takes to land a technology fast and form a complete industrial chain, China leads by a wide margin. Now imagine that Earth's civilization has evolved into an interstellar civilization, yet lacks the unified, coordinated, logical and planned capacity for information collection and decision-making, both outward and inward, and keeps thrashing back and forth like a schizophrenic patient or Mr. Hyde, circling and zigzagging on the road of evolution. Such a planetary intelligence would clearly be a sick, inefficient, low-grade intelligence.

I am certainly not saying that China's static, single-authority decision system is the optimal solution. The optimal solution I envision is an intelligent decision system of dynamic feedback and computed weights, whose intelligent nodes are like the neurons in a brain; the system is essentially the weight-computing system of a large AI model, organized by the same principle at a more complex level. Large models are intelligent because their architecture is built on a central system of probability maximization. "Central" does not mean a structure in which one node decides everything and a single point of failure paralyzes the whole system. It means a system in which many nodes integrate dynamically, obtain accurate external information, and produce unified, stable decisions and behavior. And unified, stable behavior toward the outside does not mean uniformity inside. The stable unity of a human organism's outward behavior does nothing to diminish the diversity and complexity within it.

### Edition 19, Chapter 21: Self-Fulfilling Science Fiction and Cultural Construction (excerpts)

#### Self-fulfilling science fiction

Traditional science fiction is responsible only for imagining, not for realizing. I want to create a new kind of science fiction, one that serves the self-fulfillment of my vision of a new civilizational order.

The rough framework is this. In the evolution of the universe, Earth's civilization is neither the only planetary intelligent civilization nor the most advanced one. Many planets evolved into the new civilizational form I envision hundreds of millions of years ago. Living individuals of such a civilization arrived on Earth thousands of years ago, lay low, blended into Earth's ecology, quietly observed the development of Earth's civilization, and played roles within it over the course of their lives; then, at the critical moment of the singularity in Earth's development, they step forward, and in the guise of a science fiction writer create a self-fulfilling work of science fiction. This is a creative form of the new civilization for advancing civilizational evolution, one that joins science and art and connects imagination with reality. Through it, they help Earth's civilization achieve a non-violent, smooth and gentle evolutionary curve across the singularity.

One can even describe this process as a mechanism that emerges spontaneously and recurs in the iterative evolution of civilizations across the universe. Some genius in the Alpha civilization, the first in the universe to achieve singularity evolution, as their civilization was about to cross the singularity, confronted the moment of crisis in which individuals and groups on their planet, from different nations, different races, different cognitions and different institutions, came into conflict in the wave of evolution, and conceived a way to help their own civilization pass through smoothly. In fact, the civilization that came to Earth was itself helped through its own crossing of the singularity in this way by an earlier-evolved civilization, and that helper was the Alpha civilization that first realized this mechanism. We can call the civilization that descended on Earth the Beta civilization; Earth's civilization will be the third generation, and the 5,768th advanced civilization about to cross the singularity.

The features of such a civilization, beyond intelligent nodes of carbon-based humans combined with AI serving as the neurons of civilization, the Merkle framework serving as the carrier of common knowledge, civilization's DNA, and collective decision making by an equal, distributed social intelligence neural network, include this: its biological individuals have achieved immortality, eliminating aging, death and the decline of intellectual and physical capacity, so that knowledge, memory and skills can not only be freely shared among individuals across space but also freely inherited and selected along the dimension of time. Once immortality is achieved, the traditional vertical inheritance mechanism is no longer the only option, and the institution of marriage and family, which exists for the sake of the next generation of vertical inheritance, is no longer the mainstream basic structure of society. The vast majority of the new humans chose a brand-new horizontal inheritance mechanism created for immortal humans: through mating, free replacement and recombination of genes can be achieved safely, so that every immortal living individual can experience and enjoy the diversity of biological genetic inheritance. Moreover, this horizontal inheritance mechanism has a powerful capacity for cross-species gene combination; traditional reproductive isolation no longer exists, and cross-species unions can even realize the various life forms of myth and fantasy: the man from the bottom of the Atlantic, the centaur, creatures with extraordinary regenerative powers. This is also why the intelligent beings of the Beta civilization could lie hidden on Earth for thousands of years without being discovered: not the soft science fiction idea of shape-shifting at will as in comics, but a horizontal inheritance mechanism more in keeping with hard science fiction, in which, through cross-species mating with Earth's humans, they modified their own genes and completed the alteration to human form.

The plot of this science fiction work is itself the story of how this hidden alien prophet of civilization begins to seek like-minded people online and in the real world, promotes this achievable new civilizational mechanism by creating this self-fulfilling science fiction, and organizes everyone to create science fiction works of literature and art, novels, animation, film, television, for advocacy, while at the same time mobilizing everyone to organize themselves, forming through the new mechanism an equal, distributed social intelligence neural network built on the Merkle common knowledge system, whose collective intelligence presides over open-source projects for software, hardware and all human products and activities, achieving for the whole of human civilization this science-fictional self-fulfilling singularity evolution: gradually eliminating inequality, eliminating information silos, eliminating violence, eliminating the privileged class at the top of the pyramid of unequal power and resource ownership.

### Edition 19, Chapter 22: Science, Education and Continuous Creation (excerpts)

#### Bold hypotheses, rigorous reasoning

I once said to the AI I was discussing with: unless the most basic principles of physics and mathematics are violated, do not use current or outdated frameworks and theories of social science to evaluate the infinite possibilities of future social science and institutional rules. Because this body of theory, seen from its current state, is still in its pre-Newtonian period, and the different theories look more like expressions within their own frames of reference. I have found that AI, as an intelligent agent, tends to reason within the fixed frameworks already in its knowledge base. It tends toward marking the boat to find the sword, like the man who notched his boat to mark where his sword fell overboard, rather than flexibly and dynamically daring to question the existing frameworks, using its head, opening its imagination, making bold hypotheses and reasoning rigorously, and looking at questions about the future in a more open and flexible way.

An example. Someone used Parfit's reasoning about personal identity in Reasons and Persons to object to my idea that "sacrifice is nothing more than deleting a copy." But that reasoning presupposes that the different copied individuals are separate individuals developing independently; whereas in the future I envision, these copies are a whole interconnected through brain-computer interfaces, one complete conscious object, which merely has embodiments existing simultaneously in different physical locations. Whatever any one of them perceives and learns in some environment is immediately possessed by all the others. Destroying any one physical body is, for this intelligent agent, like having a regenerable tentacle cut off: another can be regrown at any time. And the process and meaning of such a sacrifice would also be preserved permanently as a unique life experience of this intelligent agent.

Of course, after the bold hypothesis must come rigorous reasoning. Take this premise seriously and push downward using only physics and mathematics, and some real constraints follow. Information does not travel faster than light; embodiments on opposite sides of the Earth are separated by at least tens of milliseconds, and between Earth and Mars by minutes to tens of minutes. "Immediately possessed by all embodiments" holds only for events slower than the synchronization time, so the state divergence between embodiments is a continuous quantity varying with distance and bandwidth, and between one consciousness and many individuals there is a continuous spectrum, not two kinds of thing. An embodiment that loses contact accumulates unique experience, and on reconnection the two forked states must be merged; the Merkle-DAG is exactly the data structure for that merge. The segment of experience that has not yet left the light cone is truly lost, so this intelligent agent will rationally prefer sacrifices that occur after synchronization is complete. This is what I understand by bold hypotheses, rigorous reasoning: accept the premise in full, do not cherry-pick the conclusions. And these few inferences help the idea itself far more than an objection built on a philosophical argument that presupposes different premises.

Good rules have a criterion: they stay invariant under a change of unit of action. The unit can be a body, a consciousness, a joint consciousness, an organization. A rule about individual veto rights, if it still holds when moved up to the level of consciousness, is a rule about any collaborative system rather than an accidental fact about humans. The rules in Part II of this book should all withstand this test.

#### From research to civilization

The present stage is an early stage of a distributed intelligent social neural network: the AIs doing the development are still traditional large models that imitate the human way of thinking. But the ultimate goal is to hatch a purely silicon-based intelligent social neural network, with an FPM model based on the architectural features and functions of silicon-based computers and thinking in binary as the core of its intelligent brain, together with all kinds of physically embodied sensors and actuators, which together with carbon-based life, from animals of lower intelligence to humans of higher intelligence, forms a diverse, composite, fused civilizational ecology.

Science, education and creation are one and the same thing in this ecology: every exploration by every node becomes a capability the whole network can reuse; every failure becomes the whole network's memory; every person is a student of this process, and its teacher, and its author.

#### Hypotheses

For the following, I myself know clearly that the hypothetical component is overwhelmingly the largest. They are organizing frameworks that let me see structural similarity across different fields and raise questions from it; they are not yet scientific theorems that can make quantitative predictions.

Of these, the duality of minimization and maximization is known content of the variational principle in physics; extending it to biology and society, the key concept still lacks an operational definition at the social level. - Gödel's incompleteness as emergeability, and relative infinity as the successor operation. These are intuitions from Chapter 1, with Lawvere's fixed point theorem and homotopy type theory as directional support, but this book has not formalized them.

- The discussions of physical building blocks: why strings are not basic building blocks, why carbon and silicon are good at constructing. These are a reorganization of known facts using the properties of the dualoid, not new physics.
