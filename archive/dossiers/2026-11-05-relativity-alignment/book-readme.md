# 《新原理》 / Nova Principia

**社会科学的数学原理 · Mathematical Principles of Social Science**  
作者 / Author: **Tangram Liu（langram）**

《新原理》从存在、变化与可构造性出发，讨论知识与智能如何相互促进，以及人类如何在 AI 时代共同构造新的社会组织方式。全书将数学与自然科学的探索，连接到公共知识、个人数据主权、动态权重决策、按需保障与共同资源治理，并提出从一个人的知识库和云存储开始、逐步扩展合作的实践路径。

个人与文明层级的注意力、持续变化的社会状态和博弈机制，在书中与数据主权和能力复用相连接。书中讨论技术红利、财富分配与生活体验之间的落差，也追问：超级智能为何常被想象成老大哥，合作所需的信息怎样避免变成控制个人的工具，新的制度又怎样通过自愿合作、公开检验与和平试验持续自我修正。相对性与认知参考系的讨论，将不同人的视角接到共同核验与合作规则；对齐问题则从单个助手延伸到人与人、人与AI及文明与生态的协调。

本书强调公平的发展条件、能力多样性与跨领域合作。防止支配不以任何预设的能力分布为保证，而要通过可以核验、质疑、修正和退出的机制接受实践检验。数学假说、经验依据、制度主张与已实现能力在正文及末尾自我反思附录中分别讨论。

*Nova Principia* explores how knowledge and intelligence can reinforce one another, and how people might build new forms of cooperation in the age of AI. Beginning with questions about existence, change, and what can be constructed, it connects scientific inquiry with shared knowledge, personal data sovereignty, dynamic decision weights, provision according to need, and the governance of common resources. Its practical path starts small: one person's knowledge archive and cloud storage, growing through voluntary cooperation.

The book connects attention at personal and civilizational scales with changing social states, strategic interaction, data sovereignty, and reusable capabilities. It examines gaps between technological gains, wealth distribution, and lived well-being. It also asks why superintelligence is so often imagined as Big Brother, how information sharing might support cooperation without surrendering personal control, and how institutions can keep learning through voluntary participation, public scrutiny, and peaceful experiments. A discussion of relativity and cognitive frames connects different perspectives with shared tests and cooperative rules. Alignment extends beyond an individual assistant to relations among people, between people and AI, and between civilization and its ecological conditions.

The book argues for fair conditions in which diverse abilities can develop and complement one another across disciplines. Freedom from domination is a goal to test through accountable, revisable institutions—not a consequence assumed from a statistical distribution. The final appendix critically assesses the mathematics, scientific hypotheses, social arguments, and feasibility of the proposed systems. The complete English edition is written for English readers, with the original arguments, examples, and qualifications retained.
