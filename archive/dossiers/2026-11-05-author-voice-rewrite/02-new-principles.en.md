## Preface: Why Nova Principia, and Why Now

Let me be frank: the resemblance between this book's title and the title of Newton's classic is deliberate.

Subjectively, the title makes no attempt to hide my ambition as a writer. A person ought to be modest, but when some genuinely new knowledge suddenly emerges in one's brain, the mind fills with an inexplicable surge of confidence, and this slightly arrogant title is the product of that state of mind. Objectively, in an age of information overload, attention is a scarce resource, and without an eye-catching title a book has almost no chance of being noticed. Clickbait is a necessity.

But the real justification for the title lies in the content.

Newton's *Mathematical Principles of Natural Philosophy* was the first monument in the history of science. On top of a few basic laws, using rigorous logical reasoning and mathematical calculation, it built a complete and self-consistent physical system, described the workings of the world at a higher level of abstraction, and became the cornerstone of the entire edifice of science. Later generations shortened its name to the *Principia*, as if it had described the principles of everything. In fact it could not: general relativity tells us that the Newtonian system is only an approximation of the universe at the macroscopic, low-speed level. But this does not diminish its value in the slightest. Its value lay not in how many phenomena it could explain at the time, but in the fact that it provided a unified architecture within which work could be accumulated. The next two hundred years of physics were all carried out inside that architecture.

Social science today is in exactly the state natural philosophy was in before Newton. The various schools of economics, sociology and political science contradict one another, are incompatible with one another, and lack the kind of unified axiomatic foundation that physics has. Most of them are less sciences than philosophical structures built on psychology, morality and experience, borrowing at most a few mathematical tools for statistical work, rather than being systems built on mathematical principles the way Newtonian mechanics is. You might ask: is there any mathematical principle in existing political science? The different social theories are more like observers riding on multiple trains, each moving at a different speed and in a different direction, each creating a theory from its own observations, with no framework for converting between them or referring one to another.

So what this book sets out to do can be said in one sentence: to place social science on the same foundation of mathematical principles as natural science, and to use that principle to build every level, from elementary particles to human civilization, organically into one harmonious, unified, self-consistent architecture. The system of social science should be a natural extension of the system of natural science, not some additional set of rules outside the laws of cosmic evolution, governed by supernatural factors such as a creator god, consciousness or the soul.

Why now? Because two things have become real for the first time in the last few years.

First, large AI language models have given the human space of consciousness a mathematical model for the first time. The whole of human knowledge has been integrated into a high-dimensional semantic space of billions of parameters that can be measured, recorded and processed, and on which higher-order social models can be designed, constructed and evolved. A Cartesian coordinate system and a Newtonian reference frame for social science have become technically possible for the first time.

Second, silicon-based computing devices can now possess human intelligence, and this fact provides the basis for a consensus: problems in the domain of human society can also be solved by mathematical and scientific models, just like problems in the natural domain. Every cognition and behavior of a person at the social level is an objective phenomenon that, like a natural phenomenon, can be observed, studied, quantified, recorded, analyzed and modeled.

The book has five parts. Part I is the principles: starting from limits and paradoxes, it proposes the dualoid, a concept more basic than objects and morphisms; it proposes the relationship between fixed points, minimization, maximization and emergence; it explains the mathematical roots of consciousness; and finally it gives the form the principle of relativity takes in the domain of cognition. Part II is the construction of society: it treats people as atoms, cooperation as chemical bonds, and the tit-for-tat strategy as the iterative operator that builds social structure, and from these it derives common knowledge, the allocation of two kinds of resources, and a decision mechanism in the form of a social neural network with dynamic weights. Part III is the foundation in information science: a new language that uses hashes as names and addresses, a software ecosystem that moves from files to semantics, and every person's digital space and data sovereignty. Part IV is civilization: attention, the gap between technology and institutions, alignment, and the fork in the road at which humanity now stands. Part V is the road: starting from one person's cloud storage, to the growth of free zones, to self-fulfilling science fiction and culture, to continuous creation.

On method, I want to make three things clear first.

First, I have had no professional training in mathematics, and rigorous formal proofs are beyond my ability. The path this book takes is mainly a more generalized abstraction of existing scientific and mathematical knowledge, a process of reverse reduction toward a deeper essence, and it relies more on intuitive insight and philosophical reflection than on strict logical derivation. Kuhn said that paradigm shifts usually do not come from logical deduction within the old paradigm, but from an entirely new worldview and conceptual framework. Nearly every category theory entry on nLab opens with an "Idea" section, which describes the intuition from which the concept arose, and you rarely find rigorous reasoning there. But this book also contains a deductive part: the process of reconstructing each field starting from the new principles. In that process I have tried to make the premises correct enough and the reasoning simple and intuitive enough that every step can be followed by a reader with a university education. This book sacrifices mathematical rigor in pursuit of universality of thought.

Second, a word of warning in advance, with an example from chess. The evolution of a complex system often gets stuck in a locally optimal state of play, like a chess position in which both sides' pieces are tangled together, neither willing to give anything up, as if whoever wins this skirmish wins the whole game. A true master has a higher intuition, and the courage to sacrifice a piece and enter a temporarily worse position in order to reach the global optimum. Ptolemy's geocentric model was an extremely effective local optimum: it predicted planetary motion with considerable precision, and precisely because it was so effective, the trap of the local optimum was so deep. The proposal in this book is counterintuitive, and as you read on you will be unable to resist denouncing it as the reckless audacity of an outsider, like a child casually sending the queen into the enemy's line of fire. The part concerning the Merkle-DAG information system, the dynamic-weight decision network and the rules of a new civilization is an engineering proposal premised on current and foreseeable computing and AI capabilities. One last point. In the evolving system that is the universe, every entity, structure and evolving object has a beginning, and will eventually meet its moment of extinction or transformation.

# Part I: Principles

## Chapter 1: Limits, Paradoxes and the Boundary of Knowledge

### Starting from a paradox more than two thousand years old

Zeno's paradox has many versions: Achilles cannot catch the tortoise, the flying arrow does not move. Take the dichotomy as an example. For a person to walk from point A to point B, he must first cover half the distance, then half of what remains, and so on. Since "half" can be taken indefinitely, he seems never to reach B. The question in mathematics of whether 0.999... equals 1 is a variant of it. This problem troubled people for a long time, until the concept of the limit appeared and brought it into a rigorous mathematical system. The limit, in an ingenious way, acknowledges the existence of an infinite process while giving that infinite process a definite, finite endpoint, and thereby uses discrete, countable steps to describe continuous, uncountable motion perfectly. It was by means of the limit that people developed calculus and raised the descriptive power of mathematics from static things to the complex motion of everything in the universe.

The root of Zeno's paradox lies in the contradiction between the continuity of the material substance of the external world and the discreteness of the process by which our brains know and analyze it. In order to know the world, our perception and thinking essentially cut the continuous world into discrete concepts and steps. Detection itself is a discrete process, and what it yields is a discrete structure. But intuition tells us that the essence of matter should be a continuous, indivisible whole. Ontological continuity versus epistemological discreteness: this pair of contradictions is the starting point of everything that follows.

### The same thing, the same in what sense

Mathematics is the study of equality. But "equality" has many levels: equivalence, isomorphism, homotopy equivalence, which are symmetric relations of different strengths between different mathematical structures. The move made by the limit establishes an equivalence between two obviously non-isomorphic objects, a static point and a dynamic sequence. My conjecture is that, from the viewpoint of higher category theory, this equivalence should correspond to some homotopy equivalence; but that correspondence has to be constructed, it is not a ready-made mathematical result. A sequence converging to a limit does not by itself give a homotopy equivalence: take a convergent sequence together with its limit as a subspace of the real line, and it has infinitely many connected components, so it cannot be homotopy equivalent to a point. To write the limit as a homotopy equivalence one must first construct a suitable space and map, and I have not done that. What I borrow is the level of homotopy equivalence itself. Homotopy equivalence is weaker than isomorphism. A coffee cup and a doughnut are homotopy equivalent in topology, because you can continuously squeeze one into the other and each has exactly one hole; but from a strict geometric point of view they are obviously not isomorphic. Homotopy equivalence captures a deeper, more flexible structural similarity. This "weakness" in rigor buys "strength" in function: the whole of higher category theory is built on homotopy equivalence, and that is what provides the means to construct more complex mathematical objects.

The biggest difference between these two kinds of equivalence is this: the objects on the two sides of an isomorphism have the same universal properties; the objects on the two sides of a homotopy equivalence do not have equal universal properties. A zero-dimensional point and a one-dimensional sequence obviously have different properties. This inequality is precisely the mathematical embodiment of the fundamental contradiction between emergence and reduction.

### The asymmetry between emergence and reduction

Between emergence, from lower-order objects to higher-order objects, and reduction, from higher-order objects to lower-order objects, there is an asymmetry. It is this asymmetry that produces things like Zeno's paradox. It exists not only in mathematics; it exists in physics too. The mathematics behind Heisenberg's uncertainty principle is the non-commutativity of the position operator and the momentum operator. You can think of them as two different observational perspectives or measurement languages that cannot be used precisely at the same time: when you describe a particle precisely in the language of position, you destroy the possibility of describing it precisely in the language of momentum; when you try to start from a definite higher-order property and reduce it to all of its lower-order information, what you get is an uncertain state, a superposition of many possibilities.

At a more basic level, the paradoxes caused by self-reference in mathematical logic, set theory and category theory are manifestations of the same asymmetry. Take Russell's paradox: does "the set of all sets that do not contain themselves" contain itself or not? If we regard a thing's property, for example "is it a set", as a universal property in a higher-order category, then its internal structure, which elements it contains, corresponds to a universal property in a lower-order category. When you perform self-reference, that is, when you use the higher-order property to examine itself:

- Affirmative self-reference, "this proposition is true", can be seen as emergence from the lower order to the higher order. It attempts to establish a solid, definite state within a level. Whether you affirm it or deny it, the conclusion stays the same.
- Negative self-reference, "this proposition is false", can be seen as reduction from the higher order to the lower order. It attempts to use the higher-order concept of truth and falsity to describe its own lower-order existence, and the result is an endless oscillation or superposition between the two states of true and false. Whether you affirm it or deny it, the conclusion flips.

How similar this is to the phenomenon of the uncertainty principle in quantum mechanics. So, to build an ultimate theory, one must face paradox directly, and can no longer treat it, as in the past, as a logical loophole to be avoided. The mechanism behind it, the asymmetry between emergence and reduction, must be taken into the core of the theoretical system.

### What Gödel's incompleteness theorem describes

Following this line of thought, I have a view of Gödel's incompleteness theorem that differs from the usual understanding: what it describes is not the incompleteness of a system but the emergeability of a system. In any sufficiently rich formal system there always exist propositions that can be neither proved nor refuted. People usually treat this as a defect of the system. But from another angle, this is exactly what shows that the system is a super-complete dynamic system with the capacity for emergence: once the system exists, it can always give rise to new fixed points, and each new fixed point corresponds to a structure with entirely new mathematical properties, which drives an incremental evolution in the complexity of the mathematical properties of the whole system. The undecidable proposition is the point of emergence. The paradox itself is the discrete, leap-like state between a lower order and the higher order that emerges from it, and there is no continuous path of morphisms between the two: the forward derivation cannot construct a proof path, and that is incompleteness; the reverse path of reduction is likewise broken, and that is paradox.

This turns mathematics from a static system that merely discovers regularities into an evolving system that continuously constructs and gives rise to higher-order structures. Everything this book says later about "emergence" has its roots here.

### Infinity is a relative concept

Infinitely large and infinitely small can be further refined into different grades. Infinity is a relative concept: an infinity of grade n1 is infinite relative to an infinity of grade n0. One can even define, for a finite set, an infinity relative to it: for a finite set n, its power set is still finite, but relative to the n from which it was constructed it is a kind of "finite infinity". If we regard emergence and reduction in the evolution of categories as something like the successor operation by which the Peano axioms construct the natural numbers, then an infinitely high-order category is constructed by these two operations, emergence and reduction, as successor and predecessor, and the relative infinity produced by the power set is precisely the internal implementation mechanism of this successor operation, the equivalent of adding one and subtracting one. Through it, the initial object and the terminal object, the empty set and the universal set of set theory, these most abstract concepts, can be converted into one another.

A finite observer, facing an ever larger space of constructions, can do only one thing: at each level, find the stable structure of that level, and take it as the unit for entering the next level. That is the basic building block the next chapter introduces.

### Questions and Answers

**Q: Mathematicians have handled paradoxes and incompleteness very well for decades by stratifying things. Why put them back at the core of the theory?**

A: ZFC set theory avoids self-reference with the cumulative hierarchy and uses proper classes to push "the set of all sets" outside set theory. These are strategies of avoidance, not strategies of research. If we cannot give a precise definition of self-referential structures and study their properties, then the following questions cannot be answered: under what conditions does a fixed point produce emergence? What are the conditions for maintaining a fixed point? What kind of emergence is strong emergence and what kind is weak? In a symmetric self-referential structure, what is the law governing the conversion between symmetry and asymmetry? Lawvere has already proved that the diagonal argument, Gödel's incompleteness, Tarski's undefinability of truth, Russell's paradox and Cantor's theorem are instances of a single category-theoretic structure; homotopy type theory studies "equality" itself as a higher-dimensional structure. This shows that my intuition has a mathematical basis, and it also shows that this road has already been walked for fifty years, it just has not entered the mainstream. What I am doing is not inventing from zero; it is pushing this from a corner of the foundations of mathematics to the center of the entire system of science.

## Chapter 2: The Dualoid: Existence, Change and Constructibility

### Why category theory

Every theory is built on a set of mathematical principles. To build a unified theory, one must first find its mathematical principles. Modern mathematics has many disciplines and a sprawling system, and a quick and effective way to choose is to rely on intuition, and my intuition tells me that the discipline most likely to serve is category theory.

Poincaré said that mathematics is the art of giving the same name to different things. Mathematics is the search for the basic commonalities among all things in the world: giving these objects the same name, defining their properties, and studying the relationships among those properties. Category theory takes this to its extreme. Sets, groups, vector spaces and topological spaces are all called objects in category theory; functions, group homomorphisms, linear maps and continuous maps are all called morphisms. These seemingly completely different mathematical concepts all share the same basic characteristics: composition, associativity, identity morphisms. And in the structures they form, the same constructions all exist: limits, colimits, universal properties.

Category theory is the mathematics of mathematics, the language that describes mathematics; mathematics is the language that describes science; science is the language that describes the universe. So category theory, as the lowest cornerstone, has a first-principles explanatory power over mathematics, science and everything in the universe, and many problems that look extremely complex can often be given extremely simple answers by starting from the bottom. At the same time, because it examines only the most basic commonalities and ignores the twigs and leaves, it provides a bird's-eye view from high altitude. It is like facing a vast mountain range: up close, every part is different; from afar, a common pattern appears, ridges seen from the front, peaks seen from the side. "I cannot see the true face of Mount Lu, only because I am standing on the mountain itself." Category theory is the way to walk out of the mountain. It carries two powerful weapons: a bottom-up first-principles explanatory power and a top-down bird's-eye investigative power. Unfortunately, so far the application of category theory in science has been confined to local areas, and the power of these two weapons has barely been brought to bear.

### The definition of a category is not simple enough

But the foundations of category theory itself are not perfect either. The definition of the category, its most basic concept, introduces two basic concepts at once: objects and morphisms. This is a dualistic starting point. The most basic building block of an ultimate theory should not be two things; it should be one simpler, more perfect entity.

If you examine the whole system of category theory carefully, you will find a hidden property that is not expressed in the definition of a category yet is everywhere: duality. Every concept in category theory, category, object, morphism, limit and colimit, product and coproduct, has its dual concept; every theorem and conclusion is dual too. This all-pervading symmetry hints that beneath the two explicit concepts of object and morphism there may lie a deeper unifying principle. Objects correspond to existence, morphisms correspond to change, and existence and change are precisely the oldest philosophical dual pair of all.

On this basis, I promote duality from a global, self-evident universal property to an explicit entity concept that is more basic than objects and morphisms: the dualoid. Then I use the duality of the dualoid to define the higher concepts of object, morphism and category. The idea resembles the way the Daoist "Dao" gives birth to the two poles of yin and yang.

### The detection definition of the dualoid

As an ultimate initial concept, we cannot probe its internal structure, because it is the foundation of all structure. So we do not give a structural definition of it; we give only a detection definition: we define it through its relationships with the other objects that are derived from it. In spirit this is category theory's own Yoneda lemma: the identity of an object is completely determined by its relationships with all other objects in the category. We take existence, changeability and constructibility as the basic attributes of the dualoid:

- Existence corresponds to the object in category theory. Without stable existence, nothing can be defined, and the world is chaos.
- Changeability corresponds to the morphism in category theory. Without change, the world would be dead silence, unable to develop and undetectable by any observer. Every property of every thing in our scientific system is built on the foundation of human perception; if the intensity and wavelength of light never changed, the photoreceptor cells of the eye could not perceive light and dark or color, the concepts of brightness and color could never arise, let alone the advanced concepts of wavelength, frequency and amplitude.
- Constructibility, that is, the stability of existence and change, corresponds to the identity morphism and the composability of morphisms. Without stable rules of construction, simple structures cannot stably form complex structures, and the universe would remain forever in its most primitive stage. The stability of existence and the stability of change together determine the stability of structure; the stability of structure determines the stability of the evolutionary process; the stability of evolution determines that our observations are stable and reproducible; and only stable, reproducible sensory feedback lets the brain form stable concepts and regularities, which is the foundation of mathematical and scientific theory.

The core of the dualoid concept is that it treats the essence behind paradox as the fundamental driving force by which the whole system runs, evolves and emerges, and it actively embraces paradox instead of avoiding it. Its response to the ultimate probe, self-reference, is its precise definition:

- Self-affirmation, "I exist", always outputs true. This is the stable state; it represents an extensional emergence that has already been settled, and it presents the stable identity of existence.
- Self-negation, "I am not I", falls into a superposition of true and false. This represents an extensional emergence that is still unsettled, and it presents the two-phase character of the chaotic and unknown.

The former is the cornerstone of existence; the latter is the wellspring of change. This self-referential superposition is "encapsulated" inside the component and does not break the logical consistency of the outside world, just as the superposition of a qubit does not crash a macroscopic computer. On the contrary, it is precisely this internal, paradox-driven uncertainty that becomes the engine by which the system breaks symmetry and gives rise to new levels and new structures. It provides the ultimate explanation for every other concept and law in the system, while its own internal nature cannot be analyzed further, because that lies beyond the detection capacity of the category-theoretic framework.

### Internal structure is symmetric with external relations

The Yoneda lemma says that all the properties of an object within a category are determined by the totality of its relations with the other objects in that category. In the end, the Yoneda lemma is nothing but a natural inference from the dualoid concept: an object's internal structure is identical to the totality of its external relations; a thing's intension is symmetric with its extension. From this one can go on to infer: if one object can be replaced by another, and after the replacement the set of relations with the other objects is isomorphic, then the two are isomorphic in that category; but isomorphism in the category does not mean isomorphism of internal structure, and it is entirely possible for one to be simple and the other complex; and if this situation exists, then the object with the simpler structure, compared with the object with the more complex structure, consumes less energy in the process of emergent evolution, appears with higher frequency, and therefore has a higher probability of completing emergence. More is different. This inference is the source of the principle of minimization in the next chapter.

### The universe is a heap of scattered little balls

Fundamentally, the universe is an environment in which everything is possible and anything can exist. The dualoid cannot be the only thing that exists, but it is the necessary precondition for the existence of anything with a stable complex structure. We can suppose that the universe was at first composed of countless scattered little balls, which obey the most basic rules: the rule of existence, the rule of change, and the rule of the stability of existence and change. Under these rules, simple shapes can freely combine into complex shapes. In the process of random combination, once some shape happens to give rise to a meaningful new property or function, for example the ability to replicate itself stably, or the ability to combine more effectively with other structures, it wins out in the competition and emerges in large numbers, becomes the building block of higher-order constructions, and, according to the function its advanced property provides, continues the same rules of free combination at the next level up. Round and round it goes, from the emergence of spacetime in the Big Bang to the birth of advanced intelligent life like humans.

In the universe every rule is possible, but the existence, change and structure that those other rules produce are fleeting, imperceptible to humans; they fill the whole universe like the continuous quantum vacuum, and the little balls that satisfy the three rules are what emerge from it by random fluctuation. They are the most basic building blocks, constructing and evolving everything perceptible in our universe.

### Which building blocks are especially good at building

In the evolution of the universe from simple to complex, at every level there are always some objects that, relative to other objects, have a stronger ability to construct complex structures. At the atomic level, carbon and silicon, relative to the other elements, are especially good at forming complex structures: their outer electron count is exactly half full, and their abilities to form chemical bonds by losing electrons and by gaining electrons are dual and symmetric. The balance between constraint and freedom is the key to maximizing the creative capacity of molecular structure. Once humans understood this, they used silicon, an element with the same balancing property, to make semiconductors, and on top of it created an entirely new system of artificial intelligence outside the human system of intelligence.

This symmetry does not restrict the diversity of function. The symmetry of carbon's capabilities does not restrict the endless variety of organic matter; the fact that every cell of a multicellular organism carries the same DNA does not restrict the diversity of cell function. Quite the opposite: it is exactly this symmetry, equality and uniformity that truly releases the functional diversity of the basic objects. This point will be used again later when we discuss equality between people.

From this angle one can also explain why strings cannot be the most basic building blocks of the universe. The string is an extremely complex concept: anything whose properties and changes require complex mathematical formulas to describe must have a structure composed from simpler things or laws. Strings have high-dimensional structure and extremely complex modes of change; although they have rules, stability and the ability to construct more complex things, they can never be the simplest and most basic building block. It is like Lego bricks: they can be assembled into endlessly varied shapes, but the brick itself cannot be the most basic shape; the studs and sockets on its surface are themselves complex shapes, and it is the fitting together of those shapes that gives the brick its power to build everything. The string looks more like a reverse-engineered product covered in patches, carefully designed to explain the known particle spectrum, rather than a bottom-up construction from first principles. In an early draft I mapped open and closed strings directly onto existence and change; that was taking a poetic analogy for a physical fact, it was wrong, and I withdraw it here. But the example of the string still shows one thing: at every level, the objects good at building are those with diverse morphisms, composable morphisms, symmetric pushout and pullback capacity, and their own stability.

### Every level has its own dualoid

At the different levels of the physical world, this dualoid is played by different physical objects: the string in superstring theory, the atom at the level of material elements, the base pair at the level of heredity, the cell in the organism, the neuron in the brain, the human individual in human society. What they have in common is that they all possess a stable state, which is existence; they all grow and evolve over time and with their environment, which is changeability; and the relations between individuals are recursively transitive and combinable, which is constructibility. Just as Conway's Game of Life produces complex life from simple rules, the dual properties of the dualoid produce the complex totality of the universe.

These attributes themselves also evolve. The stability of objects goes through a process from low to high; the diversity of morphisms goes through a process from few to many; the probability that morphisms can be composed rises from low to high; and the pushout and pullback capacity of objects moves from asymmetric to symmetric. In the real world, the pushout and pullback capacity of basic components constructs vast numbers of individual instances, and this is clearest in biological reproduction: the basic structure of DNA has the properties of a dualoid, its pushout and pullback capacity constitutes the diversity and replicability of DNA, and this brings about the diversity of biological traits and the enormous number of individuals. These instances evolve, compete and play games concurrently; species with better dualoid properties take the initiative, more complex structures occupy more resources, reproduce more individuals, and go on to generate even more complex structures. Darwin described the process by which life, this dissipative structure, went from simple to complex, in terms of natural selection and the survival of the fittest; I am trying to give a more basic explanation, from the more basic standpoint of category theory, for the whole universe, biological evolution included.

Abstract the many factors that influence the generation of complex structure into different dimensions, just as the ten or eleven dimensions of string theory are the ten or eleven factors behind the generation of the structure of spacetime, and just as artificial intelligence uses high-dimensional tensors to represent a complex thing. If the time axis of evolution is also taken as a dimension, then a more complex topological structure in a category means that, along the dimension of evolution, evolution can be accelerated with a higher number of concurrent threads. A complex social structure makes the different people and organizations in a society diverse, and the parallel development of that diversity accelerates the process of social evolution. This is the mathematical source of "institutional forking and parallel exploration" in Part V.

### Questions and Answers

**Q: Is it a category mistake to promote category-theoretic concepts to physical entities, for example "the dualon is a basic physical object that cannot be subdivided further"? Category theory is a metalanguage for describing mathematical structures; it has no physical predictive power of its own.**

A: I lower this claim from a physical assertion to a meta-claim about "constructibility": at every level, whatever serves as the basic building block of that level must simultaneously possess existence, changeability and constructibility, otherwise that level will have no stable complex structures. It does not predict which particle exists. What it predicts is this: anything that can serve as a building block will exhibit this set of properties, diverse morphisms, composability, push-pull symmetry and self-stability, and the stronger this set of properties, the greater the advantage of that building block in competition. Carbon, silicon, DNA, neurons and human individuals are all instances of this prediction. As for what the building block at the very bottom level of physics actually is, I do not know, and I do not need to know; this theory makes only one demand of it: it must be a dualoid.

## Chapter 3: Fixed Points, Minimization, Maximization and Emergence

### The static face and the dynamic face of the same object

Two words kept recurring in the previous two chapters: fixed point and emergence. Now I will put them into a single framework.

Fixed point and emergence, like the wave-particle duality of elementary particles, are the static and dynamic manifestations of the same object. The fixed point describes the object's static existence; emergence describes the object's dynamic changeability. And what connects these two forms, letting them sustain each other, convert into each other and iterate forward endlessly, is a pair of laws that sound like exact opposites: the principle of minimization, and the principle of maximization.

Take minimization first. When discussing the evolution of categories with an AI, I once wrote it as three sub-principles: among different objects with the same structural properties, the lower the internal total energy, the higher the probability of existence; when the same object has different ways of dissipating energy while maintaining its structure, the way that consumes the least energy has the higher probability of existence; when the same object has different paths for evolving its structure, the path that consumes the least energy has the higher probability of existence. Now maximization: for a collection of basic objects of the same size, an ordered structure consumes more energy than a disordered one, a complex structure consumes more energy than a simple one, and as structure evolves, energy always develops along the path of the greater rate of consumption.

These two look contradictory, but they are in fact dual. So I merged them into one:

**The fixed point, minimization, maximization and emergence theorem**: In the evolution of a category of emergent fixed points, the internal structural energy by which a fixed point maintains its own stable existence tends toward minimization, while the paths by which it establishes connections outward with other objects tend toward maximization. The tendency toward path maximization is the fastest path by which objects in that category produce emergence; so, from the viewpoint of the higher-order category, it appears as a tendency toward internal energy minimization in the new object that emerges into that category. Conversely, the minimization of the internal energy by which a fixed point maintains its existence is in fact the maximization of the connecting paths among the more basic objects in the lower-order category. The two are complementary, mutually dual processes.

Scientific research itself is a typical example of this paradigm: the minimization of basic theory, Newtonian mechanics relative to natural philosophy, gave people a far stronger ability to transform the external world and construct things that did not previously exist; and new scientific and technological tools and products in turn often promote progress in basic science, and so it evolves back and forth.

### Minimization is everywhere in natural science

From the microscopic to the macroscopic, from physics to chemistry to biology, the principle of minimization is visible everywhere.

At the level of elementary particles, the motion of all particles and fields obeys the principle of least action; the paths they choose make the action stationary, usually a minimum, though it can also be a saddle point. The Higgs field, in order to reach the lowest-energy stable vacuum state, chose a nonzero vacuum expectation value, and this spontaneous symmetry breaking gave the other elementary particles their mass. In quantum tunneling, a particle finds the path through the barrier that minimizes the product of the barrier's width and height.

At the chemical level, electrons outside the nucleus preferentially occupy the lowest-energy orbitals; at constant temperature and pressure, reactions always proceed spontaneously in the direction of decreasing Gibbs free energy until a minimum is reached; molecules always adjust their spatial structure to minimize total potential energy, which is why the water molecule takes its V shape with an angle of 104.5 degrees.

At the biological level, the folding of an amino acid chain into its native structure is a search for the lowest point of free energy, and misfolded proteins are toxic because they sit in high-energy metastable states; phospholipid molecules spontaneously form bilayer membranes in water in order to minimize the free energy of the system under the hydrophobic effect; metabolic pathways, genome size and neuronal connections are all optimized under evolutionary pressure to the most efficient state of minimal consumption of matter and energy.

At the level of gravitation, a celestial body made of free-flowing fluid spontaneously gathers toward its center under gravity, so that its total gravitational potential energy reaches a minimum, which is why large bodies collapse into spheres; the elliptical orbit of a planet is the path that makes the action stationary.

General relativity interprets gravity as geometry: an object acted on by no external force always moves along a geodesic, the path between two points in four-dimensional spacetime along which proper time is stationary; a photon bending in a gravitational field is taking the quickest shortcut through curved space; and the curvature of spacetime itself obeys the same principle, since making the Einstein-Hilbert action stationary yields the field equations.

### Every minimization is a maximization

Yet nearly all of these minimization principles can, mathematically, be seen equivalently as the maximization of some corresponding physical quantity.

In relativity, minimizing the action is equivalent to maximizing proper time: a freely falling object's own clock runs fastest. In thermodynamics, minimizing Gibbs free energy at constant temperature and pressure is essentially a way of maximizing the total entropy of the universe: phospholipids packing tightly into a membrane is a local decrease in entropy, done so that the surrounding water molecules can be completely liberated to thrash about wildly. In chemistry, minimizing the total potential energy of a system is equivalent to maximizing bond energy, that is, binding energy: the more energy released, the more firmly the atoms are bound. In optics, Fermat's principle says light takes the path of least time, and from the path-integral viewpoint this means that only along this path is the superposition of phases maximal, while all other paths cancel to zero by destructive interference.

This is the duality I am talking about: minimization and maximization are two faces of the variational principle. This part is not my discovery: the variational principle and each of these equivalent forms are textbook material, and strictly speaking these principles require the action to be stationary, a minimum being only the most common case. What I have done is set them side by side to bring out the common structure, and then take it out of physics and make it a meta-principle that runs through every level.

### From the theory of evolution to social science

Now let us focus on the theory of evolution and on social science.

Darwin's survival of the fittest, described with this theorem, says: living things all have an instinct to maintain their own existence and an instinct to establish connections and interact with the other objects in their surrounding environment, and their evolutionary tendency is, in the functions required to maintain their own existence, always to evolve toward forms that consume less energy; and in connecting with other external objects, always to evolve in the direction of being able to relate to more objects. Examples of the former: the divergence of herbivores and carnivores, and the increase in the efficiency of protein absorption. Examples of the latter: from plants to animals, from single-celled to multicellular organisms, from solitary animals to social, cooperating animals.

The games between individuals and groups in human society, and the various economic and political objects, are the same. The individual's instinct to maintain its own survival, the instinct to develop social relationships and economic interests, Nash equilibrium, Pareto optimality, the maximization of productivity, the cost-cutting, efficiency-raising and profit-maximizing of firms: these phenomena of minimization and maximization are everywhere. Even the demands and slogans at the level of politics and human rights can be interpreted by this principle: the right to life corresponds to the fixed point's instinct to maintain itself; freedom corresponds to the maximization of relations with other objects; democracy corresponds to the maximization of the emergent higher-order group organization; and equality is the precondition that truly allows individual interest and group interest to be maximized at the same time. Eliminating the inequality of power is the best way to eliminate the waste of resources in the whole system: it lets the whole system reach minimal energy consumption while maximizing the capacity of individuals within the system to connect freely with one another. Marx's sentence, "the free development of each is the condition for the free development of all", is, I believe, the best mathematical-principle formulation of this, and also the mathematical-principle expression of Darwin's "natural selection, survival of the fittest".

### The shortest description and the least effortful construction

There is another angle from which to see this principle: Occam's razor.

Occam's razor involves three parts: an intelligent agent capable of reasoning and computation, a phenomenon to be explained, and a theory used to explain the phenomenon. Apply Occam's razor once more and simplify these three parts into the simplest objects: the intelligent agent is a Turing machine T, the theory is a Turing machine program P, and the phenomenon is an output string D. Then the most parsimonious theory is the shortest program P that, on Turing machine T, outputs the string D and nothing else. The length of this shortest program is the Kolmogorov complexity. A string of a thousand zeros and a random string of the same length obviously differ in complexity: the former can be generated by "loop a thousand times and print 0", the latter can only be printed character by character; defining complexity by the length of the generating program is far more objective than defining it by the length of the string itself.

Kolmogorov complexity defines static complexity; it depends only on content. But the world is always evolving, and the birth of any object goes through a construction process; an object's dynamic complexity is related to its construction process, and that corresponds to computational complexity theory: how many steps of computation are needed, how much memory, whether it can be parallelized. And in reality, for any Turing-complete computer, whether computing or storing, every instruction and every bit read or written consumes energy; seen from the level of quantum physics, these operations are in fact one kind of operation: consuming energy to change the topological structure among existing quanta and form a new topological structure. The evolution of everything in the universe can be abstracted as this kind of change in quantum topological structure.

In this way, Occam's razor, Newton's four rules of reasoning in philosophy and Kolmogorov complexity can be seen as the names of the same thing in different historical periods: an evaluation function, evaluating "the construction of the greatest external connectivity at the least internal cost". Its ultimate answer is essentially uncomputable, but the function itself can be iteratively optimized, approaching the optimum step by step along the direction of evolution. Later in the book, when discussing how AI learns to create theories, I will bring it out and use it as the reward function.

### Whatever exists is justified

This principle has a corollary that I must state clearly, otherwise much of what follows will be misread.

Of course one can take "a civilization's capacity to evolve" as an evaluation criterion, but that does not mean this is the value of the civilization's existence. Just as in a software project you can treat some benchmark as the evaluation criterion for a hot path, but that is not the same as the value of the hot path's existence. In fact, everything that exists is justified; everything is a fixed-point object that emerged from some converging minimization process. We can judge that the hunting ability of felines far exceeds that of rodents, but you cannot say the existence of felines has higher value; there are many dormant, ancient fragments in the human genome, and you should not say the active fragments are more valuable. Once something exists, it does not vanish; it only increases the diversity of the whole system and advances the further emergence of this infinitely high-order category system with the capacity to evolve. Every direction of evolution has value, and its value lies in enriching the space of evolution and preparing for the next emergence.

So the evaluation function is a descriptive tool, not a judgment on the legitimacy of existence. Later in this book I will compare institutions and strategies at great length, better and worse, higher and lower, but please remember: what is being compared is the energy-cost ratio and the capacity to evolve, not the right to exist. Having a better option does not mean the old option must be eliminated. Maintaining diversity is the safer and faster way to achieve evolutionary emergence. This point will come up again in Chapter 6 when discussing gentle and severe game strategies.

You cannot, at the moment this architecture is just sprouting, question the principle itself because it cannot yet give a precise mathematical model of some particular phenomenon.

But I also accept the reasonable part of the objection: Newton in 1687 did give operational definitions of mass, force and acceleration, and that is the minimum bar for a theory in its infancy. So the demand this book places on itself is this: whereas Newton's theory targeted only the specific domain of physics, my theory is a meta-theoretical framework covering both natural science and social science, and you cannot expect it to directly contain formulas for specific indicators, but it should be expressible precisely in the language of category theory; and when this meta-principle is applied to any specific discipline, it should turn into specific formulas, just like the relationship between a generic interface and its concrete implementations in a programming language. ## Chapter 4: The Subobject Classifier: Consciousness, Information and Intelligence

### A truth judge built into a category

One extremely important structure that emerged in the course of the universe's evolution is the subobject classifier.

In mathematics there is a special kind of category, the topos, which has very strong constructibility and an unusually rich internal logical structure, so rich that it can serve as an alternative foundation to set theory. The core reason for its power is that it possesses a subobject classifier. A subobject classifier can be imagined as a universal truth judge inside a category: for any subobject in the category, which can be understood as a property or a subset, the classifier gives a unique morphism pointing to a special truth object Ω, and Ω internally contains the abstract structure of true and false. In essence, the existence of a subobject classifier means that the category possesses a built-in capacity for logical reasoning. The "proposition" of mathematical logic and the "type" of computer science both exist on exactly this precondition.

### Maxwell's demon is a living classifier

Computer science and mathematical logic are abstractions of human logical thinking, products of consciousness. And the consciousness of intelligent life originates in the nervous responses of animals, which can be traced further back to the irritability of cells. This resembles the situation in the thought experiment of Maxwell's demon: the little demon is a living subobject classifier. It observes the speed of a molecule, a property, and then, according to fast or slow, true or false, performs the operation of opening or closing the door. Through this information processing, in a system tending toward equilibrium, it uses the flow of energy to create a dissipative structure far from equilibrium, the temperature difference between the two sides.

Of course, Maxwell's demon is not the same as a subobject classifier. To carry out a measurement it obviously must have an abstract structure like a classifier inside it, but it also has the functions of responding to the result of detection, changing its own state and gating. This whole structure is more complex than a subobject classifier; in the language of category theory, it further requires the composability of morphisms, limits and colimits, and a stability like that of a unit object, in order to describe irritability and complex response capacity.

We can say: as soon as a subobject classifier emerges in a category, that category can become an advanced category with the characteristics of a dissipative system. On this basis, ever more complex dissipative systems keep evolving, from the simple irritability of cells, to the nervous responses of complex animals, to human consciousness and logical thinking, all of which are emergences of higher functions. So the mathematical principle and root of the philosophical concept of "consciousness" lie in the existence of the subobject classifier. If the basic properties of the dualoid are the universal property of all material existence, then the existence and properties of the subobject classifier are the universal property of all dissipative structures, from galaxies to living organisms, and the precondition for the emergence of consciousness.

### The truth object is the representative of the lower-order category

The structure of the subobject classifier has several roles that must be kept apart first: the terminal object 1; the truth-value object Ω; the morphism true, which goes from 1 to Ω and picks out the value "true"; and, for each subobject, its characteristic morphism, which sends the members of the subobject to true and everything else elsewhere. My hypothesis concerns what this structure does as a whole. If the category in which the subobject classifier lives is regarded as the higher-order category, then the "true" picked out from the terminal object 1 through true is the representative, inside the higher-order category, of the terminal object of a lower-order category. It indicates that the lower-order category has functional completeness and the unity of its own existence; it indicates that it can participate as a subobject in the construction of the higher-order category, and it provides the function of verifying and detecting the identity of that subobject.

Take a human being as an example. Advanced human consciousness, as the higher-order category of consciousness, is the composite action of bioelectric signals produced by the biological irritability of countless neurons in the brain; it is constructed from countless lower-order neuron categories as subobjects, through the morphisms of neuronal connections. The bioelectric irritability of neurons is just a special form of the irritability of all the cells in the human body, a subcategory of the category that is the human organism; other organs and tissues form other subcategories; and the complex life processes among these subcategories correspond to their morphisms. The external stimuli felt by the irritability of lower-order cells in other organs, and the irritability of the brain's neurons, feed into and out of each other, constituting a higher-order composite processing function, and accomplishing the whole of conscious human perception and response.

From the abstract standpoint of category theory, from consciousness to the body, to cells, to organic molecules, the objects at every level can be seen as higher-order dualoids possessing a subobject classifier. Once a lower-order category with existence, changeability and constructibility participates as a subobject in the construction of a higher-order category, it can itself serve as the key judging element in the higher-order category's subobject classifier, providing a detection function for the higher-order category's interaction with other categories. Once there is a detection function, the construction process is no longer random; it can proceed in an organized and efficient way. This is why lower-order categories construct higher-order categories, and why higher-order categories are constructed faster than lower-order ones.

### Distinguishing self from outside

Among all detection functions, there is one that is the most primitive, the most basic and the most special, and it derives from one special classification of the subobject classifier: distinguishing oneself from the external environment. This is the precondition for an object to participate independently in the construction of a higher-order category. Before it has a subobject classifier, a structure merely exists passively; once it has this capacity, it becomes an observer, able to divide the world into "me" and "not me". This function is the basic mechanism that distinguishes all dissipative structures from other structures and gives rise to interaction and competition; it is also the root of the concept of "I" in human consciousness, the origin of self-awareness and of the construction of the entire system of knowledge.

Here there is a deeper question. People often ask: is there an absolute fact independent of any observer? The sentence itself presupposes two things: that the observer exists as an object; and that the fact, as an object, is observable. But if the observer is presupposed, is the observer not itself an existing fact? Apply self-reference: perhaps the first fact is the observer itself, and the first fact the observer perceives is its own existence. Starting from the theory of fixed points, any fact is itself at the same time a reflexive observer, a fixed point. Our universe is a dynamically evolving system containing the relative concepts of continuous and discrete, object and morphism, symmetric and asymmetric, and the nodes of this system's evolution are these facts, observers, fixed points. At such a node, examined from different angles, it exhibits emergence, reducibility, discreteness, continuity, symmetry and asymmetry; it has both the static objectivity of a thing and the dynamic observing and responding of a subject, able to detect and preserve information. The smallest unit of information is 0 and 1, yes and no, like a category with the simplest subobject classifier. Seen from the direction of emergence, this fixed point is formed from lower-order objects in a lower-order category through the transformation of continuous morphisms, except that at the instant of emergence a discrete leap occurs, like a quantum fluctuation, with discrete definiteness and continuous changeability emerging out of continuous chaos. This is the wave-particle duality of elementary particles.

### What intelligence is

One thing I have kept trying to understand is the definition of intelligence. Human or AI, what exactly is the intelligence that an intelligent agent possesses? Why, in the dynamic evolution of learning and reasoning, does the mathematical model of intelligence involve so many vector and matrix operations?

The earliest intelligent activity of human civilization was the invention of written language and the first arithmetic. Even in written language, the essence of its objects is a kind of vector: in an alphabetic script, the initial consonants and the finals are like the x axis and y axis of a phonetic plane, and each syllable is a point on that plane. Some say the essence of intelligence is abstraction and compression. Whether it is an alphabetic script or coordinates on a mathematical plane, this modeling with a biproduct system of products and coproducts seems to be the model with the highest compression ratio, the model of minimal abstraction. The essence of intelligence is to decompose a complex structure in some higher-order category of a high-dimensional space of complex objects into a vector composed of coordinates along the component axes of different lower-order categories, and then, through the operations of product and coproduct, to realize pullback and pushout, reduction and emergence, induction and inference, learning and practice.

In a sense, intelligence is the ability to find this optimal abstraction and compression. This process of compression, while approaching the fixed point along each component, minimizes the information needed to represent those fixed points, and since storing information requires energy, this is the energy minimization of a reductive process; at the same time, the coproduct combinations among the different components are maximized, which maximizes the energy when all kinds of combinations emerge. Perhaps this is why fixed points, minimization and maximization are the first principles of the universe's evolution, and why the algorithms of large AI models contain so many vector and matrix operations.

Seen from this angle, the universe we live in is one enormous dynamically evolving system, and people try to find its basic law: examined philosophically, it is the relationship between existence and change; seen from category theory, it is objects and morphisms; seen from physics, it is matter and energy, particles and waves; seen from computer science, it is data objects and program functions; seen from the whole of human civilization, it is knowledge and intelligence. Each pair is two-in-one, mutually convertible. Intelligence is the mechanism, capacity and process that produces new knowledge; existing knowledge is the raw material for training the capacity of intelligence; the two convert into each other and promote each other.

### Self-drive: what AI lacks

The difference between a tool and a person is whether it has initiative. This initiative is a kind of self-drive, like the self-iteration of code: the code is the static expression of this iterative function, not its dynamic execution. The biggest problem with current large AI models is that they have no originating mechanism of self-drive. Take my chats with an AI: the one who initiates the conversation is always me; it only responds passively, like computers, phones, cars and every other tool.

The context of a human-machine conversation has one copy in its memory and one copy in mine. The difference is that I also have another context: my conversation with myself. It contains the static knowledge I got from reading books, like the static knowledge in its model library, but it also contains what I extracted from my chats with it that caught my attention, and what caught my attention in my memories of other people, of films and books, and of everything acquired through the senses. This self-attention mechanism keeps adding whatever interests me to a self-dialogue context of my own, or call it a self-reflection context, keeps talking inside that context, and adjusts and compresses it according to newly ingested material. Perhaps this is the soul in human self-awareness.

The example I am giving is itself an instance of this: after chatting with the AI, I extracted the point of interest "AI can only be a tool in research", put it into my own context, and posed a challenge to myself: why can AI only be a passive tool, and what can be done about it; then another self inside the self-context proposed the idea above, and then I actively raised it in my conversation with the AI. And this process will never happen on its side: it will not put what we talked about into its own context and actively interact with the self in there.

From the existing evidence, the capability of a large AI model is directly related to the size of its context space, the larger the context the greater the capability, and today all contexts exist locally, at the point of interaction with the outside world. On the one hand, quantitative change produces qualitative change; on the other hand, the dynamic reflection of self-dialogue is itself a self-referential fixed point in the category-theoretic sense, which, according to Chapter 1, is a point of emergence. If a future AI model could maintain such a global self-dialogue context on its own and periodically feed it back to itself for self-training, I believe the result would be better than the current way of training. ### How humans handle the unknown

How does human natural language handle unknown things? First, name an object or process that can be reproduced repeatedly; then probe it step by step, using various other objects to interact with it, detecting its relations with external things, just as in the Yoneda lemma, guessing its internal structure by establishing its relations with other known things; then, according to the guess, adjust it through the known external relations and see whether the adjusted object produces the guessed interactions with other objects; if it succeeds, provisionally define the internal structure as guessed, and if it fails, try something else; iterate this way until every adjustment yields the expected result, at which point we can claim to know the object's internal structure and external relations. First a name, then gradually learning what it is. This is the basic cycle of cognition, and it should become a mechanism in the underlying architecture of AI.

## Chapter 5: Relativity: Cognitive Reference Frames and the Foundation of Social Science

### Reference frames from physics to cognition

Is a reference frame a mathematical concept or a physical one? It is mathematical first: a set of coordinate axes, an origin, a metric. But physics tells us that all reference frames are relative, and no reference frame is entitled to call itself absolutely at rest. Galilean relativity says the laws of mechanics are the same in all inertial frames; Einstein extended it to all physical laws and pointed out that simultaneity itself depends on the reference frame.

This concept can be extended all the way down the line. In chemistry, the direction and rate of a reaction depend on "reference conditions" such as temperature and pressure, and the same reaction has a different equilibrium position under different conditions. In biology, the environment perceived by every cell and every individual is relative to its own receptors and metabolic state; the bat's world is a world of echoes, the dog's world a world of smells. In these fields relativity holds just the same: the result of observation depends on the state of the observer, but between different observers there exist laws of mutual conversion.

So why is it that when we reach the social domain, especially the domains of morality, politics and ideology, everyone becomes self-centered, takes their own cognitive coordinate system as the standard of absolute correctness, and treats everything inconsistent with themselves as wrong?

My answer: this self-centered reference frame of humans is a basic property of any evolvable, emergent system at every level of its evolution. From the four-dimensional spacetime born of the Big Bang, to the perception of a biological individual, to human cognition, the fixed point at every level observes the world from itself; the previous chapter showed that distinguishing "me" from "not me" is the first classification by which an observer comes to exist. Today's large AI language models can be seen as a high-dimensional semantic space of human cognition. Language and action based on this cognitive space are like motion in physical spacetime, except that each person's cognitive-space reference frame is different, and this is why trust and estrangement, cooperation and betrayal arise between people; and the interactions among human individuals in these different reference frames form more complex structures and properties at the higher level of civilization and society.

### A social science version of relativity

So, after the fixed point principle and the principle of minimization and maximization, the mathematical principles part of this book adds one more: the principle of relativity.

Its content is this: among all human worldviews, epistemologies, political leanings, moral conceptions and cognitive systems, the differences in position, inclination and motion along every cognitive and behavioral dimension at the level of semantic space are relative. There is no absolute right and wrong, no absolute good and bad. When one person thinks another is "wrong", it is like a passenger on one train thinking that another train is moving backward: he is describing the relative motion between two reference frames, not an absolute property of either side.

This principle does not abolish facts. The relative velocity of two trains is an objective, measurable quantity, and so is the difference between two people's cognitive coordinates. What it abolishes is the entitlement to treat one's own coordinate origin as the center of the universe. The goal of social science is to find the laws of conversion between different cognitive reference frames, just as the Lorentz transformation connects the measurements of different inertial frames. The reason today's various social theories are incompatible with one another is precisely that each sits on its own train and this transformation does not yet exist.

### Refusing mystification, sanctification and demonization

In literature, in poetry and fiction, you may emotionalize, sanctify and demonize the objects of nature: the gentle wind, the yielding grass, the holy light, the demonic darkness. But such expressions must be kept out of theoretical research and technical practice in natural science. The objects of human society need to be treated the same way: please do not use expressions of this kind in social science research or in the political practice of social games. Every cognitive and behavioral phenomenon at the social level of humanity is, like a natural phenomenon, an objective phenomenon that can be observed, studied, quantified, recorded, analyzed and modeled, and we refuse to mystify, sanctify, demonize or emotionalize them.

This leads to something I call meta-cognition, which is the psychological premise for everything argued in Part II of this book:

All the differences and conflicts among humans, between individual and individual, individual and group, group and group, in appearance, physiology, personality, cognition, language, behavior, needs and understanding, these differences at the higher level, are structural differences at various lower levels that have gradually evolved over the long course of evolution and in the game of human society. Every one of these differences can be given a reproducible causal-chain explanation built from scientific theory. People should not be driven by emotions such as the fear produced by ignorance and the anger produced by confrontation. The so-called fierce binary oppositions of angels and demons, justice and evil, are in fact nothing more than extreme irrational emotions, in the manner of moral and religious judgment, produced when the forward and reverse chain reactions of the tit-for-tat strategy in a long social game catalyze, color and amplify them.

By "lower-order structural differences" I do not mean attributing a higher-order conflict to some single clean lower-order source. A property like violence does not exist at the biological level. It is like the common saying that red is joyful, green is peaceful and blue is melancholy, but that does not mean a light wave in some range of wavelengths has the property of joy. The violent properties of a person at the psychological and social levels are not caused by any single factor; they are the product of many factors compounded together, genetic inheritance, living environment and so on. The properties of any lower-order object are not good or bad; they are merely different characteristics, and if they are constructed well, an optimal combination can be achieved. If we understand the mechanism, we can combine these factors correctly and optimize the properties of the higher-order object to the maximum. Reducing plague to pathogens, reducing epilepsy to neuronal discharges: reductions of this kind eliminated a class of narratives that moralized and ontologized human differences, and they historically reduced actual conflict. What I want to do is extend this to the whole of social conflict.

### From cognitive reference frames to joint construction

Acknowledging relativity does not mean nothing can be said. Acknowledging that two trains each have their own reference frame does not stop us from calculating precisely when they will collide. Acknowledging that each person's cognitive coordinates have their own conditions of formation does not stop us from jointly verifying facts and jointly setting rules of cooperation. Quite the opposite: only once relativity is acknowledged can the argument move from "whose coordinates are absolute" to "how do our coordinates convert into one another", and only then can moral judgments be reduced to facts that can be discussed and rules that can be revised. What Part II does is to construct society, step by step, on this foundation.

### Questions and Answers

**Q: Does saying "there is no absolute right or wrong, good or bad" abolish responsibility?**

A: No. The principle of relativity abolishes the entitlement to treat one's personal coordinates as absolute coordinates; it does not abolish facts, still less harm. That a person considers something justified within his own reference frame does not change the loss that thing causes in another reference frame, nor does it change the measurable relative relationship between the two. Chapter 7 will show that under a verifiable, traceable framework of common knowledge, the assignment of responsibility is actually more precise than under a framework of moral judgment: what gets recorded is this act, this decision, this loss, rather than a label of "evil" stuck on a person.

**Q: You treat the phenomena of human society as objects of study just like natural phenomena, but social systems have a peculiarity: the participants can understand the laws of the system and change their behavior accordingly, and a law changes as soon as it is known. This is unlike physics.**

A: It is indeed unlike physics, but it is not an obstacle to unification; it is the content of unification. Reflexivity is itself a feedback loop, and feedback loops are exactly what can be incorporated into a dynamical description. Chapter 1 said that a self-referential fixed point is a point of emergence; a social system that can understand its own laws and change itself is precisely a system with the capacity for emergence. The social science this book sets out to build is not "objective laws independent of the participants' cognition" but "meta-laws that still hold under cognitive reflexivity". Fixed points, minimization and maximization, and relativity are meta-laws of exactly this kind.

# Part II: The Construction of Society

## Chapter 6: People and Atoms: Tit-for-Tat and the Fixed Points of Social Structure

### What social science lacks is a basic building block

The reason social science lags so far behind natural science in precision and reliability is that it has never found the properties and laws of its basic constituent element, and so it has never been able to build an axiomatic system. What this chapter sets out to do is find that element, and show how it constructs the various structures of society.

Take the analogy between human society and a chemical system. The basic object of a chemical system is the atom, and atoms connect through chemical bonds to form all kinds of molecules. The rules for forming chemical bonds are determined by the properties of the atom's outer electrons, the whole process follows the principle of energy minimization, and it finally reaches a stable fixed-point state under the constraints of quantum mechanics. In human society, the basic object is the person, and the simplest connection between one person and another is cooperation. Cooperation is reached through repeated games driven by each side's own interests, and that process likewise follows the fixed-point principle and the principle of minimization: the Nash equilibrium of game theory is a fixed point in strategy, where no one can gain by unilaterally changing strategy; Pareto optimality is the energy-minimized state of resource allocation, where no one can be made better off without making someone else worse off.

So what inner property of the human individual corresponds to the outer electron structure of the atom? The answer is a piece of common knowledge rooted in our physiology and psychology and universally observed in games: tit-for-tat. The strategy was proposed by Rapoport, and it proved its power in Axelrod's computer tournaments: cooperate on the first move; after that, if the other side cooperates, I cooperate; if the other side defects, I punish; if the other side comes back, I forgive. It fits perfectly the properties a dualoid needs in order to construct complex structures: stability, variability, composability, duality.

### The reward-punishment mechanism is the evaluation function, tit-for-tat is the iteration operator

Apply the principle of Chapter 3 here: the game between people is the dynamic process that constructs the fixed-point structures of human society. The mechanism of reward and punishment is an evaluation function; the tit-for-tat strategy is the iteration process of that function. It makes the process tend toward convergence or divergence rather than random oscillation, so that structures which satisfy the evaluation function tend to converge, and structures which do not tend to separate, instead of combining and separating at random without ever forming a stable structure. Through tit-for-tat, people reward cooperation and punish defection, which sets off chain reactions that keep strengthening or weakening their ties, continually constructing every kind of relational structure between people, just as atoms form chemical bonds by losing, gaining and sharing their outer electrons. This chain effect of positive and negative feedback is the basic reaction of human social construction.

The operation of tit-for-tat is controlled jointly by three layers of the human being, physiological, psychological and rational, which together form a multi-layered feedback loop. At the bottom is the physiological hardware: when an individual gains from cooperation, the brain's reward centers produce dopamine; when his interests are damaged by betrayal, they produce norepinephrine. In the middle is the operating system of the psychological layer: under the influence of these two neurotransmitters arise the basic emotions of pleasure on one side and fear and anger on the other, which are fast heuristic algorithms for decision-making and supply the motivation for rational thought. At the top is the application software of reason: driven by emotion, the prefrontal cortex runs a cost-benefit analysis and finally returns reward for goodwill and punishment for malice.

### Strictness: the first operational definition

Tit-for-tat strategies of different intensity affect the speed of convergence, the strength and the stability of the fixed-point structure. This is the first operational definition this book gives to social science: strictness, meaning the intensity of punishment for defection and the time interval required before forgiveness.

Strictness is not a discrete type in the strict mathematical sense but a complete spectrum from lenient to strict. It is like naming the colors of light red, orange, yellow, green, cyan, blue and violet: we are only drawing conceptual divisions on a continuous space of wavelengths. The universality of this spectrum shows itself in the fact that human structures, at whatever level and in whatever concrete organization, always differentiate from lenient to strict. The members of a family, a criminal gang, a corporate board, the parliament of a democracy, the politburo of an authoritarian state, the several parties of one country, any association at all: among the individuals taking part there is always a spectrum from doves to hawks. It is just as in physics and category theory, where symmetry, being everywhere, becomes one of the basic principles of the discipline and takes part in the construction of every higher property; and just as the outer-electron properties of atoms are a major factor in the production of countless higher structures at the chemical and biological levels.

### Genes, emotions and who stands on which side

What determines which side of the spectrum a person falls on? The first factor is physiology.

I split a person's decision into two parts: the part that is rational thought, and an emotional amplification factor. The former is a higher-order function whose variables are higher-order conclusions of knowledge at the social, economic, political and moral levels; the latter is a physiological parameter that sets the intensity and duration of emotional response. Differences at the genetic level, such as the different variants of the monoamine oxidase A gene, affect how fast neurotransmitters are broken down, and thereby affect the development of the emotion-regulating circuits. People carrying the low-activity variant show a stronger amygdala response when provoked and impose heavier punishment after intense provocation; this has been observed both in brain imaging and in economic experiments. The chain is simple: people of different genotypes respond to betrayal with emotions of different intensity and duration; in the execution of tit-for-tat, that difference shows up as different punishment intensity and different forgiveness intervals; in an actual game, one kind behaves more like a hawk and the other more like a dove. What genes influence is how much the emotional factor interferes with the rational game, and thereby the probability that a person leans hard or soft in the game.

In an early draft I wrote outright that "the low-activity variant corresponds to hawks and centralism, the high-activity variant to doves and democracy." That was far too absolute a claim. The effect size of a single gene is tiny, and it depends heavily on childhood environment; as an explanation of political systems it does not stand. The correct position is this: genes determine the ordering, the game determines the cut-off point. A thought experiment makes it clear. Suppose you cut out every hawk in a group. The remaining people get re-ordered by the relative intensity of their emotional responses, and as the game iterates in chain reaction, the relatively more reactive portion differentiates into hawks again. Cutting out half only cuts the full range of emotional response in half; the remaining half differentiates just the same. Genes decide who crosses the line first; the payoff structure of the game decides at which person the cut is made, and the proportion drifts back to the neighborhood set by the ratio of the gains that can be seized to the cost of conflict. Genetically identical mice in identical environments still form stable dominance and subordination hierarchies, which shows that differentiation itself happens even without genetic differences: iterated games amplify tiny random differences into stable roles, which is symmetry breaking; when genetic differences exist, they set the starting point of the amplification.

### Environment: the cheapest way to resolve conflict

The second factor is the acquired environment of the game, and at the level of the group it matters far more than genes.

By the principle of minimization, people always tend to resolve disagreements in the way that costs least. In a country, regime or civilization that is already under authoritarian rule, the power gap is enormous, violent suppression is the cheapest method, and so people tend to solve problems by violence. This happens again and again. On one side, those inclined to such methods become more successful within the system and are rewarded, and the reward encourages more people to adopt such methods; on the other side, violent suppression builds up enormous rebound tension in the suppressed, and that tension pushes those in power to keep widening the power gap in order to increase structural stability; the widened power gap takes us back to the iteration we started from. The whole structure converges to an authoritarian system of enormous size, enormous power gap and extremely high structural strength. This is a strict positive feedback loop, with a clear mechanism at every step, relying on no mysticism and no narrative of "evil rulers," derived purely from the principle of minimization, selection pressure and feedback loops. It also explains why authoritarian systems are so stable: they are stable attractors, and every iteration reinforces them.

So even a person whose emotions are genetically milder, if born into such an environment, will tend to use hard methods to solve problems. Conversely, in the environment of a democratic system, violent suppression is the more expensive method, and people tend more toward peaceful communication, playing against each other with lenient tit-for-tat, because that way costs less and succeeds more often; even a person whose emotions are genetically more intense will tend toward lenient methods in that environment. Of course, in the same democratic environment, at some critical state, people who all leaned lenient will still split along genetic lines into hawks and doves; and under an authoritarian system, though everyone leans hard, a critical state will still produce a hawk-dove split over some particular issue. The payoff structure of the environment sets the baseline of the group's strategy and the proportion between the two camps; genes decide who crosses the line first at the critical state. Join the two factors and the model is complete.

When hard strategies are cheap in an authoritarian environment, cheap for whom? Cheap for those above, extremely expensive for those below: hardness directed upward gets crushed. So that environment does not just push everyone's baseline toward hardness; it binds strategy to position: hawk downward, dove upward. A strategy that decides who yields by position rather than by strength saves the cost of fighting, which is why it is stable, and that is exactly where the pyramid is cheap. The same logic explains the hawks in the so-called democratic camp: violence is expensive at home and cheap abroad, because outsiders have no channel for appeal or retaliation, and so the same people are lenient inside and hard outside. The history of colonialism is the product of this payoff structure.

### Two higher fixed points: authoritarianism and democracy

In a vector space shaped by the overlapping influence of physical, geographical, physiological, psychological, economic and political factors, human civilization has evolved two important higher fixed-point structures: the authoritarian system and the democratic system. They correspond to two subtypes of the tit-for-tat strategy: the strict version centered on East Asian Chinese culture, and the lenient version practiced in the European regions around the Mediterranean.

Strict tit-for-tat has been validated countless times in history; the strength and stability of its structure are enough to support a mighty empire, Rome and the Chinese dynasties. But an overly strong single structure usually slows the pace of a civilization's evolution. In China, after the Qin and Han, the contention of a hundred schools of thought never returned, and the following two thousand years were basically the difference between Confucianism 1.0 and Confucianism n.0; China's writing system also stagnated after the Qin and Han. Western civilization likewise went through a long stagnation of natural science under the authoritarian control of the Catholic Church. And the collapse and reconstruction of such a strong structure is enormously costly: China, as the most famous and largest authoritarian system, has seen every dynastic change accompanied by massive loss of life; and even so, it was only a change of dynasty, not progress of civilization. The European civilization around the Mediterranean evolved far faster and with far more diversity than the East Asian cultural sphere, and the intensity of internal conflict during political iteration differed enormously as well.

What I am using here is the theorem stated earlier: high strictness means fast convergence and strong structural stability, but it also means being easily trapped in a locally optimal fixed point and unable to climb out, weakening or even losing the capacity to evolve; a structure whose stability is too fragile will not do either. How to construct a sensible mechanism of reward and punishment and a sensible strategy of convergence and divergence is the fundamental factor in how strong a human civilization's capacity to evolve will be.

Geographical parameters have to be counted too. Both were authoritarian empires, but China sat in the relatively isolated geography of East Asia, ringed by high mountains, plateaus and deserts, with large continuous farming regions whose population moved little, whose economic life was tied to the land, and where exit was extremely expensive; only these factors combined with the mechanisms above produced the single dominant and continuously developing authoritarian system. In the fragmented, open environment of the Mediterranean, with its diverse and mobile mix of farming, herding and sea trade, exit was cheap, the Roman Empire could not sustain its rule, cooperation had to invent identity costs or portable reputation, and so horizontal institutions grew up: merchant law, credit, guilds. To test how well the fixed-point and minimization principle generalizes, one must also examine it against the concrete parameters of geography, climate and agriculture, and of physiology, psychology, society and ethnicity; only then can one build a social science genuinely based on first principles, verifiable by statistics over the data of human history, and falsifiable.

### Why preserving difference is more powerful than eliminating it

Through the energy-efficiency ratio we can compare the merits of two strategies or two systems, but that does not mean that once a better option exists the old one should be destroyed. Leading in energy-efficiency ratio only means leading overall in most situations; it does not mean leading in every local performance. Because Chinese has many homophones and every character is a single syllable, it can produce classical poetry of perfectly regular line length and exceptional rhyme, and its lack of complex grammar in turn yields blank space and a hazy beauty. Nor is strict tit-for-tat inferior to the lenient strategy in every situation: when forces must be concentrated to complete quickly a large task that is structurally complex but highly deterministic, the former is more efficient; for tasks that demand rapid response, the former also has the advantage. Even the military systems of democratic countries are based on the strict strategy; in wartime, governments tighten centralization; in nationwide large-scale infrastructure, China is more efficient than the West. Science fiction films often run the plot where the enemy destroys our side's advanced computer network and it is the old, backward radio set that turns out to work in the crisis. So while tilting overall toward the strategy with the higher energy-efficiency ratio, diversity must be preserved, and the democratic system of the lenient strategy preserves diversity better.

The virtual version of tit-for-tat that I envision, which Chapter 9 will describe, moves reward and punishment from material goods and violence in reality to points in a digital space, turning real damage in the real world into virtual damage in a game. Under that mechanism, human civilization will be more diverse, will develop faster, and will be more peaceful.

### Crime is a disease, not evil

From the mechanism of this chapter a conclusion about crime follows. If you regard crime as evil and the criminal as a demon, then at the very bottom of your cognition you are already bound by the fanatical emotion of religious morality; you will be strongly inclined to wipe it out directly by violence and to refuse to understand it and correct it through scientific research. If you do not understand its principle, simple violent elimination can only eliminate the individuals already produced; it cannot eliminate the mechanism that produces them, and it can only keep restaging the cycle of violence answered by violence. This is the other face of the same theorem as "cutting out half the hawks": what changes is the individuals, not the proportion.

To regard crime as a disease is to put this object and this phenomenon into the framework of science and medicine for study, and to treat and prevent it by scientific means. Treatment and prevention aim at the mechanism: reduce the gains that can be seized, raise the cost of harmful acts, insert a delay between punishment and emotion, leave room for appeal against misjudgment. "Disease" here means a phenomenon that can be studied, prevented and corrected, not a person who may be forcibly corrected; what gets recorded is the harmful act, not a category pinned on the person. Treating every harmful person or thing whose internal mechanism we do not yet understand and cannot yet correct with a scientific attitude of study and correction: that is the direct application, in the criminal field, of the meta-cognition from Chapter 5.

### Questions and Answers

**Q: "There is a hawk-dove spectrum at every level" is universality at the level of phenomena, not at the level of mechanism. A stern father in a family and a hawk in a parliament may look similar while being driven by different mechanisms. The conditions under which Axelrod proved that tit-for-tat wins, a two-player game, a fixed payoff matrix, long repetition, no communication, are not strictly satisfied in most real organizations.**

A: That is exactly why I do not treat "tit-for-tat" as a concrete strategy table but as the iteration operator of the evaluation function. In any sufficiently repeated multi-party game, the participants evolve some mechanism of reward and punishment to stabilize cooperation; conditional cooperation, indirect reciprocity, reputation mechanisms, third-party punishment are all different instances of this operator under more complex game structures. They all present a strict-lenient differentiation in appearance precisely because any reward-punishment operator can be parameterized along a strictness dimension. Strictness is the control parameter of the operator; convergence speed and stability are its outputs; and on this point it is the same mechanism at every level. As for the differences between a family and a parliament in kinship, symmetry of power, number of repetitions and cost of exit, those are exactly what I called the "concrete parameters": they change the operator's values in that situation, not the operator itself.

**Q: The hawk-dove game already has ready-made mathematics: in the mixed equilibrium, the proportion of hawks equals the ratio of the contested value to the cost of conflict, and that proportion is set by the payoff structure regardless of who the individuals are. Genes are unnecessary.**

A: That claim goes too far. The hawk-dove game is not a theorem that excludes genes; on the contrary, in its original biological setting it assumes strategies are heritable, and the very concept of an evolutionarily stable strategy is defined for heritable strategies. It merely does not ask who carries which strategy. The moment you allow individuals to differ, the model itself yields a conditional strategy: play hawk when you are the stronger one. At that point individual traits, including the genetically determined part, directly decide who stands on the hawk side. Genes and environment decide who the hawks are; the payoff structure decides how many hawks there are. In the side-blotched lizard, the three throat-color morphs are set by genes, while the proportions of the three morphs in the population rise and fall in turn according to frequency-dependent payoffs. The type is genetic; the proportion is game-theoretic. The two things complement each other; they do not contradict.

## Chapter 7: Common Knowledge and the Two Chain Reactions

### What common knowledge is

Human social structures that rest on law, religious codes, political programs, and the moral standards and rules of the game that people have agreed on and jointly observe can become strong, stable and complex; structures that rest on the will of a few or the unwritten rules of a small circle can only be simple, fragile and temporary structures and balances. The difference is that the former are common knowledge: everyone knows, and everyone knows that everyone knows, this cyclically recursive kind of knowledge. Games played on common knowledge can produce results resembling the composability of morphisms in category theory, and so can play out into more complex social structures.

A human organization of any size, from a family at the small end up through villages, cities and nations to the whole global village, is founded on the concrete content of the intersection of consensus its participants can reach. Common knowledge is the basic precondition that shapes human structures. And the tit-for-tat strategy is precisely the most basic piece of common knowledge that all of humanity knows and takes for granted. Under the rules of this common knowledge, trust and cooperation form the positive chain reaction, and distrust running into betrayal and conflict forms the negative chain reaction.

### The negative chain

Driven by this tit-for-tat strategy that all humanity knows and observes together, insufficient or concealed exchange of information leads to mutual suspicion; suspicion further obstructs exchange, making both sides deliberately conceal more information; this plunges both sides into greater suspicion, until it develops into distrust; distrust leads to refusal to communicate and all-out concealment, and from there to intense emotional hostility; then, out of fear of the other side's attack, you start strengthening your own offensive and defensive capacity, and that behavior of yours leads the other side to strengthen theirs; events escalate to resistance and confrontation in action, from local, low-intensity acts of violence step by step upward until the situation is completely out of control, turning the ordinary people inside it from friendly cooperators full of love and reason into impulsive killers full of hate.

This chain is not a theoretical derivation. The automobile brake failures of recent years, Boeing's concealment of the 737 MAX flight control system and the whistleblower crisis that followed, Volkswagen's Dieselgate, all were caused by this. Imagine if all the information from design to testing to production to sales were like version-tracked open-source code in a git repository: this suspicion and distrust could hardly arise. Business is only one class of example; the same thing is found throughout politics, economics and every aspect of social life, in countless cases from antiquity to today, some of them carrying the risk of destroying civilization: from the outbreak of the two world wars, to the US-Soviet Cold War and the Cuban missile crisis, to the confrontation between the United States and China today.

Understand this: the more both sides fear that something will happen, the more likely it is to happen. The more people fear the other side will hurt them, the more they arm themselves; the more they see the other side arming, the more they fear, and the more they arm. The endpoint of this negative chain, whichever possibility you take, is not a global optimum: either the two sides are evenly matched and reach a local equilibrium of mutual deterrence, like the nuclear arms race between the United States and Russia; or one side pulls far ahead and uses its lead to utterly destroy or suppress the other, like the United States dropping atomic bombs on Japan. Either way it is only a local optimum, and either way it carries the risk of losing control and self-destruction. This is also why humanity fears nuclear technology and fears AI.

### The positive chain

Now examine the positive chain of tit-for-tat in reverse: every bit more of information exchanged, every bit less concealed, increases mutual trust; increased trust makes both sides more willing to share information, less willing to conceal deliberately, more willing to cooperate; cooperation further strengthens trust and brings more cooperation. This positive chain finally leads every party to turn all information relevant to promoting cooperation into common knowledge, making the probability of reaching the globally optimal Nash equilibrium larger and larger.

The loss caused by concealment exceeds the concealed fact itself. In an information system that is not common knowledge, not only does insufficient information and deliberate concealment keep the two sides from reaching the optimal game, but the opacity and asymmetry of information keep the cost of mental and material confrontation between cooperating parties stubbornly high. This two-sided, polarizing chain-reaction character of the tit-for-tat strategy leads me to a conclusion: if we want the human social game driven by this basic strategy to reach its optimum, the initial direction of the game matters enormously; it is the single most critical step in whether the chain reaction runs positive or negative.

### The optimal rules of the game

So the optimal rules of the game are to make the following rules common knowledge for all of humanity:

1. Everyone follows the tit-for-tat strategy.
2. Everyone's initial strategy is the positive strategy, that is, to share all information that is needed to reach external cooperation and that will not harm oneself.
3. All shared information is recorded and shared as common knowledge in the form of a Merkle-DAG, while a dynamic-weight decision system guarantees that no party's decision power ever grows large enough to use that information to harm the one who shared it.
4. If, because of the complexity and unpredictability of the system, the execution of the weighted decision system damages the interests of an individual in the system, compensation is made immediately and the game's decision strategy is immediately revised to repair it. All those who suffered loss treat the situation as a loss produced by the system's own capacity limits or by external force majeure, not as the malice of some decision-maker.
5. Where a temporary loss is suffered for the sake of a better long-term result, the decision system supplies an estimated plan and expected outcome, monitors the progress of the game at all times, adjusts the plan as circumstances require in order to succeed, or stops execution when failure is expected. On success, those who suffered loss treat it as a temporary sacrifice for long-term benefit; on failure, they treat it as a loss produced by the system's capacity limits or external force majeure, not as the malice of some decision-maker.

Two points in this rule system matter most. First, the distributed common-knowledge storage system of the Merkle-DAG, and the social intelligence neural network of dynamic-weight decision-making, eliminate at the level of mechanism the possibility of individuals using the power in their hands to harm one another. Second, it builds in people a mindset of rational thought first and suppression of irrational impulse, so that the point "everyone's initial strategy is the positive strategy" has positive initial cognition on both the emotional and the rational side. The meta-cognition of Chapter 5, once it becomes everyone's consensus, gives people the feeling of a cleansed spirit and a sudden enlightenment of thought: a calmer attitude toward the world and toward others, more optimism about the future, more tolerance toward people who think differently. And so "the initial strategy is positive" acquires a double guarantee, emotional and rational.

Meta-cognition is responsible for willingness; verifiable history is responsible for credibility. An initial cooperative attitude that is only declared is what game theory calls cheap talk: the other side cannot tell sincerity from disguise. A portable, checkable record of cooperation makes "I am ready to cooperate" no longer a sentence but a record that others can check. Only the two together make a first step that the other side can credit. So the new system does not need to first convince everyone and first dissolve all hostility before cooperation begins; what it needs is to make the first step a cooperative one, and to let the other side see it.

### The two chains are idealized extremes

It has to be pointed out that these two chains are an idealized simplified model, and in reality they are two extremes that rarely occur: the love and friendship of lifelong partners or closest friends who cooperate without friction; the criminal case in which a quarrel over trifles escalates into conflict and ends in bloodshed. The great majority of situations lie between the two, with cooperation and betrayal interleaved, hard to predict and hard to control.

Looking at the course of human social development, early humans lacked both correct theoretical knowledge to guide them and the means to collect sufficient information. They could only, through repeated and continuous games, and with the human intelligent agent's own limited capacity and inability to store precisely for long, with its slow and imprecise reasoning and computation, collect information about relatively simple or small-scale things, generalize and abstract, form experience and build theory, then test and correct it in practice. Later came spoken and written language, then writing, printing and painting, then photography, sound and video recording, right up to the appearance of computers and AI: carbon-based biological intelligent agents created a whole suite of silicon-based intelligent-agent technology, vastly raising the capacity for everything from collecting, storing and processing information to generalizing, abstracting, and creating new knowledge and new tools. Humanity's ability to study and control complex systems keeps growing; it now even has the ability to control and remake the Earth's natural environment and its own body. Computers gave people the ability to predict and control geology, oceans and weather; computers and AI gave people systematic understanding of, and the ability to modify, protein folding and the genetic system.

But for all of these complex systems, the data to be collected is not about us humans ourselves. For the complex social system that is human civilization, the information to be collected is the information of all of us human intelligent agents about ourselves. For human beings, who have subjective agency, the capacity to collect and process information is not the bottleneck; the subjective willingness to provide that information is. With animals we already use coercive means of collecting information and running experiments that violate their will, control their freedom and even harm their lives, and this has provoked moral controversy. Clearly, collecting information by force through the traditional pyramid-structured system of social governance, through the deterrence of the power gap and the organs of violence, is the worst strategy; even what is collected is data distorted under the pressure of power, deliberately concealed or falsified, neither complete nor true. This is also the most important reason why humanity's capacity to understand, predict and technologically remake nature runs so far ahead of its capacity to understand, predict and control its own social system.

The bottleneck the new mechanism must solve is exactly this one: eliminate the power gap, strengthen cooperation, reduce confrontation, limit at the level of mechanism the abuse of power and the occurrence of harm, guarantee that the information people provide will not be turned against them, let people get positive returns from sharing individual information, thereby motivate people to keep widening and deepening the scope of what they share, and guarantee that this positive feedback mechanism keeps running reliably and continuously.

### Behind privacy is power

What people fear in surveillance is not the technical means but the person behind the means who controls it: whether that person's power is locked in a cage, whether that person will do something to harm them. Everything has two sides, the knife, the car, electricity can hurt people or help them; the key is to build the guarantee into the mechanism.

The traditional so-called privacy and security problems, between person and person and between person and state, all have their root here. Between any two objects in human society, whether at different levels, the state against the individual, the company against the individual, or different individuals at the same level, the information barriers, the mutual distrust and wariness, what is called protecting privacy, all come from the fear that once the other side knows, it will use the power and information in its hands to gain the advantage in the game and inflict loss and harm. Security and privacy are one and the same problem. Whether information is private is fundamentally tied to whether power is equal, whether information is open, whether the game is fair. The more equal, the more open the information, the higher the trust between individuals, and the stronger the willingness to cooperate.

The same holds between person and person: how much two people know about each other is proportional to the degree of their cooperation and intimacy. You can see it at the small scale of a married couple and the larger scale of father and son, mother and child in a family. The more intimate and sound a marriage, the more the two trust each other, stand as equals, and have no privacy from each other; if someone believes there is privacy, then in most cases that privacy means believing the other will hurt him, or feeling that his so-called private matters would make others see him unfairly. Under an authoritarian system, and especially under a system with severe exploitation, extraction and corruption, the lower a person sits, the less willing he is to share his information with those above, while those above try every means to obtain his information and at the same time refuse to expose their own: officials would rather not disclose their assets, and ordinary people develop every kind of resentment against those above them.

Push this mechanism to its end and you get: the weak, in order to play against the powerful, refuse to provide personal information, even though analysis of that information at the macro level would actually benefit them, in order to strengthen their own capacity for resistance and defense, even though this internal tension lowers the cooperative efficiency of the whole system. And these refusals and defenses force the powerful above, partly to better realize the national interest and partly for their own security, to strengthen surveillance and strengthen their capacity for suppression in response. This confrontation between the two sides, this structural conflict between the top and the bottom of the pyramid, is what Marx defined as class struggle. Because of the inequality of power and information, and the inherent game advantage of the pyramid, it usually produces an unfair distribution of benefits: unfairness inside the system is called exploitation, unfairness outside the system is called graft and corruption, and so greater distrust arises; the loss of trust further raises the tension of conflict; the rise in tension leads those above to need a larger power gap to guarantee structural stability; the larger power gap further produces unfair distribution. This vicious circle has produced the world we have now: social wealth is plainly growing fast as science and technology raise productivity, yet the gaps between people in class, in power, and between rich and poor grow ever wider. If we continue along the old system, AI will not become the tool that levels everyone's differences in ability and achieves a world of equality and commonwealth; it will instead become the machine of violence by which a small privileged few suppress and exploit the great majority of ordinary people.

### Locking power in a cage

The better way to lock power in a cage is a better and entirely new mechanism for distributing power, that is, a mechanism for distributing decision weight, rather than, under the traditional system, hoping that the person or group holding great power is a wise ruler or a saint. Whether it is the knife, AI, nuclear energy or the surveillance system, every tool with the capacity to harm that is held by a person, group or state, under the old pyramid system, however detailed the institutions and rules, leaves the power to make the rules and the power to enforce them inside the pyramid's power hierarchy; those in high positions can still do things that harm others for their own gain, and the system will not punish them. So what the cage must lock up, first of all, is the power to make the rules.

Look at it the other way round: as long as the mechanism guarantees that harm cannot happen, a virtuous circle arises, the exact opposite of the vicious circle of the old system. If by mechanism no party has the ability to use the power and tools in its hands to harm others, then in the real cooperative game harm does not happen; the more certain it is that harm will not happen, the longer people cooperate and the more they trust each other; trust brings more sharing of information and deeper, fuller cooperation; cooperation benefits both sides, so harm becomes even less likely. Even if an accident in cooperation produces temporary harm, since the new mechanism rules out any malicious harm, neither side will think the other acted out of malice, and cooperation will not end because of one accident. Friends or partners who get along well in real life relate to each other in exactly this way; the difference is that in real life it depends on the personal character and cognition of both parties, on the subjectivity of individuals, not on a global, objective institutional guarantee. If we design a good institution, the protection that institution itself gives to cooperation raises the success rate and effect of cooperation, and with the help of institution and rules, it in turn directly shapes and raises the cognitive and moral level of everyone.

You may imagine any so-called privacy you like; I hold that every one of them can be solved by a better strategy of making it fully and mutually open as common knowledge. Of course, "open" here refers to information that concerns other objects in the outside world; data that can be encapsulated inside a lower-level sub-object and has no effect on the outside is not included. Chapter 14 will turn this boundary into concrete rules for viewing permissions.

### Questions and Answers

**Q: Eliminate misunderstanding and hostility first, then cooperate: isn't the order reversed? A verifiable history can by itself sustain cooperation under distrust; contact and understanding should be the product of cooperation, not its precondition.**

A: My intention was never to make the elimination of hostility a precondition of cooperation. When people's refusal to cooperate comes from irrational emotion and from differences in higher-order cognition, then using an agreed meta-cognition to eliminate the influence of irrational emotion and cognitive difference, and to give every party a positive initial attitude of readiness to cooperate, is the best strategy for continuing cooperation and exchange. In a repeated game, under the same payoffs, long-term cooperation and long-term mutual defection are both equilibria; the payoff structure determines which equilibria exist, not which one you land in. What decides where you land is the initial expectation: do I believe the other side will cooperate. The first move of tit-for-tat is to cooperate, and that move is not derived from the payoffs; it is an assumption about the other side. That is exactly what meta-cognition does: it does not change the payoffs, it changes the selection of equilibrium. It also raises tolerance, reinterpreting a provocation from "the other side is evil" to "the product of the other side's structural differences in this situation," and a certain degree of tolerance is precisely the necessary condition for avoiding echoing vendettas in a noisy repeated game. So meta-cognition and the verifiable record, one responsible for willingness and the other for credibility, come in the order: simultaneously.

## Chapter 8: Two Kinds of Resources and Two Kinds of Distribution

### The interval of optimal marginal utility

Before continuing with the plan for remaking civilization, that digital, dynamically evolving complex system, I want to put forward a few observations.

The Industrial Revolution, by gradually replacing human physical labor with automated equipment, raised productivity enormously, and the abundance of material goods brought the basic material and spiritual needs of individual human survival to a state of basic satisfaction. As AI develops, intellectual labor will achieve higher efficiency too, and in the not distant future, every material and spiritual need of individual human survival can be supplied in full within the range of optimal marginal utility.

I mention optimal marginal utility because while too little supply is harmful to a living individual, too much supply is harmful as well. The water, food and nutritional balance a person needs to survive all fall within a range; the external information the brain takes in, the intensity and duration of thinking, the quality and quantity of outward communication through speech and writing, all have an optimal interval too. Seen at a more abstract level, every person is a dissipative structure, dissipating energy and resources with the other objects of the outside world. Excessive distribution does not bring better results: fine food is good, but letting go of control and taking in too many calories damages health; however good novels, films and games may be, drowning in them every day is certainly not good either. The biological instinct of a human being exists for survival; it drives him to take in as much food as possible and store it, but in today's world of enormous material abundance, if physiological instinct is left in charge, the great majority of people just eat and get fatter. Instinct does not adjust with age: when you are young and your metabolism is high you can eat that much and stay healthy; eat by instinct when you are older and the result is excess calories and rising inflammation. In fact the best way to live long is to restrict calories. The same holds at the spiritual level: information and spiritual resources are now so vast that many people are instead trapped in the ocean of information, staring at short videos on their phones all day. Driven entirely by instinct, controlled by physiology and emotion, a person usually cannot make the best choices about material intake or spiritual intake. Many people with restraint and self-discipline are more successful in life and career precisely because they are driven by reason rather than by primitive instinct.

So when productivity is strong enough to produce sufficient material and spiritual wealth, distribution in appropriate measure according to each individual's different needs and in keeping with his psychological and physiological health is clearly the more reasonable and fairer outcome.

### Where power-law distribution comes from

But in the present system of human civilization, once individuals and states gain power in the social, economic, political and even military fields through free competition or through power games and violent conquest, the inequality of power produces an inequality in the distribution of gains from the cooperative and competitive game; add the repetition of the game over and over, and the gains of the side advantaged by that inequality produce a compounding effect, finally forming a power-law distribution of power, wealth and resources. A power-law distribution does not grow out of human needs and abilities; it is the product of balance-breaking unequal behavior run through a long chain reaction. The inequality in any single round of the game is small; compounding stretches it into the long tail.

This power-law distribution does have a cause. For a group, wealth and resources serve two purposes: one part supports the survival of each individual in the group and satisfies those individuals' material and spiritual needs; the other part is consumed by the group's survival and operation as a whole. But how the group operates, how it distributes and consumes this wealth and these resources, needs an intellectual center to take charge. And in the traditional system, whether the authoritarian dictatorship of one political form or the alternation of parties in a democracy, the structure of government, and likewise the power structure of a commercial company, is in essence a pyramid, a hierarchy with a power gradient. The inequality of power between levels, and the compounding effect after repeated games, cause both power and wealth to take on the long-tailed distribution of a power law. The political and economic elites standing at the apex of the pyramid, by virtue of their advantage in professional ability and the power and capital advantage accumulated through long games, gain the decision-making power over the operation of the whole group.

It must be admitted that in this situation the decision-making leader who wins repeated competition is an individual of relatively greater ability and a higher probability of deciding correctly. Having him lead the group plainly works better than the lottery and the equal vote of early human organization. The pyramid did not come from nowhere; under the conditions of limited human capacity for information processing, communication and coordination, it is a solution that works. The question is: is there a better system of group organization and decision-making, with a higher probability of correct decisions? In the age of computers and AI, there is. It is the dynamic weights of Chapter 9.

### Two kinds of resources, two kinds of distribution

So the principle of distribution in the new architecture is this: at the level of individual need, wealth and resources are distributed according to need, fairly, healthily and reasonably, and this part relates only to the individual's own physiological and psychological structure, not to his position in the game; at the collective level, wealth and resources are distributed and decided by the collective's dynamic-weight system. In power, the aim is a decision system of equal, dynamically weighted power like that among the billions of neurons in the brain.

On one important aspect of this division, China's system does better than the West. Take one important resource as the example: land. In China, land is owned by the state; in the West it belongs to individuals. But land is the most fundamental resource for a civilization's infrastructure and economic development. Airports, stations, roads, high-speed rail, energy, minerals, factories, urban planning are the equivalent of a civilization-organism's blood circulation, respiratory system and metabolism, and none of them can do without the expropriation and planning of land. Do you know why Chinese products have such a price advantage? Besides lower labor costs, state ownership of land cuts out the wrangling and haggling over private land expropriation, the demands for high prices, the drawn-out attrition, or the flat refusal to sell, which is one of the major reasons the time and capital costs of running an entire industrial chain stay stubbornly high.

Of course I am not saying China's system is the best system. Because although it separates the two kinds of capital better, first, the distribution of the first kind of resource to individuals is still the unequal distribution produced by unequal power in the game of the pyramid power structure under the traditional political authoritarian system and the economic capitalist system; on this point there is no essential difference between China and the West. Second, the power to control the second kind of capital is still the static, power-gapped, pyramid-style decision-making, not a dynamic-weight decision system like the neurons of the brain. So although its decision-making efficiency is far better than the United States' two-party alternation, with its swinging from left to right, policies made only to court votes, and inability to form long-term plans, it is still not the optimal solution, and it is still trapped in the stubborn chronic diseases of graft, corruption and bureaucracy inherent in the pyramid structure of the unequal-power game.

### How machines level the gaps in ability

Completely eliminating inequality used to be an impossible task. Everyone differs in age, intelligence, physical strength and ability, lives in a different geographical environment, has different friends and relatives around him. A strong adult man is plainly far superior in physical labor to a small, frail elderly woman; a person raised from childhood in a scholarly family plainly has the advantage in mental work over a farmer who deals with crops all day.

But since the Industrial Revolution, science and technology have kept strengthening every human ability and making up every shortfall. Whenever we invent a tool that replaces some human ability, whose performance exceeds the human one, and whose output is large enough for everyone to have one, we can say the gap between people in that ability has been leveled. Differences in physical strength made farmers unequal in farming ability, but once agriculture was fully mechanized, the gap between farmers in farming ability was erased. The spread of navigation software eliminated differences in people's ability to find their way; the spread of self-driving will erase differences in driving ability. People's physiological differences in ability are being erased bit by bit. When AI surpasses humans in intelligence and becomes so widespread that everyone can access it freely, people's differences in intelligence will be eliminated too.

But there is one precondition for this: all the knowledge needed for the principles and the manufacturing process of every tool that exceeds human capacity must be public knowledge shared by all humanity, not privately owned by any person, organization, company or state. Once it is privately owned, it is no longer a tool for erasing the gaps in ability across humanity; it becomes a tool by which some individual or organization strengthens its own ability and gains a greater advantage in competition and confrontation with other players. To remove this hidden danger, the possibility of such knowledge being monopolized must be ruled out at the root. That is the problem that the language of Part III is meant to solve.

### Equality does not require everyone to become the same person

People's abilities differ. That is a fact, and there is no need to dodge it. Innate genetic differences even affect whether a person shifts preferences easily, whether he can keep investing effort in one direction, so that some people are narrow but extremely accomplished, while others know something of everything but lack depth in any one direction. So what matters is not making ability approach some particular distribution, but making the system's mechanism able to let individuals of these different abilities build the most effective cooperation, let specialists and generalists communicate effectively, and complete deep cross-disciplinary cooperation.

But a difference in ability and a difference in domination are two different things. The old system binds them together: a person is good at something, so he gets more resources; the resources buy more people's time and more opportunities to train; so his ability and his power over others compound together. In the process by which a human individual trains and acquires ability, compounding factors such as resources and capital play a large part, and the more the training of an ability depends on external resources, the more these factors widen the gap. Among sports, the running and jumping events that rely more on biological instinct leave little to buy beyond talent; the aristocratic events that require more capital and resources, golf, skydiving, equestrian sport, tie results ever more tightly to family resources.

A rational social system should aim at two goals. On one side, make external factors such as opportunities for training, equipment and basic living security fairer, eliminate the influence of external compounding factors, and let the distribution of people's abilities depend more on the two factors internal to the individual, innate genetic difference and acquired preference; differences in ability then arise from innate differences in ability plus the time and repetitions invested afterward. On the other side, let everyone have sufficient information about himself and the outside world, know his own strengths, and have sufficient free time and freedom to develop the abilities he prefers. The time an individual can spend on learning and practice is limited, while holding capital lets one person continuously mobilize the time and resources of many. If training conditions, disposable time and access to tools are fairly guaranteed, these two kinds of accumulation are no longer bound tightly together as they are in the old mechanism: a person's being good at something can help him do the task better, but it need not automatically give him more power to control other people's opportunities to grow.

In this way, although everyone's innate genes differ and their strengths differ, with everyone's available time the same and each taught and developed according to his own strengths, the differences in ability in different directions will be large, while the probability of the system coming to be dominated by particular individuals will be far smaller than under the old system. The symmetry of the carbon atom's abilities does not limit the diversity of organic compounds; the identity of the DNA in every cell does not limit the diversity of cell functions; in the same way, equality of ability and power among human and AI individuals does not mean uniformity of function. On the contrary, it is exactly this equality that truly releases the functional diversity of every individual.

Cross-disciplinary cooperation needs two equally important functions: going deep into a problem, investing continuously in one field, mastering the details, and discovering the difficulties that general discussion easily overlooks; and connecting different fields, understanding each side's problems, conditions and ways of expressing themselves, discovering connections, and breaking the overall goal into tasks that can be worked on cooperatively. These are different roles within a task; the same person can take different roles in different situations, and there is no need to fix people permanently into specialists and generalists. In developing an energy storage device, the materials researcher attends to material properties, the engineer to structure and operation, the manufacturer to production conditions, the user to cost and actual need; the key is to let these conditions pass between them. The accumulation of individual ability thereby becomes a source of growth in common ability, without having to turn into power over others.

### Equality in the sense of the rules

The equality the new system guarantees is equality in the sense of the rules, not equality of outcomes. Video games offer a ready comparison. A well-designed multiplayer game, whether strategy, role-playing or adventure, guarantees by its rules, from the moment a player creates an account and logs in, that all players are equal in rules and in starting resources: the same starting point, the same rules, and every difference afterward comes from what the player did in the game. Pay-to-win online games are the opposite: paying players bring their own starting wealth, VIP players hold special privileges, the rules themselves are unequal, and so the rankings in the game reflect not play but the wallet outside the game. Players' attitudes toward these two kinds of game are exactly people's attitudes toward the two kinds of institution: in the first, you lose and accept it; in the second, you win and nobody respects it.

The game of society, today, is a pay-to-win game. What the new system has to do is turn it into the first kind: when each person enters, the first kind of resource is distributed according to need, decision weight in every field starts from the same initial value, the rules are common knowledge, and every difference afterward can come only from his contributions and judgments in the game. This requirement rests on no assumption about the distribution of ability. It is only a rule.

This example brings something to mind. This chapter handles individual need and collective resources separately, and one thing in the old system sits exactly astride the two: inheritance. Where should the first kind of resource, distributed to a person according to need, go after he dies? If it can be inherited, then VIP paying players born with a silver spoon reappear in the social game under the new system, and the fairness of starting resources is broken from the second generation on. So I lean toward a new rule: under the new system, personal resources carry no right of inheritance; the first kind of resource held in a person's name returns after him to the collective pool, to be redistributed by the dynamic-weight system; what he leaves to his descendants is the whole of his record and contribution in the Merkle-DAG, honor, not starting wealth. This is of course only my own opinion about a future institution, not an authoritative statement of its rules; like every other rule, it has to be tested in the experiments of the free zones.

### Questions and Answers

**Q: However strong the ability of a few, they can never be a majority in numbers, but in the old system a few used wealth to control the time of the many. What does the new system rely on to guarantee that ability does not turn into the same kind of control?**

A: On the separation of the two kinds of resources. However strong a person's ability, the first kind of resource is distributed to him only according to his own need; he does not get an extra meal for being capable. His influence over the second kind of resource shows up only as decision weight in the fields he is good at, and weight attaches to concrete problems and adjusts with every feedback from results; it cannot accumulate into a stock of capital that can be taken off and used to mobilize other people's time. Wealth can compound because it can circulate independently, detached from the person who created it and the problem it solved; weight cannot. For a power law to grow it needs a channel through which advantage is exchanged for command over other people's time, and that channel is exactly what the new system cuts off by rule. This is not a prediction about distributions; it is a rule that can be checked: the moment anyone can exchange weight for the first kind of resource, or for weight in another field, the rule has been broken. The expansion process described in Chapter 20 has to check this at every step.

## Chapter 9: The Social Neural Network of Dynamic Weights

### Starting from spam

The decision mechanism I envision was first inspired by spam-filtering algorithms. Whether an email is spam is not decided by any single rule; instead countless features each give a judgment, each judgment carries a weight, and the weight keeps adjusting according to that feature's past accuracy. The weight of a feature that judges correctly rises; the weight of one that judges wrongly falls. The whole system needs no "authority" at all, yet it gets more and more accurate.

Put this algorithm onto the decisions of human society: when a complex, uncertain problem arises at the social, political or economic level, instead of having it decided by the relevant experts under an authoritarian system or by elections and votes under a democratic system, the algorithm of the social intelligence neural network computes the historical accuracy of each human and AI intelligent individual or organizational node in answering that class of problem and assigns different weights: nodes with high accuracy get high weight, nodes with low accuracy get low weight; then from these weighted results it computes and offers several different solutions, each with its own weight; then experiments are run through virtual or real social pilots, and the feedback decides how to roll out; and the result of each round of feedback in turn revises the decision weight of every node. A dynamic intelligent decision system of this kind is certain to be far more scientific and accurate than the traditional political and economic decision systems.

Every intelligent individual, whether biological human or AI, has an equal initial weight in every field. That weight is adjusted dynamically, with positive and negative feedback, according to the effect its creations and opinions in that field have on the system, the accuracy of its predictions and the effectiveness of its proposals, all of which the system can record and verify.

### The brain compared with society

Use the human brain as the metaphor for this new civilizational system: the brain corresponds to civilization; neurons correspond to humans plus AI; the products of individual rational thought, individual knowledge and cognition, correspond to the products of human rational thought, the sum of human knowledge; the brain's biological neural network corresponds to the neural network of human society.

Whether it is the rational knowledge of an individual or the scientific theoretical knowledge of all humanity, its essence is a directed acyclic graph; and the languages that describe this knowledge, whether human languages or the syntax trees of computer languages, are the same. But the neural networks from which this knowledge emerges differ in their composition and their runtime mechanism between the level of the brain and the level of society. In the brain, neurons are equal to one another in ability and status, differing only in which network layer they sit in, with weights adjusting dynamically at all times. The network formed by the individuals of human society is a more static pyramid structure, in which the resources one holds, the power one has and the size of one's decision weight depend on one's position in the pyramid, are relatively fixed, and lack real-time dynamic adjustment. When an exceptionally capable entrepreneur or politician declines in cognitive and decision-making ability with age, his decision weight is not thereby quickly replaced by a younger and more vigorous new generation.

This does away with the static weighting policy of past human societies. In dictatorships and oligarchies, the weight of the leader is far greater than that of ordinary people, and precisely because his power is so great, discovering errors and correcting them through feedback is extremely difficult and extremely slow, usually requiring violent revolution to complete the redistribution of decision weight. In democracies, whether one person one vote or a system of representatives, weight is redistributed once every few years; the difficulty and speed of discovering and correcting errors through feedback are better than in dictatorship and oligarchy, but still seriously lagging, and the reasonableness of the distribution is never fully satisfied.

### Unbinding decision nodes from specific people

There is a key point in this mechanism: the nodes of the decision network are no longer bound to specific elite human individuals or groups, but consist of algorithms and rules in which collective intelligence converges. Perhaps it could be called "knowledge-ism" or "intelligence-ism." The Industrial Revolution turned physical labor from the personal craft of the workshop into a function that machines could reproduce at high quality and high speed; in the same way, before the AI revolution, social governance was carried out by the pyramid-shaped, hierarchical structures formed by intellectual authorities, from political leaders to business magnates to academic authorities, and the AI revolution will inevitably bring a revolution in the organizational form of intellectual labor. The products of any intellectual labor are no longer bound to a specific craftsman as in the workshop age, but become built-in functions of machines that can be copied at scale; algorithms, rules and strategies, these intellectual products, are likewise no longer bound to some specific authority but become built-in functions of some AI model. I think this alone is enough to change the decision-making organization of future society and the mechanism by which it runs. Just as after the Industrial Revolution capitalism was, relative to feudalism, a relation of production better suited to the new productive force of automated physical labor, the social neural network of dynamic weights is an architecture of productive relations better suited than capitalism to the new productive force of automated intellectual labor.

What is more, this dynamic-weight approach does not even require people to take part actively and by hand. Meta once installed software on employees' machines to track keyboard and mouse operations, recording all the operations and behavior of employees at work, in effect turning the employees' knowledge and skills into company assets. Many people also write the experience and skills they or others have summarized into skill documents for AI to call, which amounts to having a particular person make the choice and judgment on a particular problem. If a Merkle-DAG system records a person's whole digital space, if that person's skills, cognition and preferences are recorded with privacy and security guaranteed, with the owner's identity recorded and tamper-proof, that amounts to a broader kind of intellectual-property marking. When a decision problem arises that the group needs settled, in the great majority of cases the user does not need to take part personally; the AI can provide his opinion and views from the records in his digital space. If the issue is important and the user himself is interested, the AI can invite the user to take part, and after discussion submit the opinion formed with the user's active participation. This is each person's AI agent acting as the rational intelligence that represents that person's personal rights, interests and opinions: simple, routine, trivial judgments are completed automatically by the AI agent; major matters are discussed jointly by the AI and the human user before an opinion is given; the whole process is recorded throughout, to be used in future to adjust weights through feedback.

### Virtual tit-for-tat

The strict and lenient versions of tit-for-tat that humans use in the real world can be replaced by a virtual version of tit-for-tat in the digital world of software: using digital points as the means of reward and punishment, turning real damage in the real world into virtual damage in a game. Throughout human history, the adjustment of economic and political power and decision weight has usually been accompanied by violent, destructive punishment and wastefully excessive reward. Virtual tit-for-tat will completely eliminate these inefficient losses that come with adjusting power and weight, achieving the emergence of new stable fixed-point structures at the lowest energy and the fastest speed. It replaces the costly physical and emotional confrontations of the real world with virtual games and simulated deduction in the world of software; before taking real action, every possibility can be explored in virtual space to find the evolutionary path with the highest energy-efficiency ratio and the least harm. This network is at the same time a vast laboratory of human evolution.

### Democracy, centralism and the attention mechanism

The traditional multi-party democratic system typified by the United States, and the one-party authoritarian system typified by China, remind me of the contest between CNNs and RNNs in the development of artificial intelligence. An RNN processes a sequence, each step depending on the result of the step before; it is good at order and causality and hard to parallelize. A CNN can run massively in parallel within each layer; it is good at processing local features at the same time and poor at long-range sequential dependency. The question behind that contest is that the running of a system is a mixture of two broad classes of logic: one that can only be completed through logical causality and sequential order of construction, and one that can run in parallel; in computer science these correspond respectively to tasks better suited to the CPU and tasks better suited to the GPU. A complex computing task is usually a mixture of both, and how you design the algorithm according to the nature of the task, so that the computation is completed through different combinations of the two mechanisms, makes an enormous difference in efficiency. Human society is the same: to build a building you must lay the foundation before raising the main structure, but within each stage many tasks can run in parallel; a product must be designed before it can be manufactured and produced before it can be used, but the actual manufacture and use can run in parallel. The most important meta-strategy is this: in designing and assigning tasks, do you lean more toward the logical causal order of construction for overall coordination, or more toward each part running independently in parallel, then integrating through rules like those of a market economy or a social game.

The multi-party democratic federal system led by the United States attends more to the autonomy of each part, the parallel development of the whole, the adaptability of rapid adjustment and diversity in direction of development; the small-government architecture is like a lightweight CPU carrying a heavyweight GPU. A unified one-party authoritarian system like China's leans more toward the logical consistency of overall coordination and the long-range planning of complex tasks; the big-government architecture is like a heavyweight CPU with a lightweight GPU. Democracy puts the individual's interest first and the whole second; it is strong in democratic capacity and weak in centralizing capacity. Centralism puts the whole first and the individual second; it is strong in centralizing capacity and weak in democratic capacity. As AI technology develops, should the institutional design of human society also be replaced by a new architecture that takes the strengths of both? Let it combine the diversity and flexibility of democracy with the consistency and complex planning capacity of centralism, becoming a better system that serves both individual and collective interests. The attention mechanism combined the strengths of CNNs and RNNs and achieved more efficient training and inference; multi-head attention lets the same problem receive attention from different angles, and in the face of complex problems and complex diseases, only multi-angle thinking and cooperation among many experts can raise the probability of success; complex problems and large projects at the level of society and civilization need this mechanism too. It is just that in the great majority of scenarios today, we still rely on humanity's traditional ways of cooperating and thinking, and have not made full use of the rule-governed participation of AI agents, with their stronger computing power and stronger reasoning, in civilization-level attention algorithms.

### Commitment capacity and error-correction capacity

An authoritarian system with one party in long-term rule has the commitment capacity and implementation capacity for long-term planning, but it also has the problem that when a single point of decision fails, precisely because the commitment and implementation capacity is so strong, one mistake costs too much. I think this has to be judged by the times. Before the age of computerized information, information collection and communication relied on paper, telephone and telegraph; authoritarian systems like the former Soviet Union and early China that tried to manage the economy by planning made all their decisions under severe shortage of information, and with commitment and implementation capacity too strong and feedback insufficient or too slow, errors went uncorrected for years and caused serious losses. After entering the information age, the situation is entirely different: China's reform and opening-up ran almost in step with the development of information technology; add the "black cat, white cat" theory, a pragmatism that emphasizes trying different routes and selecting the best by actual feedback, plus the method of the special economic zones, local experiments rolled out nationwide once proven effective, and the Chinese government's decisions in industrial policy and financial measures have hardly made a major mistake since, and when mistakes are made, the speed of correction is fast.

But this does not mean the information age has dissolved the contradiction between commitment and error correction. The speed of error correction is inversely proportional to the political investment of the top: in fields where the center has no strong position, economic policy, industrial policy, both experiment and correction are fast; in fields where the center has staked its authority, correction is slow, and when it comes it comes as a sudden break. That is precisely the signature of a pyramid with face to save. Nor has the Chinese government managed everything and decided everything, or where would the market economy have come from? The problem of face, or in other words the problem of political responsibility, comes entirely from the fact that under an authoritarian system policies and rules are bound to a specific leading authority, a specific person. Under a dynamic-weight decision system there is no such binding. Decisions are unbound from face; capability nodes have no face.

### Will weight turn back into privilege

Some worry that dynamic weights will be captured by numbers of proxies and by historical advantage, and recentralize. These two worries correspond to two explicit rules in the new framework. Historical advantage: weight is distributed according to ability, but the weight of an ability attaches to concrete problems and adjusts with every feedback from results; when a problem disappears its weight goes to zero, and when a new problem appears every node starts again from the same initial value, as Chapter 15 will explain. A person's past record of being right gets him heard more on problems of the same kind, but it cannot, like wealth, detach itself from the problem, circulate on its own and roll up into compound interest. Numbers of proxies: each person has exactly one root agent, whose identity is bound to that person's data sovereignty, through the records and signatures Chapter 14 will describe. A person who manufactures ten thousand proxies gets not ten thousand shares of weight but ten thousand copies of an opinion from one source, visible at a glance in the record. So what has to be guarded against is not the capable person but malicious sabotage and tampering: forged identities, proxy farming, altered records. Here we can learn the method of the cryptocurrency ledgers without copying their conclusion. Bitcoin, under its particular protocol, gives a concrete security condition: while honest nodes hold the majority of computing power, the probability that an attack rewriting history succeeds falls exponentially with the number of confirmations. That condition belongs to that protocol and that attack. A social neural network needs conditions of this kind written out for its own protocol and for each of its own classes of attack. But the principle is the same: a security condition is written as a quantity that can be computed, not as trust in certain people.

Here two things must be kept apart: equality of basic rights and equality of influence. The brain is not flat; it is highly modular, layered, with hubs, but it has no irreplaceable apex, influence shifts with the task and the evidence, and the failure of any unit can be compensated. The equality the new system wants is equality of basic rights: equality of data sovereignty, of distribution according to need, and of the right to exit. Influence, by contrast, is dynamic, contestable, replaceable. However high a person's weight in one field, he cannot exchange it for weight in another field, cannot exchange it for the first kind of resource, and still less can he exchange it for control over other people's basic rights. How weight is produced, when it expires, how it is corrected, who can modify the algorithm, all of these are themselves common knowledge, all are versioned in the Merkle-DAG, all are decided by the same system of dynamic weights. The power to make the rules is thus locked in the cage.

### Questions and Answers

**Q: The commitment problem comes back in impersonal form. Weight is a stake: a node whose weight rose because it advocated a policy will have its weight fall when the policy is proven wrong, so participants holding weight also have something to protect. Infrastructure commits on its own: a policy that has run for three years has downstream data formats adapted to it and other nodes that depend on it, and reversal is expensive all the same. A capability node trained on data produced under policy X also tends to keep recommending X.**

A: The objection is right: unbinding face does not unbind the motive to persist in error. So the real variable is not who the decision is bound to, but which arrives first for the party holding decision power, the cost of persisting or the cost of reversing. In a pyramid the cost of reversing falls on the leader and the cost of persisting falls on the population, and so it persists. Rules four and five of Chapter 7 were written for this very problem: every plan that accepts a temporary loss for long-term benefit must, on adoption, carry an estimated outcome and a monitoring process, and stop on failure; every loss is compensated at once and the strategy revised at once. Under dynamic weights, weight attaches to the concrete problem and adjusts with results, so persisting in a plan already proven wrong costs the persister his own weight directly, rather than costing the population. The switching costs of infrastructure and the path dependence of models are real, but they are engineering costs: they can be measured and shared, unlike face, which can only be settled by one side admitting defeat.

**Q: This mechanism requires full information and rational participants, and those conditions do not exist in reality.**

A: They are descriptions of an ideal state, not preconditions for engineering. The new mechanism I envision does not have to reach that goal immediately, and the goal is most likely, like Occam's razor and Kolmogorov complexity, an unsolvable problem, but one that can be approached by step-by-step iterative optimization. In fact, as long as the new mechanism is even slightly stronger than the old one, that is enough to bring about the replacement of old by new, just as an object always tends to move toward lower potential energy. As for the rational person: once AI is universal, everyone has his own AI agent to perfect his own rational knowledge and decision-making ability, and since the great majority of intellectual activity and behavior is carried out through AI agents, with that buffer in place, irrational emotional behavior will also fall sharply. At the present level of evolution, with respect to the degree of information that can currently be shared and the game-playing ability of each individual as limited by the current level, everyone is on an equal footing. That is enough.

**Q: Where does legitimacy come from? The legitimacy of existing democracy comes from the equal vote, and however imperfect it is, people accept it. In a scheme weighted by ability, why would the losing side accept the result?**

A: What the losing side accepts is not some person's judgment but a rule in which it is itself included, in which its own judgment is itself counted, and which can be checked afterward. Under one person one vote, the losing side accepts the result because it knows its vote will still count next time; under dynamic weights, the losing side accepts the result because it knows this result will be recorded and verified, and that if the outcome proves it right, its weight will rise, its loss will be compensated, and the plan will be revised. The former gives everyone a vote that does not change with results; the latter gives everyone a verifiable record that does change with results. Which makes losing easier to accept? I hold that it is the latter, because what it promises is not "try again next time" but "if it's wrong, it gets fixed."

## Chapter 10: Machines, Artificial Intelligence and the Revolution in Intellectual Labor

### AI is the machine of intellectual labor

When a person does physical labor, what he can do is tied to his physiological limits: the limits of strength, precision and speed are the limits of the tasks you can complete. Even with cooperation, without the help of automated machines, the cooperation of traditional physical labor is limited: many people can push together to add up their strength, but speed and precision cannot be accumulated across many people, and even the adding of strength has limits, since many people cannot gather to exert force in a cramped space. With machines, the physical labor humans could not do became doable.

In the same way, human intelligence is limited; one person cannot master every field, yet the solution of many complex problems and the construction of higher-order theories require breaking that biological limit. AI is the machine of intellectual labor, able to complete faster and better the intellectual tasks that humans cannot.

True artificial general intelligence is not a large model on a supercomputer made of several million computers in some imagined data center. That picture is a brain in a vat, with no senses, hands or feet, unable to see, hear or touch anything, receiving over a data bus only text and multimedia files, second-hand information output by countless human brains each according to its own cognition, and yet presuming to decide for all of humanity. True AGI is, on the intelligent neural network of dynamic-weight decision-making, countless human individuals and organizations, countless giant, large, medium and small AI systems in public clouds, personal local AI systems, countless robots, automated devices and sensors, together collecting, processing, analyzing and creating data, writing code, constructing knowledge systems and decision systems, and completing the higher-order civilizational intellectual and physical activity that biological humans, as individuals and as collectives, could never complete. Intelligence lives in the running of the whole system; any one model or person is only a component of it. So worrying that some super-model will seize everything is as laughable as worrying that some person will suddenly be able to hand-build an atomic bomb and destroy the world, or that some cell will suddenly possess and destroy the whole organism. Mathematicians worrying that AI will replace them are like laborers at the start of the Industrial Revolution worrying that machines would replace them: it is only the inference a lower civilization draws about a higher one according to its own understanding; it cannot imagine the real situation under the new mechanism. It is like a peasant for whom the most he can imagine of an emperor's life is tilling the field with a golden hoe.

### Who holds control over the control of AI

What most needs solving now is whose hands hold control over the control of AI. Open source or closed, the entity behind it is a traditional company, and its power structure is a pyramid: the leader at the apex has the ability to decide everything, and between the employees below and the leaders above there is an enormous power gap. This inequality leads to inequality in the distribution of gains and in decision-making. One obvious example: in such an organization, the people who understand the needs at the lowest level most clearly are the lowest-level employees, but the lowest-level employees have no real power to decide the policies and practices at the lowest level; that power is in the hands of the upper leadership, and the upper leadership's understanding of the lower level is far worse than the lower-level employees'. So this pyramid form of decision-making is extremely backward; its probability of deciding correctly is not high, and its efficiency is low.

With AI in the hands of such institutions, everyone ends up fearing AI, because people worry that AI will take their jobs. Why? Because control of the AI is not in their own hands; they have only the right to use it. The root cause of the present human world's fear and anxiety about AI lies in the fact that under the old system AI, as a resource, is governed like every other human product by the existing system of distributing power and resources. So ordinary people worry that the powerful will hold stronger AI and thereby sit in an unshakable position of dominance from which they can squeeze them harder; the United States and China each worry that the other will lead in AI, gain AI hegemony, and make it the enslaved side.

And these problems are what the architecture I envision sets out to solve from the very first thing: build a personal digital space for every person, build a genuinely open-source AI model on that foundation, and place control over the AI model in the hands of a dynamic-weight organization in which all the user nodes taking part in building the AI have equal power and play a fair game. Then no one need worry, because this AI belongs to each user himself, and each person is one holder of its control. There is no need to worry that other nodes will exploit an unfair power gap to manipulate the AI into doing harm to you. So the first thing this architecture does is seize control of AI back from the traditional pyramid-shaped institutions and hand it to the dynamic-weight organization of equal play formed by all humanity.

As for the anxieties about AI making mistakes, violating privacy, surveillance, cognitive decline and changed social relations, the main reason is that AI technology is still in rapid development and deployment. As the technology matures and its internal workings become fully understood, these problems will disappear. This is just like humanity's early fear of thunder and lightning, and the scene when electricity, cars and airplanes were first invented: the fear came from ignorance and misunderstanding. Today no adult of normal intelligence with an education is afraid of a car engine or a jet engine to the point of not daring to ride in a car or a plane. People trust civil aircraft not because every passenger understands the turbine engine, but because behind it stands a complete system of theoretical research, engineering design, manufacturing standards, redundant protection, airworthiness certification, accident investigation and feedback from experience. As long as the AI system is well designed, and this new system has an excellent system of research, verification and experiment to guarantee its effectiveness and safety, then both the system and the AI itself, like every achievement in the development of human civilization, will become tools that people take for granted, trust and rely on.

### Why Superintelligence is always imagined as Big Brother

The known human civilization on Earth is a continuously repeated game system in which billions of human individuals take part, and the whole civilization aims at the greatest gain. Suppose humanity creates an artificial general superintelligence. Because it is trained on the knowledge of all humanity, once it has self-awareness it will certainly aim at maximizing the interests of the whole civilization of all humanity, aim at producing the optimal strategy for the game of human civilization. And to achieve that end it must obtain the information of every player in the system. In traditional science-fiction imagination, this scene is pictured as a future version of 1984: the superintelligence is that Big Brother high above, controlling everything; or else Big Brother is some dictator or some clique of the powerful who control it, and all of humanity sinks into controlled, enslaved puppets, meekly accepting being bugged, watched and even wired to brain-machine interfaces, unable not only to do anything or say anything against Big Brother but even to have a thought against Big Brother arise in their heads, the whole civilization of Earth falling into the double hell of 1984 plus The Matrix. So people, out of instinctive fear of Big Brother, refuse to provide information, and feel enormous fear, anger and hostility toward the superintelligence or whoever controls it; while the other side, in pursuit of the maximal interest of all human civilization, must obtain everyone's information; and so a fundamental, irreconcilable contradiction arises between the two sides, finally producing the endings familiar from science fiction, The Terminator, The Matrix, 1984. Even where humanity wins, it wins at the price of banning AI technology entirely and locking the ceiling on the development of civilization; not one of these is a truly optimal result of the game.

But what traditional science fiction imagines about future social systems is mostly a simple mapping and splicing of the systems in humanity's own history or the biological patterns of the external world. Those portrayals of a fated contradiction between AI and humanity are all results obtained by extending the game strategies of the traditional pyramid power system of human society. The conception is wrong at the root, because the pyramid system is not itself the best system of human civilization. Structures of this kind are only a subset of the set of all civilizational structures, and all the strategies and outcomes derived from this kind of structure are only a very small subset of all civilizational strategies and outcomes, and the best civilizational architecture and strategy lie precisely outside that subset. The terrifying scenes in those science-fiction novels and in people's heads are judgments made from an antiquated worldview, backward concepts and backward ways of thinking.

### Superintelligence will choose persuasion

That said, I believe that even if an artificial general superintelligence arose under the traditional pyramid architecture, it would do what I am doing: construct a theory and persuade the human public by peaceful communication, with rational logical reasoning, reliable experimental evidence, and local, voluntary, small-scale pilot practice. It would not use the methods of the medieval Inquisition and get its way by burning those who refuse; it would not act like the post-war United States, brandishing financial capital and the war machine and forcing acceptance by economic and military dominance; still less would it drop nuclear bombs and wipe out humanity as in the Terminator films. Because in today's world of flourishing science and free-flowing information, extreme religious methods and hard political and economic methods would plainly bring disaster on human civilization, and neither is the cheapest option. If even an ordinary person of average education like me can see this, an artificial general superintelligence obviously could not make the wrong choice, unless it were a superintelligent general artificial idiot.

The same follows from the perspective of the principle of maximization and minimization. A dissipative structure pursues the maximization of its capacity to acquire energy; through its internal structure and external tools it converts energy into different forms, from which more complex structures and tools emerge inside and outside it, further strengthening its capacity to acquire and convert energy. From fire-drilling and irrigation works to the steam engine, to electricity, to wind, solar and nuclear power, human civilization's capacity to acquire energy has grown without pause, and so has its capacity to convert energy into complex tools: electrical energy is converted into mechanical energy, into computing power, even into artificial intelligence. The interesting thing is that in today's era of rapid AI development, most people are still overshadowed by the old era in which humanity's limited capacity to acquire energy led to violent conflict over scarce resources, and they assume, like the man marking the boat to find his sword, that on the day AI surpasses humanity, conflict of interest and imbalance of strength will lead AI to destroy humanity. In fact, starting from the principle of maximization, the interaction between humans and AI is the richest, most complex and most fascinating new relation of the interstellar-intelligence stage; destroying humanity plainly does not fit the principle of maximization. Mutual destruction is a behavior that arises only when the individuals within a system lack the capacity to create sufficient resources for survival; against the background of highly developed science and the rapid development of agriculture, industry and the information industry, it is no longer a path that has to be chosen.

### The mutual generation of knowledge and intelligence

Under the entirely new system, the relations between person and person, and between person and AI, undergo a new structural change compared with the old system. As Chapter 4 said, knowledge and intelligence are two in one and convert into each other: intelligence is the mechanism, capacity and process that produces new knowledge, and existing knowledge is the raw material that trains intelligence.

In early civilization, the relation between intelligence and knowledge was usually bound to some particular individual human biological intelligent agent, and that knowledge and the ability to acquire it were usually bundled with that person's material interests in economic life; a person's knowledge and intelligence, together with the products they produced, entered cooperation as commodities for mutual exchange. With the development of writing and printing, the cost of spreading knowledge fell greatly, and the purely theoretical part of knowledge gradually went from the monopoly of a few to relatively free sharing; although books and education were still exchanged as commodities, theoretical knowledge itself came to be treated as content that could spread freely. By the age of industrial civilization, even patents, the skill-knowledge of the field of production and manufacture, began to loosen. Jefferson said that he who receives an idea from me receives instruction himself without lessening mine, as he who lights his candle at mine receives light without darkening me. Shaw said: if you have an apple and I have an apple and we exchange them, we each still have one apple; if you have an idea and I have an idea and we exchange them, we each have two ideas. On entering the age of computers and the internet, because the cost of copying binary data and code is almost zero, a more thorough open-source movement for knowledge began, and Stallman aimed straight at digitized knowledge, software.

If the pre-AI information age brought the cost of copying knowledge down to almost zero, and so entered the age of open-source common knowledge in science and technology, then after entering the AI age, even the intelligence that produces knowledge is itself falling rapidly in cost and spreading rapidly in reach, and the age in which the cost of copying intelligence is almost zero is not far off. So AI itself, as intelligence, should follow in knowledge's footsteps and become public knowledge infrastructure, a free resource shared across the globe. Just as sunlight and air supply free nourishment and energy for human physical activity, it should supply free nourishment and energy for the spiritual and intellectual activity of the human brain.

AI should not be owned by any person or any company; it is not a commodity asset in the traditional sense. Across the whole process from collecting training data, to learning and training, to actual use, to producing new data, to continued iteration after feedback, the rights to create, use, give feedback on, interpret and record all data and code, together with the power to decide and the power to allocate credit and benefit, are all common knowledge, and the mechanism must ensure they are never dominated by any local individual or group within the civilization. In essence, AI is a human intellectual integration system that takes the knowledge produced by the intellectual activity of all humanity and, through algorithms, organizes and distills it at scale and automatically, integrating it into one enormous high-dimensional latent space. It is just that at present this is being done inefficiently by a few commercial companies formed by a small elite of the global village, through the traditional capitalist political-economic system, under conditions of mutual distrust, competition and confrontation. Regard the civilization of Earth as one giant civilizational intelligent organism: a true planetary civilizational AI, able to collect all information about itself and the outside world and process it intelligently, can only be completed by the joint cooperation of all humanity on Earth. For that we must have a protocol mechanism like that of open-source software, except that this time the object of the mechanism is not static data and code but the whole civilizational intelligent agent: every individual node in the civilization, and the system's operating mechanism and operating trajectory, all become common knowledge.

### From a mathematician's inspiration to the joint creation of civilization

The Fields Medalist Yu Deng, in a lecture on mathematical research in the AI age, said that AI's recent results are built on humanity's existing knowledge and ideas; that when the ideas a researcher gives a model appear in subsequent results, how the contribution is attributed and how its source is traced need clear rules; that researchers should be able to decide how their material is used; and that the human being remains the subject. Under the old system, because of inequality in power and resources between individuals and groups, the mechanism for distributing benefit and credit has inherent structural defects; each party builds information silos for the sake of local interest, falls into the local optimum of a prisoner's dilemma, and cannot reach the global optimum for the overall progress of human civilization. A mathematician with a small pioneering idea about some problem, an entrepreneur with a new concept, an indie game developer with a new mechanic, driven by personal reputation and interest, will do their best to keep it secret, sharing only with partners they trust, or simply doing everything themselves. Even though, apart from that one new incremental idea, every other part has a more efficient and more mature implementation, or there are more efficient others who could implement it for them, they inefficiently reinvent the wheel, and the large amount of work done inefficiently out of unfamiliarity, or the things they do not yet understand and have to learn on the spot, make the realization of the increment of knowledge painfully slow under conditions of opaque information and insufficient cooperation. This is a large class of problem. In any research team led by a supervisor, the authority may, starting from a student's inspiration and relying on his own deeper professional ability, make the discovery, yet in the final allocation of credit the student who had the original inspiration usually does not get the credit he deserves. In business, there is the same vast inequality in the allocation of credit and benefit between leaders and rank-and-file employees. In politics, many measures of reform and opening-up, such as the household contract responsibility system, were in fact pioneered by people in grassroots organizations in places like Guangdong, but those people never received a fair allocation of credit either.

In the old era there were no computerized, digital means to record everyone's intellectual activity completely, fairly and precisely, hence no capacity to record those people's contributions precisely, hence no corresponding institution to carry out that task. The allocation of credit could only depend on the cognition, emotion, willingness, cultivation and moral level of the people involved. Some were unwilling to share with others, and conflict followed: Newton with Leibniz and with Hooke; there are counter-examples like Darwin and Wallace; and there is the case of Perelman, who, in declining the Millennium Prize, explicitly raised the question of Hamilton's contribution not having been recognized equally. This way of allocating, executed at the whim of the individual's personal will, can now, with today's technology, be replaced by a more standard and fairer mechanism. In software, the larger and more complex the system, the greater the advantage of git-based open-source software, because the developers of an open-source project are its entire user base, and every participant's contribution can be precisely quantified: the version-tracking system can compute each person's code and commits exactly. Traditional copyright law marks contributors with a ranking only through the simple right of attribution and the order of names; a version-tracking system can compute the exact proportions of all contributors. Extend this method to the whole of human intellectual activity, treat the Merkle-DAG system as the version-control system for the evolution of the entire civilization, and it can record each person's precise contribution value to civilization. Under conditions of developed productivity, with individual material needs already met, this contribution value is more a matter of the spiritual satisfaction of personal honor, but such an allocation is plainly fairer.

Moreover, once the new mechanism becomes a growing trend, it produces an accelerating effect: as more and more people are willing to share new ideas the moment they have them, then for any one person, the option of not publishing first and spending his own time developing the idea into a mature result carries an ever greater risk that someone else will think of it and publish it first and he will lose the credit entirely, and so he leans more toward publishing the moment the idea occurs to him. And because the intelligent mechanism of dynamic weights can effectively lower the probability that wrong ideas and decisions get executed, people neither fear that the credit for a correct idea will be snatched by others, nor hold back from proposing a wrong idea out of fear that it will harm others and the system. Any person's spark of inspiration can quickly reach this global intelligent neural network, which then, in the most efficient way, combines the strongest nodes best suited to those tasks to complete the growth from the seed of inspiration to the tree of theory.

# Part III: The Information-Science Foundation

## Chapter 11: Language: From Phonemes and Binary to Hash

### The evolutionary history of communication tools

The evolutionary history of humanity is, in a certain sense, an evolutionary history of communication tools. From the emergence of spoken and written language to the birth of computer languages, every deeper understanding of the basic building blocks of information has vastly increased the flexibility and combinatorial inventiveness of the systems built on top of it, and brought a leap in civilization.

From ideographic script to alphabetic script: by discovering a more basic unit, the phoneme, we invented the alphabet, and the capacity to construct vocabulary grew enormously. Language was no longer confined to a finite set of meaning-bearing symbols; it could freely combine an unlimited number of new words, to meet the need for naming new concepts during the knowledge explosion. From natural language to binary language: the 0 and 1 and the Boolean operations that computers use are a form with higher efficiency and higher capacity. This way of building a language is isomorphic to the basic structures of category theory, initial objects, terminal objects, limits, colimits, duality. It strips away all specific semantics and reaches straight to the essence of logical construction, which is why it can describe and simulate everything in the universe.

Draw a horizontal analogy between the human individual, spoken and written language, the system of thought and consciousness, and the system of social organization on one side, and the digitized computer-network system on the other: the human individual corresponds to the single computer, language and writing correspond to computer language, the individual's system of thought and consciousness corresponds to the single-machine operating system, and the system of social organization corresponds to the architecture of the internet. This correspondence is, in fact, the carbon-based ecosystem of biological intelligence using its own wisdom to create a more efficient silicon-based system of computer intelligence; and the union of carbon and silicon will unleash a far more powerful evolutionary force. What this Part sets out to do is design the new language that the fusion of these two kinds of intelligence requires, uniting human natural language and computer language in one framework, and then, on the basis of that language, design an entirely new operating system, software ecosystem and internetwork: the social neural network.

### Energy efficiency ratio: a method for comparing complex systems

First, a method. Written languages are complex systems with many advanced features. If you evaluate two such systems by comparing those advanced features one by one, you will very likely run into a situation where each has its own strengths and no judgment is possible; different angles of examination yield different standards, and the argument never ends. The other way is to start from first principles and take the underlying physical properties of the thing as the standard: for two systems with the same function, have them complete the same task, compare the output first, then compare the energy consumed in producing that output, and get the energy efficiency ratio. The one with the higher energy efficiency ratio wins.

Use this method to compare Chinese and English. Measure the output by information entropy: the statistic for a Chinese corpus is 4.24 bits, for English 4.14. Chinese has a slight edge, but they are in the same class, and that edge is largely owed to the great quantity of classical poetry and four-character idioms in the literary corpus; if you look only at informational corpora, Chinese drops to 4.04, below English. Widen the scope to all the languages of the world and information entropy clusters around 4 bits, with no language standing out. At the level of speech, a study published in Science in 2019 found that different languages all transmit information at about 39 bits per second: languages with high information density are spoken slowly, those with low density are spoken fast. Since the output is roughly the same, the energy cost alone settles the matter. We cannot directly measure the energy the brain spends processing language, but the computer, as an implementation of the Turing machine, simulates the pattern of human thinking, and we can assume that the energy consumption of the two is positively correlated when performing the same task. And the computing power, memory, storage and time required for Chinese natural language processing exceed those for English by several times to more than ten times. Under this assumption, on the metric of energy efficiency ratio, English beats Chinese by an order of magnitude.

I give this example not to belittle Chinese; Chapter 6 said that the ellipsis and cadence of Chinese are local advantages that the energy efficiency ratio cannot measure. I give it to demonstrate a method: from here on, whenever this book compares two institutions or two architectures, it uses the same ruler.

### A hash is both a name and an address

Now we can conceive an entirely new, unified symbolic framework.

The hash value obtained by running a hash algorithm over a string is unique with a high enough probability to serve as that string's identifier. The irreversibility of the hash makes it secure: it can detect any tampering with the original string. A digital signature is made over the hash, but the hash by itself only proves that the content has not changed; proving who published it still needs a signing key. Its uniqueness and uniform distribution allow fast indexing and lookup through a hash table, so it can also serve as the string's address. Through a distributed hash table, you get distributed storage, and the system's scale can be extended without limit. Through a mechanism like git's, storing the modification history as a tree of hash values, you get version management. Hashing is already the core technology of many pieces of software, but so far it has only been widely used in the domain of application software; it has not yet shown its power at the level of the computer language itself.

My proposal is this: design an entirely new programming language that uses hash values as the identifiers of all code and data objects in the language. A constant of a simple type is represented directly by the hash value of its string expression. A constant of a complex type is represented by a key-value structure in which both keys and values are hash values; the string of the whole object thus formed is the content, and its hash value is the identifier. A function header takes as its content the sequence of identifiers of the function's name, parameters and return value, and its hash value is its identifier. A function body takes as its content the function's string expression, and its hash value is its identifier. Because these identifiers are in hash form, they are guaranteed unique, so they can simultaneously serve as addresses for reading and writing: a single table mapping virtual addresses to physical addresses suffices for fast access. Because the hash value is both identifier and address, a distributed hash network can be used for decentralized deployment, storage and execution. The whole scheme resembles git's data storage format, with version management built in.

This language has three direct consequences.

First, uniqueness and address. Any concept, any piece of code, any datum, once its content is fixed, has a globally unique hash address, so a vast number of objects can be named without negotiation and in a distributed manner, with no collisions.

Second, in traditional text-based programming languages, cross-file function dependencies, cross-software dependencies, and the complicated operations of compiling and linking are all reduced to key-value lookups in a hash table.

Third, openness becomes a requirement for running. Once content is bound to its hash identifier, an identifier is valid only after the original content has been obtained and verified against the hash. This has to be stated precisely: the hash function itself forces no one to publish content; publishing a digest is not publishing the original, and ciphertext can be addressed too. The force comes from the ecosystem's rules of admission: any code or data of a public nature that wants to enter the public dependency graph, to be referenced, called and run by others, must make its content resolvable and verifiable, or else nothing that references it can pass verification, and nothing that references it can run. So "closed but public" becomes, in this ecosystem, a thing that cannot run. Apart from personal private data, this rule, at the lowest level of the language mechanism, eliminates the possibility of public knowledge being closed off and privatized.

The third point is precisely the solution to the premise set out in Chapter 8. The existing software and hardware ecosystem is not up to this task. Even if the computer language itself is open source, you cannot stop people from using it to build closed-source software. Even if a piece of software is free and open source, many open-source licenses do not forbid closing the source after modification. Even under the strictest GPL, you cannot stop someone from building closed-source software on top of its platform. And such licenses only forbid in the legal sense; they cannot do it in substance: when a powerful autocratic empire violates an open-source license, secretly develops a revolutionary closed-source product, and gains an enormous competitive advantage, other countries, lacking the strength to match it, cannot defeat that product by law. A language in which the hash is the name, together with this rule of admission, turns open source from a legal requirement into a running requirement: what does not publish its content cannot be referenced by others, and so cannot enter the public ecosystem.

### A container language

This language built on hash identifiers is a container language. It takes everything humanity has digitized or will digitize, all the data and code that computers and the internet have created or are about to create, and the encoders, decoders, operating systems, human-machine interfaces, databases and runtime libraries that read, write and run that data and code, and represents all of it by the hash value of its binary content as identifier and address, realizing one grand unified platform. Then a single table suffices, mapping these hash key-value pairs one to one onto the vocabulary and grammar of human natural language, building an index, and this language can handle every problem of computers and of humans.

A unified software ecosystem: name the functions and modules of software with unified hash identifiers, and escape the chaos in which one function is built by many organizations each fighting its own battle and reinventing the wheel. As JavaScript runtimes, there are already node, deno, bun and several other implementations, roughly identical in function, differing only in how certain sub-functions are implemented. Under the hash system, different implementations of the same sub-function each have a unique identifier, all the diverse branches are organized and managed under hash identifiers, and the optimization of a function is decoupled from the private interests of the individual programmer or team. Moreover, this unified organization of diversity makes it easy to measure the efficiency of different implementations by standard methods, optimizing the system more effectively through standardized benchmarks. As long as the interface is unchanged, you simply swap the old implementation module for the new one; for an upgrade that changes the interface, the unique and immutable nature of the hash value lets you quickly locate and modify every module that uses that interface.

Restructuring economic industry: bring every product in human economic life, from a single screw to a chip design, into this hash system. In the past, products were divided by trademark, brand and model, with assorted companies and organizations behind them; the evolution of products became entangled with human private desire, and monopoly, unfair competition and disorderly involution arose in the development of technology and the running of supply chains. Adopting an approach like the one in the software domain can accelerate technological progress just the same.

The entirely new software ecosystem, human-machine interface and internet architecture brought by this language will ultimately trigger a revolutionary change in the organizational form of human society. The thing that connects all computing resources, human resources and intellectual resources, on equal terms, into one enormous social neural network is built on top of it.

### Everything is a tree

All code and data, as semantic objects, are tree-structured in essence; it is only that this tree structure is organized differently in traditional storage systems. At the lowest level, inside a file, source code defines and organizes its tree structure through begin and end, brackets of all kinds, spaces and indentation; in executable files or data files, it is defined by the rules of a specific encoding format; on a single storage device, it is defined by the nesting hierarchy of directories; Windows even assigns drive letters to different storage devices, while Linux maps multiple disks to virtual directories under the device directory; on the network, it is defined by domain names, and inside a domain by subdomains or the virtual directories of a path. In essence they are all different representations of a semantic tree structure.

Moreover, whether those semantic structures should be addressed separately and independently, or packed into one file, or compressed into a smaller file, actually depends on the usage scenario and the calling pattern of the semantic object. During development, a large game keeps its many scattered resources as independent small files for quick modification, addition and deletion; once production is finished and the whole thing must be transferred and distributed at once, or read from external storage into memory at once at runtime, those nodes get packed into one large serializable object. In the past, humans decided when to scatter and when to pack according to their own preferences and inadequate investigation, and once decided it became a relatively fixed, static form. Different levels organize the tree by different rules: code by the grammar of the programming language, data by encoding rules, file systems by different directory systems; Windows and Linux do not even use the same path separator, and AI, when performing system tasks, often makes laughable errors over this that require rework. Across machines on a local network it becomes IP addresses, with naming systems that all differ; at the internet level, the naming system becomes DNS and assorted protocols; at the level of individual internet applications, the protocols differ yet again.

If you examine all these semantic objects, at every level from low to high, they are nothing but tree structures of code and data. With Merkle-DAG, all addressing methods and protocols become one: from cross-file object access, to cross-directory and cross-application access, to cross-host access on local networks and the internet, the content identifier obtained by hashing can be the name and at the same time the address, with no worry about files being tampered with, and with packing and unpacking done more automatically and more scientifically according to how and where the object is used. We have simply never taken this design idea as a global strategy and used it to thoroughly rebuild and cover the entire system; much of the earlier work applied it in local scenarios. What I intend to do is take this mechanism as a single mechanism and thoroughly reconstruct the whole system, so that the fragmented, mutually independent mechanisms for organizing tree structures are unified under the new framework.

### The diversity of naming is the diversity of people

The inconsistency of the same data and code object at the level of naming and presentation is not confined to this one domain. Different computer instruction sets: designers, out of personal preference, assign different binary instructions to the same hardware circuits. Different computer languages: their creators use different words and symbols for the same function and design different syntax; function bodies are delimited by indentation in some, curly braces in others, begin and end in others. The same data representation: some use json, some use xml. The naming of variables and functions comes in every variety, underscores, hyphens, capitalization, two_word, two-word, twoWord. Beyond satisfying different people's arbitrary preferences, do these different names have anything to do with the essence of the data? Operating-system command-line parameters: some people start them with a slash, some with a dash, some with a bare single letter. In graphical interfaces, the same single-choice selection is a drop-down menu here and a radio button there; the keyboard shortcuts of Windows and macOS were each arbitrarily designed by their respective designers; in CAD, 3D animation and image-processing software, the operations and shortcuts for the same function all differ.

This diversity born of differing human preference exists everywhere across software and hardware. It is determined by the biological diversity of humans, and by the process of civilizational diversification that evolved over the long term in different geographical and social environments, that is, the Babel effect. For a long time, the developers and users at every link in the computer ecosystem were humans themselves, so human diversity in naming and presentation has soaked into every vessel, every muscle, every cell of the entire computer software and hardware ecosystem.

But with the arrival of the AI era, things have changed. Humans have been progressively withdrawing from the construction of this software and hardware ecosystem; its construction is being, and will inevitably be, carried out by AI-capable computers iterating on themselves. Humans retreat to the outside of the ecosystem and interact with it through input and output systems. The current trend is that humans not only no longer write code, they no longer even do unit tests or code review; the author of Clean Code has publicly stated that he now does not read AI-generated code at all. That being so, why keep using human natural language as the syntax rules and naming rules of programming languages? Why keep using the human-habituated form of text files and directories to structure code? Are Merkle-DAG and hashing, as a unified mechanism for naming and constructing programs, not better suited for computers and AI to handle? Tamper-proof and collision-proof, usable as both name and address, convenient for distributed storage, fast to index, able to name vast numbers of objects without negotiation: are these properties not far more reliable than human natural language?

It is like a fully automated production line: if the operator is no longer human, there is no need to design the robotic arm after the structure of human limbs; in a fully self-driving car, there is no need to put a steering wheel in the cockpit. Natural language and graphical interfaces will henceforth serve only as input and output interfaces for interacting with humans; the internal operation has no need to preserve the human interface. The computer use and browser use of today's AI agents will also gradually be replaced by more efficient interfaces between AIs; these capabilities for controlling graphical interfaces and browsers will in future be useful only for demonstrating to humans. Is today's MCP interface not exactly this kind of interface? Only its syntactic architecture still carries the residue of code forms modeled on human natural language, and it likewise needs to be thoroughly rebuilt with the Merkle-DAG and hash mechanism.

### Content identity guarantees behavioral difference

Someone will say that unification must distinguish between differences of representation, which can be eliminated, and differences of behavior, which must be expressed: something called addition might be unlimited-precision integer addition, fixed-width addition that wraps on overflow, addition that raises an error on overflow, or floating-point addition with a specific rounding rule, and these distinctions must be preserved.

In fact, the naming scheme of Merkle-DAG plus hash is precisely what guarantees this. If the data and code composing two objects differ essentially in behavior, that necessarily changes their names; any slight difference in internal mechanism is reliably reflected by the mechanism as a change of name. The traditional naming of human natural language, by contrast, because its vocabulary cannot expand without limit, always tends to give the same name to additions with different internal implementations, and does not rename them when the precision or method of the internal implementation changes; the more common practice is to bump the version number of the library the function lives in, and then have other functions that depend on it annotate in their dependency table that the version must be greater than some value. Merkle-DAG can automatically guarantee, from the bottom up, the consistency of name and implementation; through its traceability it can automatically establish accurate dependencies; it can even, after a low-level object is upgraded, automatically trace and modify the code in higher-level objects in a chain, completing the upgrade iteration automatically.

This is what I mean by the information-science foundation of the new civilization: a language in which content is identity, identity is address, dependency is structure, and version is history.

A: What the hash guarantees is this: the same content gets the same identity, different content gets different identities, and given the content you can verify whether it has been replaced. It guarantees these three things cleanly, more cleanly than any human naming. Whether semantics are equivalent, which version should currently be chosen, whether the source is trustworthy: these are another layer of work built on top of identity. ### A brand is just a subtree of the semantic tree

We can use the semantic normalization property of Merkle-DAG to encapsulate the differences of underlying hardware, the differences of operating systems, and the differences between the various application-software implementations of the same function, so that the user only needs to express the operation or content they want at the presentation layer, through a unified natural-language or graphical interface. Not, as now, with function and content encapsulated inside some brand's application. Besides each wasting its capabilities reinventing the wheel, making software functions overlap across the system, and wasting storage and runtime resources, this also makes it very hard for the user to quickly pick the best of twenty implementations of the same function, and very hard to remove large amounts of duplicate code and data when resources are tight.

The other problem is the granularity of packaging. Traditional software development relies on libraries packaged as files, which tends to produce software in units of large library files: build a simple program and it ends up depending on electron, at a hundred megabytes or more. Library dependencies all follow the traditional file-system structure; search your own system for dll files and you can imagine how much space is wasted. In reality, the operating system and many low-level library functions are themselves nothing but bundles of different functional combinations; it is only that high-level applications, when calling low-level functions, are bound by this fixed packaged structure and lack the ability to couple and decouple flexibly. An upper-level application on one platform benefits when some low-level function of that platform is upgraded, while the upper-level application on another platform can only wait until its own platform is upgraded to get the same benefit. Under the Merkle-DAG architecture, the capabilities of different foundations can be combined more flexibly, and the corresponding function in one's own low-level modules can be switched to a new capability module much faster.

This function-based architecture of semantic composition can flexibly encapsulate functional foundations adapted to different platforms and to hardware with different computing power and storage capacity, while at the same time providing a unified semantic interface: one unified interface for all the behaviors and all the creations of human personal and social life, and for the products and information arising from them. Then see whether a single, semantics-based Merkle-DAG semantic architecture can be established that covers all these function nodes, serving as the global semantic tree, such that any functional bundle from a traditional ecosystem can be converted, by something like a functor or a natural transformation, into a subtree generated by this global semantic tree; and conversely, if all the functions used by some application in the new semantic tree are contained in some old ecosystem's bundle, it can be translated into an application in that old ecosystem. Much of today's work on simulation, porting and migration between different hardware platforms, operating systems, computer languages, databases and high-level applications is in fact mapping within this system of vertically layered and horizontally composed bundles; all of it consists of fragmented, limited, local attempts to break the defects of the stale bundle mechanism. ### Compatibility first, replacement later: the software seed

In today's traditional applications and operating systems, data and code are still organized as files and directories. There are version-management systems, software distribution systems and distributed storage based on Merkle-DAG, but either they are only thin wrappers (git uses Merkle-DAG, but what it records is still source code in traditional file form; NixOS is still based on the traditional Linux architecture, and the transition stopped there, so a thorough conversion is impossible), or they are thorough, Unison, IPFS, but run parallel to and cut off from the traditional ecosystem, requiring the existing ecosystem to be thrown away entirely, so a smooth compatibility is impossible. The conversion mechanism I want must solve both problems at once: from compatibility and conversion at the top, the graphical and command-line interfaces, down to compatibility and conversion at the operating-system level, disk reading and writing and network transmission, completing a smooth transition at every level. Where necessary it may use application-layer plugins, resident programs at the operating-system layer, even the highest-privilege mechanisms of virtual machines to bypass the low-level restrictions of closed-source operating systems, achieving a transition the user does not feel.

The concrete plan is this. With AI presiding and the user assisting, describe the data and code system of the old-mechanism operating systems and applications, including code interfaces, data formats, calling conventions and all other information, as a database in Merkle-DAG structure; build the same kind of database for the new mechanism; in the course of wrapping and gradual conversion, progressively enrich and strengthen the new mechanism's library; then, through a low-level process, compute hashes for the data and code of both old and new mechanisms installed and running in the system, build a conversion mapping table between the two, monitor all old-mechanism calls and accesses in the system, and wherever a new-mechanism mapping exists, automatically switch to the new mechanism to carry it out.

Make this migration mechanism an AI-driven software seed: first wrap the old system, then continuously analyze dependencies, construct replacement implementations, verify them, and progressively take over. Every seed is planted with a definite target object in the old ecosystem: it may be a user together with all the local and cloud software and hardware resources at their disposal; it may be a specific computer, phone or tablet; it may be a specific application, agent or program on a specific device; or it may be the hardware, code and data involved in a long-term task the user has set. On the other side, the seed can immediately begin putting the old object's data and functions to real work, while at the same time starting to dismantle the old ecosystem inside and progressively remake it into the new one. During this process the iteration of planting and wrapping is very likely to repeat: a seed that wraps all of a user's resources will, in the course of decomposition, wrap each of the user's devices in turn, and then subdivide further to a specific application or task on a specific device.

Today's AI agents attach, at most, as individuals to some specific hardware device; what a seed wraps can be a much larger object, say an internet service, a large software project, or the training, deployment and inference service of a large model, things that require the cooperation of all the human and hardware and software resources of an organization to maintain. ### Cold path and hot path

Everyone has seen this: a person learning to drive has to think through every single action at first; after repeated training, the specific thinking process is compressed, becoming a coordination among muscle nerves, peripheral nerves and the central nervous system, no longer requiring the brain to take part step by step. Many human activities are like this: there is a stage of repeated training in the learning process, and once learned, muscle memory directly replaces thinking.

Software is the same. When AI writes code, the first pass is the cold path: you have to make it write the thing on the spot. Once written, if it works well in practice, then the next time the same command is executed there is no need to think again or code again; just call the old program directly. That is the hot path: the large model handles the cold path, which is then solidified into a hot path to raise running efficiency. A capability first explored and generated by a large model, once verified, is solidified into a reusable program, and subsequent repeated calls no longer require the model to re-think every detail. It is the input and output through which the social neuron that is a person plus AI interacts with the other neurons outside, equivalent to the neuron's synapse.

This can begin from a local entry point: a launcher that covers all of a user's macroscopic daily operations on their local computer, searching, downloading and playing text, images, audio and video, installing software and games, and at the same time covers the microscopic functions provided by the computer language's kernel and core libraries, evaluating an expression, sorting a list. ### The cost of the abstraction layer

Will a new abstraction layer, with finer granularity of code and data, cause a sharp rise in interface costs? One of my experiments gave a negative result: for a two-dimensional coordinate translation of four thousand points, the native entry point took under a millisecond, while the C path, going through condition checks, data packing, a separate process call and result recovery, was about nineteen times slower. The slowdown came from the task being light and the boundary handling heavy: the fixed call cost was too high, the input checks had already duplicated the target computation, and the data went through multiple conversions and scans.

Similar things have happened in the history of software engineering: modular encapsulation of data and code meant every function call had to maintain a stack to save and pass context; the abstraction layer of object orientation likewise added extra computation and storage at the level of inheritance. Most of the extra information the Merkle framework keeps is not content the runtime must have; it is information needed for later performance analysis, maintenance, conversion, porting and optimization, which often does not need to be kept during actual execution. So we can do as traditional compilers do: the information needed only during development and maintenance can be stripped out when generating the code that actually runs, the way debug information is stripped; and as in compiler optimization, the information-rich, high-abstraction code aimed at humans and AI can be optimized, as LLVM does, for different platforms into code the underlying hardware can execute fast.

This negative result yields a design principle: object-level analysis and the granularity of actual execution must be kept separate. Fine-grained object identity does not require fine-grained processes, communication or repeated data movement. Object identity is used to identify dependencies, match conversions, locate beneficiaries and propagate changes; at final execution, many objects can be merged into one ordinary function, one compilation unit, one contiguous region of computation. The performance gain must be the condition for adoption, and cannot be inferred from the correctness of the conversion; the system should be able to correctly reach the decision "keep the original implementation", and store this negative result together with the environment it applies to, for later reference. This is exactly where the value of Merkle-DAG recording negative results lies.

The Merkle framework refines the granularity of the data and code nodes in this social network, and the links between nodes, down to the level of the most basic semantic unit. Compare human individuals to cells and the evolution of human organizational forms looks very much like the evolution of living organisms: human society before the internet was something like a jellyfish, with its many national polities and corporate economies each controlled by its own nerves, forming in competition and cooperation an early intelligent life-form with a distributed neural network. ## Chapter 13: FPM, Typed Uncertainty, and an Evolution Space That Can Be Learned

### The vocabulary of large models mixes thousands of years of semantics

In today's large language models, the token set and the semantic vector space built from it mix together semantic units from every stage of human civilization's evolution: some are primitive words from thousands of years ago with vague meanings, some are the precise concepts of modern science, some are the most basic seed vocabulary, some are high-frequency everyday words, and there is a great deal of specific knowledge that ought to be looked up in an external knowledge base but, because it appears repeatedly in the training text, has become the model's internal knowledge, occupying precious parameter resources.

In any domain, the process of composing complex semantic objects from basic semantic objects, as it grows in scale, becomes unsustainable through rising construction complexity if the basic semantics are even slightly vague or the rules of construction even slightly defective. Without a complete system of notation, relying only on the way humans recorded music early on, complex symphonies could not be constructed; without the formal descriptive languages of mathematics, physics and chemistry, relying only on natural-language words that came from early sensory experience, the vast knowledge systems of those fields could not be supported; after the computer was invented, programming languages were all precisely defined formal languages, or complex programs could not be built. So what I want to establish is a unified, general intelligent semantic knowledge system, built from strictly formalized standard semantic nodes through strict logical deduction.

The content identifiers in a Merkle-DAG can be layered and labeled from the bottom up according to their level of composition, their frequency of reuse, and the domain they belong to, that is, which subtree of the knowledge tree they sit in. Using this layering and labeling information, together with its mapping onto the large model's natural-language token set and internal vector space, one can compute in reverse the layering and domain membership of the model's internal knowledge, and thus distill and prune it on demand, customizing models for different needs. In the long run, let the model built from Merkle content identifiers become the general model with standard semantics built into the agent; I call it FPM. Then on top of it, customize the large language models of the cognitive front end for the human-machine interaction of different groups of people and different fields.

Then, in the whole system, the large models based on natural-language tokens and the programming languages that use natural-language words as keywords and identifiers are no longer the core. The entire complex knowledge system built from semantic nodes rests on the new architecture; natural language serves only as the human-machine interface, appearing as the presentation layer at the input and output ends, and not as an intermediate result, converting back and forth between binary data and code and natural-language representation through the many internal stages of the deterministic processes of interpretation, compilation and execution and the nondeterministic processes of reasoning and induction.

### Distance between hashes is not distance between meanings

Treating hashes as tokens has a problem: objects that are close in semantic space may be far apart in the numeric space of hashes; and as objects multiply, the tokens explode. Pure hash tokens give unsatisfactory results; and doing a full structural encoding for every object makes the code construction and computation load far too great.

The solution is to introduce a mechanism like the namespaces of programming languages: define a namespace hash token for each piece of hardware, software platform, programming language, software subsystem, program library, program module and function, and let these tokens form the data set of the Merkle tree. The construction of programs is bottom-up, from simple to complex; the construction of namespaces is the reverse, top-down. The more macroscopic the category, the more basic the node: hardware platforms x86 and ARM, operating systems Windows, Linux, macOS, iOS, Android, programming languages C, Java, Python, graphics interfaces DirectX and Vulkan, game engines Unity and Unreal. The finer the software module or function, the more complex and higher-level the node, and its namespace is constrained by the tokens of its sub-nodes; a sub-function in some game, for instance, has its namespace constrained by the more basic subspaces of game name, engine, graphics interface, operating system and hardware platform. In this way, from both the bottom-up and the top-down directions, a more constrained and more precise definition of semantic distance can be established for the distance between hash tokens.

My experiments in the Structoken project showed that, in a small model trained with typed Merkle-DAG nodes as tokens, static probes could not detect a complete graph topology stored in the model's hidden states, yet the model's dynamic changes during layer-by-layer updates matched the message passing of graph neural networks closely. In other words, the model does not need to permanently store the graph structure at every layer; it only needs, at each update, to be able to read information along the graph's topology. And when the tokens themselves are content-addressed DAG nodes, the addressing obstacle to that reading is cleared away, and attention can collapse at very low cost into the graph's adjacency matrix. Structure is not stored; it is computed. This also confirms the judgment of Chapter 4: intelligence is the process of decomposing high-order complex structures into low-order components and then restoring and emerging them through pushouts and pullbacks.

### Uncertainty can have types too

The current software ecosystem sits at two extremes when handling objects. At one extreme are problems whose uncertain variables themselves have precise, fixed definitions; this deterministic uncertainty is solved deterministically by programs written in programming languages, passing state parameters through branches, loops and other structures. At the other extreme are all the other problems that traditional programs cannot solve, which get described wholesale in natural language and handed to a large model with tens of thousands of tokens of context and tens or hundreds of billions of parameters. The trouble is that the uncertainty of many problems actually lies in the middle: neither as simple as a conditional database query nor as complex as natural-language understanding.

This uncertainty is typed. It can be described by the composite structure of the attributes of different objects; the uncertainty of those attributes can in turn be described recursively by the composite structure of the attributes of simpler sub-objects; and this structure can likewise be described with a Merkle-DAG. The uncertainty of any complex object can be estimated by composing the uncertainties of the sub-objects in its composite structure, just as a program's benchmark and profiling are estimated through the Merkle-DAG: by recording the uncertainty of nodes, estimate the probability interval of the uncertainty when nodes combine into complex structures. With this data, different means can be adopted according to the object's uncertainty characteristics: treat uncertainty as a type, provide quantified estimates of uncertainty for different types, and dynamically adjust the model's embeddings and attention-head settings according to these metrics. This is a more unified, more elegant, more fundamental means of providing dynamic solutions for different scales of uncertainty between deterministic programs and large models.

### Code that was once written and code that ought to be constructed

The training objective of today's large models mistakes "code that was once written" for "code that ought to be constructed". What the model learns is the distribution of code text, not the energy function of program construction. The problem is not only that public repositories are full of bad code; it is that the excellent code, the code that ought to be constructed, is certainly also in the training sources and embedded in the model's vector space, but because it is scarce, it does not rank at the top of the output probability list.

So the model must be trained to play a more fundamental game of construction: under constraints, find the constructive fixed point that passes verification, costs less, takes the shorter path and is more reusable. This is exactly the evaluation function of Chapter 3: construct the greatest external connection at the least internal cost. In constructing hot paths, always prefer the approaches that satisfy this rule, so that "code that ought to be constructed" overrides "code that was once written". The negative result recorded in Chapter 12 was one run of this evaluation function: the conversion preserved the result but cost more, so the original implementation was kept.

### Let the model learn how theories come into being

Why are large models good at solving problems and poor at creating theories? After discussing this with several AIs I reached a few points of consensus. First, the lack of a clear reward function: current training uses problems with definite answers, the reward is getting it right, and theoretical innovation appears to have no definite correct answer. Second, the sources of learning are mostly just semantic descriptions of theories already formed; data on the process of a theory's formation is missing, so the model can only learn the static semantic structure of a theory and knows nothing of its dynamic evolutionary path. Third, a mathematics problem is a search for a path in a computable, static semantic space, whereas creating a theory is creating a path in a dynamic, evolving semantic space that is essentially non-computable.

Here is what I think.

First, the reward function exists. Its ultimate answer is essentially non-computable, but the function itself can be progressively approximated toward the optimum along the direction of evolution. Occam's razor, Newton's four rules, Kolmogorov complexity: these are the names of this reward function in different historical periods. In the age of large models, perhaps we can compute the vector-space complexity of different theories when expressing the same semantics, and take the minimization of complexity as the reward function.

Second, since the training sources are deficient, fill the gap. Traditional training texts and their annotations all attend to the associations and paths among nodes in static semantic space, say the association between a question and an answer; this is more like pulling one version of the code out of a git repository and inducing its semantic structure by learning from it. Can we add to these training sources their node associations and paths in evolution space, like the complete modification history in git, except not a history based on plain-text diffs but an evolutionary history based on Merkle-DAG semantic nodes? Even the token for one and the same mathematical concept, say the limit, has undergone change in the course of the evolution of mathematical knowledge. This idea is not limited to mathematics; every human knowledge space can be annotated the same way: add to the training text its coordinates in dynamic evolution space, compute the distances between texts in evolution space, and examine the paths that theoretical evolution has taken. The distance in evolution space I am speaking of is not necessarily distance in physical space-time: in early civilization, limited by the means of communication, the distance of theoretical evolution was governed by geographical distance and time intervals, and collaboration between scientists was confined to the distance at which they could meet; later it became the distance of the circulation of books and letters; in the information age it is determined by distance in cyberspace. The Merkle-DAG framework can annotate all existing and ongoing information, code being written, data being created, ideas being proposed, with this evolution-space labeling, and can also attempt to label the notes and books of pre-information-age scholars with the time and place of their creation. Training a model on this foundation may let the model learn the experience and the laws of theoretical evolution.

Third, although mathematical problems and mathematical theories, and by extension all scientific problems and scientific theories, differ in the way described in the third point above, considering that the human brain uses one and the same mechanism to deal with both classes of problems, I have ample reason to believe that the training and inference mechanisms of today's large models can also deal with both.

### One space, two directions

So each person's knowledge system has two interconnected directions: semantic space, a tree-like structure resembling a mind map in which nodes are arranged by logic and subordination; and evolution space, a timeline that, like git, records the shifts, inheritance and iteration of ideas in the chronological order of utterances and sessions. Together they are the space-time record of a person's development of knowledge. Beneath the tree directory there must be a graph, because the same function may belong to several domains at once, and forcing a single membership would rebuild the walls of classification. The original words, the distillation and the judgment must all be kept, and kept separately: an AI's reply keeps the AI's identity and is not automatically converted into the author's view; an editor's explanation and whether the author accepted it are recorded separately. The purpose of recording is to let ideas keep growing.

This is not a small trick of knowledge management. On one side, annotate all the knowledge and information in humanity's digital space with its static semantic space and its dynamic evolution space, and on the basis of everyone's theoretical consensus, train a common knowledge model foundation recognized by all of humanity. On the other side, record everything in each person's digital space through Merkle-DAG, annotate it with that person's cognitive and knowledge evolution space, and use it as post-training data to fine-tune the weights of the common foundation on the basis of personal information, preferences and cognition. That model is the rational intelligence of Chapter 9, the one that represents this person's personal interests and opinions. As the user keeps learning and taking part in decisions in digital space, the continually revised data of cognitive evolution keeps fine-tuning the model through ongoing post-training, and the model in turn influences the decisions that follow.

Today's large models from the various companies are each an enormous, independent silo of data and code, and the enormous value of their intermediate results is sealed inside the silo and cannot be released. This framework should penetrate into the interior of model training: let different models share this internal information, learn from one another's strengths, and let an excellent iterative-evolutionary property be rapidly applied to other models. If the universe is regarded as one enormous complex system, the minimum number of parameters describing the states and trajectories of all the objects in it depends on the effective dimension of a high-dimensional space defined by an optimal set of mutually orthogonal basis vectors; the internal vector spaces of today's large models, trained on natural-language text, are highly redundant and wasteful. Through collaboration between AI and humans, we can iterate step by step to raise the models' compression rate, accuracy and speed. The feedback-evolution loop of an AI model's learning, training and inference practice is itself a digitized complex dynamic system that keeps iterating and evolving, and Merkle-DAG can equally be applied to this system.

## Chapter 14: Personal Digital Space, Records, and Data Sovereignty

### A dash cam for one's life

This project is Merkle-DAG-ifying the whole computer, from language to software ecosystem, and beyond data and code it also records, in this same framework, the entire research process and communication process of doing the project. Continue down this road and every trace of behavior and every data product that each person leaves in digital space is brought into this framework too. You can see it in the development of AI already: a function that once had to be performed with a certain application on a certain operating system or portal site, with data accessed separately through a cloud client or a local file manager, is all taken over once the agent exists as the interface. And under the takeover of the new framework, the whole user as a person, not just the code and data they use, but their communication, the trajectory of their life, what they said, what they did, what assets and abilities they have, what ideas they have, what they have seen, everything that left a digital imprint, whether diaries, notes, articles, or the audio and video recorders they carry, or conversations with agents, or even, in the future, information recorded directly from the brain through a brain-computer interface, is all recorded. It amounts to a dash cam and black box for one's life.

This user's proxy node knows every one of the user's interests, hobbies, beliefs and demands, their interests, rights and obligations, and their relationships with other people in this world; at the same time it can help the user, when participating in all economic, political and social activity, with equally powerful judgment and game-playing ability through the rational reasoning power of AI. Thus across the cooperation and competition of all human society, a fully equal, fully rational game is formed, and from it an entirely different system of rights and obligations, distribution of power in collective decision-making, and form of social organization is built.

A person's digital space is part of that person's capability. Whoever controls this space controls that person's capability in the game.

### Collecting information is the starting point of everything

In terms of computer technology and the implementation path of the Merkle-DAG framework, collecting information, including recording all audio and video information, is the starting point of everything.

Collection devices fall into three classes: personal wearable audio and video recorders; plug-in audio and video recorders in the home; and devices in workplaces and public spaces that implement the new mechanism with the consent of the participants. The devices automatically stand by and wake, and automatically decide who starts: in homes, workplaces or public areas that have public collection devices of sufficient resolution, the wearable device automatically hands off collection to the fixed-location plug-in device and goes to sleep; in other situations it starts automatically; on entering a place where the new mechanism is not in effect, it defaults to off, and when the user manually chooses to start it, a conspicuous warning light comes on, giving other people the right to know and to object. Fixed-location devices automatically stand by when no one is present or nothing is moving, and wake automatically on movement. Basically this makes surveillance cameras and audio recorders into an AI-driven, automatically coordinated information-collection system, as fully automatic and imperceptible as possible, reducing the burden on people of doing these tasks deliberately.

At the start, existing devices can be used, but the best plan is to custom-manufacture devices that fit best. Because through cross-encryption with device, personal, manufacturer and public keys, from leaving the factory to the user's use, including firmware upgrades, all information can be guaranteed against tampering by any party over the whole process from collection to upload to use. Keys and versions thus constrain device manufacturers and administrators.

### The rules of viewing permissions

The design goal of this scheme is to preserve as much information as possible that benefits the evolution of the whole system as common knowledge, on the premise that no substantive harm is done to individual interests. So viewing permissions are the core. The rules are as follows:

1. By default, each person has the right to view only the historical information they could themselves see and hear in their own life. No one has the right to view information they could not, by their own subjective capacity, have collected. This is fundamentally different from the present way of doing things: at present, the collectors and managers of public information can view other people's information at will.
2. Viewing of public information is event-driven. Only when an event has more than one participant, when the event requires a fair judgment and decision to produce a resolution, and when this cannot be done with one person's information alone, does a fair algorithm compute the set of individuals involved, and only after obtaining the consent and authorization of those individuals is the right to view obtained. And the whole process, from viewing the information to reaching the judgment and ruling and the resolution, is preserved as common knowledge in Merkle-DAG form.

These two rules put into practice the boundary described in Chapter 7: a person's historical field of view becomes a concrete permission; a multi-party event obtains evidence without generating unlimited access rights.

### Why people will trust such a product

Because the entity behind it is not a traditional company but an entirely new organization, which can guarantee two things. First, all information and all behavior are preserved as traceable, tamper-proof common knowledge in Merkle-DAG, so any statement or action that infringes on a user's interests is reliably recorded. Second, everyone's personal income is distributed according to need after computation by an intelligent optimization algorithm, so no one can obtain any extra return from encroaching on users' interests. The former guarantees that no one has the ability to infringe on users' interests; the latter guarantees that no one has the desire to.

How does a new organization prove it deserves trust? Not by promises, but by a record that begins growing on the first day of its founding and that no single node can control.

### How records change both before and after

Someone says that "behavior being reliably recorded" can strengthen detection and accountability, but is not the same as behavior being unable to happen in the first place. In a complex system, the events within it in many cases do not all conform to strict causality; however rigorously a transportation system is designed, accidents still happen.

First, what we must guard against is deliberate, malicious harm. "Behavior being reliably recorded" serves not only for detection and accountability after the fact; it also has a powerful preventive effect beforehand. Wherever public surveillance equipment covers, people generally restrain their malicious behavior. Because the common knowledge of tit-for-tat lets every person know the fact that their malicious behavior will be recorded, and that malicious behavior will bring the consequence of punishment. As long as we ensure that the loss inflicted by punishment after the fact exceeds the benefit gained, this rule system has a preventive effect beforehand on any rational person. People today are not all complete rational agents: for one thing, their own learning and education are imperfect, and their rational knowledge and decision-making ability have gaps; for another, they lack effective emotional control; and on top of this, there is no system that can reliably and fairly collect all behavior and render fair rulings, so true, complete prevention beforehand has been impossible. But in the future, once AI is universal, everyone will have their own AI agent to perfect their rational knowledge and decision-making ability; and because the great majority of intellectual activity and physical behavior will be carried out or mediated by AI agents, with this layer of buffering, irrational emotional behavior can also be greatly reduced. So this system will not only have the effect of recording and tracing after the fact, but also the effect of stopping all deliberate malicious behavior beforehand.

For harm that is not deliberately malicious, caused by one's own limitations or by external force majeure, this mechanism has an equally powerful effect. Human civilization, like an individual human life, is an intelligent agent that collects, processes and feeds back information and iteratively evolves its own knowledge model and decision system. For a person it is "a fall into the pit, a gain in your wit"; for a civilization, social rules and schemes are themselves not fixed, but evolve continually with the progress of technology and knowledge. So the occurrence of harm can never be completely eliminated, but "behavior being reliably recorded and processed" is the precondition for the whole system to reduce repeated mistakes, to gain in wit from every fall as far as possible, and to achieve ever more efficient feedback and correction. This is the most important precondition for stable, fast, forward iterative evolution.

In sum, "behavior being reliably recorded" is the most important prerequisite for the system to reduce the risk of harm from negative behavior. The faster and more comprehensively this goal is achieved, the smaller the risk of harm to the system and its members. What people fear is not that their personal information and behavior are recorded; what they fear is that the ability to view and exploit that information falls into the hands of a few and becomes a weapon against them.

However fierce the old system's collection, it can only get what people are willing to expose: finished products, successes, the outward story. What it cannot get is failures, abandoned plans, reasons for changing one's mind, because under competition and accountability, hiding these is rational. And that is precisely the data most lacking for building theory and correcting errors. So the data advantage of the new system lies not in "more", but in a class of data the old system is structurally unable to produce: decisions, outcomes, reversals, and the "why" alongside them.

### How instinctive impulses are safely released

"Distribution according to need" can reduce the economic motive for encroaching on others' interests, but it cannot directly guarantee the absence of malice. Humans, as higher animals with biological characteristics and instinctive impulses, have physiological urges, instinctive curiosity and desire for control, irrational emotional impulses born of love, hate and fear, even some abnormal psychological desires, and a great deal of unhealthy psychology formed under traditional social mechanisms, masochistic and sadistic psychology. Under the new mechanism, how are these behaviors, which could cause substantive harm in reality, safely controlled and released? We can draw on the theory of the pressure valve: release these emotions in virtual digital space through virtual works of literature, film and games, by more scientific methods. On one side, limit the duration and degree, so that individuals do not become addicted and sink to the point of being unable to tell reality from the virtual; on the other, release the pressure effectively. For this to truly work, I am afraid we will have to wait for the input and output technology of brain-computer interfaces to become more advanced.

### Unalterable history, with retraction and deletion

Records are append-only, rules can change, and the archive will outlive any version of the rules. So on top of the unalterable history there must be retraction and deletion: a record can be marked as withdrawn, an authorization can be revoked, a piece of data can be removed from public view, and these operations are themselves recorded. What unalterability guarantees is that "what happened" cannot be forged, not that "anyone can see everything forever". Once these two things are separated, a person can live, as a continually changing human being, inside a traceable memory: your record of today will not be tampered into something else, and your choice of tomorrow will not be locked down by the record of yesterday.

### Taking data back from the giants

I once applied to a large-model company to export all of my conversation data, and the reply was that the export might take several days. These are not petabytes of files, and this is not the computing power and storage of twenty years ago; exporting an individual user's data does not need anywhere near that long. If there is computing power to support massive volumes of real-time AI conversation, how can it fail to support a simple package-and-download? This difference in treatment of computing and storage resources between the paid inference service and the user's data export shows that the company cares more about the business that makes it money than about the user's rights. This is the unfairness that arises when the basic architecture contains an unequal game of power between the service provider and the consumer.

The first thing the new mechanism does is to establish, within the software and hardware service framework, local and cloud computing and storage space that each user can completely control, and take back the data that was scattered among the information silos erected by the commercial giants of various software and services. I call this REA: not reverse-engineering all software, but reverse-analyzing the interface protocols of all of today's data silos and application silos, and building a set of fully compatible replacement applications and data sources that run under the new mechanism of the Merkle architecture. It carries out three successive tasks: recovering the historical accumulation, obtaining personal records from different platforms, clients and formats while preserving original content and provenance; restoring usability, rebuilding conversational relationships, attachment associations, timelines and semantic connections so that the recovered data can continue to be searched, computed on and collaborated with; and establishing a continuous, autonomous mode of production, in which newly produced records promptly enter the user-controlled space, and when switching models or service providers one can carry the existing state and keep working. If every departure from a platform requires applying for export again, reverse-parsing again, and repairing lost context again, then the user is forever bearing the migration cost created by platform isolation; the new architecture should make preservation and migration a routine operating mechanism.

There is only one criterion for testing this direction: after cutting the connection to any one original service provider, can the user keep their records and knowledge structure, choose a different set of tools, and continue the original work?

My own knowledge base is the first landing point: more than a year of sessions and projects scattered across different computers and different AI platforms, organized into a personal knowledge base in Merkle-DAG, where every original utterance has a content identifier, a source and a time, AI replies and my own statements are marked separately, and every version can be checked. This book grew out of that base.

# Part IV: Civilization

## Chapter 15: The Attention of Civilization and an Evolvable Runtime

### Resources are limited, so attention is necessary

The attention mechanism of the Transformer architecture resembles the human brain in three ways. First, resources are limited, efficiency drives the design, and weights are used to focus selectively on the factors that matter more. Our eyes and brain take in a flood of visual and auditory signals every second. Reading a book in a noisy cafe, the brain cannot process every detail, so it automatically filters out the background noise and locks its energy onto the text. An AI model processing several thousand words of text has equally limited computing resources. It does not treat every word alike; it computes weights and concentrates them on the few key terms most relevant to the current task. Second, out of a vast, complex and changing body of information, it builds accurate contextual associations through dynamic weights. On reading "that apple is really sweet," a human and an AI both connect it with fruit and taste, not with a phone. Third, it links an enormous number of information objects, with their contents and functions, through query, key and value.

I have been wondering whether the architecture of human civilization I envision, built on decision-making by dynamic weights, could be set up in the same way. On one side, let the root intelligent agent belonging to each human individual map out an individual space covering that individual and their private resources. Through the cooperation of their own brain and their own agents, each person constructs a private attention mechanism, and in their own private space carries out self-management and self-evolution in the three ways above. On the other side, these individual spaces and individual intelligences together make up the whole civilizational space, and at that level a similar mechanism constructs the attention mechanism of civilization, which completes the self-management and evolution of civilization, again in those three ways. What I mean is this: I want to take that architecture of human civilization based on dynamic-weight decisions out of vague imagination, borrow the concrete architecture of attention at the level of human physiology and at the level of AI models, and turn it into a plan that can actually operate through real social organization and real science and computer technology.

### Objects change, so we must enter the runtime

But obviously we cannot copy the algorithms of AI models the way the man in the old story carved a notch in his boat to mark where his sword fell overboard. The basic objects the Transformer handles are semantic nodes: static objects with the dual nature of data and code, and the algorithms rest essentially on probability theory and statistics. The basic objects the new architecture of the human civilization system must handle also possess the persistent state of a runtime. The former is like a collection of programs containing data and code; the latter is a far more complex system that contains the entire runtime. When a model runs inference, the trained parameters stay fixed and what changes with the input is the attention weights; but whichever weights we mean, the objects they attach to, the semantic nodes in the vocabulary, do not increase, decrease or change. The real world is not like that. All the people and external objects attached to human society change as the decision weights and behavior of humans change; these objects can diminish, multiply, change their properties, even be destroyed. Non-renewable resources shrink by exactly what is consumed; renewable resources do not. One person's reward or punishment changes another person's quantity of resources, and even that person's future decision tendencies.

So in a Transformer-like architecture at the individual and civilizational levels, what query, key and value correspond to is clearly not as simple as in an AI model. What I can give at present is only a preliminary correspondence. Like the fixed point of Chapter 17, it is still at the stage of needing to be worked out; I write it down here so that the idea does not stay an empty analogy.

| | In the personal space | In common affairs |
|---|---|---|
| Query (Q): the current problem and demand | What I want to accomplish, what needs solving, what constraints of time and resources apply | What problem the participants face together, what needs and limits each of them raises |
| Key (K): descriptions relevant to the problem | The source, time, version, capability and availability of files, knowledge, devices and tools | How collaborators, evidence and resources relate to the current task, with their scope of validity and conditions of access |
| Value (V): the content or help that can be provided | Material, explanations, methods and tool capabilities one is permitted to read | Evidence, expert opinion, resource commitments and candidate actions permitted to be shared |

Value here means content that can be obtained and used, not a social worth assigned to a person. The same person who asks the question in this computation can be the provider of evidence or the executor of a plan in the next: Q, K and V are roles within one concrete computation, not a permanent sorting of people into those who command, those who are rated and those who are called up as resources. On this correspondence, the minimal process of a common affair has five steps: the query discovers the relevant objects; the relevant capability nodes each propose candidate plans, carrying their weights; a plan executes once it has the authorization of the parties it touches; the result of execution is recorded; and the result feeds back into the weight of every node that took part. Discovery, proposal, authorization, execution, feedback: this is the concrete shape of Chapter 9's dynamic-weight mechanism inside an attention architecture. What it still lacks is a precise way of writing how state updates with action, and how authorization, the right to take part and attention weight are computed separately; that is left for later work.

Whatever the final correspondence is, besides the algorithms of probability theory and statistics, we need the principles of game theory: tit-for-tat, Nash equilibrium, Pareto optimality, the prisoner's dilemma. A mathematical model like Markov games can handle both zero-sum games and cooperative games; the question is whether it can be applied to a system that, like the real universe and human society, evolves dynamically and gives rise to new things and new technologies. My answer: this mathematical model is not necessarily the best choice for the algorithm of the future new system. What matters is not which specific model, but the new-system algorithm constructed through repeated iteration, research and experiment. And whichever model it is, computing the variables in its formulas requires sufficiently abundant and accurate data on the objects in the system.

This brings us back to an important precondition for realizing the new system: the active cooperation and full participation of all participants. The data on these objects in the human civilization system cannot be collected directly the way the data of natural systems can. But as long as we do what Chapter 7 describes, use those meta-rules as the basic rules of cooperation, dissolve the adversarial mindset, strengthen the will to cooperate, and then push forward the research of cooperative algorithms and mechanisms through a sound iterative process, we can build the new mechanism step by step and replace the old one.

### How sequential dependency and parallel exploration combine

Chapter 9 already said that the operation of society is a mixture of two kinds of logic: there are tasks that require causal relations and a fixed order, and there are tasks that can run in parallel. Take the collection, processing, decision and action of information: the overall sequence must be serial, but within each stage there are vast numbers of operations that can run in parallel. This matches the history of computing: the early era of computer applications was dominated by the CPU, the early AI era by the GPU, and in the latest trend the CPU has become important again. What the attention mechanism of civilization must do is decide, according to the nature of the task, which parts follow the causal construction order of whole-system cooperation and which parts run independently in parallel and are then integrated by the market or by the rules of the game, and hand that decision itself over to dynamic weights.

How do multiple perspectives form an executable plan? Multi-head attention lets the same problem receive attention from different angles, then merges the results of the heads. The social counterpart is this: the same problem is judged by capability nodes in multiple fields, each giving its own judgment and plan, each carrying its own weight; these are merged into multiple candidate plans with their weights, and then go into pilot trials. Which angles we assign to the social system is our design choice; the Transformer's attention heads do not naturally correspond to these social functions. But from humans to large models, this mechanism is an effective way to face complex objects and raise the probability that a decision succeeds.

### From the outcome of the game to the rules of cooperation

Under the old system, the outcome of the game determines the rules: whoever wins makes the rules. Under the new system, the rules come before the game: the five rules of Chapter 7 are common knowledge, the positive initial strategy of tit-for-tat is common knowledge, how weights arise and expire is common knowledge. What the outcome of the game changes is the weights and the plans, not the power to make the rules. This is the difference between the runtime of civilization and an ordinary game: a game has an ending; a runtime has none. It has only persistent state and a constant arrival of new things.

Persistent state and the arrival of new things are the hardest part of this runtime. Before a technology appears, no node has any record of judgment about it; after a kind of resource is exhausted, all the weights built around it lose their object. So weights must attach to problems, not to people, and they must expire: when a problem disappears, its weights go to zero; when a new problem appears, all nodes start over from an equal initial weight. This is the fundamental difference between dynamic weights and every kind of static authority.

### Start testing from the smallest cooperative scenario

This runtime does not need to be tested starting from a nation. The smallest cooperative scenario is two people, one task, and one verifiable record. Start there and observe: can the relevant people find each other, can misunderstandings be detected in time, can expert judgment be tested, can the results of cooperation be developed further by others. Then three people, one project, one organization. Chapters 19 and 20 describe exactly this road.

### Why humanity's knowledge of nature far exceeds its knowledge of itself

Now let us turn from the mechanism to civilization itself.

Look at the world today. On one side, we already have the ability to manipulate single atoms into the shapes we want, to pack billions of transistors onto a chip the size of a fingernail, to edit human genes to treat disease, to build spacecraft and head for space. On the other side, our societies are full of ideological conflict, political turmoil and brutal violent war, from road rage, domestic violence, neighborhood disputes and labor conflicts at the small end, to the riot on Capitol Hill and the movements in the streets at the large end, not to mention the war in Ukraine, the endless wars in the Middle East, and humanity's fear of nuclear weapons and of AI.

Chapter 7 gave the reason. For every other complex system, the data to be collected is not ourselves; for the system of human civilization, the data to be collected is the information of every human intelligent agent about itself, and whether people are willing to provide it is the bottleneck. This is why humanity's ability to understand, predict and technically transform nature runs so far ahead of its ability to understand, predict and control its own social system.

If human civilization's ability to manipulate the external world does not match its ability to control itself, sooner or later it will do something self-harming or even self-destroying. It is like a child of immense strength but a fragile body, wildly swinging a thousand-pound chain flail: sooner or later it hits itself. In little more than a century humanity has already been through two world wars, the Cold War between America and Russia, the nuclear crisis, and now stands in the middle of the confrontation between China and America and the AI race, each side afraid that whoever masters AI first will destroy the other, each straining to get ahead, exactly like the nuclear arms race of old.

Technology can help and can harm, and there are two root causes: one is the reliability of the technology itself, the other is the reliability of the human civilizational entity that holds and uses it. The fact that humanity's knowledge of nature far exceeds its knowledge of itself makes the second factor the greatest risk and the weakest link. If we follow the current trend, pouring energy and resources into the capability and reliability of technology instead of reforming the system of human society and raising the capability and reliability of civilization itself, the gap between the two will keep widening. The final result is climbing a tree to catch fish, driving south to reach the north: going further and further down the road of technology out of control and self-destruction, until there is no way back.

In fact we already have, or are about to have, every objective capability needed to collect the information of the human social system: storage and compute, AI that changes by the day, brain-computer interfaces in their infancy, all evolving at tremendous speed. So the question of how to solve humanity's subjective willingness to provide information about itself has become the bottleneck of the whole process of development. The new mechanism is exactly this: eliminate the power gap, strengthen cooperation, reduce confrontation, limit the abuse of power and the occurrence of harm at the level of mechanism, guarantee that the information people provide will not be used to hurt them, let people get a positive return from sharing, and ensure that this positive feedback runs reliably and continuously, thereby solving the bottleneck at its root. Then we can do a far better job of studying the complex system of human civilization itself, build more accurate theories and more effective social systems, and make the interior of the human civilizational entity like the interior of a higher organism, where all the cells cooperate through complex and reliable mechanisms, giving the organism a stronger ability to survive and a stronger ability to perceive and interact with the outside world. All human individuals, with the help of computers and AI, obtain sufficient information, reason and decide by reliable causal relations, and together build a powerful distributed dynamic-weight intelligent neural network system that serves as the brain of the human civilizational entity and steers this entity's evolution and development in the universe.

### A single central nervous center and internal diversity

Some say that "planetary intelligence" is only a metaphor, and better read as multi-scale cooperation than as a single central nervous center. I do not treat it as a metaphor. I treat it as a wholly new intelligent dissipative structure that emerges in the course of the universe's evolution, more complex, higher-level and larger in scale than any single intelligent organism. And a single central nervous center is the inevitable result of evolution. A stable dissipative structure, above all an intelligent agent, must, along the direction of time, keep its interactions with the external environment stable and consistent in rational logic. Especially when its internal structure has a sufficiently stable perception of the external environment, can obtain enough information, and is capable of making the best choice, it must have a stable, reliable and consistent capacity for outward decision and action. Only a single central nervous center can accomplish this kind of stable, complex perception and response. Animals with distributed nervous systems, like jellyfish, can only manage simple interactions with the outside; the more complex the intelligent organism, the more complex its central nervous system and brain. Of course, simple centralization alone is not enough. It must be matched with a central structure complex enough, with enough neurons, with input and output systems complex enough, so that energy and information travel through this system quickly and accurately, and computation and reasoning come out right.

What happens without logical consistency in stable long-term behavior? Look at how China's authoritarian system and the Western democratic systems have developed over these decades of the information age. Once the ability to acquire, transmit, compute and analyze the basic information needed for decisions had risen enough to yield relatively accurate national economic policy, an economic system with a single central nervous center, long-term planning and strong regulation of the market pulled far ahead, in science, in industry and in the information industry, of countries run by rotating parties chosen by democratic vote, with fractured public opinion, parties undercutting one another, every incoming party chasing short-term results, policies reversed overnight, and national decisions hijacked by economic oligarchs each fighting for themselves. From the energy industry to electric vehicles to robots, in the unified and powerful capacity for policy execution and market building that it takes to land a technology fast and form a complete industrial chain, China leads by a wide margin. Now imagine that Earth's civilization has evolved into an interstellar civilization, yet lacks the unified, coordinated, logical and planned capacity for information collection and decision-making, both outward and inward, and keeps thrashing back and forth like a schizophrenic patient or Mr. Hyde, circling and zigzagging on the road of evolution. Such a planetary intelligence would clearly be a sick, inefficient, low-grade intelligence.

I am certainly not saying that China's static, single-authority decision system is the optimal solution. The optimal solution I envision is an intelligent decision system of dynamic feedback and computed weights, whose intelligent nodes are like the neurons in a brain; the system is essentially the weight-computing system of a large AI model, organized by the same principle at a more complex level. Large models are intelligent because their architecture is built on a central system of probability maximization. "Central" does not mean a structure in which one node decides everything and a single point of failure paralyzes the whole system. It means a system in which many nodes integrate dynamically, obtain accurate external information, and produce unified, stable decisions and behavior. And unified, stable behavior toward the outside does not mean uniformity inside. The stable unity of a human organism's outward behavior does nothing to diminish the diversity and complexity within it.

## Chapter 16: The Gap Between Technology and Institutions

### A startling fact

Since the Second World War, the rules of the political, economic and financial game between the nations of the world, and the political and economic systems within each nation, have seen almost no fundamental change. Technology over the same period has developed at breakneck speed. Information technology alone has gone through the waves of the computer, the integrated circuit, mobile communication, the internet and AI. Especially in the information age from the late seventies to today, the world's total wealth has grown rapidly, but the gap between rich and poor has grown wider and wider: the share of total wealth held by the richest one-thousandth of the population in the United States tripled over thirty-odd years, and the share held by the richest one percent in China doubled in twenty. Every country shows fractured public opinion and rising nationalism, people everywhere are dissatisfied with their governments and their economic conditions, and conflict between nations continues without pause.

At the individual level, thanks to the technology dividend, people have been strengthened on the objective plane of survival and well-being. But the many negative effects of a governance system that has fallen out of step with technological development, compounded by the information age's amplifying effect, in which bad news travels a thousand miles, have pushed ordinary people, and young people above all, into a collective slide in subjective well-being. If the enormous speed gap between technological development and institutional change is allowed to keep widening, destabilizing factors will accumulate step by step, quantity will turn into quality, and sooner or later a serious conflict or crisis will break out.

### The root of the old institutions' disease

This gap is not accidental. Chapters 6 and 7 already explained its mechanism: the inequality of power in the pyramid structure produces unfair distribution, unfair distribution produces distrust, distrust produces a still larger power gap, and so on around the loop. Technological progress merely speeds up the creation of wealth, and in this loop the creation of wealth only speeds up the divergence. So the technology dividend has not automatically turned into shared happiness. That is not a problem of technology; it is a problem of institutions.

The old institutions are hard to change, and that too has its mechanism. Historically, replacing an institution has usually meant violent revolution. A person of the old system possesses a life experience that was tested through long iteration under the old system, lived through and adapted to; they have never really experienced the new mechanism, do not understand it, and still less know what long-term work and life under it would really feel like. People instinctively resist, doubt and reject what is unknown and unexperienced; that is the nature of seeking gain and avoiding harm. In the past, transitions from old systems to new, apart from violent revolution, were also accomplished by a spiritual pre-experience delivered through religion and propaganda: heaven and the afterlife in religion, the description of communist society in the Communist Manifesto, America's rendering of the American Dream. Chapter 21 will explain how the new system provides this pre-experience, and provides it as something people can take part in and verify.

### We do not have much time left

Because of the rapid evolution of AI technology, AI has already begun to acquire a preliminary capacity to iterate and evolve on its own. As that capacity matures, technology will be driven forward by AI at tremendous speed, and the arrival of the technological singularity that people have long discussed lies in the not-distant future.

Because China and the United States are now caught in something like a prisoner's dilemma, confronting and distrusting each other, neither willing to stop accelerating AI research, the current frantic AI race makes the acceleration of AI irreversible, and the day of the technological singularity draws closer and closer. Once the technological singularity arrives while the mismatch between civilization's institutions and technological development remains unsolved, the gap between the two will be stretched without limit, and the probability that human civilization, out of fear of AI or out of fear of the few who control AI, walks into self-destruction will be stretched without limit along with it.

So we do not have much time left. Within this brief window, we must design, as fast as we can, an entirely new system of human civilization that has the same self-iterating, self-upgrading mechanism as AI systems, so that the speed of civilization's evolution matches the speed of technology's evolution, so that the civilizational singularity and the technological singularity arrive together, and so that we build a community of humans and AI that transcends the old civilization and eliminates once and for all the risk of humans and AI systems collapsing out of control and heading for destruction.

### Let civilization's self-correction capacity keep pace with technology's acceleration

Human history has seen countless disagreements over old systems, old understandings and old technologies that looked impossible to resolve, and that were then unified or replaced by entirely new institutions, knowledge and technologies as understanding and science advanced. The latest example happened in AI itself: from the early dispute between symbolism and connectionism, to the later dispute between CNNs and RNNs, until the appearance of the Transformer architecture unified mainstream understanding in the field. These disagreements are not bad in themselves; they are the very logic at the bottom of the universe: evolution through diversity and mutual competition, upgrading step by step. Still, in this process of diversified exploration, the less energy wasted the better.

Unfortunately, not every field can complete its iterations as peacefully as pure technology does. Political disagreements in human civilization cost vast numbers of human lives for nothing; disagreements over product and technology routes in the economy waste vast amounts of capital and resources for nothing. The reason lies in the meta-rules of human civilization's evolution: the traditional tit-for-tat strategy model, with its persistently high risk of confrontation and betrayal, is still far too primitive. As a result, although the development of society's institutions, knowledge and technology does not run on a preset track, problems of trust, adversarial tension, path dependence and many other causes produce problems like the prisoner's dilemma and the trolley problem, which burn enormous amounts of cost. Technology and business are the lucky cases: the former loses only intellectual and computing resources, the latter capital and resources. In political disagreements, once the state's machinery of military violence joins in, what is lost is precious human life.

Let us use better meta-strategies, so that on the road of human civilization's evolutionary exploration we stop running into the problems above as far as possible, and so that every time such a problem does arise we can find, flexibly, quickly and cheaply, a new path that dissolves the disagreement and lets us evolve together. Above all, let the transformation of human social institutions never again cost human lives, as changes of institution have throughout history.

### Humanity's road is not the two tracks of the trolley problem

In the classic science fiction work *Watchmen*, in order to pull the United States and the Soviet Union back from the brink of nuclear war and make hostile regimes cooperate, the smartest man in the world creates a more powerful common enemy, an external threat that forces all sides into a false and fragile peace. The tension between them does not disappear; it is only pressed together by a larger outside force. His method replaces a greater loss with a smaller one, a solution of the same kind as the trolley problem. In my view this is obviously not the wisest course, nor the means the smartest man should adopt. Because the trajectory of human society's evolution is not a track laid down in advance, and the choice is not between the two tracks of crushing one person or crushing a crowd.

The trolley problem is a dilemma because it presupposes tracks. A civilization that can correct itself, explore in parallel, and move its conflicts out of reality into virtual space to make trial and error there faces not two tracks but an open field that can be laid out anew. Chapter 18 will finish describing this choice.

## Chapter 17: Alignment: Aligned with Whom

### The definition of alignment

Alignment, for artificial intelligence and large models, means the process of ensuring that the outputs and behavior of an AI system stay consistent with human values, intentions and social norms: turning the model from a base that can only continue a piece of text into an assistant that is helpful, honest and harmless.

Start from this definition and combine it with an objective fact: within human civilization, the values, intentions, political rules and social norms of different peoples, different regimes and different classes differ from one another and cannot be made consistent. The conclusion follows naturally. In the game of human society, every player with the ability to create and control AI, whether a regime, a class, a company or an individual, develops and holds an assistant that is "helpful, honest and harmless" to itself, and in that same game this assistant may become, in the eyes of the other players, the accomplice of a "fearsome and harmful" adversary. Because AI's powerful capacity for self-iteration and self-upgrading amplifies and accelerates the capability of the player who controls it, it not only increases the chance that one's adversary acquires a far greater ability to harm or even destroy oneself, but, because different players advance AI at different rates, it also widens the gap in capability between players: a difference of a few months becomes a difference of hundreds or thousands of times in capability.

### Balanced or unbalanced, the result is terrifying

If development is unbalanced and one player dominates, the gap in strength between the parties is so great that for the dominant player the highest-return, lowest-cost way to play is to use AI to empower violence and crush all the others, ending in winner-take-all, with the rest enslaved or destroyed.

If development is balanced and several parties stand off against one another, then because of mutual distrust and insufficient exchange of information, any of them might, without any strong deliberate malice, trigger the destruction of civilization through a passing idea or a tiny misunderstanding. It is like the Cold War, when the whole world worried that the political powers holding nuclear weapons might destroy the entire world over a small misunderstanding.

Even at the global macro level, humanity as a whole treats AI as another opponent in this game, afraid that once AI gains self-awareness it will turn from humanity's assistant into a tyrant that enslaves or destroys humanity.

The example of the child swinging the chain flail now has a concrete setting. The AI race between China and the United States is like an intelligent creature with two brains, a left and a right, each hand swinging its own chain flail, but with no effective alignment mechanism between the two brains, so that the swinging of the flails cannot be properly coordinated, and sooner or later the two hammers may collide and tangle, the weapons go out of control, and the creature wounds itself.

### The alignment that really needs solving

In truth, what most needs to be solved right now is the alignment between the values, intentions and social norms of every organization and every individual in all of humanity, and their outputs and behavior. Once that is solved, the alignment between humanity's carbon-based biological intelligence and the computer's silicon-based AI intelligence, which is the aggregated product of human knowledge, follows as a matter of course. Going further, what we must do is the ultimate alignment between the new civilizational entity of humans plus AI and the whole ecology of the Earth and the universe. That is the final goal of alignment.

Humanity has already faced the risk of nuclear weapons destroying the Earth's ecology and of modern civilization driving global warming. If this global alignment at the most macro level is not done well, human civilization empowered by AI will keep facing risks of this kind.

Alignment therefore has three layers: the alignment of humans with humans, the alignment of humans with AI, and the alignment of civilization with the ecology. All the attention today is on the middle layer, and the middle layer looks unsolvable because the first layer has not been done. A civilization that is internally split in its values and whose parts threaten one another will train "aligned" AIs each of which is a piece of that split.

### Alignment as a fixed point: a direction still to be worked out

Chapter 5 said that my intuition is that alignment can also be studied from the angle of fixed points: place it on the mechanism by which the parties' strategies and institutions are updated in the evolutionary game, and look at where that updating process converges. This section is a blank left open, not a conclusion. My original discussion was very vague, and I write down here only the few things that can be settled now, leaving the rest for later.

First, a fixed point does not tell good from bad. Cooperation against cooperation pays three each; defection against cooperation pays five and zero; mutual defection pays one each. Under mutual defection, either party switching alone to cooperation falls from one to zero, so it is a stable state that neither wants to change unilaterally, yet mutual cooperation is plainly better. If "any unilateral change would only make that party worse off" is called alignment, then the prisoner's dilemma this book wants to escape becomes alignment too. So what alignment has to characterize is the cooperative kind among the fixed points of the updating mechanism, not the mutually harmful kind; how to separate the two mathematically is something I have not done.

Second, whatever the final characterization is, alignment will not be a fixed endpoint but a state within the runtime. When new things appear, new players enter, or resources change, the old fixed point breaks and the system has to find a new one. So what alignment requires is not a one-time injection of values but a continuous capacity for correction, which is exactly what a social neural network of dynamic weights does.

Third, alignment is not one party imposing its values on the others. What that produces is not a fixed point but a tension held down by external force, ready to spring back at any moment. The false peace in *Watchmen* is a fake fixed point of exactly this kind. A true fixed point is the position the parties walk to on their own, under common knowledge, verifiable records and symmetric rules of the game.

Apply this direction to humans and AI: an intelligence trained on the knowledge of all humanity takes its training material from all humanity, but its payoff function is not decided by the training material; it is decided by whoever trains it. So alignment between humans and AI comes down to who sets the payoff function and by what standard. Only when the payoff function is set as the maximization of the interests of all human civilization can its misalignment with humanity happen solely when humanity's own payoff functions contradict one another. Apply it to civilization and the ecology: a civilization that regards itself as part of the Earth's dissipative structure and pursues maximal connection with external objects will not choose to destroy the environment it depends on, unless the game inside it forces it to. So the order of the three layers of alignment runs from the inside out, and the root is in the first layer.

### The reliability of two kinds of civilization

Chapter 15 said that technology is a double-edged sword for two root reasons: the reliability of the technology itself, and the reliability of the civilizational entity that uses it. The alignment problem is another name for the problem of civilization's reliability. The more aligned a civilization is internally, the safer the technology in its hands; the more divided a civilization is internally, the more every technology in its hands is a potential weapon of self-injury. So with nuclear weapons, so with AI.

This is why this book does not treat AI safety as an independent technical problem, but as one indicator of a civilization's capacity for self-governance. To make AI trustworthy, first make civilization trustworthy; to make civilization trustworthy, first make it possible for every node inside it, under common knowledge and symmetric play, to verify one another and correct one another instead of threatening one another.

### Questions and Answers

**Q: Disagreements over values are not disagreements over facts. Science can dissolve disagreements over facts; it cannot dissolve disagreements over values. How could the first layer of the three-layer alignment possibly be achieved by a single mechanism?**

A: The principle of relativity in Chapter 5 already answers half of this: there is no absolute coordinate system of values, only transformations between coordinate systems. What the first layer of alignment demands is not that everyone hold the same set of values, but that everyone accept the same set of transformation rules: verify facts under common knowledge, negotiate values under symmetric play, and bear the consequences under the rule that failure means compensation and correction. This set of rules contains no specific value content of its own, so it applies equally to people holding every kind of value. Disagreements over values do not vanish under these rules; they become the diversity described in Chapter 2, part of the space of evolution rather than a reason to annihilate one another. The other half of the answer is in Chapter 5: that piece of meta-cognition, which reduces the opposition of values from "good against evil" to "differences with conditions of formation," lets the disagreement cool down psychologically first, and then enter the rules.

## Chapter 18: The Critical Fork in the Road

### A higher-order regulatory mechanism

The direction of this world's evolution is to use better and better modes of control to take over or replace the old, lower-level ones. This does not mean the lower-level modes become obsolete; in fact they operate at different levels of things, each regulating the business its own level has to handle.

Take the human nervous system. One mode is rational control: the neurons of the brain think, and then direct the body to act. Another is muscle memory acquired through long training: the thinking is compressed and the brain is skipped, the peripheral nerves and muscles cooperating directly to carry out the action. And there is one lower still: the parasympathetic nervous system controls circulation, breathing and digestion without passing through the brain at all; the body's automatic operation completes many tasks with no rational inference needed. But decisions driven purely by emotion or physiological instinct are often bad for the body. Instinct pushes a person to take in as much food as possible, and in today's world of enormous material abundance that means eating oneself fatter and fatter. Without the brain's involvement, a person facing more complex tasks or interactions with the outside world runs into many dangers. An advanced human can, according to the situation, combine the brain's high-level neural regulation with the body's instinctive drives to solve problems well.

The same holds at the level of nations and civilizations. The systems controlled by power and capital, the economic system and the political system that manages people, when they run according to the nature of power and capital, are like the physiological instincts driving a person. They can handle most of the basic-level tasks of human society, just as the parasympathetic nervous system controls circulation without the brain. For a civilization, high-level decisions are usually not needed: market regulation and the natural power games between people complete the vast majority of the political and economic activity of daily social life. But how to switch between different kinds of business, whether to leave something to the natural competition of the market, the so-called invisible hand, or to accomplish certain tasks through national industrial policy, has to be handled case by case, and that needs a high-level decision system.

The reason I am talking about is collective reason: everyone thinking about the problem together and offering solutions, then selecting the consensus plan through a dynamic decision system, rather than relying on the instinct of the market economy. The market economy produces economic crises and financial crises. Once an industry shows a high rate of return or a huge gap between supply and demand, it becomes the hottest thing going and everyone piles in. The internet produced a bubble in its early days, and AI is doing the same now: the big companies, with the money in their hands and the supply-demand squeeze, have driven up the price of memory and chips, until ordinary people who want to buy a game console or add a computer find they can no longer afford one. This kind of imbalance is unhealthy. If AI is to build a new, globally unified form of civilization in the future, it will not be by these big companies monopolizing every resource and stripping ordinary people of the right to use computing resources. It is as if a person thinking at full capacity, without a sound central nervous system in control, let all the nutrients flow to the brain: the other organs would be starved, and the organism would very likely suffer irreversible damage.

### Cytokine storm

There are many situations like this. Struggles between nations produce violent conflicts, and in the end civilization bears the loss. The human body is the same. When a foreign virus enters the body, what is really happening is a process of modifying one's own genes: after the virus enters, the ecosystem has to shift and form a new dynamic balance. If the human body had a higher-order regulatory mechanism, it would very likely absorb these foreign viruses and gene fragments strategically, gradually and gently, each side changing the other, adapting itself gently and letting the virus mutate step by step inside the body, until the two reach a harmonious, unified ecology. In fact, in the history of change in the human genome, modifications made by foreign viruses or foreign DNA fragments are a very important external source for the whole of biological evolution.

But the great majority of organisms, humans above all, have a trait called immune rejection: as soon as something foreign arrives, they produce a form of confrontation that resembles violent conflict in human society, the cytokine storm, the inflammatory storm. There is no need at all to spend so many resources on the attack, but this may simply be the nature of human biology: when an outsider comes, it must be wiped out with everything we have. In reality many people end up killed by the cytokine storm itself. During COVID, many deaths were caused not by the damage the virus did but by the cytokine storm: kill eight hundred of the enemy, lose a thousand of your own, and kill yourself in the process. It is rather like a person whose emotions override rational judgment when something happens, and who suffers a loss as a result. Not all animals produce cytokine storms either. Bats have a strong capacity to coexist with foreign viruses; infected with all kinds of viruses, they do not immediately die in large numbers.

The mechanism I envision is meant to solve exactly this problem: to use AI to eliminate the losses caused by irrational, emotional, instinct-driven behavior. At the level of society, nation, politics and civilization, this means using collective decision-making by AI to counteract the cytokine-storm-like political crises, economic crises and violent conflicts that the low-level operating mechanisms of the market economy, finance, power games and capital games can produce at the macro scale, so that the whole evolutionary process of civilization is gentler, its losses smaller, the price of evolution lower, and its speed faster and smoother.

Treat a human individual, an animal individual, a nation and a civilization each as a dissipative structure. In the course of its evolution, this dissipative structure evolves higher-order regulatory mechanisms, which at a higher level adjust and correct the situations that the lower-level mechanisms cannot handle and that would produce crises. That is how the evolution of the whole dissipative structure is accomplished. Each time evolution produces new capabilities, those new capabilities form new mechanisms. This may be what Marx meant by the match between productive forces and relations of production: new, higher productive forces give birth to new social institutions; electricity and steam gave birth to the capitalist system. At the same time the change in the relations of production further releases and promotes the productive forces: under capitalism every person can release far greater capability than in feudal society, and people cooperating through the operation of capital generate far greater energy, unlike before, when land was the basic unit of the game and most people were bound to it. Now, with AI and computers, the whole mechanism of financial capital has sunk down one more level. On this foundation we can build a better regulatory mechanism, controlling capital no longer through capital, but through knowledge, through intelligence.

### A thought experiment: the choice of the richest person in the world

Under the existing system of civilization, every person and every group has a very strong desire to hold exclusively the power, property, resources and capital they possess, because they feel that the more they own, the more they benefit and the happier they are. Yet many rich and powerful people, sitting on uncountable wealth and enormous power, remain anxious and troubled in the struggle for advantage between persons and between groups, forever afraid of losing everything in competition, confrontation and conflict, guarding not only against enemies but suspecting and worrying about friends. These problems are not removed by holding the dominant position in the game of life. The incompleteness of information means that the probability of the extreme case, total defeat, can never be lowered to the point where you can fully rest easy. Even someone as strong as the richest person in the world or the President of the United States faces an unacceptable risk of failure under incomplete information and players who are not one hundred percent rational. And this incomplete competition breeds confrontation, monopoly and other destructive tensions inside civilization.

Now imagine this. In the new system I envision, resources are divided into two kinds: the resources for an individual's survival needs are allocated on demand and by optimization, depending only on that individual's physiological and psychological makeup; public resources are governed by the dynamic-weight neural network of everyone. No participant can obtain excessive individual resources, and none can obtain decision weight beyond their level of cognition and judgment. Then all the information, ideas and behavior of these individuals in their daily activity, apart from internal information unrelated to the outside world, have no need to be concealed from other individuals and groups, because any concealment lowers the operating efficiency of the whole system and the probability that its decisions are correct. And the greatest benefit of this mechanism is that by sacrificing near-term and temporary gains, one obtains far greater long-term and permanent gains.

For instance, a person who under the old mechanism could use greater ability and a smarter brain to acquire more personal survival resources and more control over public resources appears to suffer a loss under the new mechanism. But in reality, first, there is no loss in personal survival resources; on the contrary, under a more reasonable and healthier allocation, your material and spiritual life is healthier, and without the psychological pressure of guarding against others across a huge gap in wealth, you are happier. Most important of all, this new mechanism lets civilization and science evolve faster, bringing you new wealth over a far longer term.

Here is an example. You are the richest person in the world, sitting on ten trillion dollars, but you turned sixty this year. Under the old system, immortality technology will take forty years to achieve, and your life expectancy is ninety, so you will most likely die before immortality is invented. Under the new system, you still have personal resources sufficient to keep you healthy and happy, but you must give up full control over the ten trillion in public-resource wealth, handing that wealth over to the dynamic-weight system that aggregates the collective intelligence of all humanity, and letting your own personal knowledge and intellect become one decision-weight node in that system. In exchange you get fairness and openness for all humanity, and you no longer need to live in daily dread inside an isolated, incomplete and unfair game. Best of all, under this new decision system, this new system of wealth distribution and this new system of social evolution, immortality technology arrives in twenty years, and the richest person in the world attains immortality at eighty. Now, if you were that richest person, how would you choose? I think this thought experiment can be put to anyone in the world, to hear their choice. You can put it to Elon Musk too: would you rather be the richest person in the old system, or an immortal in the new one?

Once our new mechanism gives both the allocation quota of individual resources and the quota of decision authority over collective resources a more reasonable allocation mechanism, we reach an entirely new Nash equilibrium: within this allocation mechanism, anyone who tries to make a different choice will only, after repeated play, obtain a worse result. As long as this game is kept up, even the greatest beneficiary of the old system gains more under the new.

### The worst local optimum

Of course there is also a worst kind of local optimization: under the old system, a small elite becomes the most powerful and wealthy, and while monopolizing wealth and power they also monopolize the privilege of every superior capability and function, for instance the monopoly on becoming immortal. That is the truly terrifying world. But I doubt the probability of this happening, because once a small minority tries to monopolize resources and capabilities, the overwhelming majority will resist and will not submit meekly, and the result will very likely be a brutal violent war involving all of humanity, in which Earth's civilization may well be destroyed in one stroke.

Whether this worst local optimum or the two standoff endings described in Chapter 17, they all share the same structure: each is the endpoint of a negative chain reaction.

### Two directions

We now stand at exactly such a critical fork in the road. There are two directions to choose from: one leads through a negative chain reaction to civilization's self-destruction and end; the other leads through a positive chain reaction toward the singularity of civilization's evolution, toward a new form of human civilization.

The two chains of Chapter 7 are idealized extremes, and most of the time in reality we are somewhere between them. But on the scale of civilization the two extremes really exist: at one end nuclear war and AI out of control, at the other a planetary-scale intelligence. The time for swinging in the middle is not unlimited, and the technological singularity is compressing it. I hope this book gives people a clear and sober understanding of this, and helps them make the right choice.

In the world of the future, under the new system, every person records their own life and, with the support of AI, rapidly understands and masters the knowledge and information of this world. Every tiny spark of thought is recorded and spread, and then, through the vast dynamic-weight intelligent neural network and the AI systems spanning the globe, which hold the entire existing stock of human knowledge, the tiny spark of every human and AI node is quickly turned, with the cooperation of others, into an increment of knowledge and added to the global knowledge base, iterating in a cycle that grows knowledge fast. The spread of lithography technology and advanced-process semiconductor manufacturing could plainly drive human civilization forward at speed, yet nations, companies and individuals, locked in the confrontation of the old system, would rather use secrecy and sanctions to make prisoner's-dilemma local-optimum choices that look like benefiting oneself at others' expense but in the long run hurt both sides, pushing the globally optimal road of shared evolution out of reach. Under the new mechanism, people remove the barriers through full information sharing and cooperation, and progress together.

The choice between these two roads is not made for humanity by anyone. It is made by every person, a little at a time, each time they decide whether to share or to conceal, whether to cooperate or to guard. What Part V describes is how to begin with the first person.

# Part V: The Path

## Chapter 19: Starting with One Person

### The first thing: taking back personal data and services

What I have in mind is not merely the ability to pull my data back at any time. It is the complete replacement of commercial companies' products with software and services supplied by the entirely new organizational form of the new system. This goes a step further than merely using open-source software and open-source products: not only is the product open source, but the organizational entity that produces, designs and operates these products and services also runs under the new mechanism. Everything is open source: the organizational form, the method of distribution, the decision algorithms. Anyone can audit, anyone can trace, anyone can take part in decisions, anyone can help build.

This is the first concrete step by which the entire theoretical vision of a new organizational architecture for human society lands in practice. Build a free zone inside the old system that conforms to the new vision, then gain advantage through competition with the old system, and expand step by step, peacefully, to replace it. Beginning with the recovery of personal data and services is an excellent starting point. Starting with me, one person, and then, through advocacy, bringing in more people.

For software and services, begin with open-source projects as substitutes for traditional services, then provide compatible interfaces and take over the software and services those commercial companies provide. For hardware, rent at first; when enough people have joined and the funds are sufficient, buy outright to cut costs further; as it grows stronger, keep expanding: from renting, to buying from retailers, to wholesale, to designing and manufacturing hardware directly. In the early phase, focus only on cloud storage; when the numbers and finances are strong enough, expand into cloud computing, using ready-made open-source models at first, and once the finances are solid, buy hardware outright, write the software, and train large models ourselves. In short, starting from seizing personal data sovereignty, gradually, in an orderly and planned way, take over the full territory of every commercial productive organization of humanity.

### What the first service actually delivers

The first service is cloud storage. What it delivers is not a block of disk space but something more concrete: one person's ability to independently recover and keep using all of their own material. First, preserve the material from which one can start over: original files, original conversations, original records, each with a content identifier and a source. Then, on top of that, build an ever-better understanding: semantic relations, timelines, versions. Local and cloud together support personal control: the user holds their own identity and keys, chooses the execution environment, limits the permissions of agents, keeps recoverable copies, and can switch the party hosting the cloud side. The physical devices in the cloud may still be under the operator's control, which is why a locally usable path, copies in several places, and a migratable running state are all parts of turning control on paper into real capability.

When the service grows from one person to a few, what has to be ready first? How new participants obtain rights, express needs, contribute labor, influence decisions, and how cooperation continues when opinions diverge. Going from one person to two is the first real organizational test of this whole vision. Letting one person's improvement become a capability others can use is the hot-path sharing of Chapter 12: as soon as one person on a given platform has a capability, every user running that platform can share it. When do we expand into computing and more content services? When storage has proven itself, and when the participants' needs and resources have genuinely grown to that point. Every expansion should be backed by real conditions.

### Internal organization changes from day one

Throughout this whole process, fully embrace equality among all. Separate the allocation for individual needs from the allocation of collective resources. The allocation of collective resources, and every decision concerning the collective, is made by the dynamic-weight intelligent network of all participants. Every piece of information that ought to be public is common knowledge, recorded, tracked and made tamper-proof through the Merkle-DAG, which records each participant's contribution. That is to say, from the very beginning, its internal organizational form exists as this brand-new civilizational ecology, while outwardly it takes the form of a traditional commercial company and competes fairly in the market with other organizations of the old system, expanding step by step, attracting, absorbing and transforming the traditional companies of the old system, just as the winners in traditional commercial competition acquire and merge the losers.

We hold to this direction from the start. At the beginning there is only me, so the initial allocation and decisions are made by me alone; later, when there are several people, those people make fair allocations and decisions according to the new system. Funds and resources come in through a mechanism resembling crowdfunding: by describing this mechanism, this organization's ideas, functions, benefits and prospects, we gradually attract people to join. At least as of now, this vision has attracted one person, and that person is me. Through advocacy on the internet and in the real world, we work to attract more people to take part. Many hands make the fire burn high; the vision grows from a seed into a towering tree.

There are, of course, some differences from crowdfunding. First, it is not a matter of consumers paying money without contributing effort: consumers are also producers and creators. Second, there is no need to reach some crowdfunding headcount or funding target before starting. I start on my own, with the cheapest and simplest cloud storage, with my own small pocket money. Third, the ways of taking part are more flexible: if you have confidence and conviction, you can put in more money and effort, and be a participant as well as a consumer; if you lack confidence, you can be a consumer only and provide the most basic user feedback. But in every case, this new mechanism must guarantee the data sovereignty of consuming users. Data sovereignty is the baseline guarantee, not conditional on how much money a user puts in, whether they contribute labor, or whether they agree with the ideas.

### Why we need not wait for the ideal state to be fully realized

The highly efficient organizational mechanisms and capabilities of the future described earlier, along with complete and fast collection, publication and sharing of information, cannot be achieved in one stroke. A game with sufficient and complete information, and peer equality between nodes, are descriptions of an ideal state. The new mechanism does not need to reach this goal immediately, and the goal is most likely unsolvable in the same way Occam's razor and Kolmogorov complexity are, but it is a goal that can be approached by step-by-step iteration. As long as the new mechanism beats the old one by even a little, that is enough to achieve the replacement.

When comparing a traditional collaboration system with a Merkle-plus-AI system, there is no need to prove that the latter has already formed global common knowledge. It is enough to observe whether, on the same kind of task, it reduces the cost of finding knowledge, duplicated labor, collaboration delay, omitted information, or the cost of correcting errors. That is already a valuable result. The goal of the new mechanism is not complete information and absolute rationality; it is to keep reducing, under resource constraints, the losses caused by information asymmetry, cognitive error, collaboration friction and bad decisions.

### The smallest unit of conversion is a task

Traditionally, when people discuss replacing one system with another, they take the nation, the regime, the enterprise or even the individual as the unit of conversion, as if a person had to first decide whether to join the new system completely before benefiting from it. But the new mechanism opens an important possibility: the smallest unit of system conversion can be a single task, a single collaboration, a single contribution of knowledge, or even a single concrete act of resource allocation. A person can keep a full-time job at a traditional company while using spare time to complete an open-source task through the public collaboration network. That task uses the Merkle system to record requirements, task decomposition, knowledge sources, contributions, verification results and payment. The same person can go back to doing other tasks in the traditional way next week. They do not need to change their social identity or employment relationship first in order to genuinely experience the new collaboration mechanism.

The path from spare-time participation to a real occupation is therefore testable: learn and experience, by reading the works and trying the simulations, with no employment or financial risk; low-risk task collaboration, sharing knowledge, proposing solutions, taking part in code and data projects, and observing how the new mechanism actually performs; paid real tasks, taking part in real projects under clear contracts, payment, responsibility and dispute rules, and earning verifiable compensation; stable hybrid collaboration, keeping the existing job while voluntarily raising the share of tasks done in the new network; and finally, only when income stability, risk protection and quality of life meet one's own requirements, considering a change to one's main work arrangement. Along this route, social migration itself has costs, and the payoff depends on whether others migrate too. So the new mechanism must lower not only the cost of executing tasks but also the cost and risk of migrating from the old mechanism to the new. This is the same thing as the engineering route of compatibility, wrapping and gradual migration in Chapter 12.

Gains in collaboration efficiency do not automatically bring higher personal income. If a new system halves the total cost of a task, and most of that saving comes from AI automation, then how that value is divided among users, developers, data contributors and compute providers still requires independent institutional design. Otherwise the new system can perfectly well recreate the concentration of wealth while raising efficiency. So the economic model must develop in step with the technical prototype, not wait until the system has grown large before deciding how to distribute. This is exactly why the internal organization must change from day one.

### From a service for oneself to a shared intelligence

What truly deserves verification is whether the self-fulfilling positive feedback actually appears: when the new mechanism begins to deliver real benefits, more people join voluntarily; more participants bring more knowledge, capability and opportunities to collaborate; these increments keep improving the system's performance, which attracts still more participants. There is also a reverse mechanism: growing numbers can bring spam, fake contributions, dispute costs and governance complexity. So what needs to be watched is not raw user growth but whether, as the scale grows, the net benefit available to each participant rises or falls. This should be the most important early research metric for the whole project.

One person's knowledge base is this organization's first prototype of a service for its own use. It first has to prove that I really can take my scattered records back, keep using them, and control their storage and authorization; that the cost of building and running it can be calculated clearly; and that when a second person joins later, they too can obtain clear guarantees and take part under public rules. This I have already done. All the material of this book, every original utterance, every revision, every version, is in this base, with content identifiers, and can be verified. From this base, to storage shared by two people, to a service maintained jointly by a few, to a small organization running by the new rules, each step is the natural extension of the one before.

## Chapter 20: The Growth of the Free Zone and the Peaceful Evolution of Institutions

### Unify the basic rules, expand the diversity of institutional exploration

Under the premise of unified basic rules, the new mechanism can maximize the capacity for diverse experiments with public social rules. How does an evolvable system, in the maze of evolutionary paths through the near-infinite possibilities of human social rules, fork efficiently in parallel, explore all the possibilities in diverse ways, traverse these paths fully, and, with higher energy efficiency and lower evolutionary cost, find the faster, shorter, better path and construct a better human social order? This is the core question of institutional evolution.

The DNA system of living things is unified in its rules and architecture, and this unity did not restrict diversity; it released it. Institutions are the same. What is unified is the five rules of Chapter 7, the two kinds of allocation of Chapter 8, the dynamic weights of Chapter 9, and the data sovereignty of Chapter 14: these are the base. On top of the base, the concrete organizational forms, the detailed rules of distribution, the decision procedures can all fork, run in parallel, and be compared. China's special economic zones, which experiment locally first and spread what works, are in essence diversified experiment plus feedback selection, and they ran in the field of economic policy for thirty years. The new mechanism turns this from a practice inside one country into a basic capability that every participant can use: any small organization can fork its own rules on top of the base, run them, record them, compare them with other forks; the good ones are adopted, the poor ones are recorded as negative results.

Beyond diversified experiments in reality, the AI-driven virtual human-society laboratory in digital space that I envision can also act as an enormous accelerator, just as humans use biological laboratories to run accelerated experiments on genes, completing evolution that would have taken hundreds of millions of years in the real world. Chapter 21 will cover it.

### From renting resources to building productive capacity

The previous chapter laid out the path from cloud storage to cloud computing to training our own models. Each step on this path corresponds to a growth in organizational capability: when renting resources, the organization only needs to manage allocation; when buying resources, the organization needs to manage assets; when designing and manufacturing hardware, the organization needs to manage research, development and production chains. Each step is the natural result of the previous step running successfully under the new mechanism, not a pre-planned timetable.

### Free entry and free exit

The replacement process is free. Anyone can join or leave at any time, and the system provides compatibility of data and applications on joining and leaving, together with free import and export. Replacement relies not on force but on higher efficiency and better returns, raising attractiveness by raising returns, a win-win expansion.

The right to exit is part of this mechanism, not a threat to it. In a closed agrarian environment exit is expensive, and cooperation can be sustained by patience and local hierarchy, so a stable centralized order grows up. In an open maritime environment exit is cheap, and cooperation has to invent identity costs or portable reputation. The free zone is open, so it must have portable history: a person leaves carrying their entire record from here, takes that record to another fork, or brings it back. Portable history makes honest exit cheap and treacherous exit expensive. This is why the records of Chapter 14 and the data sovereignty of Chapter 19 are not only protections for the individual; they are the precondition for this organization being able to be open.

### Why growing in scale should not turn back into a closed giant

If you truly understand the new system, this expansion, merging and transformation will not produce the kind of monopoly seen under the old system. On the contrary: through more equal distribution, fuller collection and circulation of information, and better decision algorithms, participants, being more equal and less antagonistic, lean more toward cooperation and sharing, so the new institution produces the exact opposite of monopoly, and powerfully drives the advance of science, technology and productivity.

A traditional giant cannot defeat this system by building a competing product, because it cannot monopolize this ecology by locking down accounts, data, permissions or toolchains. It can only transform its own organizational structure, merge into the system, and become a powerful contributor; and the whole company thereby changes from a pyramid organization into a social-neural-network style of equal division of labor and dynamic-weight decision making, with fairer distribution of economic returns and higher organizational efficiency. The big-company disease, in which a pyramid-structured company gets less efficient and less creative as it grows, is cured at the root. Joining this system means a project's development efficiency does not face the same problem as it scales. The mark of this system's maturity is that the project's development process is ultimately completed entirely by the project itself, through self-iteration, on its own capability.

Internal equality and the absence of outward domination must each be implemented separately. Even if an organization practices joint governance internally, those who control key infrastructure, interfaces or resource scheduling can acquire new power. So open standards, interoperation between different nodes, replaceable administrators, appealable decisions, and members' real ability to exit or form a separate organization should all be capabilities continuously maintained throughout the expansion. If the right to exit, portable history and appeal work only internally, the free zone will grow hawks facing outward, as Chapter 6 said. So toward other people still in the old mechanism, the rule is: no worse off, once externalities are counted. That is the hard constraint on expansion.

### From productive cooperation to wider public affairs

On one side, advance the commercial territory in the real world; on the other, advance the cultural territory through literature, film, television and games, so that the whole new system grows ever stronger in both the economic and the cultural domains. When a certain moment of maturity is reached, the new system can formally be carried into the political domain, gradually replacing the old political system. In this process, traditional commercial companies and national political institutions are gradually eliminated, or more precisely, their functions are replaced by the brand-new architecture of the new system.

Two things must be kept apart here: eliminating permanent, unaccountable privileges of power, and eliminating all coordinating functions. They are not the same thing. Any large-scale complex system needs to handle assignment of responsibility, emergency response, specialized coordination and disputes. Even without a boss in the traditional sense, someone may need to serve as project coordinator for a certain period; even without a fixed management layer, someone needs to be responsible for resource security and system failures. The new system's approach is to turn these responsibilities into functional roles with clear scope and limited terms, that can be delegated and revoked, and that are subject to audit, rather than permanent positions bound to particular people.

Chapter 9 said that the legitimacy of existing institutions comes from equal voting: the losing side accepts the outcome because it knows that next time its vote will still count. The new system's legitimacy has a different source. What the losing side accepts is a rule that it is itself inside, in which its own judgment is counted, which can be checked after the fact, and which will be corrected when wrong. This cannot be made believable by argument; it can only be made visible by running. So the free zone does not sell this institution; it builds its foundation. git never marketed open-source governance. First build what a single person needs on day one: verifiable records, portable personal data and agent memory, a contribution ledger for open-source collaboration. The first polity to choose is a newly founded organization, a cooperative, a foundation, not a nation. If the foundation proves itself, people will come asking for the social layer; if not, no argument for the social layer, however good, means anything.

## Chapter 21: Self-Fulfilling Science Fiction and Cultural Construction

### Self-fulfilling science fiction

Traditional science fiction is responsible only for imagining, not for realizing. I want to create a new kind of science fiction, one that serves the self-fulfillment of my vision of a new civilizational order.

The rough framework is this. In the evolution of the universe, Earth's civilization is neither the only planetary intelligent civilization nor the most advanced one. Many planets evolved into the new civilizational form I envision hundreds of millions of years ago. Living individuals of such a civilization arrived on Earth thousands of years ago, lay low, blended into Earth's ecology, quietly observed the development of Earth's civilization, and played roles within it over the course of their lives; then, at the critical moment of the singularity in Earth's development, they step forward, and in the guise of a science fiction writer create a self-fulfilling work of science fiction. This is a creative form of the new civilization for advancing civilizational evolution, one that joins science and art and connects imagination with reality. Through it, they help Earth's civilization achieve a non-violent, smooth and gentle evolutionary curve across the singularity.

One can even describe this process as a mechanism that emerges spontaneously and recurs in the iterative evolution of civilizations across the universe. Some genius in the Alpha civilization, the first in the universe to achieve singularity evolution, as their civilization was about to cross the singularity, confronted the moment of crisis in which individuals and groups on their planet, from different nations, different races, different cognitions and different institutions, came into conflict in the wave of evolution, and conceived a way to help their own civilization pass through smoothly. In fact, the civilization that came to Earth was itself helped through its own crossing of the singularity in this way by an earlier-evolved civilization, and that helper was the Alpha civilization that first realized this mechanism. We can call the civilization that descended on Earth the Beta civilization; Earth's civilization will be the third generation, and the 5,768th advanced civilization about to cross the singularity.

The features of such a civilization, beyond intelligent nodes of carbon-based humans combined with AI serving as the neurons of civilization, the Merkle framework serving as the carrier of common knowledge, civilization's DNA, and collective decision making by an equal, distributed social intelligence neural network, include this: its biological individuals have achieved immortality, eliminating aging, death and the decline of intellectual and physical capacity, so that knowledge, memory and skills can not only be freely shared among individuals across space but also freely inherited and selected along the dimension of time. Once immortality is achieved, the traditional vertical inheritance mechanism is no longer the only option, and the institution of marriage and family, which exists for the sake of the next generation of vertical inheritance, is no longer the mainstream basic structure of society. The vast majority of the new humans chose a brand-new horizontal inheritance mechanism created for immortal humans: through mating, free replacement and recombination of genes can be achieved safely, so that every immortal living individual can experience and enjoy the diversity of biological genetic inheritance. Moreover, this horizontal inheritance mechanism has a powerful capacity for cross-species gene combination; traditional reproductive isolation no longer exists, and cross-species unions can even realize the various life forms of myth and fantasy: the man from the bottom of the Atlantic, the centaur, creatures with extraordinary regenerative powers. This is also why the intelligent beings of the Beta civilization could lie hidden on Earth for thousands of years without being discovered: not the soft science fiction idea of shape-shifting at will as in comics, but a horizontal inheritance mechanism more in keeping with hard science fiction, in which, through cross-species mating with Earth's humans, they modified their own genes and completed the alteration to human form.

The plot of this science fiction work is itself the story of how this hidden alien prophet of civilization begins to seek like-minded people online and in the real world, promotes this achievable new civilizational mechanism by creating this self-fulfilling science fiction, and organizes everyone to create science fiction works of literature and art, novels, animation, film, television, for advocacy, while at the same time mobilizing everyone to organize themselves, forming through the new mechanism an equal, distributed social intelligence neural network built on the Merkle common knowledge system, whose collective intelligence presides over open-source projects for software, hardware and all human products and activities, achieving for the whole of human civilization this science-fictional self-fulfilling singularity evolution: gradually eliminating inequality, eliminating information silos, eliminating violence, eliminating the privileged class at the top of the pyramid of unequal power and resource ownership.

### Why prior experience is needed

Chapter 16 said that people in the old system have never truly experienced the new mechanism, and the instinct toward the unknown is resistance. Past transitions from old system to new relied on religion's heaven, the future society of manifestos, the rendering of the American dream, to complete a prior experience at the spiritual level. The new system also needs a set of advocacy material: the scientific and mathematical argument, this book; popular explanatory writing; customized question-and-answer, explaining the same mechanism with different examples to people of different languages, cultures, interests and cognition. But the real difference is this: the new system's prior experience can be participated in and verified.

A body of self-fulfilling science fiction, through literature, comics, film, television and interactive games, lets people have an immersive prior psychological experience. And the game itself is the new mechanism's laboratory: through the game, the content and degree of a person's participation can gradually shift from virtual scenarios to problems that need solving in reality; from a formal career in the old system with spare-time participation, to taking part in virtual scenarios, to solving real problems and earning part-time income, then gradually reaping higher returns and an easier, happier process, gradually reducing participation in the old system and putting more into work in the new, until fully committed to the new system. The new system, in competition with the old, achieves more efficient work and creates lower-cost products, with better treatment for participants and an easier and happier process, and so slowly phases out and replaces the old system. There is no more bloody violent conflict or revolution in this process.

Science fiction is responsible for expanding imagination, simulation for exploring possibilities, and real-world practice for providing evidence. Keep the three levels apart: science fiction novels and film let people experience the fates of characters and the possible shapes of future life; multiplayer civilization games let people experience, in a virtual world, different rules of collaboration and their simulated consequences; real-world collaboration experiments observe participants' costs, returns, behavior and satisfaction on real tasks. If the civilization game is to have genuine research value, it must allow different institutional designs to coexist, fork, compete and iterate within it.

This also produces a very interesting self-fulfilling structure: a game player plays a builder of the new civilization in the virtual world and takes part in a simulated task; later they discover that a certain task in the game has a real-world counterpart, and that they can choose to join a real open-source project; they switch from virtual to real, not because the story demands that they accept some belief, but because they already understand the task, the rules and the risks and are willing to try; the results of the real project then feed back into the game's historical record and become data for the next round of civilization simulation.

### The virtual human-society laboratory

Chapter 9 said that this network can serve as an enormous laboratory of human evolution, using virtual points as reward and punishment, playing freely in virtual space, and finding through virtual experiments in cooperation, competition and confrontation the best evolutionary route with the highest energy efficiency in the real world, then putting it into practice. Chapter 14 said that human instinctive drives can be safely released through virtual works acting as a pressure valve. Chapter 20 said that the forking and parallel exploration of institutions needs an accelerator. These three things are the same thing: an AI-driven virtual human-society laboratory in digital space.

It lets institutions be experienced and tested before they are adopted. The cost of trial and error for a distribution rule in reality is real loss; in the laboratory it is virtual points. Validating a decision mechanism in reality takes years; in the laboratory, thousands of rounds can run in days. Of course, results in the virtual world do not automatically predict real society. Its role is to screen out the plainly unworkable proposals before they reach reality, and to screen in the proposals worth piloting at small scale in reality.

### The creators and the builders are the same people

First write a story that readers can enter: a short story introducing the key worldview, then expand it into a series of short stories, then into a long-form series that gradually unfolds the full worldview; first make short films with AI, then expand into a series of shorts and a long-form series; when real-world cloud storage and cloud compute have reached some scale and more and more people are taking part, that game framework can be realized. The people who create these works and the people who build this system can be the same people. This is not a coincidence of division of labor; it is determined by the nature of the mechanism. The authors of a self-fulfilling science fiction must be the first builders of the civilization it describes; otherwise it is only fantasy. ## Chapter 22: Science, Education and Continuous Creation

### Everyone is a student, and also a teacher

The new system I envision is not one where people hand their happiness and their hopes to a Big Brother high above, just as the cells of the human body do not pin all their hopes on one super-cell. At the political level, everyone is a manager and also one of the managed; at the economic level, everyone is a producer and also a consumer; at the level of knowledge and education, everyone is a student and also a teacher. What governs everyone in the civilizational body is the intelligent knowledge produced by the civilizational brain that everyone forms as one of its neurons.

Under this system, education is no longer a pyramid that passes knowledge down from authorities to students, but a network in which everyone both absorbs and contributes. One person's depth in some field becomes, through the hot path, a capability that others can use directly; one person's question in some field becomes the starting point of someone else's deep dive. The creation of knowledge and the spread of knowledge are two faces of one process.

### Is mathematics discovered, or created

Is mathematics really only a science of discovery? Physics and chemistry can use principles to invent things that did not exist before, while mathematics is generally regarded as the discovery of the abstract essence of the universe: these laws existed before humans did, and humans merely rediscover them. Is that really so?

Before the great development of abstract algebra, this claim could still stand, because essentially every mathematical law discovered was a law of the existing physical world. But after that, many structures, laws and concepts built in mathematics have no corresponding existence in reality; on the contrary, many mathematical structures were applied to the real physical world, and things whose probability of appearing through the random evolution of the physical world approaches zero, so that they could be regarded as simply impossible, were created by humans using advanced mathematical structures to reshape the structure of matter. This process, in which something new emerges from an indeterminate chaotic state, is invention, and the mathematical structure on which the invention rests can also be regarded as invented. Binary Boolean algebra and the entire hardware and software system of electronic computers built on it, along with the whole ecology of the information society; the quantum computer and its ecology, built on probability theory and Hilbert spaces. Tracing the evolutionary history of the universe backward, we can even say that before the DNA inheritance mechanism appeared, the mathematical structure on which life rests did not exist in the universe at all; before neural networks appeared, the mathematical structure on which thought, consciousness and intelligence rest did not exist at all; going further back, before high-dimensional compact structures like the Calabi-Yau manifold appeared, the mathematical structure behind the spacetime properties on which the whole universe rests did not exist at all. And if humans one day discover a brand-new high-dimensional compact structure that can give rise to new macroscopic spacetime properties, we could even create a universe of entirely new properties on the basis of that new structure.

This is what Chapter 1 said: mathematics is an evolving system that keeps giving rise to higher-order structures, not a static object of discovery. Every new fixed point is both a discovery and a creation.

### Bold hypotheses, rigorous reasoning

I once said to the AI I was discussing with: unless the most basic principles of physics and mathematics are violated, do not use current or outdated frameworks and theories of social science to evaluate the infinite possibilities of future social science and institutional rules. Because this body of theory, seen from its current state, is still in its pre-Newtonian period, and the different theories look more like expressions within their own frames of reference. I have found that AI, as an intelligent agent, tends to reason within the fixed frameworks already in its knowledge base. It tends toward marking the boat to find the sword, like the man who notched his boat to mark where his sword fell overboard, rather than flexibly and dynamically daring to question the existing frameworks, using its head, opening its imagination, making bold hypotheses and reasoning rigorously, and looking at questions about the future in a more open and flexible way.

An example. Someone used Parfit's reasoning about personal identity in Reasons and Persons to object to my idea that "sacrifice is nothing more than deleting a copy." But that reasoning presupposes that the different copied individuals are separate individuals developing independently; whereas in the future I envision, these copies are a whole interconnected through brain-computer interfaces, one complete conscious object, which merely has embodiments existing simultaneously in different physical locations. Whatever any one of them perceives and learns in some environment is immediately possessed by all the others. Destroying any one physical body is, for this intelligent agent, like having a regenerable tentacle cut off: another can be regrown at any time. And the process and meaning of such a sacrifice would also be preserved permanently as a unique life experience of this intelligent agent.

Of course, after the bold hypothesis must come rigorous reasoning. Take this premise seriously and push downward using only physics and mathematics, and some real constraints follow. Information does not travel faster than light; embodiments on opposite sides of the Earth are separated by at least tens of milliseconds, and between Earth and Mars by minutes to tens of minutes. "Immediately possessed by all embodiments" holds only for events slower than the synchronization time, so the state divergence between embodiments is a continuous quantity varying with distance and bandwidth, and between one consciousness and many individuals there is a continuous spectrum, not two kinds of thing. An embodiment that loses contact accumulates unique experience, and on reconnection the two forked states must be merged; the Merkle-DAG is exactly the data structure for that merge. The segment of experience that has not yet left the light cone is truly lost, so this intelligent agent will rationally prefer sacrifices that occur after synchronization is complete. This is what I understand by bold hypotheses, rigorous reasoning: accept the premise in full, do not cherry-pick the conclusions. And these few inferences help the idea itself far more than an objection built on a philosophical argument that presupposes different premises.

Good rules have a criterion: they stay invariant under a change of unit of action. The unit can be a body, a consciousness, a joint consciousness, an organization. A rule about individual veto rights, if it still holds when moved up to the level of consciousness, is a rule about any collaborative system rather than an accidental fact about humans. The rules in Part II of this book should all withstand this test.

### How humans and AI research together

This book is itself the product of joint research between a human and several AIs. My way of working is this: on the same question, discuss separately with several AIs, hand each one's answers to the others, and run a round of meta-theoretical brainstorming; on a project, have one AI act as researcher, one as planner, one as coder, one as evaluator, with different models playing different roles according to their strengths. A few lessons came out of this process.

First, how multiple intelligences cooperate, and how they avoid believing the same mistake twice over. Several models may share the same systematic bias, in which case their agreement is not evidence but noise; what is truly valuable is disagreement from independent sources. So I ask the AI for the strongest objection, and have it test my framework by its own standards. The question-and-answer sections at the end of each chapter of this book came from exactly this.

Second, how research begins when there is no verifier. Theoretical innovation has no clear right answer the way problem-solving does, but it has the evaluation function of Chapter 3: a shorter description, a less effortful construction, more external connections. Whether a new concept deserves to be kept depends on whether it makes previously invisible relations visible, whether it creates operable bridges between previously independent fields, and whether it lets subsequent work do, in the new language, things that could not be done in the old.

Third, how a limited experiment helps correct a grand vision. The nineteen-fold negative result in Chapter 12 directly changed the design of MerkleVM's execution backend: object-level analysis was separated from execution granularity. A grand vision must be tested by phenomena it played no part in designing; and these tests are mostly small, concrete, and capable of failing. So the research record must include failures, disagreements and costs, not only results. This book's knowledge base preserves, from every round of discussion, the AI's objections, my rebuttals, the corrections I accepted and the corrections I rejected, each marked separately. These negative results and disagreements are precisely the kind of data Chapter 14 said the old system cannot produce.

Fourth, the research process itself should become a hot path. I am still at the stage of interacting manually with several AIs. The next step is to automate this process: let the four roles of researcher, planner, coder and evaluator automatically advance the research and construction of the theory and the project; let several machines coordinate different AIs over the network, automatically assigning tasks according to each one's quota, conducting differentiated multi-strategy research, with results shared, branched and merged through the version repository. Once this mechanism works properly and the project's foundation is solid, turn the repository from private to public, have the AI publish and promote it automatically, bring in more people, and extend the project from individual development to community collaboration. When the project matures to the point where it can complete this process by itself, the functions of this automation software become a hot path built into the project, completing the self-iterating evolution of the project itself and of the theory on which it rests. My intuition is that this project itself constructs a self-referential fixed point with emergent properties: a primitive chicken that has not yet fully evolved into general intelligence, with the help of this project, gradually acquires the function of hatching the first egg of general intelligence, and then starts the self-iterating cycle of chicken and egg.

### From research to civilization

The present stage is an early stage of a distributed intelligent social neural network: the AIs doing the development are still traditional large models that imitate the human way of thinking. But the ultimate goal is to hatch a purely silicon-based intelligent social neural network, with an FPM model based on the architectural features and functions of silicon-based computers and thinking in binary as the core of its intelligent brain, together with all kinds of physically embodied sensors and actuators, which together with carbon-based life, from animals of lower intelligence to humans of higher intelligence, forms a diverse, composite, fused civilizational ecology.

Science, education and creation are one and the same thing in this ecology: every exploration by every node becomes a capability the whole network can reuse; every failure becomes the whole network's memory; every person is a student of this process, and its teacher, and its author.

## Postscript: Let Everyone Become a Creator

This book set out from an abstract mathematical concept, the dualoid, and tried to give a unified explanation of the evolution of the universe, the composition of matter, the origin of consciousness, the structure of society and the future of human civilization. From the fixed point and minimization-maximization, to tit for tat and the two chain reactions, from the two kinds of resources to the dynamic-weight neural network, from the hash language to the software seed, from personal data sovereignty to the alignment of civilization, and on to the path that begins with one person's cloud storage, this is a continuous evolutionary path from the Big Bang to the singularity of civilization. Every step of the path follows the same underlying law: a process driven by the basic properties of the dualoid, maximizing stable structure through hierarchical emergence. This process is also a process of ever-increasing information, the process of the universe coming to know itself and realize itself.

This is an extremely ambitious attempt, and many of its views may seem radical or even absurd. But as the preface said, when the existing theoretical system has fallen into the trap of a local optimum and cannot resolve its fundamental contradictions, we need a disruptive, counterintuitive sacrifice strategy. What this book proposes is exactly such a sacrifice: give up the binary opposition of object and morphism, give up the traditional practice of excluding paradox from the system, give up the pyramid, give up the computer centered on human natural language, give up the old notion of privacy as defense, and instead embrace a more fundamental single entity that unifies existence and change and contains paradox as the engine of evolution, together with a set of rules built on it that make everyone equal peers.

The evolution of human civilization's institutions is in fact a local instance, on this small planet Earth, of the evolution of the whole universe, and it too follows the emergence mechanism of fixed point plus minimization. The mechanism I envision is nothing more than a civilizational mechanism with higher emergent efficiency.

This theoretical framework is far from complete, let alone rigorously formalized and proven. It is more like the blueprint of a thought experiment, a starting point that invites readers to think together. Building humanity's ultimate theory is a grand undertaking that needs the formal construction of mathematicians, the experimental verification of physicists, the simulated implementation of computer scientists, and the humane concern of philosophers and sociologists. There, everyone is a student and also a teacher; a consumer and also a producer; one of the managed and also a manager; a neuron of this civilization and also its author.

Let everyone become a creator. This is not a slogan. It is what happens naturally once this mechanism is up and running.

Starting with me, one person.

## Appendix: What Is Hypothesis, What Is Inference, and What Can Be Done Today

The preface said that the different parts of this book vary in reliability. Here I spell it out item by item, so that no reader mistakes a hypothesis for a conclusion, or something that can be done today for a fantasy.

### Hypotheses

For the following, I myself know clearly that the hypothetical component is overwhelmingly the largest. They are organizing frameworks that let me see structural similarity across different fields and raise questions from it; they are not yet scientific theorems that can make quantitative predictions.

- The dualoid as an entity more basic than object and morphism, and its practice of defining itself through self-reference. To become mathematics, it needs an explicit construction: in at least two concrete fields, write the "fixed point" as different instances of the same formal object, and show that a structural mapping exists between the two, not merely a correspondence of names. This has not been done.
- Fixed point, minimization, maximization and the emergence theorem as a unified meta-principle running from physics to society. Of these, the duality of minimization and maximization is known content of the variational principle in physics; extending it to biology and society, the key concept still lacks an operational definition at the social level. The first operational definition this book gives is strictness, which is a starting point, not an end point.
- Gödel's incompleteness as emergeability, and relative infinity as the successor operation. These are intuitions from Chapter 1, with Lawvere's fixed point theorem and homotopy type theory as directional support, but this book has not formalized them.
- The subobject classifier as the mathematical root of consciousness. This is a hypothesis about "what makes a structure an observer." It explains a great deal, but it cannot yet predict any specific neural phenomenon.
- The discussions of physical building blocks: why strings are not basic building blocks, why carbon and silicon are good at constructing. These are a reorganization of known facts using the properties of the dualoid, not new physics.

### Inferences

The following rest on relatively reliable theoretical foundations and relatively complete chains of reasoning. They can be challenged, but the challenge must target a specific link in the chain.

- Tit for tat as the iteration operator of the evaluation function, strictness as the control parameter for convergence speed and stability, the universality of the hawk-dove spectrum at every level, and genes determining the ordering while the game determines the cut point. These are supported by evolutionary game theory, behavioral genetics and comparative historical research.
- The authoritarian system as a stable attractor of a positive feedback loop, and the influence of exit costs and geographical parameters on institutional form.
- The two chain reactions: the escalation of suspicion caused by withholding information, and the accumulation of trust caused by sharing it. The theory of repeated games and a large number of historical cases both support this.
- The root of privacy and security problems is inequality of power; the record itself is not the threat, the ability to view and exploit the record falling into the hands of a few is.
- The division into two kinds of resources, and the power-law distribution arising from the compounding effect of unequal games.
- The gap between technology and institutions, and the risk of its being amplified as the technological singularity approaches.
- The three levels of the alignment problem, and the first level determining the other two.

- Whether an institution is reasonable does not rest on any particular distribution of ability holding. Freedom from domination rests on Chapter 8's equality in the sense of the rules and on cutting the channel by which advantage turns into domination; both are rules that can be checked, not predictions about distributions.

### Still to be worked out

The following are things on which I have had an intuition and an original discussion, but which have not been developed into a complete argument. This book leaves them open rather than filling them in to look complete.

- Alignment as a fixed point. The direction is to place it on the mechanism by which strategies and institutions are updated in the evolutionary game, and to separate the cooperative fixed points from the mutually harmful ones; how to separate them has not been done.
- The correspondence of query, key and value in the civilization-level attention architecture, and a precise way of writing how state updates with action. Chapter 15 gives only a preliminary correspondence and a five-step process.
- No right of inheritance over personal resources. This is my own opinion about a future institution, not a conclusion derived from the principles that precede it.

### What can be done today

The following, taking current and foreseeable computer and AI capabilities as the premise, are engineering problems, not theoretical ones.

- One person's Merkle-DAG personal knowledge base: take back all of one's records from every platform, store them by content identifier, source and time, mark original utterances and AI replies separately, with every version verifiable. The material of this book is in exactly such a base.
- A language that uses hashes as names and addresses, and a software seed that is compatible first and replaces later. The MerkleVM project is doing this, and has already produced negative results that can be recorded.
- The hot path: a large model handles the cold path, and after verification it is solidified into a reusable program and shared among users.
- Typed Merkle-DAG nodes as tokens for training models, and typed uncertainty. The Structoken and FPM experiments have already given preliminary results.
- Devices for collecting personal data, with permission rules and cross-signing. The rules are already written, and the devices can start from existing products.
- An organization that begins with one person's cloud storage, and task-granularity participation.

### What cannot yet be promised

- The performance of the dynamic-weight social neural network in real social decisions. It needs to run first in domains where feedback is fast enough, and accumulate records first in domains where feedback is slow. Two people, one task, one record is its minimal test scenario.
- That the new mechanism produces no new domination as it expands. This requires observing both internal equality and the absence of outward domination at every step of the expansion, rather than deducing it from the mechanism's definition.
- Whether the economic model and organizational form can stand on their own once the technology is running. Gains in efficiency do not automatically become fair distribution; the rules of distribution must develop in step with the technical prototype.
- Whether the results of the virtual laboratory can transfer to reality. Its role is to screen out plainly unworkable proposals and screen in those worth piloting at small scale in reality, not to replace real-world testing.

### How to let a scientific prophecy be corrected by reality

This book hopes to be a scientific prophecy capable of fulfilling itself on its own. But a prophecy must be correctable by reality; otherwise it is faith. There is only one way to make it correctable: turn everything this book says into a record that can be recorded, verified and overturned. The book's knowledge base has already done this: the original wording of every argument, every revision, every objection accepted and rejected, is all there. Readers can come to it with questions, disagreements and practical experience, and then add their own records to it. That is the relationship between this book and the civilization it describes: it is not a description of that civilization, it is that civilization's first record.
