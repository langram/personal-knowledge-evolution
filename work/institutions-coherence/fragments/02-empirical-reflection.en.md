### Testing the claim of an institutional gap

Evidence about wealth concentration, trust in government, and young people's well-being gives us concrete questions to investigate. Taken together, it does not prove that existing institutions necessarily cause every problem or that the proposed system will solve them all. Total wealth must be distinguished from shares of wealth, income from assets, the top 0.1 percent from the top 1 percent, and one period or region from another. A global average cannot describe everyone's circumstances; deterioration in some places cannot stand for a worldwide trend. Experiments on negative headlines measure clicking behavior, not long-term mental health.

The claim that institutions lag behind technology also needs to become more precise. There is no common ruler with which to measure their respective speeds. For a particular service, organization, or public decision, we can instead examine how technology expands the reach of an action and accelerates the spread of an error. We can then ask how long affected people need to discover the problem, raise an objection, stop the harm, and secure a remedy. Shortening that second chain without sacrificing basic rights or shifting costs onto weaker participants would be progress relevant to this book's aims.

Further research should compare arrangements for distribution and governance, examining material conditions, discretionary time, meaningful participation, trust, and people's evaluations of their own lives. It must distinguish the effects of technology, public policy, demographics, and other circumstances. If an existing arrangement proves more reliable for the same task, or if a new attention and weighting system merely increases clicks, uploads, and apparent agreement without improving lives, the proposal should change. Even higher reported satisfaction requires us to look for people who left, remained silent, or bore uncounted costs. A civilizational system that hopes to evolve must allow evidence to revise its account of its own superiority.
