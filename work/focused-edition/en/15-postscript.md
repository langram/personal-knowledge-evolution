## Postscript: Let Everyone Become a Creator

This book set out from a few of the most reliable principles in natural science: minimization and maximization, the fixed point, emergence, relativity. It then climbed along one continuous chain: tit-for-tat as the iteration operator that builds social structure, common knowledge and the two chain reactions, the two kinds of resources and equality in the sense of the rules, the social neural network of dynamic weights, intelligence as public infrastructure, the Large Civilization Model in which training and inference are one, the carrier of common knowledge in which content is identity, personal data sovereignty, and on to the path that begins with one person's cloud storage. Every step on this chain follows the same underlying law: a stable structure maintains itself at the least internal cost, and with the greatest external connectivity gives rise to a structure one level higher. The evolution of human civilization's institutions is a local instance, on this small planet Earth, of the evolution of the whole universe. The mechanism I envision is nothing more than a civilizational mechanism with higher emergent efficiency.

Let me state the position of this book once more. I have not discovered and defined the specific rules and structures of a new civilizational order. What this book gives is a foundation of mathematical principles, strategies for the meta-game and a plan for getting started: a compass, a telescope and a chart, not the destination; a torch held up to scout the road toward the singularity of civilization, so that the road may be traveled a little more smoothly and a little faster, and the ever-widening gap in speed between institutions and technology does not bring disaster on human civilization.

And the direction it points in is the "bitter lesson" at the level of the evolution of civilization's institutions. In 2019 Sutton looked back over seventy years of AI and drew a conclusion many researchers found hard to swallow: hand-writing the knowledge and experience of human experts into a system always works in the short run, and always loses in the long run to general methods plus enormous data and computing power. Chess, Go, speech, vision, language, without exception; in protein folding, methods built on decades of accumulated expert experience were overtaken by one model at a stroke. The social domain has long shown the same signs: more than half a century of research has shown again and again that simple statistical models match or beat the personal judgment of experts in prediction, and that a group weighted by its track record beats the individual authority. Yet humanity still hands its most complex problem, the construction and organization of society, to the personal experience and learning of political, economic and academic authorities, to be solved through simple cooperation. That is how the traditional edifice of science was built, brick by brick, by geniuses and authorities. I do not deny its achievements: with the experience and knowledge of individuals, humanity built today's system of science and its political and economic institutions; today's large language models were trained on exactly what those geniuses and authorities wrote; and in the Large Civilization Model they are not abolished either, but become nodes carrying weights. But to reach the singularity of technology and civilization, to make a leap to a higher level, the best road is the other one: use mathematics and computers to model mathematically the complex problems of social construction and organization that all of humanity faces, gather data on an enormous scale, and, in the loop of learning, training and inference in practice, construct that Large Civilization Model. This is the bitter lesson of civilization's institutions. As the preface said, at the level of civilization its price is blood and lives, so it is in truth a cruel lesson. Humanity will have to swallow it sooner or later, and the sooner the better.

Now it is time. Throughout the history of human civilization, the governance of civilization has rested on leaders and sages who, each drawing on his own knowledge and experience of the social domain, designed ingenious rules; and in the conflicts their differing ideas produced, humanity has tasted the "bitter lesson" again and again. It is time to bid farewell to that scene and embrace a new civilizational order: one driven by a social intelligent neural network, founded on general algorithms grounded in mathematical and scientific theory, sustained by computing power on a massive scale, and capable of recursive self-improvement and self-evolution.

For more than a hundred years, humanity's visions of the ideal future society have been split by one central divide: liberalism (also called liberal democracy) and Marxism (also called socialism or communism). Since the middle of the nineteenth century these two systems of thought have been the two central ideological currents shaping the political and social landscape of the modern world. Under the new civilizational order, all these differences of human cognition will gradually iterate and merge, and in the end forge a civilization of the people, by the people, for the people, that shall long evolve and shall not perish from the earth; in place of the old bourgeois society, with its classes and class antagonisms, we shall have an association, in which the free development of each is the condition for the free development of all.

The first half of that sentence comes from Lincoln's address at Gettysburg in 1863; the second half comes from the Communist Manifesto of Marx and Engels in 1848. Two sentences written fifteen years apart, and since assigned to two opposing camps, join into one complete sentence.

So the most important thing is for all of humanity to reach consensus on this direction as soon as possible and begin trying together. Where consensus comes from, AI's own history has already answered. No one first coordinated all of humanity into a consensus on deep learning: in 2012 one small group won one competition with one model, and the whole world followed. First a working instance, then consensus. And one more thing: when language models took off, the corpus was already lying on the internet; but the corpus the Large Civilization Model needs, every problem, every node's judgment, every collective decision, every real-world result, has never yet been systematically recorded. The dataset comes before the model. So the two most urgent things today are to build the first instance that runs as a closed loop, and to begin accumulating the first batch of records. Neither needs anyone's permission, and one person's knowledge base is its first seed.

This theoretical framework is far from complete. It is a torch, a starting point that invites readers to think together. Finishing it needs the formalization of mathematicians, the experimental verification of scientists, the implementation of engineers, and also the participation and testing of every ordinary person. And in the system I envision, these people need not belong to any particular profession. There, everyone is a student and also a teacher; a consumer and also a producer; one of the managed and also a manager; a neuron of this civilization and also its author.

Let everyone become a creator. This is not a slogan. It is what happens naturally once this mechanism is up and running.

Starting with me, one person.
