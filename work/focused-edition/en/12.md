## Chapter 12: Alignment and the Fork in the Road

### Aligned with whom

Alignment, for artificial intelligence, means ensuring that the outputs and behavior of an AI system stay consistent with human values, intentions and social norms, making the model an assistant that is helpful, honest and harmless.

Start from this definition and combine it with an objective fact: within human civilization, the values, intentions and norms of different peoples, different regimes and different classes differ from one another and cannot be made consistent. The conclusion follows naturally: in the game of human society, every player with the ability to create and control AI, whether a regime, a company or an individual, holds an assistant that is "helpful, honest and harmless" to itself, and that assistant may become, in the eyes of the other players, the accomplice of a fearsome adversary. And AI's powerful capacity for self-iteration amplifies and accelerates the capability of the player who controls it, and also widens the gap between players: a difference of a few months is a difference of hundreds or thousands of times in capability.

### Balanced or unbalanced, the result is terrifying

If development is unbalanced and one player dominates, then for the dominant player the highest-return, lowest-cost way to play is to use AI to suppress all the others, ending in winner-take-all.

If development is balanced and several parties stand off against one another, then because of mutual distrust and insufficient exchange of information, the parties still run the danger of triggering destruction through one small misunderstanding, without any strong malice, just as during the Cold War the whole world worried that the powers holding nuclear weapons might destroy the world over a small misunderstanding.

The child of Chapter 11 swinging the chain flail now has a concrete setting. The AI race between China and the United States is like a creature with two brains, a left and a right, each hand swinging its own chain flail, but with no effective alignment mechanism between the two brains; sooner or later the two hammers will collide and tangle, and the creature will wound itself.

These two endings share the same structure: each is the endpoint of the negative chain reaction of Chapter 4.

### Three layers of alignment

What truly most needs to be solved right now is the alignment between the values, intentions and social norms of every organization and every individual in all of humanity, and their behavior. Once that is solved, the alignment between humanity and AI, which is the aggregated product of human knowledge, follows as a matter of course. One step further still is the alignment between the new civilizational entity of humans plus AI and the whole ecology of the Earth.

Alignment therefore has three layers: the alignment of humans with humans, the alignment of humans with AI, and the alignment of civilization with the ecology. All the attention today is on the middle layer, and the middle layer looks unsolvable because the first layer has not been done. A civilization that is internally split in its values and whose parts threaten one another will train "aligned" AIs each of which is a piece of that split. An intelligence takes its training material from all humanity, but its goal is set by whoever trains it; so alignment between humans and AI comes down to who sets the goal and by what standard.

Two things can be settled now. First, alignment is not a fixed endpoint but a state in operation: when new things appear, new players enter, or resources change, the old balance breaks. So what alignment requires is not a one-time injection of values but a continuous capacity for correction, which is exactly what a social neural network of dynamic weights does. Second, alignment is not one party imposing its values on the others; what that produces is a tension held down by external force, ready to spring back at any moment, like the false peace in *Watchmen*. True alignment is the position the parties walk to on their own, under common knowledge, verifiable records and symmetric rules of the game.

This is why this book does not treat AI safety as an independent technical problem, but as one indicator of a civilization's capacity for self-governance. To make AI trustworthy, first make civilization trustworthy; to make civilization trustworthy, first make it possible for every node inside it, under common knowledge and symmetric play, to verify one another and correct one another instead of threatening one another.

### A thought experiment: the choice of the richest person in the world

What does moving to the new system mean for the greatest beneficiaries of the old system?

Under the existing system, many rich and powerful people, sitting on uncountable wealth and enormous power, remain anxious in the struggle for advantage between persons and between groups, forever afraid of losing everything in competition and confrontation, guarding not only against enemies but suspecting friends too. These problems are not removed by holding the dominant position in the game: the incompleteness of information means that the probability of total defeat can never be lowered to the point where you can fully rest easy.

Suppose you are the richest person in the world, sitting on a vast fortune, and you are sixty this year. Under the old system, immortality technology will take forty years to achieve, and your life expectancy is ninety, so you will most likely die before it appears. Under the new system, you still have personal resources sufficient to keep you healthy and happy, while you give up full control over that vast sum of public resources, handing it over to the dynamic-weight system that aggregates the collective intelligence of all humanity, and letting your own knowledge and intellect become one decision node in that system. What you get in exchange: you no longer need to live in daily dread inside a game of information silos; more important, under this new decision system and mechanism of evolution, immortality technology arrives in twenty years. Now, would you rather be the richest person in the old system, or an immortal in the new one?

What the new mechanism sacrifices is near-term and temporary gains; what it obtains is greater long-term gains. Once both the allocation of individual resources and the decision authority over collective resources have more reasonable rules, a new equilibrium is reached: anyone who tries to make a different choice will only, after repeated play, obtain a worse result. Even the greatest beneficiary of the old system gains more under the new.

Of course there is also a worst local optimum: a small elite, while monopolizing wealth and power, also monopolizes every superior capability, for instance the monopoly on becoming immortal. That is the truly terrifying world. But once a small minority tries to monopolize, the overwhelming majority will not submit meekly, and the result will very likely be a war involving all of humanity.

### Two directions

We are standing at a critical fork in the road. One direction leads through a negative chain reaction to civilization's self-destruction; the other leads through a positive chain reaction toward the singularity of civilization's evolution, toward a new form of human civilization.

The two chains of Chapter 4 are idealized extremes, and most of the time in reality we are somewhere between them. But on the scale of civilization the two extremes really exist: at one end nuclear war and AI out of control, at the other a planetary civilization able to know itself and correct itself. The time for swinging in the middle is not unlimited, and the technological singularity is compressing it.

The spread of advanced process nodes and lithography technology could plainly drive human civilization forward at speed, yet nations, companies and individuals, locked in the confrontation of the old system, use secrecy and sanctions to make prisoner's-dilemma choices that look like benefiting oneself at others' expense but in the long run hurt both sides. Under the new mechanism, every person records their own life and, with the help of AI, rapidly understands and masters the knowledge of this world; every tiny spark of thought is recorded and spread, is quickly turned, with the cooperation of others, into an increment of knowledge, and is added to the knowledge base of all humanity, and so the cycle continues.

The choice between these two roads is not made for humanity by anyone. It is made by every person, a little at a time, each time they decide whether to share or to conceal, whether to cooperate or to guard. What comes next is how to begin with the first person.

### Questions and Answers

**Q: Disagreements over values are not disagreements over facts. Science can dissolve disagreements over facts; it cannot dissolve disagreements over values. How could the first layer of alignment possibly be achieved by a single mechanism?**

A: The principle of relativity in Chapter 2 already answers half of this: there is no absolute coordinate system of values, only transformations between coordinate systems. What the first layer of alignment demands is not that everyone hold the same set of values, but that everyone accept the same set of transformation rules: verify facts under common knowledge, negotiate values under symmetric play, and bear the consequences under the rule that failure means compensation and correction. This set of rules contains no specific value content of its own, so it applies equally to people holding every kind of value. Disagreements over values do not vanish under these rules; they become the diversity described in Chapter 1, part of the space of evolution rather than a reason to annihilate one another. The other half of the answer is that piece of meta-cognition: it reduces the opposition of values from "good against evil" to "differences with conditions of formation," lets the disagreement cool down psychologically first, and then enter the rules.
