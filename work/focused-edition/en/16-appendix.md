## Appendix: Grounds, Inferences and Unfinished Work

The preface said that the different parts of this book vary in reliability. Here I separate them item by item, so that no reader mistakes an inference for a settled conclusion, or something that can be done today for a fantasy.

### Grounds: settled mathematics and science

The following are not discoveries of this book; this book only uses them.

- Variational principles and thermodynamics: least action, free-energy minimization and entropy maximization, and the equivalent forms between them.
- Fixed points and equilibria: equilibrium states, attractors, the Nash equilibrium and its existence; dissipative structures.
- Symmetry and relativity: the invariance of physical laws under transformations of reference frame, and the correspondence between symmetries and conserved quantities.
- Emergence: higher-level structures have laws of their own, while obeying the constraints of the lower levels.
- Game theory: in repeated games both cooperation and defection can be equilibria; the performance of tit-for-tat in repeated prisoner's dilemma tournaments; the mixed equilibrium of the hawk-dove game; the definition and role of common knowledge; the security dilemma.
- Probability and networks: processes of proportional growth and "the rich get richer" produce long tails and power-law distributions.
- Machine learning: online learning algorithms that aggregate multiple judges weighted by their past performance, and their guarantees; the attention mechanism; methods of training through self-play and feedback when there is no standard answer; the history summed up by the "bitter lesson".
- Forecasting research: the comparison of statistical models with the personal judgment of experts, and group forecasts weighted by track record.
- Social choice and collective judgment: the anonymity of rules; the jury theorem, and the optimal aggregation weighted by the log-odds of accuracy; the theorem that individual rights of decision and the Pareto standard cannot be reconciled; empowerment and capability as measures of an agent's reachable future.
- History of science: the measurement of temperature came before thermodynamics, and the definition of temperature was corrected step by step in the course of measuring.
- Software engineering: cryptographic hashes, Merkle trees and content addressing, digital signatures, version control.

### Inferences: the generalizations and designs this book makes

The following rest on the grounds above and on complete chains of reasoning. They can be challenged, but the challenge should target a specific link in the chain.

- "Minimize internal cost, maximize external connectivity" as a working principle running through physics, biology and society. At the social level it has only one operational definition so far, strictness; this is a starting point, not an end point.
- The relativity of cognitive reference frames, and the meta-cognition derived from it.
- Tit-for-tat as the iteration operator of the evaluation function, strictness as the control parameter for convergence speed and stability; genes determining the ordering while the game determines the cut-off point; the authoritarian system as a stable attractor of a positive feedback loop, and the influence of exit cost on institutional form.
- The two chain reactions, the five meta-rules; the root of privacy and security problems is inequality of power.
- The division into two kinds of resources; power-law distribution arising from the compounding of unequal games; equality in the sense of the rules. Whether an institution is reasonable does not rest on any particular distribution of ability holding.
- The social neural network of dynamic weights, weights attaching to problems and expiring, and the equality of basic rights kept separate from the dynamics of influence.
- The training and inference of a civilizational body cannot be separated; every person's evaluations and decisions are the labels on the data; artificial general intelligence is the whole civilizational body, not any one model; the direction of the Large Civilization Model and social mechanics.
- Freedom, equality and democracy as three types of mathematical object: a quantity, a symmetry, an aggregation operator. Their specific forms are only initial values, to be corrected by running forks and comparing them; the same standing under the rules, the ability to exit and fork, and every person's judgment entering, these three meta-rules are the precondition for the iteration itself to be trustworthy.
- The three layers of alignment, and the first layer determining the other two.
- No right of inheritance over personal resources. This one is my own opinion about a future institution, not a conclusion derived from the principles that precede it.

### What can be done today

The following, taking current and foreseeable computer and AI capabilities as the premise, are engineering problems.

- One person's Merkle-DAG personal knowledge base: take back all of one's records from every platform, store them by content identifier, source and time, mark original utterances and the AI's replies separately, with every version verifiable. The material of this book is in exactly such a base.
- A language that uses hashes as names and addresses, and a migration mechanism that is compatible first and replaces later. My project is doing this, and has already produced negative results that can be recorded.
- The hot path: a large model handles a task the first time it appears, and after verification it is solidified into a reusable program and shared among users.
- The collection of personal data, the rules of viewing permissions, and verifiable devices.
- An organization that begins with one person's cloud storage, and task-granularity participation.
- The minimal test of cooperation: two people, one task, one record.

### What cannot yet be promised

- The performance of the dynamic-weight social neural network in real social decisions. It needs to run first in domains where feedback is fast enough, and accumulate records first in domains where feedback is slow.
- That the new mechanism produces no new domination as it expands. This requires checking both internal equality and the absence of outward domination at every step of the expansion, rather than deducing it from the mechanism's definition.
- Whether the rules of distribution and the organizational form can stand on their own once the technology is running. Gains in efficiency do not automatically become fair distribution.
- The Large Civilization Model and social mechanics. Each of its units is itself a high-dimensional semantic model, and its demand for computing power is several orders of magnitude above today's large models; its training material can only accumulate once the social neural network is running. This is a direction, not a capability of today.
- The specific correspondences given for freedom, equality and democracy. How the reachable future is computed and how the weights are set are still only initial values, to be corrected by running forks in free zones and comparing them.
- Whether the results of the virtual laboratory can transfer to reality. Its role is to screen, not to replace real-world testing.

### Extensions

There is a further body of material that did not enter this book: category theory and the dualoid; the relationship between paradox, incompleteness and emergence; the subobject classifier and the mathematical root of consciousness; the fixed-point characterization of alignment; the correspondence of query, key and value in civilizational attention; the engineering details of MerkleVM, the software seed and the hot path; FPM and typed uncertainty; the specific setting of the self-fulfilling science fiction; and so on. They are part of my long-term thinking, but they either lack broad consensus, or have not yet been developed into complete arguments, or lie far from the main line of this book. I have organized them into a separate document, "Extensions: Experimental Hypotheses and Ideas", for interested readers to consult. The argument of this book does not take any one of them as a premise.

### How to let a scientific prophecy be corrected by reality

This book hopes to be a scientific prophecy capable of fulfilling itself on its own. But a prophecy must be correctable by reality; otherwise it is faith. There is only one way to make it correctable: turn everything this book says into a record that can be recorded, verified and overturned. The book's knowledge base has already done this: the original wording of every argument, every revision, every objection accepted and rejected, is all there. Readers can come to it with questions, disagreements and practical experience, and then add their own records to it.
