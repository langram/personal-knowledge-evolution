## Chapter 6: The Social Neural Network of Dynamic Weights

### Starting from spam

The decision mechanism I envision was first inspired by spam-filtering algorithms. Whether an email is spam is not decided by any single rule; instead countless features each give a judgment, each judgment carries a weight, and the weight keeps adjusting according to that feature's past accuracy. The weight of a correct judgment rises; the weight of a wrong one falls. The whole system needs no "authority" at all, yet it gets more and more accurate.

Algorithms of this kind have a mature theory in machine learning. Treat each judge as an "expert" and aggregate them, weighted by each one's past performance, adjusting the weights after every outcome; the collective judgment obtained this way carries a provable guarantee: in the long run it will not be much worse than the one expert who, in hindsight, performed best, and there is no need to know in advance who the best one is.

Put this algorithm onto the decisions of human society: when a complex, uncertain problem arises at the social, political or economic level, instead of having it decided by the relevant experts under an authoritarian system or by elections and votes under a democratic system, the historical accuracy of each human and AI agent node in answering that class of problem is computed and different weights are assigned; then from the weighted results several different solutions are offered, each with its own weight; then experiments are run through virtual or real social pilots, and the feedback decides how to roll out; and the result of each round of feedback in turn revises the decision weight of every node.

Every intelligent individual, whether human or AI, has an equal initial weight in every field. That weight is adjusted dynamically, with positive and negative feedback, according to the accuracy of its predictions and the effectiveness of its proposals in that field, all of which the system can record and verify.

### The brain compared with society

Use the human brain as the metaphor for this new civilizational system: the brain corresponds to civilization, neurons correspond to humans plus AI, an individual's knowledge corresponds to the sum of human knowledge, and the brain's biological neural network corresponds to the neural network of human society.

In the brain, neurons are equal to one another in status, differing only in which network layer they sit in, with the weights of their connections adjusting dynamically at all times. The network formed by the individuals of human society is a static pyramid structure, in which the resources one holds, the power one has and the size of one's decision weight depend on one's position in the pyramid and are relatively fixed. When an exceptionally capable entrepreneur or politician declines in cognitive and decision-making ability with age, his decision weight is not thereby quickly replaced by a younger and more vigorous new generation.

In dictatorships and oligarchies, the weight of the leader is far greater than that of ordinary people, and precisely because his power is so great, discovering errors and correcting them through feedback is extremely difficult and extremely slow, usually requiring violent revolution to complete the redistribution of decision weight. In democracies, whether one person one vote or a system of representatives, weight is redistributed once every few years; the speed of correction is better than in dictatorship and oligarchy, but still seriously lagging, and a vote does not distinguish between problems, nor does it change with results.

### Unbinding decision nodes from specific people

There is a key point in this mechanism: the nodes of the decision network are no longer bound to specific elite human individuals or groups, but consist of algorithms and rules in which collective intelligence converges. The Industrial Revolution turned physical labor from the personal craft of the workshop into a function that machines could reproduce at high quality; in the same way, before the AI revolution, social governance was carried out by the pyramid formed by political leaders, business magnates and academic authorities, and the AI revolution will inevitably bring a revolution in the organizational form of intellectual labor. Algorithms, rules and strategies, these intellectual products, are no longer bound to some specific authority but become functions that can be copied at scale. Just as after the Industrial Revolution capitalism was, relative to feudalism, a relation of production better suited to automated physical labor, the social neural network of dynamic weights is a relation of production better suited than capitalism to the new productive force of automated intellectual labor.

What is more, this dynamic-weight approach does not even require people to take part by hand at every moment. If a person's digital space is recorded in full, if his skills, cognition and preferences are recorded with privacy and security guaranteed, with their owner marked and tamper-proof, then when the group needs a decision, in the great majority of cases he does not need to take part personally; his AI agent can provide his opinion and views from these records. Simple, routine, trivial judgments are completed automatically by the AI agent; on major matters the AI invites the person himself to take part, and an opinion is given after joint discussion; the whole process is recorded throughout, to be used in future to adjust weights through feedback. Each person's AI agent is the rational intelligence that represents that person's rights, interests and opinions.

### Virtual tit-for-tat

The strict and lenient versions of tit-for-tat described in Chapter 3 can be replaced in this network by a virtual version: using digital points as the means of reward and punishment, turning real damage in the real world into virtual damage in a game. Throughout human history, the adjustment of power and decision weight has usually been accompanied by violent punishment and wastefully excessive reward. Virtual tit-for-tat reaches a new stable structure at the lowest energy and the fastest speed; before taking real action, the various possibilities can be explored in virtual space to find the path with the highest energy-efficiency ratio and the least harm. This network is at the same time a vast social laboratory.

### Serial and parallel: beyond democracy and centralism

The running of any system is a mixture of two broad classes of logic: one must be completed in causal and sequential order, the other can run in parallel. To build a building you must lay the foundation before raising the main structure, but within each stage many tasks can run in parallel. In designing and assigning tasks, whether to lean more toward the causal order of overall coordination, or more toward each part running independently in parallel and then integrating through the rules of the market or of the game, is the most important meta-strategy.

The multi-party democratic federal system attends more to the autonomy of each part, parallel development and diversity of direction; the centralized system of one-party rule leans more toward the consistency of overall coordination and the long-range planning of complex tasks. What is strong in democratic capacity is weak in centralizing capacity; what is strong in centralizing capacity is weak in democratic capacity. The history of artificial intelligence has a similar story: architectures good at sequential dependency and architectures good at parallel processing contended for years, and were finally unified by the attention mechanism, which lets every position attend directly to every other position, preserving long-range dependency while running fully in parallel; multi-head attention further lets the same problem receive attention from different angles at the same time. Complex problems and large projects at the level of society need exactly such a mechanism, one that combines the two.

The contradiction between commitment and error correction lies here too. A centralized system has the commitment capacity for long-term planning, but the commitment capacity is so strong that one mistake costs too much. Its speed of error correction is inversely proportional to the political investment of the top: in fields where authority has not been staked, both experiment and correction are fast; in fields where authority has been staked, correction is slow, and when it comes it comes as a sudden break. That is the mark of a pyramid with face to save: policy is bound to specific people. Under a dynamic-weight decision system there is no such binding.

### The central nervous center is not a single point

A stable intelligent agent must be stable over time and logically consistent in its outward behavior. A distributed nervous system like the jellyfish's can only manage simple interactions; the more complex the intelligent organism, the more complex its central nervous system. A civilization that lacks the unified, coordinated, logical and planned capacity for information collection and decision-making, both outward and inward, with policies reversed overnight, will keep circling on the road of evolution.

But a central nervous center does not mean the kind of structure in which one node decides everything and one failure paralyzes the whole body; it means a system in which many nodes integrate dynamically, obtain accurate external information, and produce unified, stable decisions and behavior. The brain is highly modular, layered, with hubs, but it has no irreplaceable apex, influence shifts with the task and the evidence, and the failure of any unit can be compensated. Nor does unity and stability toward the outside mean uniformity inside: the unity of a person's outward behavior does nothing to diminish the diversity and complexity within the body.

### Will weight turn back into privilege

Some worry that dynamic weights will be captured by historical advantage and by numbers of proxies, and recentralize. These two worries correspond to two explicit rules.

Historical advantage: weight attaches to concrete problems, adjusts with every feedback from results, and expires. When a problem disappears, its weights go to zero; when a new problem appears, all nodes start over from an equal initial weight. A person's past record of being right gets him heard more on problems of the same kind, but it cannot, like wealth, detach itself from the problem, circulate on its own and roll up into compound interest.

Numbers of proxies: each person has exactly one root agent, whose identity is bound to that person's data sovereignty. A person who manufactures ten thousand proxies gets not ten thousand shares of weight but ten thousand copies of an opinion from one source, visible at a glance in the record. What really has to be guarded against is malicious sabotage and tampering: forged identities, proxy farming, altered records. Here we can learn the method of the cryptocurrency ledgers: write the security condition as a quantity that can be computed, not as trust in certain people. The concrete conditions have to be written out separately for this network's own protocol and for each of its own classes of attack.

Two things must also be kept apart: equality of basic rights and equality of influence. The equality the new system wants is equality of basic rights: equality of data sovereignty, of distribution according to need, and of the right to exit. Influence, by contrast, is dynamic, contestable, replaceable. However high a person's weight in one field, he cannot exchange it for weight in another field, cannot exchange it for the first kind of resource, and still less can he exchange it for control over other people's basic rights. How weight is produced, when it expires, how it is corrected, who can modify the algorithm, all of these are themselves common knowledge, all have a version record, all are decided by the same system of dynamic weights. The power to make the rules is thus locked in the cage.

### Questions and Answers

**Q: This mechanism requires full information and rational participants, and those conditions do not exist in reality.**

A: They are descriptions of an ideal state, not preconditions for engineering. The goal most likely can never be fully reached, but it can be approached by step-by-step iteration. As long as the new mechanism is even slightly stronger than the old one, that is enough for the replacement to begin, just as an object always moves toward lower potential energy. As for rationality: once AI is universal, everyone has his own AI agent to supplement his rational knowledge and decision-making ability, the great majority of intellectual activity is carried out through AI agents, and with that buffer in place, emotional behavior will also fall sharply.

**Q: The commitment problem comes back in impersonal form. A node whose weight rose because it advocated a policy also has something to protect; a policy that has run for years has downstream dependencies, and reversal is expensive all the same.**

A: So the real variable is not who the decision is bound to, but which arrives first for the party holding decision power, the cost of persisting or the cost of reversing. In a pyramid the cost of reversing falls on the leader and the cost of persisting falls on the population, and so it persists. Rules four and five of Chapter 4 were written for this very problem: every plan that accepts a temporary loss for long-term benefit must, on adoption, carry an estimated outcome and a monitoring process, and stop on failure; every loss is compensated at once and the strategy revised at once. Weight attaches to the problem and adjusts with results, so persisting in a plan already proven wrong costs the persister his own weight directly. The switching cost of infrastructure is real, but it is an engineering cost: it can be measured and shared, unlike face, which can only be settled by one side admitting defeat.

**Q: Where does legitimacy come from? However imperfect one person one vote is, people accept it. With weighting by ability, why would the losing side accept the result?**

A: What the losing side accepts is not some person's judgment but a rule in which it is itself included, in which its own judgment is itself counted, and which can be checked afterward. Under one person one vote, the losing side accepts the result because it knows its vote will still count next time; under dynamic weights, it accepts because it knows this result will be recorded and verified, and that if the outcome proves it right, its weight will rise, its loss will be compensated, and the plan will be revised. What the former promises is "try again next time"; what the latter promises is "if it's wrong, it gets fixed."
