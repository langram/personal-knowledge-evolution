## Chapter 2: Relativity: From Physical Reference Frames to Cognitive Reference Frames

### Reference frames from physics to cognition

A reference frame is first of all a mathematical concept: a set of coordinate axes, an origin, a metric. Physics tells us that all reference frames are relative, and no reference frame is entitled to call itself absolutely at rest. Galilean relativity says the laws of mechanics are the same in all inertial frames; Einstein extended it to all physical laws and pointed out that simultaneity itself depends on the reference frame. The principle of relativity is a symmetry: the form of the laws stays unchanged under a transformation of reference frame. Symmetry is not decoration in physics: Noether proved that every continuous symmetry corresponds to a conserved quantity.

This concept can be extended all the way down the line. In chemistry, the same reaction has a different equilibrium position at different temperatures and pressures. In biology, the environment perceived by every individual is relative to its own receptors and metabolic state: the bat's world is a world of echoes, the dog's world a world of smells. In these fields relativity holds just the same: the result of observation depends on the state of the observer, but between different observers there exist laws of mutual conversion.

So why is it that when we reach the social domain, especially the domains of morality, politics and ideology, everyone becomes self-centered, takes their own cognitive coordinate system as the standard of absolute correctness, and treats everything inconsistent with themselves as wrong?

Because taking oneself as the origin is a basic property of any stable structure: every fixed point described in the previous chapter relates to the outside world starting from itself. The problem is not that people have their own coordinates, but that they treat their own coordinate origin as the center of the universe. Today, large AI language models have for the first time made human cognition into a measurable high-dimensional semantic space. Each person's cognition is a position and a direction in this space; language and action based on this cognitive space are like motion in physical spacetime. Each person's reference frame is different, and this is why there is trust and estrangement, cooperation and betrayal between people.

### A social science version of the principle of relativity

So, after minimization and maximization and fixed points, the principles part of this book adds one more: the principle of relativity.

Among all human worldviews, epistemologies, political leanings, moral conceptions and cognitive systems, the differences in position, inclination and motion along every cognitive and behavioral dimension of semantic space are relative. There is no absolute right and wrong, no absolute good and bad. When one person thinks another is "wrong", it is like a passenger on one train thinking that another train is moving backward: he is describing the relative motion between two reference frames, not an absolute property of either side.

This principle does not abolish facts. The relative velocity of two trains is an objective, measurable quantity, and so is the difference between two people's cognitive coordinates. What it abolishes is the entitlement to treat one's own coordinate origin as the center of the universe. The goal of social science is to find the laws of conversion between different cognitive reference frames, just as the Lorentz transformation connects the measurements of different inertial frames. The reason today's various social theories are incompatible with one another is precisely that each sits on its own train and this transformation does not yet exist.

Symmetry also has a direct counterpart in the social domain: equality. A rule is equal for two people, A and B, only if its content stays unchanged when the two of them are swapped within it. Symmetry breaking, in turn, is the starting point of division of labor, differentiation and hierarchy. Chapter 5 will turn this into equality in the sense of the rules.

### Refusing mystification, sanctification and demonization

In poetry and fiction you may emotionalize, sanctify and demonize the objects of nature: the gentle wind, the holy light, the demonic darkness. But such expressions must be kept out of theoretical research and technical practice in natural science. The objects of human society need to be treated the same way. Every cognitive and behavioral phenomenon at the social level of humanity is, like a natural phenomenon, an objective phenomenon that can be observed, studied, quantified, recorded, analyzed and modeled.

This leads to something I call meta-cognition, which is the psychological premise for everything argued in the rest of this book:

All the differences and conflicts among humans, between individual and individual, individual and group, group and group, in appearance, physiology, personality, cognition, language, behavior and needs, these differences at the higher level, are structural differences at various lower levels that have gradually formed over the long course of evolution and in the game of human society. Every one of these differences can be given a reproducible causal-chain explanation built from scientific theory. People should not be driven by emotions such as the fear produced by ignorance and the anger produced by confrontation. The so-called fierce binary oppositions of angels and demons, justice and evil, are in fact nothing more than extreme irrational emotions, in the manner of moral and religious judgment, produced when the forward and reverse chain reactions of the tit-for-tat strategy in a long social game catalyze and amplify them.

By "lower-order structural differences" I do not mean attributing a higher-order conflict to some single clean lower-order source. People often say that red is joyful and blue is melancholy, but that does not mean a light wave in some range of wavelengths has the property of joy. A property like violence does not exist at the biological level; it is the product of many factors compounded together, genetic inheritance, living environment and so on. The properties of any lower-order object are not good or bad, only different in character; once we understand the mechanism, we can combine these factors correctly. Reducing plague to pathogens, reducing epilepsy to neuronal discharges: reductions of this kind eliminated a class of narratives that moralized human differences, and they historically reduced actual conflict. What I want to do is extend this to the whole of social conflict.

### The correspondence of concepts

Mapping the concepts of mathematics and basic physics onto the concepts of society is the basic method of this book:

- Vectors and dimensions: the position and direction of an individual's cognition in semantic space; the multiple influencing factors of a social structure.
- Symmetry: equality. Symmetry breaking: the starting point of division of labor, differentiation and hierarchy.
- Relativity: cognitive reference frames.
- Fixed point: stable social structures, Nash equilibrium, institutions.
- Minimization: the lowest-cost way of resolving conflict, Pareto optimality, cutting costs and raising efficiency.
- Maximization: the maximization of relationships and cooperation, freedom, the maximization of interests.

One more pair of concepts deserves an extra sentence: active and passive. Your active behavior causes someone else's passive reaction, and your passive reaction is caused by someone else's active behavior; rights and obligations, freedom and constraint, individual and environment, each pair is two faces of one coupled system. So the individual's objective function is neither defined entirely by the individual nor designed entirely from outside; it is a game process in which the individual and the environment are coupled to each other and which keeps changing as it runs. This point will be made concrete in Chapter 6 when discussing decision weights: at every moment of evolution the individual must retain the actual ability to change the direction of its trajectory, otherwise "coupling" becomes a rhetoric for domination.

### From cognitive reference frames to joint construction

Acknowledging relativity does not mean nothing can be said. Acknowledging that two trains each have their own reference frame does not stop us from calculating precisely when they will collide. Acknowledging that each person's cognitive coordinates have their own conditions of formation does not stop us from jointly verifying facts and jointly setting rules of cooperation. Quite the opposite: only once relativity is acknowledged can the argument move from "whose coordinates are absolute" to "how do our coordinates convert into one another", and only then can moral judgments be reduced to facts that can be discussed and rules that can be revised. What the next two chapters do is find, on this foundation, the basic operator that constructs society.

### Questions and Answers

**Q: Does saying "there is no absolute right or wrong, good or bad" abolish responsibility?**

A: No. The principle of relativity abolishes the entitlement to treat one's personal coordinates as absolute coordinates; it does not abolish facts, still less harm. That a person considers something justified within his own reference frame does not change the loss that thing causes in another reference frame. Chapter 4 will show that under a verifiable, traceable framework of common knowledge, the assignment of responsibility is actually more precise than under a framework of moral judgment: what gets recorded is this act, this decision, this loss, rather than a label of "evil" stuck on a person.

**Q: Social systems have a peculiarity: the participants can understand the laws of the system and change their behavior accordingly, and a law changes as soon as it is known. This is unlike physics.**

A: It is indeed unlike physics, but it is not an obstacle; it is the content. Reflexivity is itself a feedback loop, and feedback loops are exactly what can be incorporated into a dynamical description. The social science this book sets out to build is not "objective laws independent of the participants' cognition" but "laws that still hold after everyone knows them". The equilibria that game theory studies are laws of exactly this kind: the definition of a Nash equilibrium is that everyone knows and still no one changes. Minimization and maximization, fixed points, and relativity all belong to this kind.
