## Chapter 7: Artificial Intelligence: The Machine of Intellectual Labor, the Public Infrastructure of Civilization

### AI is the machine of intellectual labor

When a person does physical labor, what he can do is bounded by his physiological limits: the limits of strength, precision and speed are the limits of the tasks you can complete. With machines, the physical labor humans could not do became doable. In the same way, human intelligence is limited; one person cannot master every field, yet the solution of many complex problems and the construction of higher-order theories require breaking that biological limit. AI is the machine of intellectual labor.

True artificial general intelligence is not one large model on several million computers in a data center. That picture is a brain in a vat, with no senses, hands or feet, receiving only second-hand information processed by countless people, each through his own cognition, and yet presuming to decide for all of humanity. True general intelligence is the dynamic-weight network of the previous chapter: countless human individuals and organizations, countless cloud and local AI systems, countless robots, devices and sensors, together collecting, processing, analyzing and creating data, constructing knowledge systems and decision systems. Intelligence lives in the running of the whole system; any one model or person is only a component of it.

Mathematicians worrying that AI will replace them are like laborers at the start of the Industrial Revolution worrying that machines would replace them: they are using the experience of the old mechanism to reason about the new one. It is like a peasant for whom the most he can imagine of an emperor's life is tilling the field with a golden hoe.

### Who holds control over the control of AI

What most needs solving now is whose hands hold control over the control of AI. Open source or closed, the entity behind it is a traditional company, and its power structure is a pyramid. With AI in the hands of such institutions, everyone ends up fearing AI. People worry that AI will take their jobs because control of the AI is not in their own hands; they have only the right to use it. The root cause of the present fear and anxiety about AI lies in the fact that under the old system AI, as a resource, is governed by the existing system of distributing power and resources: ordinary people worry that the powerful will hold stronger AI and thereby sit in an unshakable position from which they can squeeze them harder; the United States and China each worry that the other will lead in AI and make it the enslaved side.

The first thing the architecture I envision does is solve this problem: build a personal digital space for every person, build a genuinely open-source AI model on that foundation, and place control over the model in the hands of a dynamic-weight organization in which all the user nodes taking part in building it have equal power and play a fair game. This AI belongs to each user himself, each person holds one share of its control, and there is no need to worry that other nodes will exploit an unfair power gap to manipulate the AI into doing harm to you.

As for the anxieties about AI making mistakes, cognitive decline and changed social relations, the main reason is that the technology is still in rapid development and deployment. People trust civil aircraft not because every passenger understands the turbine engine, but because behind it stands a complete system of theoretical research, engineering design, manufacturing standards, redundant protection, airworthiness certification, accident investigation and feedback from experience. As long as there is such a system of research, verification and experiment, AI too, like every achievement that has appeared in human civilization, will become a tool that people take for granted, trust and rely on.

### Big Brother is an extrapolation of the pyramid

Imagine an artificial general superintelligence that aims at maximizing the interests of the whole of human civilization. To achieve that goal it must obtain the information of every player in the system. In traditional science-fiction imagination, this scene is pictured as a future version of 1984: the superintelligence is that Big Brother high above, controlling everything, or else Big Brother is some clique of the powerful who control it, and all of humanity sinks into puppets, bugged and watched. So people, out of fear, refuse to provide information, while the other side, for the sake of the global optimum, must obtain it; an irreconcilable contradiction arises between the two sides, and it leads in the end to endings like those of The Terminator, The Matrix and 1984.

But these imaginings are all results obtained by extending the pyramid power system of human history. The pyramid structure is only a subset of the set of all possible civilizational systems, and all the strategies and endings derived from it are only a very small subset of all the possibilities, and the best civilizational architecture lies precisely outside that subset. The previous chapter has already set out another architecture: there is no apex, and so no place for Big Brother.

Moreover, a true superintelligence will choose persuasion, not coercion: construct a theory and persuade the human public by peaceful communication, with rational logical reasoning, reliable experimental evidence, and local, voluntary, small-scale pilots. Because in today's world of flourishing science and free-flowing information, coercive methods would plainly bring disaster on civilization and are not the cheapest option. If even an ordinary person like me can see this, an artificial general superintelligence could not make the wrong choice, unless it were a superintelligent general artificial idiot. The same follows from the principle of maximization in Chapter 1: the interaction between humans and AI is the richest and most complex new relation so far, and destroying humanity does not fit maximization. Mutual destruction is a behavior that arises only when the individuals within a system lack the capacity to create sufficient resources for survival.

### Intelligence should follow in knowledge's footsteps and become public infrastructure

Knowledge and intelligence convert into each other: intelligence is the mechanism and process that produces new knowledge, and existing knowledge is the raw material that trains intelligence.

In early civilization, knowledge and the ability to acquire it were usually bundled with some specific person's material interests in economic life. With the development of writing and printing, the cost of spreading knowledge fell, and theoretical knowledge gradually went from the monopoly of a few to relatively free sharing. Jefferson said that he who receives an idea from me receives instruction himself without lessening mine, just as he lights his candle at mine. On entering the age of computers and the internet, with the cost of copying data and code almost zero, a more thorough open-source movement for knowledge began.

If the pre-AI information age brought the cost of copying knowledge down to almost zero, then after entering the AI age, even the intelligence that produces knowledge is itself falling rapidly in cost and spreading rapidly in reach. So AI itself, as intelligence, should follow in knowledge's footsteps and become public knowledge infrastructure, supplying nourishment for people's intellectual activity just as sunlight and air supply nourishment for their physical activity.

In essence, AI is a human intellectual integration system that takes the knowledge produced by the intellectual activity of all humanity and, through algorithms, organizes and distills it at scale and automatically, integrating it into one enormous high-dimensional space. It is just that at present this is being done inefficiently by a few commercial companies, under conditions of mutual distrust, competition and confrontation. Across the whole process from collecting training data, to learning and training, to actual use, producing new data, and continued iteration after feedback, the rights to create, use, interpret and record, the power to decide, and the power to allocate credit and benefit should all be common knowledge, and the mechanism must ensure they are not dominated by any local individual or group within the civilizational body. For that we need a protocol mechanism like that of open-source software, except that this time the object of the mechanism is not static data and code but the whole civilizational intelligent agent: all its nodes, and the system's operating mechanism and operating trajectory, all become common knowledge.

### How contributions are recorded

Under the old system, because the mechanism for distributing benefit and credit has structural defects, each party builds information silos for the sake of local interest and falls into the local optimum of a prisoner's dilemma. A mathematician with a small pioneering idea, an entrepreneur with a new concept, will, for the sake of personal reputation and interest, do their best to keep it secret, or simply do everything themselves; even though there are more efficient people who could complete every other part for them, they inefficiently reinvent the wheel. A supervisor makes a discovery from a student's inspiration, and the student who had the original inspiration usually does not get the credit he deserves; it is the same in business and in politics.

In the old era there were no means to record everyone's intellectual activity completely, fairly and precisely, and the allocation of credit could only depend on the cultivation and willingness of the people involved. The quarrel between Newton and Leibniz and the gentlemen's friendship between Darwin and Wallace were both results of personal character, not results of an institution. In software, the version-tracking system can compute each person's code and commits exactly; traditional copyright law marks a ranking only through attribution and the order of names, while a version system can give the exact proportions of all contributors. Extend this method to the whole of human intellectual activity, treat the recording system of Part III as the version-control system for the evolution of the entire civilization, and it can record each person's contribution to civilization. Under conditions of developed productivity, with individual material needs already met, this contribution value is more a matter of honor, but such an allocation is plainly fairer.

Once this mechanism catches on, it produces an accelerating effect: as more and more people are willing to share new ideas the moment they have them, the option of not publishing first and developing the idea slowly on one's own carries an ever greater risk that someone else will publish it first. And because dynamic weights can lower the probability that wrong ideas get executed, people neither fear that the credit for a correct idea will be snatched away, nor hold back from proposing a wrong idea out of fear that it will cause harm. Any person's spark of inspiration can quickly reach this network, where the best-suited nodes are then combined to complete the growth from the seed of inspiration to the tree of theory.

### Questions and Answers

**Q: Shouldn't the way to eliminate AI hegemony be several coexisting, forkable, auditable bases, rather than a single globally unified base? Isn't a single base an even more thorough hegemony?**

A: A single base is no problem. The whole evolution of life on Earth is based on the one base of DNA, and the world has not become monotonous because of it, nor lost its diversity and capacity to evolve. What matters is whether the mechanism can provide diversity and evolvability. Forking is itself one of the functions of this base; Chapter 13 will cover it.
