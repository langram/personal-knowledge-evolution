## Postscript: Let Everyone Become a Creator

This book set out from an abstract mathematical concept, the dualoid, and tried to give a unified explanation of the evolution of the universe, the composition of matter, the origin of consciousness, the structure of society and the future of human civilization. From the fixed point and minimization-maximization, to tit for tat and the two chain reactions, from the two kinds of resources to the dynamic-weight neural network, from the hash language to the software seed, from personal data sovereignty to the alignment of civilization, and on to the path that begins with one person's cloud storage, this is a continuous evolutionary path from the Big Bang to the singularity of civilization. Every step of the path follows the same underlying law: a process driven by the basic properties of the dualoid, maximizing stable structure through hierarchical emergence. This process is also a process of ever-increasing information, the process of the universe coming to know itself and realize itself.

This is an extremely ambitious attempt, and many of its views may seem radical or even absurd. But as the preface said, when the existing theoretical system has fallen into the trap of a local optimum and cannot resolve its fundamental contradictions, we need a disruptive, counterintuitive sacrifice strategy. What this book proposes is exactly such a sacrifice: give up the binary opposition of object and morphism, give up the traditional practice of excluding paradox from the system, give up the pyramid, give up the computer centered on human natural language, give up the old notion of privacy as defense, and instead embrace a more fundamental single entity that unifies existence and change and contains paradox as the engine of evolution, together with a set of rules built on it that make everyone equal peers.

The evolution of human civilization's institutions is in fact a local instance, on this small planet Earth, of the evolution of the whole universe, and it too follows the emergence mechanism of fixed point plus minimization. The mechanism I envision is nothing more than a civilizational mechanism with higher emergent efficiency.

Let me state the position of this book once more. I have not discovered and defined the specific rules and structures of a new civilizational order. What this book gives is a foundation of mathematical principles, strategies for the meta-game and a plan for getting started: a compass, a telescope and a chart, not the destination; a torch held up to scout the road toward the singularity of civilization, so that the road may be traveled a little more smoothly and a little faster, and the ever-widening gap in speed between institutions and technology does not bring disaster on human civilization.

And the direction it points in is the "bitter lesson" at the level of the evolution of civilization's institutions. In 2019 Sutton looked back over seventy years of AI and drew a conclusion many researchers found hard to swallow: hand-writing the knowledge and experience of human experts into a system always works in the short run, and always loses in the long run to general methods plus enormous data and computing power. Chess, Go, speech, vision, language, without exception; in protein folding, methods built on decades of accumulated expert experience were overtaken by one model at a stroke. The social domain has long shown the same signs: more than half a century of research has shown again and again that simple statistical models match or beat the personal judgment of experts in prediction, and that a group weighted by its track record beats the individual authority. Yet humanity still hands its most complex problem, the construction and organization of society, to the personal experience and learning of political, economic and academic authorities, to be solved through simple cooperation. That is how the traditional edifice of science was built, brick by brick, by geniuses and authorities. I do not deny its achievements: with the experience and knowledge of individuals, humanity built today's system of science and its political and economic institutions; today's large language models were trained on exactly what those geniuses and authorities wrote; and in the Large Civilization Model they are not abolished either, but become nodes carrying weights. But to reach the singularity of technology and civilization, to make a leap to a higher level, the best road is the former: use mathematics and computers to model mathematically the complex problems of social construction and organization that all of humanity faces, gather data on an enormous scale, and, in the loop of learning, training and inference in practice, construct that Large Civilization Model. This is the bitter lesson of civilization's institutions. Humanity will have to swallow it sooner or later, and the sooner the better.

So the most important thing is for all of humanity to reach consensus on this direction as soon as possible and begin trying together. Where consensus comes from, AI's own history has already answered. No one first coordinated all of humanity into a consensus on deep learning: in 2012 one small group won one competition with one model, and the whole world followed. First a working instance, then consensus. And one more thing: when language models took off, the corpus was already lying on the internet; but the corpus the Large Civilization Model needs, every problem, every node's judgment, every collective decision, every real-world result, has never yet been systematically recorded. The dataset comes before the model. So the two most urgent things today are to build the first instance that runs as a closed loop, and to begin accumulating the first batch of records. Neither needs anyone's permission, and one person's knowledge base is its first seed.

This theoretical framework is far from complete, let alone rigorously formalized and proven. It is a torch, a starting point that invites readers to think together. Building humanity's ultimate theory is a grand undertaking that needs the formal construction of mathematicians, the experimental verification of physicists, the simulated implementation of computer scientists, and the humane concern of philosophers and sociologists. And in the system I envision, these people need not belong to any particular profession. There, everyone is a student and also a teacher; a consumer and also a producer; one of the managed and also a manager; a neuron of this civilization and also its author.

Let everyone become a creator. This is not a slogan. It is what happens naturally once this mechanism is up and running.

Starting with me, one person.
