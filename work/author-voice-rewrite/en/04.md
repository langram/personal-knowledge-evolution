## Chapter 4: The Subobject Classifier: Consciousness, Information and Intelligence

### A truth judge built into a category

One extremely important structure that emerged in the course of the universe's evolution is the subobject classifier.

In mathematics there is a special kind of category, the topos, which has very strong constructibility and an unusually rich internal logical structure, so rich that it can serve as an alternative foundation to set theory. The core reason for its power is that it possesses a subobject classifier. A subobject classifier can be imagined as a universal truth judge inside a category: for any subobject in the category, which can be understood as a property or a subset, the classifier gives a unique morphism pointing to a special truth object Ω, and Ω internally contains the abstract structure of true and false. In essence, the existence of a subobject classifier means that the category possesses a built-in capacity for logical reasoning. The "proposition" of mathematical logic and the "type" of computer science both exist on exactly this precondition.

### Maxwell's demon is a living classifier

Computer science and mathematical logic are abstractions of human logical thinking, products of consciousness. And the consciousness of intelligent life originates in the nervous responses of animals, which can be traced further back to the irritability of cells. This resembles the situation in the thought experiment of Maxwell's demon: the little demon is a living subobject classifier. It observes the speed of a molecule, a property, and then, according to fast or slow, true or false, performs the operation of opening or closing the door. Through this information processing, in a system tending toward equilibrium, it uses the flow of energy to create a dissipative structure far from equilibrium, the temperature difference between the two sides.

Of course, Maxwell's demon is not the same as a subobject classifier. To carry out a measurement it obviously must have an abstract structure like a classifier inside it, but it also has the functions of responding to the result of detection, changing its own state and gating. This whole structure is more complex than a subobject classifier; in the language of category theory, it further requires the composability of morphisms, limits and colimits, and a stability like that of a unit object, in order to describe irritability and complex response capacity.

We can say: as soon as a subobject classifier emerges in a category, that category can become an advanced category with the characteristics of a dissipative system. On this basis, ever more complex dissipative systems keep evolving, from the simple irritability of cells, to the nervous responses of complex animals, to human consciousness and logical thinking, all of which are emergences of higher functions. So the mathematical principle and root of the philosophical concept of "consciousness" lie in the existence of the subobject classifier. If the basic properties of the dualoid are the universal property of all material existence, then the existence and properties of the subobject classifier are the universal property of all dissipative structures, from galaxies to living organisms, and the precondition for the emergence of consciousness.

### The truth object is the representative of the lower-order category

The structure of the subobject classifier has several roles that must be kept apart first: the terminal object 1; the truth-value object Ω; the morphism true, which goes from 1 to Ω and picks out the value "true"; and, for each subobject, its characteristic morphism, which sends the members of the subobject to true and everything else elsewhere. My hypothesis concerns what this structure does as a whole. If the category in which the subobject classifier lives is regarded as the higher-order category, then the "true" picked out from the terminal object 1 through true is the representative, inside the higher-order category, of the terminal object of a lower-order category. It indicates that the lower-order category has functional completeness and the unity of its own existence; it indicates that it can participate as a subobject in the construction of the higher-order category, and it provides the function of verifying and detecting the identity of that subobject.

Take a human being as an example. Advanced human consciousness, as the higher-order category of consciousness, is the composite action of bioelectric signals produced by the biological irritability of countless neurons in the brain; it is constructed from countless lower-order neuron categories as subobjects, through the morphisms of neuronal connections. The bioelectric irritability of neurons is just a special form of the irritability of all the cells in the human body, a subcategory of the category that is the human organism; other organs and tissues form other subcategories; and the complex life processes among these subcategories correspond to their morphisms. The external stimuli felt by the irritability of lower-order cells in other organs, and the irritability of the brain's neurons, feed into and out of each other, constituting a higher-order composite processing function, and accomplishing the whole of conscious human perception and response.

From the abstract standpoint of category theory, from consciousness to the body, to cells, to organic molecules, the objects at every level can be seen as higher-order dualoids possessing a subobject classifier. Once a lower-order category with existence, changeability and constructibility participates as a subobject in the construction of a higher-order category, it can itself serve as the key judging element in the higher-order category's subobject classifier, providing a detection function for the higher-order category's interaction with other categories. Once there is a detection function, the construction process is no longer random; it can proceed in an organized and efficient way. This is why lower-order categories construct higher-order categories, and why higher-order categories are constructed faster than lower-order ones.

### Distinguishing self from outside

Among all detection functions, there is one that is the most primitive, the most basic and the most special, and it derives from one special classification of the subobject classifier: distinguishing oneself from the external environment. This is the precondition for an object to participate independently in the construction of a higher-order category. Before it has a subobject classifier, a structure merely exists passively; once it has this capacity, it becomes an observer, able to divide the world into "me" and "not me". This function is the basic mechanism that distinguishes all dissipative structures from other structures and gives rise to interaction and competition; it is also the root of the concept of "I" in human consciousness, the origin of self-awareness and of the construction of the entire system of knowledge.

Here there is a deeper question. People often ask: is there an absolute fact independent of any observer? The sentence itself presupposes two things: that the observer exists as an object; and that the fact, as an object, is observable. But if the observer is presupposed, is the observer not itself an existing fact? Apply self-reference: perhaps the first fact is the observer itself, and the first fact the observer perceives is its own existence. Starting from the theory of fixed points, any fact is itself at the same time a reflexive observer, a fixed point. Our universe is a dynamically evolving system containing the relative concepts of continuous and discrete, object and morphism, symmetric and asymmetric, and the nodes of this system's evolution are these facts, observers, fixed points. At such a node, examined from different angles, it exhibits emergence, reducibility, discreteness, continuity, symmetry and asymmetry; it has both the static objectivity of a thing and the dynamic observing and responding of a subject, able to detect and preserve information. The smallest unit of information is 0 and 1, yes and no, like a category with the simplest subobject classifier. Seen from the direction of emergence, this fixed point is formed from lower-order objects in a lower-order category through the transformation of continuous morphisms, except that at the instant of emergence a discrete leap occurs, like a quantum fluctuation, with discrete definiteness and continuous changeability emerging out of continuous chaos. This is the wave-particle duality of elementary particles.

### What intelligence is

One thing I have kept trying to understand is the definition of intelligence. Human or AI, what exactly is the intelligence that an intelligent agent possesses? Why, in the dynamic evolution of learning and reasoning, does the mathematical model of intelligence involve so many vector and matrix operations?

The earliest intelligent activity of human civilization was the invention of written language and the first arithmetic. Even in written language, the essence of its objects is a kind of vector: in an alphabetic script, the initial consonants and the finals are like the x axis and y axis of a phonetic plane, and each syllable is a point on that plane. Some say the essence of intelligence is abstraction and compression. Whether it is an alphabetic script or coordinates on a mathematical plane, this modeling with a biproduct system of products and coproducts seems to be the model with the highest compression ratio, the model of minimal abstraction. The essence of intelligence is to decompose a complex structure in some higher-order category of a high-dimensional space of complex objects into a vector composed of coordinates along the component axes of different lower-order categories, and then, through the operations of product and coproduct, to realize pullback and pushout, reduction and emergence, induction and inference, learning and practice.

In a sense, intelligence is the ability to find this optimal abstraction and compression. This process of compression, while approaching the fixed point along each component, minimizes the information needed to represent those fixed points, and since storing information requires energy, this is the energy minimization of a reductive process; at the same time, the coproduct combinations among the different components are maximized, which maximizes the energy when all kinds of combinations emerge. Perhaps this is why fixed points, minimization and maximization are the first principles of the universe's evolution, and why the algorithms of large AI models contain so many vector and matrix operations.

Seen from this angle, the universe we live in is one enormous dynamically evolving system, and people try to find its basic law: examined philosophically, it is the relationship between existence and change; seen from category theory, it is objects and morphisms; seen from physics, it is matter and energy, particles and waves; seen from computer science, it is data objects and program functions; seen from the whole of human civilization, it is knowledge and intelligence. Each pair is two-in-one, mutually convertible. Intelligence is the mechanism, capacity and process that produces new knowledge; existing knowledge is the raw material for training the capacity of intelligence; the two convert into each other and promote each other.

### Self-drive: what AI lacks

The difference between a tool and a person is whether it has initiative. This initiative is a kind of self-drive, like the self-iteration of code: the code is the static expression of this iterative function, not its dynamic execution. The biggest problem with current large AI models is that they have no originating mechanism of self-drive. Take my chats with an AI: the one who initiates the conversation is always me; it only responds passively, like computers, phones, cars and every other tool.

The context of a human-machine conversation has one copy in its memory and one copy in mine. The difference is that I also have another context: my conversation with myself. It contains the static knowledge I got from reading books, like the static knowledge in its model library, but it also contains what I extracted from my chats with it that caught my attention, and what caught my attention in my memories of other people, of films and books, and of everything acquired through the senses. This self-attention mechanism keeps adding whatever interests me to a self-dialogue context of my own, or call it a self-reflection context, keeps talking inside that context, and adjusts and compresses it according to newly ingested material. Perhaps this is the soul in human self-awareness.

The example I am giving is itself an instance of this: after chatting with the AI, I extracted the point of interest "AI can only be a tool in research", put it into my own context, and posed a challenge to myself: why can AI only be a passive tool, and what can be done about it; then another self inside the self-context proposed the idea above, and then I actively raised it in my conversation with the AI. And this process will never happen on its side: it will not put what we talked about into its own context and actively interact with the self in there.

From the existing evidence, the capability of a large AI model is directly related to the size of its context space, the larger the context the greater the capability, and today all contexts exist locally, at the point of interaction with the outside world. On the one hand, quantitative change produces qualitative change; on the other hand, the dynamic reflection of self-dialogue is itself a self-referential fixed point in the category-theoretic sense, which, according to Chapter 1, is a point of emergence. If a future AI model could maintain such a global self-dialogue context on its own and periodically feed it back to itself for self-training, I believe the result would be better than the current way of training. ### How humans handle the unknown

How does human natural language handle unknown things? First, name an object or process that can be reproduced repeatedly; then probe it step by step, using various other objects to interact with it, detecting its relations with external things, just as in the Yoneda lemma, guessing its internal structure by establishing its relations with other known things; then, according to the guess, adjust it through the known external relations and see whether the adjusted object produces the guessed interactions with other objects; if it succeeds, provisionally define the internal structure as guessed, and if it fails, try something else; iterate this way until every adjustment yields the expected result, at which point we can claim to know the object's internal structure and external relations. First a name, then gradually learning what it is. This is the basic cycle of cognition, and it should become a mechanism in the underlying architecture of AI.
