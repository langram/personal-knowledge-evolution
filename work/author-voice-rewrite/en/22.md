## Chapter 22: Science, Education and Continuous Creation

### Everyone is a student, and also a teacher

The new system I envision is not one where people hand their happiness and their hopes to a Big Brother high above, just as the cells of the human body do not pin all their hopes on one super-cell. At the political level, everyone is a manager and also one of the managed; at the economic level, everyone is a producer and also a consumer; at the level of knowledge and education, everyone is a student and also a teacher. What governs everyone in the civilizational body is the intelligent knowledge produced by the civilizational brain that everyone forms as one of its neurons.

Under this system, education is no longer a pyramid that passes knowledge down from authorities to students, but a network in which everyone both absorbs and contributes. One person's depth in some field becomes, through the hot path, a capability that others can use directly; one person's question in some field becomes the starting point of someone else's deep dive. The creation of knowledge and the spread of knowledge are two faces of one process.

### Is mathematics discovered, or created

Is mathematics really only a science of discovery? Physics and chemistry can use principles to invent things that did not exist before, while mathematics is generally regarded as the discovery of the abstract essence of the universe: these laws existed before humans did, and humans merely rediscover them. Is that really so?

Before the great development of abstract algebra, this claim could still stand, because essentially every mathematical law discovered was a law of the existing physical world. But after that, many structures, laws and concepts built in mathematics have no corresponding existence in reality; on the contrary, many mathematical structures were applied to the real physical world, and things whose probability of appearing through the random evolution of the physical world approaches zero, so that they could be regarded as simply impossible, were created by humans using advanced mathematical structures to reshape the structure of matter. This process, in which something new emerges from an indeterminate chaotic state, is invention, and the mathematical structure on which the invention rests can also be regarded as invented. Binary Boolean algebra and the entire hardware and software system of electronic computers built on it, along with the whole ecology of the information society; the quantum computer and its ecology, built on probability theory and Hilbert spaces. Tracing the evolutionary history of the universe backward, we can even say that before the DNA inheritance mechanism appeared, the mathematical structure on which life rests did not exist in the universe at all; before neural networks appeared, the mathematical structure on which thought, consciousness and intelligence rest did not exist at all; going further back, before high-dimensional compact structures like the Calabi-Yau manifold appeared, the mathematical structure behind the spacetime properties on which the whole universe rests did not exist at all. And if humans one day discover a brand-new high-dimensional compact structure that can give rise to new macroscopic spacetime properties, we could even create a universe of entirely new properties on the basis of that new structure.

This is what Chapter 1 said: mathematics is an evolving system that keeps giving rise to higher-order structures, not a static object of discovery. Every new fixed point is both a discovery and a creation.

### Bold hypotheses, rigorous reasoning

I once said to the AI I was discussing with: unless the most basic principles of physics and mathematics are violated, do not use current or outdated frameworks and theories of social science to evaluate the infinite possibilities of future social science and institutional rules. Because this body of theory, seen from its current state, is still in its pre-Newtonian period, and the different theories look more like expressions within their own frames of reference. I have found that AI, as an intelligent agent, tends to reason within the fixed frameworks already in its knowledge base. It tends toward marking the boat to find the sword, like the man who notched his boat to mark where his sword fell overboard, rather than flexibly and dynamically daring to question the existing frameworks, using its head, opening its imagination, making bold hypotheses and reasoning rigorously, and looking at questions about the future in a more open and flexible way.

An example. Someone used Parfit's reasoning about personal identity in Reasons and Persons to object to my idea that "sacrifice is nothing more than deleting a copy." But that reasoning presupposes that the different copied individuals are separate individuals developing independently; whereas in the future I envision, these copies are a whole interconnected through brain-computer interfaces, one complete conscious object, which merely has embodiments existing simultaneously in different physical locations. Whatever any one of them perceives and learns in some environment is immediately possessed by all the others. Destroying any one physical body is, for this intelligent agent, like having a regenerable tentacle cut off: another can be regrown at any time. And the process and meaning of such a sacrifice would also be preserved permanently as a unique life experience of this intelligent agent.

Of course, after the bold hypothesis must come rigorous reasoning. Take this premise seriously and push downward using only physics and mathematics, and some real constraints follow. Information does not travel faster than light; embodiments on opposite sides of the Earth are separated by at least tens of milliseconds, and between Earth and Mars by minutes to tens of minutes. "Immediately possessed by all embodiments" holds only for events slower than the synchronization time, so the state divergence between embodiments is a continuous quantity varying with distance and bandwidth, and between one consciousness and many individuals there is a continuous spectrum, not two kinds of thing. An embodiment that loses contact accumulates unique experience, and on reconnection the two forked states must be merged; the Merkle-DAG is exactly the data structure for that merge. The segment of experience that has not yet left the light cone is truly lost, so this intelligent agent will rationally prefer sacrifices that occur after synchronization is complete. This is what I understand by bold hypotheses, rigorous reasoning: accept the premise in full, do not cherry-pick the conclusions. And these few inferences help the idea itself far more than an objection built on a philosophical argument that presupposes different premises.

Good rules have a criterion: they stay invariant under a change of unit of action. The unit can be a body, a consciousness, a joint consciousness, an organization. A rule about individual veto rights, if it still holds when moved up to the level of consciousness, is a rule about any collaborative system rather than an accidental fact about humans. The rules in Part II of this book should all withstand this test.

### How humans and AI research together

This book is itself the product of joint research between a human and several AIs. My way of working is this: on the same question, discuss separately with several AIs, hand each one's answers to the others, and run a round of meta-theoretical brainstorming; on a project, have one AI act as researcher, one as planner, one as coder, one as evaluator, with different models playing different roles according to their strengths. A few lessons came out of this process.

First, how multiple intelligences cooperate, and how they avoid believing the same mistake twice over. Several models may share the same systematic bias, in which case their agreement is not evidence but noise; what is truly valuable is disagreement from independent sources. So I ask the AI for the strongest objection, and have it test my framework by its own standards. The question-and-answer sections at the end of each chapter of this book came from exactly this.

Second, how research begins when there is no verifier. Theoretical innovation has no clear right answer the way problem-solving does, but it has the evaluation function of Chapter 3: a shorter description, a less effortful construction, more external connections. Whether a new concept deserves to be kept depends on whether it makes previously invisible relations visible, whether it creates operable bridges between previously independent fields, and whether it lets subsequent work do, in the new language, things that could not be done in the old.

Third, how a limited experiment helps correct a grand vision. The nineteen-fold negative result in Chapter 12 directly changed the design of MerkleVM's execution backend: object-level analysis was separated from execution granularity. A grand vision must be tested by phenomena it played no part in designing; and these tests are mostly small, concrete, and capable of failing. So the research record must include failures, disagreements and costs, not only results. This book's knowledge base preserves, from every round of discussion, the AI's objections, my rebuttals, the corrections I accepted and the corrections I rejected, each marked separately. These negative results and disagreements are precisely the kind of data Chapter 14 said the old system cannot produce.

Fourth, the research process itself should become a hot path. I am still at the stage of interacting manually with several AIs. The next step is to automate this process: let the four roles of researcher, planner, coder and evaluator automatically advance the research and construction of the theory and the project; let several machines coordinate different AIs over the network, automatically assigning tasks according to each one's quota, conducting differentiated multi-strategy research, with results shared, branched and merged through the version repository. Once this mechanism works properly and the project's foundation is solid, turn the repository from private to public, have the AI publish and promote it automatically, bring in more people, and extend the project from individual development to community collaboration. When the project matures to the point where it can complete this process by itself, the functions of this automation software become a hot path built into the project, completing the self-iterating evolution of the project itself and of the theory on which it rests. My intuition is that this project itself constructs a self-referential fixed point with emergent properties: a primitive chicken that has not yet fully evolved into general intelligence, with the help of this project, gradually acquires the function of hatching the first egg of general intelligence, and then starts the self-iterating cycle of chicken and egg.

### From research to civilization

The present stage is an early stage of a distributed intelligent social neural network: the AIs doing the development are still traditional large models that imitate the human way of thinking. But the ultimate goal is to hatch a purely silicon-based intelligent social neural network, with an FPM model based on the architectural features and functions of silicon-based computers and thinking in binary as the core of its intelligent brain, together with all kinds of physically embodied sensors and actuators, which together with carbon-based life, from animals of lower intelligence to humans of higher intelligence, forms a diverse, composite, fused civilizational ecology.

Science, education and creation are one and the same thing in this ecology: every exploration by every node becomes a capability the whole network can reuse; every failure becomes the whole network's memory; every person is a student of this process, and its teacher, and its author.
