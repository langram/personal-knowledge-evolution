## Chapter 7: Common Knowledge and the Two Chain Reactions

### What common knowledge is

Human social structures that rest on law, religious codes, political programs, and the moral standards and rules of the game that people have agreed on and jointly observe can become strong, stable and complex; structures that rest on the will of a few or the unwritten rules of a small circle can only be simple, fragile and temporary structures and balances. The difference is that the former are common knowledge: everyone knows, and everyone knows that everyone knows, this cyclically recursive kind of knowledge. Games played on common knowledge can produce results resembling the composability of morphisms in category theory, and so can play out into more complex social structures.

A human organization of any size, from a family at the small end up through villages, cities and nations to the whole global village, is founded on the concrete content of the intersection of consensus its participants can reach. Common knowledge is the basic precondition that shapes human structures. And the tit-for-tat strategy is precisely the most basic piece of common knowledge that all of humanity knows and takes for granted. Under the rules of this common knowledge, trust and cooperation form the positive chain reaction, and distrust running into betrayal and conflict forms the negative chain reaction.

### The negative chain

Driven by this tit-for-tat strategy that all humanity knows and observes together, insufficient or concealed exchange of information leads to mutual suspicion; suspicion further obstructs exchange, making both sides deliberately conceal more information; this plunges both sides into greater suspicion, until it develops into distrust; distrust leads to refusal to communicate and all-out concealment, and from there to intense emotional hostility; then, out of fear of the other side's attack, you start strengthening your own offensive and defensive capacity, and that behavior of yours leads the other side to strengthen theirs; events escalate to resistance and confrontation in action, from local, low-intensity acts of violence step by step upward until the situation is completely out of control, turning the ordinary people inside it from friendly cooperators full of love and reason into impulsive killers full of hate.

This chain is not a theoretical derivation. The automobile brake failures of recent years, Boeing's concealment of the 737 MAX flight control system and the whistleblower crisis that followed, Volkswagen's Dieselgate, all were caused by this. Imagine if all the information from design to testing to production to sales were like version-tracked open-source code in a git repository: this suspicion and distrust could hardly arise. Business is only one class of example; the same thing is found throughout politics, economics and every aspect of social life, in countless cases from antiquity to today, some of them carrying the risk of destroying civilization: from the outbreak of the two world wars, to the US-Soviet Cold War and the Cuban missile crisis, to the confrontation between the United States and China today.

Understand this: the more both sides fear that something will happen, the more likely it is to happen. The more people fear the other side will hurt them, the more they arm themselves; the more they see the other side arming, the more they fear, and the more they arm. The endpoint of this negative chain, whichever possibility you take, is not a global optimum: either the two sides are evenly matched and reach a local equilibrium of mutual deterrence, like the nuclear arms race between the United States and Russia; or one side pulls far ahead and uses its lead to utterly destroy or suppress the other, like the United States dropping atomic bombs on Japan. Either way it is only a local optimum, and either way it carries the risk of losing control and self-destruction. This is also why humanity fears nuclear technology and fears AI.

### The positive chain

Now examine the positive chain of tit-for-tat in reverse: every bit more of information exchanged, every bit less concealed, increases mutual trust; increased trust makes both sides more willing to share information, less willing to conceal deliberately, more willing to cooperate; cooperation further strengthens trust and brings more cooperation. This positive chain finally leads every party to turn all information relevant to promoting cooperation into common knowledge, making the probability of reaching the globally optimal Nash equilibrium larger and larger.

The loss caused by concealment exceeds the concealed fact itself. In an information system that is not common knowledge, not only does insufficient information and deliberate concealment keep the two sides from reaching the optimal game, but the opacity and asymmetry of information keep the cost of mental and material confrontation between cooperating parties stubbornly high. This two-sided, polarizing chain-reaction character of the tit-for-tat strategy leads me to a conclusion: if we want the human social game driven by this basic strategy to reach its optimum, the initial direction of the game matters enormously; it is the single most critical step in whether the chain reaction runs positive or negative.

### The optimal rules of the game

So the optimal rules of the game are to make the following rules common knowledge for all of humanity:

1. Everyone follows the tit-for-tat strategy.
2. Everyone's initial strategy is the positive strategy, that is, to share all information that is needed to reach external cooperation and that will not harm oneself.
3. All shared information is recorded and shared as common knowledge in the form of a Merkle-DAG, while a dynamic-weight decision system guarantees that no party's decision power ever grows large enough to use that information to harm the one who shared it.
4. If, because of the complexity and unpredictability of the system, the execution of the weighted decision system damages the interests of an individual in the system, compensation is made immediately and the game's decision strategy is immediately revised to repair it. All those who suffered loss treat the situation as a loss produced by the system's own capacity limits or by external force majeure, not as the malice of some decision-maker.
5. Where a temporary loss is suffered for the sake of a better long-term result, the decision system supplies an estimated plan and expected outcome, monitors the progress of the game at all times, adjusts the plan as circumstances require in order to succeed, or stops execution when failure is expected. On success, those who suffered loss treat it as a temporary sacrifice for long-term benefit; on failure, they treat it as a loss produced by the system's capacity limits or external force majeure, not as the malice of some decision-maker.

Two points in this rule system matter most. First, the distributed common-knowledge storage system of the Merkle-DAG, and the social intelligence neural network of dynamic-weight decision-making, eliminate at the level of mechanism the possibility of individuals using the power in their hands to harm one another. Second, it builds in people a mindset of rational thought first and suppression of irrational impulse, so that the point "everyone's initial strategy is the positive strategy" has positive initial cognition on both the emotional and the rational side. The meta-cognition of Chapter 5, once it becomes everyone's consensus, gives people the feeling of a cleansed spirit and a sudden enlightenment of thought: a calmer attitude toward the world and toward others, more optimism about the future, more tolerance toward people who think differently. And so "the initial strategy is positive" acquires a double guarantee, emotional and rational.

Meta-cognition is responsible for willingness; verifiable history is responsible for credibility. An initial cooperative attitude that is only declared is what game theory calls cheap talk: the other side cannot tell sincerity from disguise. A portable, checkable record of cooperation makes "I am ready to cooperate" no longer a sentence but a record that others can check. Only the two together make a first step that the other side can credit. So the new system does not need to first convince everyone and first dissolve all hostility before cooperation begins; what it needs is to make the first step a cooperative one, and to let the other side see it.

### The two chains are idealized extremes

It has to be pointed out that these two chains are an idealized simplified model, and in reality they are two extremes that rarely occur: the love and friendship of lifelong partners or closest friends who cooperate without friction; the criminal case in which a quarrel over trifles escalates into conflict and ends in bloodshed. The great majority of situations lie between the two, with cooperation and betrayal interleaved, hard to predict and hard to control.

Looking at the course of human social development, early humans lacked both correct theoretical knowledge to guide them and the means to collect sufficient information. They could only, through repeated and continuous games, and with the human intelligent agent's own limited capacity and inability to store precisely for long, with its slow and imprecise reasoning and computation, collect information about relatively simple or small-scale things, generalize and abstract, form experience and build theory, then test and correct it in practice. Later came spoken and written language, then writing, printing and painting, then photography, sound and video recording, right up to the appearance of computers and AI: carbon-based biological intelligent agents created a whole suite of silicon-based intelligent-agent technology, vastly raising the capacity for everything from collecting, storing and processing information to generalizing, abstracting, and creating new knowledge and new tools. Humanity's ability to study and control complex systems keeps growing; it now even has the ability to control and remake the Earth's natural environment and its own body. Computers gave people the ability to predict and control geology, oceans and weather; computers and AI gave people systematic understanding of, and the ability to modify, protein folding and the genetic system.

But for all of these complex systems, the data to be collected is not about us humans ourselves. For the complex social system that is human civilization, the information to be collected is the information of all of us human intelligent agents about ourselves. For human beings, who have subjective agency, the capacity to collect and process information is not the bottleneck; the subjective willingness to provide that information is. With animals we already use coercive means of collecting information and running experiments that violate their will, control their freedom and even harm their lives, and this has provoked moral controversy. Clearly, collecting information by force through the traditional pyramid-structured system of social governance, through the deterrence of the power gap and the organs of violence, is the worst strategy; even what is collected is data distorted under the pressure of power, deliberately concealed or falsified, neither complete nor true. This is also the most important reason why humanity's capacity to understand, predict and technologically remake nature runs so far ahead of its capacity to understand, predict and control its own social system.

The bottleneck the new mechanism must solve is exactly this one: eliminate the power gap, strengthen cooperation, reduce confrontation, limit at the level of mechanism the abuse of power and the occurrence of harm, guarantee that the information people provide will not be turned against them, let people get positive returns from sharing individual information, thereby motivate people to keep widening and deepening the scope of what they share, and guarantee that this positive feedback mechanism keeps running reliably and continuously.

### Behind privacy is power

What people fear in surveillance is not the technical means but the person behind the means who controls it: whether that person's power is locked in a cage, whether that person will do something to harm them. Everything has two sides, the knife, the car, electricity can hurt people or help them; the key is to build the guarantee into the mechanism.

The traditional so-called privacy and security problems, between person and person and between person and state, all have their root here. Between any two objects in human society, whether at different levels, the state against the individual, the company against the individual, or different individuals at the same level, the information barriers, the mutual distrust and wariness, what is called protecting privacy, all come from the fear that once the other side knows, it will use the power and information in its hands to gain the advantage in the game and inflict loss and harm. Security and privacy are one and the same problem. Whether information is private is fundamentally tied to whether power is equal, whether information is open, whether the game is fair. The more equal, the more open the information, the higher the trust between individuals, and the stronger the willingness to cooperate.

The same holds between person and person: how much two people know about each other is proportional to the degree of their cooperation and intimacy. You can see it at the small scale of a married couple and the larger scale of father and son, mother and child in a family. The more intimate and sound a marriage, the more the two trust each other, stand as equals, and have no privacy from each other; if someone believes there is privacy, then in most cases that privacy means believing the other will hurt him, or feeling that his so-called private matters would make others see him unfairly. Under an authoritarian system, and especially under a system with severe exploitation, extraction and corruption, the lower a person sits, the less willing he is to share his information with those above, while those above try every means to obtain his information and at the same time refuse to expose their own: officials would rather not disclose their assets, and ordinary people develop every kind of resentment against those above them.

Push this mechanism to its end and you get: the weak, in order to play against the powerful, refuse to provide personal information, even though analysis of that information at the macro level would actually benefit them, in order to strengthen their own capacity for resistance and defense, even though this internal tension lowers the cooperative efficiency of the whole system. And these refusals and defenses force the powerful above, partly to better realize the national interest and partly for their own security, to strengthen surveillance and strengthen their capacity for suppression in response. This confrontation between the two sides, this structural conflict between the top and the bottom of the pyramid, is what Marx defined as class struggle. Because of the inequality of power and information, and the inherent game advantage of the pyramid, it usually produces an unfair distribution of benefits: unfairness inside the system is called exploitation, unfairness outside the system is called graft and corruption, and so greater distrust arises; the loss of trust further raises the tension of conflict; the rise in tension leads those above to need a larger power gap to guarantee structural stability; the larger power gap further produces unfair distribution. This vicious circle has produced the world we have now: social wealth is plainly growing fast as science and technology raise productivity, yet the gaps between people in class, in power, and between rich and poor grow ever wider. If we continue along the old system, AI will not become the tool that levels everyone's differences in ability and achieves a world of equality and commonwealth; it will instead become the machine of violence by which a small privileged few suppress and exploit the great majority of ordinary people.

### Locking power in a cage

The better way to lock power in a cage is a better and entirely new mechanism for distributing power, that is, a mechanism for distributing decision weight, rather than, under the traditional system, hoping that the person or group holding great power is a wise ruler or a saint. Whether it is the knife, AI, nuclear energy or the surveillance system, every tool with the capacity to harm that is held by a person, group or state, under the old pyramid system, however detailed the institutions and rules, leaves the power to make the rules and the power to enforce them inside the pyramid's power hierarchy; those in high positions can still do things that harm others for their own gain, and the system will not punish them. So what the cage must lock up, first of all, is the power to make the rules.

Look at it the other way round: as long as the mechanism guarantees that harm cannot happen, a virtuous circle arises, the exact opposite of the vicious circle of the old system. If by mechanism no party has the ability to use the power and tools in its hands to harm others, then in the real cooperative game harm does not happen; the more certain it is that harm will not happen, the longer people cooperate and the more they trust each other; trust brings more sharing of information and deeper, fuller cooperation; cooperation benefits both sides, so harm becomes even less likely. Even if an accident in cooperation produces temporary harm, since the new mechanism rules out any malicious harm, neither side will think the other acted out of malice, and cooperation will not end because of one accident. Friends or partners who get along well in real life relate to each other in exactly this way; the difference is that in real life it depends on the personal character and cognition of both parties, on the subjectivity of individuals, not on a global, objective institutional guarantee. If we design a good institution, the protection that institution itself gives to cooperation raises the success rate and effect of cooperation, and with the help of institution and rules, it in turn directly shapes and raises the cognitive and moral level of everyone.

You may imagine any so-called privacy you like; I hold that every one of them can be solved by a better strategy of making it fully and mutually open as common knowledge. Of course, "open" here refers to information that concerns other objects in the outside world; data that can be encapsulated inside a lower-level sub-object and has no effect on the outside is not included. Chapter 14 will turn this boundary into concrete rules for viewing permissions.

### Questions and Answers

**Q: Eliminate misunderstanding and hostility first, then cooperate: isn't the order reversed? A verifiable history can by itself sustain cooperation under distrust; contact and understanding should be the product of cooperation, not its precondition.**

A: My intention was never to make the elimination of hostility a precondition of cooperation. When people's refusal to cooperate comes from irrational emotion and from differences in higher-order cognition, then using an agreed meta-cognition to eliminate the influence of irrational emotion and cognitive difference, and to give every party a positive initial attitude of readiness to cooperate, is the best strategy for continuing cooperation and exchange. In a repeated game, under the same payoffs, long-term cooperation and long-term mutual defection are both equilibria; the payoff structure determines which equilibria exist, not which one you land in. What decides where you land is the initial expectation: do I believe the other side will cooperate. The first move of tit-for-tat is to cooperate, and that move is not derived from the payoffs; it is an assumption about the other side. That is exactly what meta-cognition does: it does not change the payoffs, it changes the selection of equilibrium. It also raises tolerance, reinterpreting a provocation from "the other side is evil" to "the product of the other side's structural differences in this situation," and a certain degree of tolerance is precisely the necessary condition for avoiding echoing vendettas in a noisy repeated game. So meta-cognition and the verifiable record, one responsible for willingness and the other for credibility, come in the order: simultaneously.
