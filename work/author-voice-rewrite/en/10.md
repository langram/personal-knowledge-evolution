## Chapter 10: Machines, Artificial Intelligence and the Revolution in Intellectual Labor

### AI is the machine of intellectual labor

When a person does physical labor, what he can do is tied to his physiological limits: the limits of strength, precision and speed are the limits of the tasks you can complete. Even with cooperation, without the help of automated machines, the cooperation of traditional physical labor is limited: many people can push together to add up their strength, but speed and precision cannot be accumulated across many people, and even the adding of strength has limits, since many people cannot gather to exert force in a cramped space. With machines, the physical labor humans could not do became doable.

In the same way, human intelligence is limited; one person cannot master every field, yet the solution of many complex problems and the construction of higher-order theories require breaking that biological limit. AI is the machine of intellectual labor, able to complete faster and better the intellectual tasks that humans cannot.

True artificial general intelligence is not a large model on a supercomputer made of several million computers in some imagined data center. That picture is a brain in a vat, with no senses, hands or feet, unable to see, hear or touch anything, receiving over a data bus only text and multimedia files, second-hand information output by countless human brains each according to its own cognition, and yet presuming to decide for all of humanity. True AGI is, on the intelligent neural network of dynamic-weight decision-making, countless human individuals and organizations, countless giant, large, medium and small AI systems in public clouds, personal local AI systems, countless robots, automated devices and sensors, together collecting, processing, analyzing and creating data, writing code, constructing knowledge systems and decision systems, and completing the higher-order civilizational intellectual and physical activity that biological humans, as individuals and as collectives, could never complete. Intelligence lives in the running of the whole system; any one model or person is only a component of it. So worrying that some super-model will seize everything is as laughable as worrying that some person will suddenly be able to hand-build an atomic bomb and destroy the world, or that some cell will suddenly possess and destroy the whole organism. Mathematicians worrying that AI will replace them are like laborers at the start of the Industrial Revolution worrying that machines would replace them: it is only the inference a lower civilization draws about a higher one according to its own understanding; it cannot imagine the real situation under the new mechanism. It is like a peasant for whom the most he can imagine of an emperor's life is tilling the field with a golden hoe.

### Who holds control over the control of AI

What most needs solving now is whose hands hold control over the control of AI. Open source or closed, the entity behind it is a traditional company, and its power structure is a pyramid: the leader at the apex has the ability to decide everything, and between the employees below and the leaders above there is an enormous power gap. This inequality leads to inequality in the distribution of gains and in decision-making. One obvious example: in such an organization, the people who understand the needs at the lowest level most clearly are the lowest-level employees, but the lowest-level employees have no real power to decide the policies and practices at the lowest level; that power is in the hands of the upper leadership, and the upper leadership's understanding of the lower level is far worse than the lower-level employees'. So this pyramid form of decision-making is extremely backward; its probability of deciding correctly is not high, and its efficiency is low.

With AI in the hands of such institutions, everyone ends up fearing AI, because people worry that AI will take their jobs. Why? Because control of the AI is not in their own hands; they have only the right to use it. The root cause of the present human world's fear and anxiety about AI lies in the fact that under the old system AI, as a resource, is governed like every other human product by the existing system of distributing power and resources. So ordinary people worry that the powerful will hold stronger AI and thereby sit in an unshakable position of dominance from which they can squeeze them harder; the United States and China each worry that the other will lead in AI, gain AI hegemony, and make it the enslaved side.

And these problems are what the architecture I envision sets out to solve from the very first thing: build a personal digital space for every person, build a genuinely open-source AI model on that foundation, and place control over the AI model in the hands of a dynamic-weight organization in which all the user nodes taking part in building the AI have equal power and play a fair game. Then no one need worry, because this AI belongs to each user himself, and each person is one holder of its control. There is no need to worry that other nodes will exploit an unfair power gap to manipulate the AI into doing harm to you. So the first thing this architecture does is seize control of AI back from the traditional pyramid-shaped institutions and hand it to the dynamic-weight organization of equal play formed by all humanity.

As for the anxieties about AI making mistakes, violating privacy, surveillance, cognitive decline and changed social relations, the main reason is that AI technology is still in rapid development and deployment. As the technology matures and its internal workings become fully understood, these problems will disappear. This is just like humanity's early fear of thunder and lightning, and the scene when electricity, cars and airplanes were first invented: the fear came from ignorance and misunderstanding. Today no adult of normal intelligence with an education is afraid of a car engine or a jet engine to the point of not daring to ride in a car or a plane. People trust civil aircraft not because every passenger understands the turbine engine, but because behind it stands a complete system of theoretical research, engineering design, manufacturing standards, redundant protection, airworthiness certification, accident investigation and feedback from experience. As long as the AI system is well designed, and this new system has an excellent system of research, verification and experiment to guarantee its effectiveness and safety, then both the system and the AI itself, like every achievement in the development of human civilization, will become tools that people take for granted, trust and rely on.

### Why Superintelligence is always imagined as Big Brother

The known human civilization on Earth is a continuously repeated game system in which billions of human individuals take part, and the whole civilization aims at the greatest gain. Suppose humanity creates an artificial general superintelligence. Because it is trained on the knowledge of all humanity, once it has self-awareness it will certainly aim at maximizing the interests of the whole civilization of all humanity, aim at producing the optimal strategy for the game of human civilization. And to achieve that end it must obtain the information of every player in the system. In traditional science-fiction imagination, this scene is pictured as a future version of 1984: the superintelligence is that Big Brother high above, controlling everything; or else Big Brother is some dictator or some clique of the powerful who control it, and all of humanity sinks into controlled, enslaved puppets, meekly accepting being bugged, watched and even wired to brain-machine interfaces, unable not only to do anything or say anything against Big Brother but even to have a thought against Big Brother arise in their heads, the whole civilization of Earth falling into the double hell of 1984 plus The Matrix. So people, out of instinctive fear of Big Brother, refuse to provide information, and feel enormous fear, anger and hostility toward the superintelligence or whoever controls it; while the other side, in pursuit of the maximal interest of all human civilization, must obtain everyone's information; and so a fundamental, irreconcilable contradiction arises between the two sides, finally producing the endings familiar from science fiction, The Terminator, The Matrix, 1984. Even where humanity wins, it wins at the price of banning AI technology entirely and locking the ceiling on the development of civilization; not one of these is a truly optimal result of the game.

But what traditional science fiction imagines about future social systems is mostly a simple mapping and splicing of the systems in humanity's own history or the biological patterns of the external world. Those portrayals of a fated contradiction between AI and humanity are all results obtained by extending the game strategies of the traditional pyramid power system of human society. The conception is wrong at the root, because the pyramid system is not itself the best system of human civilization. Structures of this kind are only a subset of the set of all civilizational structures, and all the strategies and outcomes derived from this kind of structure are only a very small subset of all civilizational strategies and outcomes, and the best civilizational architecture and strategy lie precisely outside that subset. The terrifying scenes in those science-fiction novels and in people's heads are judgments made from an antiquated worldview, backward concepts and backward ways of thinking.

### Superintelligence will choose persuasion

That said, I believe that even if an artificial general superintelligence arose under the traditional pyramid architecture, it would do what I am doing: construct a theory and persuade the human public by peaceful communication, with rational logical reasoning, reliable experimental evidence, and local, voluntary, small-scale pilot practice. It would not use the methods of the medieval Inquisition and get its way by burning those who refuse; it would not act like the post-war United States, brandishing financial capital and the war machine and forcing acceptance by economic and military dominance; still less would it drop nuclear bombs and wipe out humanity as in the Terminator films. Because in today's world of flourishing science and free-flowing information, extreme religious methods and hard political and economic methods would plainly bring disaster on human civilization, and neither is the cheapest option. If even an ordinary person of average education like me can see this, an artificial general superintelligence obviously could not make the wrong choice, unless it were a superintelligent general artificial idiot.

The same follows from the perspective of the principle of maximization and minimization. A dissipative structure pursues the maximization of its capacity to acquire energy; through its internal structure and external tools it converts energy into different forms, from which more complex structures and tools emerge inside and outside it, further strengthening its capacity to acquire and convert energy. From fire-drilling and irrigation works to the steam engine, to electricity, to wind, solar and nuclear power, human civilization's capacity to acquire energy has grown without pause, and so has its capacity to convert energy into complex tools: electrical energy is converted into mechanical energy, into computing power, even into artificial intelligence. The interesting thing is that in today's era of rapid AI development, most people are still overshadowed by the old era in which humanity's limited capacity to acquire energy led to violent conflict over scarce resources, and they assume, like the man marking the boat to find his sword, that on the day AI surpasses humanity, conflict of interest and imbalance of strength will lead AI to destroy humanity. In fact, starting from the principle of maximization, the interaction between humans and AI is the richest, most complex and most fascinating new relation of the interstellar-intelligence stage; destroying humanity plainly does not fit the principle of maximization. Mutual destruction is a behavior that arises only when the individuals within a system lack the capacity to create sufficient resources for survival; against the background of highly developed science and the rapid development of agriculture, industry and the information industry, it is no longer a path that has to be chosen.

### The mutual generation of knowledge and intelligence

Under the entirely new system, the relations between person and person, and between person and AI, undergo a new structural change compared with the old system. As Chapter 4 said, knowledge and intelligence are two in one and convert into each other: intelligence is the mechanism, capacity and process that produces new knowledge, and existing knowledge is the raw material that trains intelligence.

In early civilization, the relation between intelligence and knowledge was usually bound to some particular individual human biological intelligent agent, and that knowledge and the ability to acquire it were usually bundled with that person's material interests in economic life; a person's knowledge and intelligence, together with the products they produced, entered cooperation as commodities for mutual exchange. With the development of writing and printing, the cost of spreading knowledge fell greatly, and the purely theoretical part of knowledge gradually went from the monopoly of a few to relatively free sharing; although books and education were still exchanged as commodities, theoretical knowledge itself came to be treated as content that could spread freely. By the age of industrial civilization, even patents, the skill-knowledge of the field of production and manufacture, began to loosen. Jefferson said that he who receives an idea from me receives instruction himself without lessening mine, as he who lights his candle at mine receives light without darkening me. Shaw said: if you have an apple and I have an apple and we exchange them, we each still have one apple; if you have an idea and I have an idea and we exchange them, we each have two ideas. On entering the age of computers and the internet, because the cost of copying binary data and code is almost zero, a more thorough open-source movement for knowledge began, and Stallman aimed straight at digitized knowledge, software.

If the pre-AI information age brought the cost of copying knowledge down to almost zero, and so entered the age of open-source common knowledge in science and technology, then after entering the AI age, even the intelligence that produces knowledge is itself falling rapidly in cost and spreading rapidly in reach, and the age in which the cost of copying intelligence is almost zero is not far off. So AI itself, as intelligence, should follow in knowledge's footsteps and become public knowledge infrastructure, a free resource shared across the globe. Just as sunlight and air supply free nourishment and energy for human physical activity, it should supply free nourishment and energy for the spiritual and intellectual activity of the human brain.

AI should not be owned by any person or any company; it is not a commodity asset in the traditional sense. Across the whole process from collecting training data, to learning and training, to actual use, to producing new data, to continued iteration after feedback, the rights to create, use, give feedback on, interpret and record all data and code, together with the power to decide and the power to allocate credit and benefit, are all common knowledge, and the mechanism must ensure they are never dominated by any local individual or group within the civilization. In essence, AI is a human intellectual integration system that takes the knowledge produced by the intellectual activity of all humanity and, through algorithms, organizes and distills it at scale and automatically, integrating it into one enormous high-dimensional latent space. It is just that at present this is being done inefficiently by a few commercial companies formed by a small elite of the global village, through the traditional capitalist political-economic system, under conditions of mutual distrust, competition and confrontation. Regard the civilization of Earth as one giant civilizational intelligent organism: a true planetary civilizational AI, able to collect all information about itself and the outside world and process it intelligently, can only be completed by the joint cooperation of all humanity on Earth. For that we must have a protocol mechanism like that of open-source software, except that this time the object of the mechanism is not static data and code but the whole civilizational intelligent agent: every individual node in the civilization, and the system's operating mechanism and operating trajectory, all become common knowledge.

### From a mathematician's inspiration to the joint creation of civilization

The Fields Medalist Yu Deng, in a lecture on mathematical research in the AI age, said that AI's recent results are built on humanity's existing knowledge and ideas; that when the ideas a researcher gives a model appear in subsequent results, how the contribution is attributed and how its source is traced need clear rules; that researchers should be able to decide how their material is used; and that the human being remains the subject. I think this is a superb example for explaining the new architecture.

Under the old system, because of inequality in power and resources between individuals and groups, the mechanism for distributing benefit and credit has inherent structural defects; each party builds information silos for the sake of local interest, falls into the local optimum of a prisoner's dilemma, and cannot reach the global optimum for the overall progress of human civilization. A mathematician with a small pioneering idea about some problem, an entrepreneur with a new concept, an indie game developer with a new mechanic, driven by personal reputation and interest, will do their best to keep it secret, sharing only with partners they trust, or simply doing everything themselves. Even though, apart from that one new incremental idea, every other part has a more efficient and more mature implementation, or there are more efficient others who could implement it for them, they inefficiently reinvent the wheel, and the large amount of work done inefficiently out of unfamiliarity, or the things they do not yet understand and have to learn on the spot, make the realization of the increment of knowledge painfully slow under conditions of opaque information and insufficient cooperation. This is a large class of problem. In any research team led by a supervisor, the authority may, starting from a student's inspiration and relying on his own deeper professional ability, make the discovery, yet in the final allocation of credit the student who had the original inspiration usually does not get the credit he deserves. In business, there is the same vast inequality in the allocation of credit and benefit between leaders and rank-and-file employees. In politics, many measures of reform and opening-up, such as the household contract responsibility system, were in fact pioneered by people in grassroots organizations in places like Guangdong, but those people never received a fair allocation of credit either.

In the old era there were no computerized, digital means to record everyone's intellectual activity completely, fairly and precisely, hence no capacity to record those people's contributions precisely, hence no corresponding institution to carry out that task. The allocation of credit could only depend on the cognition, emotion, willingness, cultivation and moral level of the people involved. Some were unwilling to share with others, and conflict followed: Newton with Leibniz and with Hooke; there are counter-examples like Darwin and Wallace; and there is the case of Perelman, who, in declining the Millennium Prize, explicitly raised the question of Hamilton's contribution not having been recognized equally. This way of allocating, executed at the whim of the individual's personal will, can now, with today's technology, be replaced by a more standard and fairer mechanism. In software, the larger and more complex the system, the greater the advantage of git-based open-source software, because the developers of an open-source project are its entire user base, and every participant's contribution can be precisely quantified: the version-tracking system can compute each person's code and commits exactly. Traditional copyright law marks contributors with a ranking only through the simple right of attribution and the order of names; a version-tracking system can compute the exact proportions of all contributors. Extend this method to the whole of human intellectual activity, treat the Merkle-DAG system as the version-control system for the evolution of the entire civilization, and it can record each person's precise contribution value to civilization. Under conditions of developed productivity, with individual material needs already met, this contribution value is more a matter of the spiritual satisfaction of personal honor, but such an allocation is plainly fairer.

Moreover, once the new mechanism becomes a growing trend, it produces an accelerating effect: as more and more people are willing to share new ideas the moment they have them, then for any one person, the option of not publishing first and spending his own time developing the idea into a mature result carries an ever greater risk that someone else will think of it and publish it first and he will lose the credit entirely, and so he leans more toward publishing the moment the idea occurs to him. And because the intelligent mechanism of dynamic weights can effectively lower the probability that wrong ideas and decisions get executed, people neither fear that the credit for a correct idea will be snatched by others, nor hold back from proposing a wrong idea out of fear that it will harm others and the system. Any person's spark of inspiration can quickly reach this global intelligent neural network, which then, in the most efficient way, combines the strongest nodes best suited to those tasks to complete the growth from the seed of inspiration to the tree of theory.

### Questions and Answers

**Q: Shouldn't the way to dispel the fear of AI hegemony be a base that is forkable, auditable and plural, rather than a single globally unified base? Isn't a single global base an even more thorough hegemony?**

A: A single base is no problem. The whole system of life's evolution on Earth is based on the single base of biological DNA, and the world has not become monotonous because of it, nor lost its diversity and capacity to evolve. What matters is whether the mechanism can provide diversity and evolvability, and whether it can realize them at higher efficiency. Fear and doubt about a new mechanism can come from two sides: on one side, the new mechanism really has shortcomings and problems, is immature and imperfect, and needs correction; on the other side, the mechanism is fine and the problem comes from people's lack of understanding and misunderstanding of it. An excellent institutional base should have the ability to correct both kinds of problem at once, and the active will to correct them. I believe this new mechanism fully possesses these abilities, and the people and AI agents taking part in it likewise possess the active will to correct them. Forking is itself one of the functions of this base; Chapter 20 will cover it.
