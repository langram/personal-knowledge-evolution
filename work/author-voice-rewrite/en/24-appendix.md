## Appendix: What Is Hypothesis, What Is Inference, and What Can Be Done Today

The preface said that the different parts of this book vary in reliability. Here I spell it out item by item, so that no reader mistakes a hypothesis for a conclusion, or something that can be done today for a fantasy.

### Hypotheses

For the following, I myself know clearly that the hypothetical component is overwhelmingly the largest. They are organizing frameworks that let me see structural similarity across different fields and raise questions from it; they are not yet scientific theorems that can make quantitative predictions.

- The dualoid as an entity more basic than object and morphism, and its practice of defining itself through self-reference. To become mathematics, it needs an explicit construction: in at least two concrete fields, write the "fixed point" as different instances of the same formal object, and show that a structural mapping exists between the two, not merely a correspondence of names. This has not been done.
- Fixed point, minimization, maximization and the emergence theorem as a unified meta-principle running from physics to society. Of these, the duality of minimization and maximization is known content of the variational principle in physics; extending it to biology and society, the key concept still lacks an operational definition at the social level. The first operational definition this book gives is strictness, which is a starting point, not an end point.
- Gödel's incompleteness as emergeability, and relative infinity as the successor operation. These are intuitions from Chapter 1, with Lawvere's fixed point theorem and homotopy type theory as directional support, but this book has not formalized them.
- The subobject classifier as the mathematical root of consciousness. This is a hypothesis about "what makes a structure an observer." It explains a great deal, but it cannot yet predict any specific neural phenomenon.
- The discussions of physical building blocks: why strings are not basic building blocks, why carbon and silicon are good at constructing. These are a reorganization of known facts using the properties of the dualoid, not new physics.

### Inferences

The following rest on relatively reliable theoretical foundations and relatively complete chains of reasoning. They can be challenged, but the challenge must target a specific link in the chain.

- Tit for tat as the iteration operator of the evaluation function, strictness as the control parameter for convergence speed and stability, the universality of the hawk-dove spectrum at every level, and genes determining the ordering while the game determines the cut point. These are supported by evolutionary game theory, behavioral genetics and comparative historical research.
- The authoritarian system as a stable attractor of a positive feedback loop, and the influence of exit costs and geographical parameters on institutional form.
- The two chain reactions: the escalation of suspicion caused by withholding information, and the accumulation of trust caused by sharing it. The theory of repeated games and a large number of historical cases both support this.
- The root of privacy and security problems is inequality of power; the record itself is not the threat, the ability to view and exploit the record falling into the hands of a few is.
- The division into two kinds of resources, and the power-law distribution arising from the compounding effect of unequal games.
- The gap between technology and institutions, and the risk of its being amplified as the technological singularity approaches.
- The three levels of the alignment problem, and the first level determining the other two.

- Whether an institution is reasonable does not rest on any particular distribution of ability holding. Freedom from domination rests on Chapter 8's equality in the sense of the rules and on cutting the channel by which advantage turns into domination; both are rules that can be checked, not predictions about distributions.

### Still to be worked out

The following are things on which I have had an intuition and an original discussion, but which have not been developed into a complete argument. This book leaves them open rather than filling them in to look complete.

- Alignment as a fixed point. The direction is to place it on the mechanism by which strategies and institutions are updated in the evolutionary game, and to separate the cooperative fixed points from the mutually harmful ones; how to separate them has not been done.
- The correspondence of query, key and value in the civilization-level attention architecture, and a precise way of writing how state updates with action. Chapter 15 gives only a preliminary correspondence and a five-step process.
- No right of inheritance over personal resources. This is my own opinion about a future institution, not a conclusion derived from the principles that precede it.

### What can be done today

The following, taking current and foreseeable computer and AI capabilities as the premise, are engineering problems, not theoretical ones.

- One person's Merkle-DAG personal knowledge base: take back all of one's records from every platform, store them by content identifier, source and time, mark original utterances and AI replies separately, with every version verifiable. The material of this book is in exactly such a base.
- A language that uses hashes as names and addresses, and a software seed that is compatible first and replaces later. The MerkleVM project is doing this, and has already produced negative results that can be recorded.
- The hot path: a large model handles the cold path, and after verification it is solidified into a reusable program and shared among users.
- Typed Merkle-DAG nodes as tokens for training models, and typed uncertainty. The Structoken and FPM experiments have already given preliminary results.
- Devices for collecting personal data, with permission rules and cross-signing. The rules are already written, and the devices can start from existing products.
- An organization that begins with one person's cloud storage, and task-granularity participation.

### What cannot yet be promised

- The performance of the dynamic-weight social neural network in real social decisions. It needs to run first in domains where feedback is fast enough, and accumulate records first in domains where feedback is slow. Two people, one task, one record is its minimal test scenario.
- That the new mechanism produces no new domination as it expands. This requires observing both internal equality and the absence of outward domination at every step of the expansion, rather than deducing it from the mechanism's definition.
- Whether the economic model and organizational form can stand on their own once the technology is running. Gains in efficiency do not automatically become fair distribution; the rules of distribution must develop in step with the technical prototype.
- The Large Civilization Model and social mechanics. Each of its units is itself a high-dimensional semantic model, and its demand for computing power is several orders of magnitude above today's large models; its training material can only accumulate once the social neural network is running. This is a direction, not a capability of today.
- Whether the results of the virtual laboratory can transfer to reality. Its role is to screen out plainly unworkable proposals and screen in those worth piloting at small scale in reality, not to replace real-world testing.

### How to let a scientific prophecy be corrected by reality

This book hopes to be a scientific prophecy capable of fulfilling itself on its own. But a prophecy must be correctable by reality; otherwise it is faith. There is only one way to make it correctable: turn everything this book says into a record that can be recorded, verified and overturned. The book's knowledge base has already done this: the original wording of every argument, every revision, every objection accepted and rejected, is all there. Readers can come to it with questions, disagreements and practical experience, and then add their own records to it. That is the relationship between this book and the civilization it describes: it is not a description of that civilization, it is that civilization's first record.
