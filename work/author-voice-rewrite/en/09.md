## Chapter 9: The Social Neural Network of Dynamic Weights

### Starting from spam

The decision mechanism I envision was first inspired by spam-filtering algorithms. Whether an email is spam is not decided by any single rule; instead countless features each give a judgment, each judgment carries a weight, and the weight keeps adjusting according to that feature's past accuracy. The weight of a feature that judges correctly rises; the weight of one that judges wrongly falls. The whole system needs no "authority" at all, yet it gets more and more accurate.

Put this algorithm onto the decisions of human society: when a complex, uncertain problem arises at the social, political or economic level, instead of having it decided by the relevant experts under an authoritarian system or by elections and votes under a democratic system, the algorithm of the social intelligence neural network computes the historical accuracy of each human and AI intelligent individual or organizational node in answering that class of problem and assigns different weights: nodes with high accuracy get high weight, nodes with low accuracy get low weight; then from these weighted results it computes and offers several different solutions, each with its own weight; then experiments are run through virtual or real social pilots, and the feedback decides how to roll out; and the result of each round of feedback in turn revises the decision weight of every node. A dynamic intelligent decision system of this kind is certain to be far more scientific and accurate than the traditional political and economic decision systems.

Every intelligent individual, whether biological human or AI, has an equal initial weight in every field. That weight is adjusted dynamically, with positive and negative feedback, according to the effect its creations and opinions in that field have on the system, the accuracy of its predictions and the effectiveness of its proposals, all of which the system can record and verify.

### The brain compared with society

Use the human brain as the metaphor for this new civilizational system: the brain corresponds to civilization; neurons correspond to humans plus AI; the products of individual rational thought, individual knowledge and cognition, correspond to the products of human rational thought, the sum of human knowledge; the brain's biological neural network corresponds to the neural network of human society.

Whether it is the rational knowledge of an individual or the scientific theoretical knowledge of all humanity, its essence is a directed acyclic graph; and the languages that describe this knowledge, whether human languages or the syntax trees of computer languages, are the same. But the neural networks from which this knowledge emerges differ in their composition and their runtime mechanism between the level of the brain and the level of society. In the brain, neurons are equal to one another in ability and status, differing only in which network layer they sit in, with weights adjusting dynamically at all times. The network formed by the individuals of human society is a more static pyramid structure, in which the resources one holds, the power one has and the size of one's decision weight depend on one's position in the pyramid, are relatively fixed, and lack real-time dynamic adjustment. When an exceptionally capable entrepreneur or politician declines in cognitive and decision-making ability with age, his decision weight is not thereby quickly replaced by a younger and more vigorous new generation.

This does away with the static weighting policy of past human societies. In dictatorships and oligarchies, the weight of the leader is far greater than that of ordinary people, and precisely because his power is so great, discovering errors and correcting them through feedback is extremely difficult and extremely slow, usually requiring violent revolution to complete the redistribution of decision weight. In democracies, whether one person one vote or a system of representatives, weight is redistributed once every few years; the difficulty and speed of discovering and correcting errors through feedback are better than in dictatorship and oligarchy, but still seriously lagging, and the reasonableness of the distribution is never fully satisfied.

### Unbinding decision nodes from specific people

There is a key point in this mechanism: the nodes of the decision network are no longer bound to specific elite human individuals or groups, but consist of algorithms and rules in which collective intelligence converges. Perhaps it could be called "knowledge-ism" or "intelligence-ism." The Industrial Revolution turned physical labor from the personal craft of the workshop into a function that machines could reproduce at high quality and high speed; in the same way, before the AI revolution, social governance was carried out by the pyramid-shaped, hierarchical structures formed by intellectual authorities, from political leaders to business magnates to academic authorities, and the AI revolution will inevitably bring a revolution in the organizational form of intellectual labor. The products of any intellectual labor are no longer bound to a specific craftsman as in the workshop age, but become built-in functions of machines that can be copied at scale; algorithms, rules and strategies, these intellectual products, are likewise no longer bound to some specific authority but become built-in functions of some AI model. I think this alone is enough to change the decision-making organization of future society and the mechanism by which it runs. Just as after the Industrial Revolution capitalism was, relative to feudalism, a relation of production better suited to the new productive force of automated physical labor, the social neural network of dynamic weights is an architecture of productive relations better suited than capitalism to the new productive force of automated intellectual labor.

What is more, this dynamic-weight approach does not even require people to take part actively and by hand. Meta once installed software on employees' machines to track keyboard and mouse operations, recording all the operations and behavior of employees at work, in effect turning the employees' knowledge and skills into company assets. Many people also write the experience and skills they or others have summarized into skill documents for AI to call, which amounts to having a particular person make the choice and judgment on a particular problem. If a Merkle-DAG system records a person's whole digital space, if that person's skills, cognition and preferences are recorded with privacy and security guaranteed, with the owner's identity recorded and tamper-proof, that amounts to a broader kind of intellectual-property marking. When a decision problem arises that the group needs settled, in the great majority of cases the user does not need to take part personally; the AI can provide his opinion and views from the records in his digital space. If the issue is important and the user himself is interested, the AI can invite the user to take part, and after discussion submit the opinion formed with the user's active participation. This is each person's AI agent acting as the rational intelligence that represents that person's personal rights, interests and opinions: simple, routine, trivial judgments are completed automatically by the AI agent; major matters are discussed jointly by the AI and the human user before an opinion is given; the whole process is recorded throughout, to be used in future to adjust weights through feedback.

### Virtual tit-for-tat

The strict and lenient versions of tit-for-tat that humans use in the real world can be replaced by a virtual version of tit-for-tat in the digital world of software: using digital points as the means of reward and punishment, turning real damage in the real world into virtual damage in a game. Throughout human history, the adjustment of economic and political power and decision weight has usually been accompanied by violent, destructive punishment and wastefully excessive reward. Virtual tit-for-tat will completely eliminate these inefficient losses that come with adjusting power and weight, achieving the emergence of new stable fixed-point structures at the lowest energy and the fastest speed. It replaces the costly physical and emotional confrontations of the real world with virtual games and simulated deduction in the world of software; before taking real action, every possibility can be explored in virtual space to find the evolutionary path with the highest energy-efficiency ratio and the least harm. This network is at the same time a vast laboratory of human evolution.

### Democracy, centralism and the attention mechanism

The traditional multi-party democratic system typified by the United States, and the one-party authoritarian system typified by China, remind me of the contest between CNNs and RNNs in the development of artificial intelligence. An RNN processes a sequence, each step depending on the result of the step before; it is good at order and causality and hard to parallelize. A CNN can run massively in parallel within each layer; it is good at processing local features at the same time and poor at long-range sequential dependency. The question behind that contest is that the running of a system is a mixture of two broad classes of logic: one that can only be completed through logical causality and sequential order of construction, and one that can run in parallel; in computer science these correspond respectively to tasks better suited to the CPU and tasks better suited to the GPU. A complex computing task is usually a mixture of both, and how you design the algorithm according to the nature of the task, so that the computation is completed through different combinations of the two mechanisms, makes an enormous difference in efficiency. Human society is the same: to build a building you must lay the foundation before raising the main structure, but within each stage many tasks can run in parallel; a product must be designed before it can be manufactured and produced before it can be used, but the actual manufacture and use can run in parallel. The most important meta-strategy is this: in designing and assigning tasks, do you lean more toward the logical causal order of construction for overall coordination, or more toward each part running independently in parallel, then integrating through rules like those of a market economy or a social game.

The multi-party democratic federal system led by the United States attends more to the autonomy of each part, the parallel development of the whole, the adaptability of rapid adjustment and diversity in direction of development; the small-government architecture is like a lightweight CPU carrying a heavyweight GPU. A unified one-party authoritarian system like China's leans more toward the logical consistency of overall coordination and the long-range planning of complex tasks; the big-government architecture is like a heavyweight CPU with a lightweight GPU. Democracy puts the individual's interest first and the whole second; it is strong in democratic capacity and weak in centralizing capacity. Centralism puts the whole first and the individual second; it is strong in centralizing capacity and weak in democratic capacity. As AI technology develops, should the institutional design of human society also be replaced by a new architecture that takes the strengths of both? Let it combine the diversity and flexibility of democracy with the consistency and complex planning capacity of centralism, becoming a better system that serves both individual and collective interests. The attention mechanism combined the strengths of CNNs and RNNs and achieved more efficient training and inference; multi-head attention lets the same problem receive attention from different angles, and in the face of complex problems and complex diseases, only multi-angle thinking and cooperation among many experts can raise the probability of success; complex problems and large projects at the level of society and civilization need this mechanism too. It is just that in the great majority of scenarios today, we still rely on humanity's traditional ways of cooperating and thinking, and have not made full use of the rule-governed participation of AI agents, with their stronger computing power and stronger reasoning, in civilization-level attention algorithms.

### Commitment capacity and error-correction capacity

An authoritarian system with one party in long-term rule has the commitment capacity and implementation capacity for long-term planning, but it also has the problem that when a single point of decision fails, precisely because the commitment and implementation capacity is so strong, one mistake costs too much. I think this has to be judged by the times. Before the age of computerized information, information collection and communication relied on paper, telephone and telegraph; authoritarian systems like the former Soviet Union and early China that tried to manage the economy by planning made all their decisions under severe shortage of information, and with commitment and implementation capacity too strong and feedback insufficient or too slow, errors went uncorrected for years and caused serious losses. After entering the information age, the situation is entirely different: China's reform and opening-up ran almost in step with the development of information technology; add the "black cat, white cat" theory, a pragmatism that emphasizes trying different routes and selecting the best by actual feedback, plus the method of the special economic zones, local experiments rolled out nationwide once proven effective, and the Chinese government's decisions in industrial policy and financial measures have hardly made a major mistake since, and when mistakes are made, the speed of correction is fast.

But this does not mean the information age has dissolved the contradiction between commitment and error correction. The speed of error correction is inversely proportional to the political investment of the top: in fields where the center has no strong position, economic policy, industrial policy, both experiment and correction are fast; in fields where the center has staked its authority, correction is slow, and when it comes it comes as a sudden break. That is precisely the signature of a pyramid with face to save. Nor has the Chinese government managed everything and decided everything, or where would the market economy have come from? The problem of face, or in other words the problem of political responsibility, comes entirely from the fact that under an authoritarian system policies and rules are bound to a specific leading authority, a specific person. Under a dynamic-weight decision system there is no such binding. Decisions are unbound from face; capability nodes have no face.

### Will weight turn back into privilege

Some worry that dynamic weights will be captured by numbers of proxies and by historical advantage, and recentralize. These two worries correspond to two explicit rules in the new framework. Historical advantage: weight is distributed according to ability, but the weight of an ability attaches to concrete problems and adjusts with every feedback from results; when a problem disappears its weight goes to zero, and when a new problem appears every node starts again from the same initial value, as Chapter 15 will explain. A person's past record of being right gets him heard more on problems of the same kind, but it cannot, like wealth, detach itself from the problem, circulate on its own and roll up into compound interest. Numbers of proxies: each person has exactly one root agent, whose identity is bound to that person's data sovereignty, through the records and signatures Chapter 14 will describe. A person who manufactures ten thousand proxies gets not ten thousand shares of weight but ten thousand copies of an opinion from one source, visible at a glance in the record. So what has to be guarded against is not the capable person but malicious sabotage and tampering: forged identities, proxy farming, altered records. Here we can learn the method of the cryptocurrency ledgers without copying their conclusion. Bitcoin, under its particular protocol, gives a concrete security condition: while honest nodes hold the majority of computing power, the probability that an attack rewriting history succeeds falls exponentially with the number of confirmations. That condition belongs to that protocol and that attack. A social neural network needs conditions of this kind written out for its own protocol and for each of its own classes of attack. But the principle is the same: a security condition is written as a quantity that can be computed, not as trust in certain people.

Here two things must be kept apart: equality of basic rights and equality of influence. The brain is not flat; it is highly modular, layered, with hubs, but it has no irreplaceable apex, influence shifts with the task and the evidence, and the failure of any unit can be compensated. The equality the new system wants is equality of basic rights: equality of data sovereignty, of distribution according to need, and of the right to exit. Influence, by contrast, is dynamic, contestable, replaceable. However high a person's weight in one field, he cannot exchange it for weight in another field, cannot exchange it for the first kind of resource, and still less can he exchange it for control over other people's basic rights. How weight is produced, when it expires, how it is corrected, who can modify the algorithm, all of these are themselves common knowledge, all are versioned in the Merkle-DAG, all are decided by the same system of dynamic weights. The power to make the rules is thus locked in the cage.

### Questions and Answers

**Q: The commitment problem comes back in impersonal form. Weight is a stake: a node whose weight rose because it advocated a policy will have its weight fall when the policy is proven wrong, so participants holding weight also have something to protect. Infrastructure commits on its own: a policy that has run for three years has downstream data formats adapted to it and other nodes that depend on it, and reversal is expensive all the same. A capability node trained on data produced under policy X also tends to keep recommending X.**

A: The objection is right: unbinding face does not unbind the motive to persist in error. So the real variable is not who the decision is bound to, but which arrives first for the party holding decision power, the cost of persisting or the cost of reversing. In a pyramid the cost of reversing falls on the leader and the cost of persisting falls on the population, and so it persists. Rules four and five of Chapter 7 were written for this very problem: every plan that accepts a temporary loss for long-term benefit must, on adoption, carry an estimated outcome and a monitoring process, and stop on failure; every loss is compensated at once and the strategy revised at once. Under dynamic weights, weight attaches to the concrete problem and adjusts with results, so persisting in a plan already proven wrong costs the persister his own weight directly, rather than costing the population. The switching costs of infrastructure and the path dependence of models are real, but they are engineering costs: they can be measured and shared, unlike face, which can only be settled by one side admitting defeat.

**Q: This mechanism requires full information and rational participants, and those conditions do not exist in reality.**

A: They are descriptions of an ideal state, not preconditions for engineering. The new mechanism I envision does not have to reach that goal immediately, and the goal is most likely, like Occam's razor and Kolmogorov complexity, an unsolvable problem, but one that can be approached by step-by-step iterative optimization. In fact, as long as the new mechanism is even slightly stronger than the old one, that is enough to bring about the replacement of old by new, just as an object always tends to move toward lower potential energy. As for the rational person: once AI is universal, everyone has his own AI agent to perfect his own rational knowledge and decision-making ability, and since the great majority of intellectual activity and behavior is carried out through AI agents, with that buffer in place, irrational emotional behavior will also fall sharply. At the present level of evolution, with respect to the degree of information that can currently be shared and the game-playing ability of each individual as limited by the current level, everyone is on an equal footing. That is enough.

**Q: Where does legitimacy come from? The legitimacy of existing democracy comes from the equal vote, and however imperfect it is, people accept it. In a scheme weighted by ability, why would the losing side accept the result?**

A: What the losing side accepts is not some person's judgment but a rule in which it is itself included, in which its own judgment is itself counted, and which can be checked afterward. Under one person one vote, the losing side accepts the result because it knows its vote will still count next time; under dynamic weights, the losing side accepts the result because it knows this result will be recorded and verified, and that if the outcome proves it right, its weight will rise, its loss will be compensated, and the plan will be revised. The former gives everyone a vote that does not change with results; the latter gives everyone a verifiable record that does change with results. Which makes losing easier to accept? I hold that it is the latter, because what it promises is not "try again next time" but "if it's wrong, it gets fixed."
