## Chapter 18: The Critical Fork in the Road

### A higher-order regulatory mechanism

The direction of this world's evolution is to use better and better modes of control to take over or replace the old, lower-level ones. This does not mean the lower-level modes become obsolete; in fact they operate at different levels of things, each regulating the business its own level has to handle.

Take the human nervous system. One mode is rational control: the neurons of the brain think, and then direct the body to act. Another is muscle memory acquired through long training: the thinking is compressed and the brain is skipped, the peripheral nerves and muscles cooperating directly to carry out the action. And there is one lower still: the parasympathetic nervous system controls circulation, breathing and digestion without passing through the brain at all; the body's automatic operation completes many tasks with no rational inference needed. But decisions driven purely by emotion or physiological instinct are often bad for the body. Instinct pushes a person to take in as much food as possible, and in today's world of enormous material abundance that means eating oneself fatter and fatter. Without the brain's involvement, a person facing more complex tasks or interactions with the outside world runs into many dangers. An advanced human can, according to the situation, combine the brain's high-level neural regulation with the body's instinctive drives to solve problems well.

The same holds at the level of nations and civilizations. The systems controlled by power and capital, the economic system and the political system that manages people, when they run according to the nature of power and capital, are like the physiological instincts driving a person. They can handle most of the basic-level tasks of human society, just as the parasympathetic nervous system controls circulation without the brain. For a civilization, high-level decisions are usually not needed: market regulation and the natural power games between people complete the vast majority of the political and economic activity of daily social life. But how to switch between different kinds of business, whether to leave something to the natural competition of the market, the so-called invisible hand, or to accomplish certain tasks through national industrial policy, has to be handled case by case, and that needs a high-level decision system.

The reason I am talking about is collective reason: everyone thinking about the problem together and offering solutions, then selecting the consensus plan through a dynamic decision system, rather than relying on the instinct of the market economy. The market economy produces economic crises and financial crises. Once an industry shows a high rate of return or a huge gap between supply and demand, it becomes the hottest thing going and everyone piles in. The internet produced a bubble in its early days, and AI is doing the same now: the big companies, with the money in their hands and the supply-demand squeeze, have driven up the price of memory and chips, until ordinary people who want to buy a game console or add a computer find they can no longer afford one. This kind of imbalance is unhealthy. If AI is to build a new, globally unified form of civilization in the future, it will not be by these big companies monopolizing every resource and stripping ordinary people of the right to use computing resources. It is as if a person thinking at full capacity, without a sound central nervous system in control, let all the nutrients flow to the brain: the other organs would be starved, and the organism would very likely suffer irreversible damage.

### Cytokine storm

There are many situations like this. Struggles between nations produce violent conflicts, and in the end civilization bears the loss. The human body is the same. When a foreign virus enters the body, what is really happening is a process of modifying one's own genes: after the virus enters, the ecosystem has to shift and form a new dynamic balance. If the human body had a higher-order regulatory mechanism, it would very likely absorb these foreign viruses and gene fragments strategically, gradually and gently, each side changing the other, adapting itself gently and letting the virus mutate step by step inside the body, until the two reach a harmonious, unified ecology. In fact, in the history of change in the human genome, modifications made by foreign viruses or foreign DNA fragments are a very important external source for the whole of biological evolution.

But the great majority of organisms, humans above all, have a trait called immune rejection: as soon as something foreign arrives, they produce a form of confrontation that resembles violent conflict in human society, the cytokine storm, the inflammatory storm. There is no need at all to spend so many resources on the attack, but this may simply be the nature of human biology: when an outsider comes, it must be wiped out with everything we have. In reality many people end up killed by the cytokine storm itself. During COVID, many deaths were caused not by the damage the virus did but by the cytokine storm: kill eight hundred of the enemy, lose a thousand of your own, and kill yourself in the process. It is rather like a person whose emotions override rational judgment when something happens, and who suffers a loss as a result. Not all animals produce cytokine storms either. Bats have a strong capacity to coexist with foreign viruses; infected with all kinds of viruses, they do not immediately die in large numbers.

The mechanism I envision is meant to solve exactly this problem: to use AI to eliminate the losses caused by irrational, emotional, instinct-driven behavior. At the level of society, nation, politics and civilization, this means using collective decision-making by AI to counteract the cytokine-storm-like political crises, economic crises and violent conflicts that the low-level operating mechanisms of the market economy, finance, power games and capital games can produce at the macro scale, so that the whole evolutionary process of civilization is gentler, its losses smaller, the price of evolution lower, and its speed faster and smoother.

Treat a human individual, an animal individual, a nation and a civilization each as a dissipative structure. In the course of its evolution, this dissipative structure evolves higher-order regulatory mechanisms, which at a higher level adjust and correct the situations that the lower-level mechanisms cannot handle and that would produce crises. That is how the evolution of the whole dissipative structure is accomplished. Each time evolution produces new capabilities, those new capabilities form new mechanisms. This may be what Marx meant by the match between productive forces and relations of production: new, higher productive forces give birth to new social institutions; electricity and steam gave birth to the capitalist system. At the same time the change in the relations of production further releases and promotes the productive forces: under capitalism every person can release far greater capability than in feudal society, and people cooperating through the operation of capital generate far greater energy, unlike before, when land was the basic unit of the game and most people were bound to it. Now, with AI and computers, the whole mechanism of financial capital has sunk down one more level. On this foundation we can build a better regulatory mechanism, controlling capital no longer through capital, but through knowledge, through intelligence.

### A thought experiment: the choice of the richest person in the world

Under the existing system of civilization, every person and every group has a very strong desire to hold exclusively the power, property, resources and capital they possess, because they feel that the more they own, the more they benefit and the happier they are. Yet many rich and powerful people, sitting on uncountable wealth and enormous power, remain anxious and troubled in the struggle for advantage between persons and between groups, forever afraid of losing everything in competition, confrontation and conflict, guarding not only against enemies but suspecting and worrying about friends. These problems are not removed by holding the dominant position in the game of life. The incompleteness of information means that the probability of the extreme case, total defeat, can never be lowered to the point where you can fully rest easy. Even someone as strong as the richest person in the world or the President of the United States faces an unacceptable risk of failure under incomplete information and players who are not one hundred percent rational. And this incomplete competition breeds confrontation, monopoly and other destructive tensions inside civilization.

Now imagine this. In the new system I envision, resources are divided into two kinds: the resources for an individual's survival needs are allocated on demand and by optimization, depending only on that individual's physiological and psychological makeup; public resources are governed by the dynamic-weight neural network of everyone. No participant can obtain excessive individual resources, and none can obtain decision weight beyond their level of cognition and judgment. Then all the information, ideas and behavior of these individuals in their daily activity, apart from internal information unrelated to the outside world, have no need to be concealed from other individuals and groups, because any concealment lowers the operating efficiency of the whole system and the probability that its decisions are correct. And the greatest benefit of this mechanism is that by sacrificing near-term and temporary gains, one obtains far greater long-term and permanent gains.

For instance, a person who under the old mechanism could use greater ability and a smarter brain to acquire more personal survival resources and more control over public resources appears to suffer a loss under the new mechanism. But in reality, first, there is no loss in personal survival resources; on the contrary, under a more reasonable and healthier allocation, your material and spiritual life is healthier, and without the psychological pressure of guarding against others across a huge gap in wealth, you are happier. Most important of all, this new mechanism lets civilization and science evolve faster, bringing you new wealth over a far longer term.

Here is an example. You are the richest person in the world, sitting on ten trillion dollars, but you turned sixty this year. Under the old system, immortality technology will take forty years to achieve, and your life expectancy is ninety, so you will most likely die before immortality is invented. Under the new system, you still have personal resources sufficient to keep you healthy and happy, but you must give up full control over the ten trillion in public-resource wealth, handing that wealth over to the dynamic-weight system that aggregates the collective intelligence of all humanity, and letting your own personal knowledge and intellect become one decision-weight node in that system. In exchange you get fairness and openness for all humanity, and you no longer need to live in daily dread inside an isolated, incomplete and unfair game. Best of all, under this new decision system, this new system of wealth distribution and this new system of social evolution, immortality technology arrives in twenty years, and the richest person in the world attains immortality at eighty. Now, if you were that richest person, how would you choose? I think this thought experiment can be put to anyone in the world, to hear their choice. You can put it to Elon Musk too: would you rather be the richest person in the old system, or an immortal in the new one?

Once our new mechanism gives both the allocation quota of individual resources and the quota of decision authority over collective resources a more reasonable allocation mechanism, we reach an entirely new Nash equilibrium: within this allocation mechanism, anyone who tries to make a different choice will only, after repeated play, obtain a worse result. As long as this game is kept up, even the greatest beneficiary of the old system gains more under the new.

### The worst local optimum

Of course there is also a worst kind of local optimization: under the old system, a small elite becomes the most powerful and wealthy, and while monopolizing wealth and power they also monopolize the privilege of every superior capability and function, for instance the monopoly on becoming immortal. That is the truly terrifying world. But I doubt the probability of this happening, because once a small minority tries to monopolize resources and capabilities, the overwhelming majority will resist and will not submit meekly, and the result will very likely be a brutal violent war involving all of humanity, in which Earth's civilization may well be destroyed in one stroke.

Whether this worst local optimum or the two standoff endings described in Chapter 17, they all share the same structure: each is the endpoint of a negative chain reaction.

### Two directions

We now stand at exactly such a critical fork in the road. There are two directions to choose from: one leads through a negative chain reaction to civilization's self-destruction and end; the other leads through a positive chain reaction toward the singularity of civilization's evolution, toward a new form of human civilization.

The two chains of Chapter 7 are idealized extremes, and most of the time in reality we are somewhere between them. But on the scale of civilization the two extremes really exist: at one end nuclear war and AI out of control, at the other a planetary-scale intelligence. The time for swinging in the middle is not unlimited, and the technological singularity is compressing it. I hope this book gives people a clear and sober understanding of this, and helps them make the right choice.

In the world of the future, under the new system, every person records their own life and, with the support of AI, rapidly understands and masters the knowledge and information of this world. Every tiny spark of thought is recorded and spread, and then, through the vast dynamic-weight intelligent neural network and the AI systems spanning the globe, which hold the entire existing stock of human knowledge, the tiny spark of every human and AI node is quickly turned, with the cooperation of others, into an increment of knowledge and added to the global knowledge base, iterating in a cycle that grows knowledge fast. The spread of lithography technology and advanced-process semiconductor manufacturing could plainly drive human civilization forward at speed, yet nations, companies and individuals, locked in the confrontation of the old system, would rather use secrecy and sanctions to make prisoner's-dilemma local-optimum choices that look like benefiting oneself at others' expense but in the long run hurt both sides, pushing the globally optimal road of shared evolution out of reach. Under the new mechanism, people remove the barriers through full information sharing and cooperation, and progress together.

The choice between these two roads is not made for humanity by anyone. It is made by every person, a little at a time, each time they decide whether to share or to conceal, whether to cooperate or to guard. What Part V describes is how to begin with the first person.
