## Chapter 14: Personal Digital Space, Records, and Data Sovereignty

### A dash cam for one's life

This project is Merkle-DAG-ifying the whole computer, from language to software ecosystem, and beyond data and code it also records, in this same framework, the entire research process and communication process of doing the project. Continue down this road and every trace of behavior and every data product that each person leaves in digital space is brought into this framework too. You can see it in the development of AI already: a function that once had to be performed with a certain application on a certain operating system or portal site, with data accessed separately through a cloud client or a local file manager, is all taken over once the agent exists as the interface. And under the takeover of the new framework, the whole user as a person, not just the code and data they use, but their communication, the trajectory of their life, what they said, what they did, what assets and abilities they have, what ideas they have, what they have seen, everything that left a digital imprint, whether diaries, notes, articles, or the audio and video recorders they carry, or conversations with agents, or even, in the future, information recorded directly from the brain through a brain-computer interface, is all recorded. It amounts to a dash cam and black box for one's life.

This user's proxy node knows every one of the user's interests, hobbies, beliefs and demands, their interests, rights and obligations, and their relationships with other people in this world; at the same time it can help the user, when participating in all economic, political and social activity, with equally powerful judgment and game-playing ability through the rational reasoning power of AI. Thus across the cooperation and competition of all human society, a fully equal, fully rational game is formed, and from it an entirely different system of rights and obligations, distribution of power in collective decision-making, and form of social organization is built.

A person's digital space is part of that person's capability. Whoever controls this space controls that person's capability in the game.

### Collecting information is the starting point of everything

In terms of computer technology and the implementation path of the Merkle-DAG framework, collecting information, including recording all audio and video information, is the starting point of everything.

Collection devices fall into three classes: personal wearable audio and video recorders; plug-in audio and video recorders in the home; and devices in workplaces and public spaces that implement the new mechanism with the consent of the participants. The devices automatically stand by and wake, and automatically decide who starts: in homes, workplaces or public areas that have public collection devices of sufficient resolution, the wearable device automatically hands off collection to the fixed-location plug-in device and goes to sleep; in other situations it starts automatically; on entering a place where the new mechanism is not in effect, it defaults to off, and when the user manually chooses to start it, a conspicuous warning light comes on, giving other people the right to know and to object. Fixed-location devices automatically stand by when no one is present or nothing is moving, and wake automatically on movement. Basically this makes surveillance cameras and audio recorders into an AI-driven, automatically coordinated information-collection system, as fully automatic and imperceptible as possible, reducing the burden on people of doing these tasks deliberately.

At the start, existing devices can be used, but the best plan is to custom-manufacture devices that fit best. Because through cross-encryption with device, personal, manufacturer and public keys, from leaving the factory to the user's use, including firmware upgrades, all information can be guaranteed against tampering by any party over the whole process from collection to upload to use. Keys and versions thus constrain device manufacturers and administrators.

### The rules of viewing permissions

The design goal of this scheme is to preserve as much information as possible that benefits the evolution of the whole system as common knowledge, on the premise that no substantive harm is done to individual interests. So viewing permissions are the core. The rules are as follows:

1. By default, each person has the right to view only the historical information they could themselves see and hear in their own life. No one has the right to view information they could not, by their own subjective capacity, have collected. This is fundamentally different from the present way of doing things: at present, the collectors and managers of public information can view other people's information at will.
2. Viewing of public information is event-driven. Only when an event has more than one participant, when the event requires a fair judgment and decision to produce a resolution, and when this cannot be done with one person's information alone, does a fair algorithm compute the set of individuals involved, and only after obtaining the consent and authorization of those individuals is the right to view obtained. And the whole process, from viewing the information to reaching the judgment and ruling and the resolution, is preserved as common knowledge in Merkle-DAG form.

These two rules put into practice the boundary described in Chapter 7: a person's historical field of view becomes a concrete permission; a multi-party event obtains evidence without generating unlimited access rights.

### Why people will trust such a product

Because the entity behind it is not a traditional company but an entirely new organization, which can guarantee two things. First, all information and all behavior are preserved as traceable, tamper-proof common knowledge in Merkle-DAG, so any statement or action that infringes on a user's interests is reliably recorded. Second, everyone's personal income is distributed according to need after computation by an intelligent optimization algorithm, so no one can obtain any extra return from encroaching on users' interests. The former guarantees that no one has the ability to infringe on users' interests; the latter guarantees that no one has the desire to.

How does a new organization prove it deserves trust? Not by promises, but by a record that begins growing on the first day of its founding and that no single node can control.

### How records change both before and after

Someone says that "behavior being reliably recorded" can strengthen detection and accountability, but is not the same as behavior being unable to happen in the first place. In a complex system, the events within it in many cases do not all conform to strict causality; however rigorously a transportation system is designed, accidents still happen.

First, what we must guard against is deliberate, malicious harm. "Behavior being reliably recorded" serves not only for detection and accountability after the fact; it also has a powerful preventive effect beforehand. Wherever public surveillance equipment covers, people generally restrain their malicious behavior. Because the common knowledge of tit-for-tat lets every person know the fact that their malicious behavior will be recorded, and that malicious behavior will bring the consequence of punishment. As long as we ensure that the loss inflicted by punishment after the fact exceeds the benefit gained, this rule system has a preventive effect beforehand on any rational person. People today are not all complete rational agents: for one thing, their own learning and education are imperfect, and their rational knowledge and decision-making ability have gaps; for another, they lack effective emotional control; and on top of this, there is no system that can reliably and fairly collect all behavior and render fair rulings, so true, complete prevention beforehand has been impossible. But in the future, once AI is universal, everyone will have their own AI agent to perfect their rational knowledge and decision-making ability; and because the great majority of intellectual activity and physical behavior will be carried out or mediated by AI agents, with this layer of buffering, irrational emotional behavior can also be greatly reduced. So this system will not only have the effect of recording and tracing after the fact, but also the effect of stopping all deliberate malicious behavior beforehand.

For harm that is not deliberately malicious, caused by one's own limitations or by external force majeure, this mechanism has an equally powerful effect. Human civilization, like an individual human life, is an intelligent agent that collects, processes and feeds back information and iteratively evolves its own knowledge model and decision system. For a person it is "a fall into the pit, a gain in your wit"; for a civilization, social rules and schemes are themselves not fixed, but evolve continually with the progress of technology and knowledge. So the occurrence of harm can never be completely eliminated, but "behavior being reliably recorded and processed" is the precondition for the whole system to reduce repeated mistakes, to gain in wit from every fall as far as possible, and to achieve ever more efficient feedback and correction. This is the most important precondition for stable, fast, forward iterative evolution.

In sum, "behavior being reliably recorded" is the most important prerequisite for the system to reduce the risk of harm from negative behavior. The faster and more comprehensively this goal is achieved, the smaller the risk of harm to the system and its members. What people fear is not that their personal information and behavior are recorded; what they fear is that the ability to view and exploit that information falls into the hands of a few and becomes a weapon against them.

However fierce the old system's collection, it can only get what people are willing to expose: finished products, successes, the outward story. What it cannot get is failures, abandoned plans, reasons for changing one's mind, because under competition and accountability, hiding these is rational. And that is precisely the data most lacking for building theory and correcting errors. So the data advantage of the new system lies not in "more", but in a class of data the old system is structurally unable to produce: decisions, outcomes, reversals, and the "why" alongside them.

### How instinctive impulses are safely released

"Distribution according to need" can reduce the economic motive for encroaching on others' interests, but it cannot directly guarantee the absence of malice. Humans, as higher animals with biological characteristics and instinctive impulses, have physiological urges, instinctive curiosity and desire for control, irrational emotional impulses born of love, hate and fear, even some abnormal psychological desires, and a great deal of unhealthy psychology formed under traditional social mechanisms, masochistic and sadistic psychology. Under the new mechanism, how are these behaviors, which could cause substantive harm in reality, safely controlled and released? We can draw on the theory of the pressure valve: release these emotions in virtual digital space through virtual works of literature, film and games, by more scientific methods. On one side, limit the duration and degree, so that individuals do not become addicted and sink to the point of being unable to tell reality from the virtual; on the other, release the pressure effectively. For this to truly work, I am afraid we will have to wait for the input and output technology of brain-computer interfaces to become more advanced.

### Unalterable history, with retraction and deletion

Records are append-only, rules can change, and the archive will outlive any version of the rules. So on top of the unalterable history there must be retraction and deletion: a record can be marked as withdrawn, an authorization can be revoked, a piece of data can be removed from public view, and these operations are themselves recorded. What unalterability guarantees is that "what happened" cannot be forged, not that "anyone can see everything forever". Once these two things are separated, a person can live, as a continually changing human being, inside a traceable memory: your record of today will not be tampered into something else, and your choice of tomorrow will not be locked down by the record of yesterday.

### Taking data back from the giants

I once applied to a large-model company to export all of my conversation data, and the reply was that the export might take several days. These are not petabytes of files, and this is not the computing power and storage of twenty years ago; exporting an individual user's data does not need anywhere near that long. If there is computing power to support massive volumes of real-time AI conversation, how can it fail to support a simple package-and-download? This difference in treatment of computing and storage resources between the paid inference service and the user's data export shows that the company cares more about the business that makes it money than about the user's rights. This is the unfairness that arises when the basic architecture contains an unequal game of power between the service provider and the consumer.

The first thing the new mechanism does is to establish, within the software and hardware service framework, local and cloud computing and storage space that each user can completely control, and take back the data that was scattered among the information silos erected by the commercial giants of various software and services. I call this REA: not reverse-engineering all software, but reverse-analyzing the interface protocols of all of today's data silos and application silos, and building a set of fully compatible replacement applications and data sources that run under the new mechanism of the Merkle architecture. It carries out three successive tasks: recovering the historical accumulation, obtaining personal records from different platforms, clients and formats while preserving original content and provenance; restoring usability, rebuilding conversational relationships, attachment associations, timelines and semantic connections so that the recovered data can continue to be searched, computed on and collaborated with; and establishing a continuous, autonomous mode of production, in which newly produced records promptly enter the user-controlled space, and when switching models or service providers one can carry the existing state and keep working. If every departure from a platform requires applying for export again, reverse-parsing again, and repairing lost context again, then the user is forever bearing the migration cost created by platform isolation; the new architecture should make preservation and migration a routine operating mechanism.

There is only one criterion for testing this direction: after cutting the connection to any one original service provider, can the user keep their records and knowledge structure, choose a different set of tools, and continue the original work?

My own knowledge base is the first landing point: more than a year of sessions and projects scattered across different computers and different AI platforms, organized into a personal knowledge base in Merkle-DAG, where every original utterance has a content identifier, a source and a time, AI replies and my own statements are marked separately, and every version can be checked. This book grew out of that base.

### Questions and Answers

**Q: A complete record of a person's cognition plus a model that can predict them is the most perfect target for manipulation. The threat comes not only from conflicts of interest among members, but from outside the system and from future changes to the rules.**

A: That is why this chapter's permission rules are not "record everything, publish everything", but "record the decision loop and its formation completely, with the person controlling the scope of disclosure". By default only you can see your own field of view; multi-party events have their scope computed by algorithm and require authorization; every act of viewing is itself recorded. Manipulation relies on information asymmetry: one side can see, the other cannot. When viewing is symmetric, recorded and authorized, the manipulator is first seen by their target. As for threats from outside the system, the answer is in Chapter 11: content identity and distributed storage mean the record is not attached to any single node that can be taken over; and in Chapter 19, personal data sovereignty is a basic guarantee that is not conditional on how much one has contributed or whether one agrees with the philosophy.
