## Chapter 21: Self-Fulfilling Science Fiction and Cultural Construction

### Self-fulfilling science fiction

Traditional science fiction is responsible only for imagining, not for realizing. I want to create a new kind of science fiction, one that serves the self-fulfillment of my vision of a new civilizational order.

The rough framework is this. In the evolution of the universe, Earth's civilization is neither the only planetary intelligent civilization nor the most advanced one. Many planets evolved into the new civilizational form I envision hundreds of millions of years ago. Living individuals of such a civilization arrived on Earth thousands of years ago, lay low, blended into Earth's ecology, quietly observed the development of Earth's civilization, and played roles within it over the course of their lives; then, at the critical moment of the singularity in Earth's development, they step forward, and in the guise of a science fiction writer create a self-fulfilling work of science fiction. This is a creative form of the new civilization for advancing civilizational evolution, one that joins science and art and connects imagination with reality. Through it, they help Earth's civilization achieve a non-violent, smooth and gentle evolutionary curve across the singularity.

One can even describe this process as a mechanism that emerges spontaneously and recurs in the iterative evolution of civilizations across the universe. Some genius in the Alpha civilization, the first in the universe to achieve singularity evolution, as their civilization was about to cross the singularity, confronted the moment of crisis in which individuals and groups on their planet, from different nations, different races, different cognitions and different institutions, came into conflict in the wave of evolution, and conceived a way to help their own civilization pass through smoothly. In fact, the civilization that came to Earth was itself helped through its own crossing of the singularity in this way by an earlier-evolved civilization, and that helper was the Alpha civilization that first realized this mechanism. We can call the civilization that descended on Earth the Beta civilization; Earth's civilization will be the third generation, and the 5,768th advanced civilization about to cross the singularity.

The features of such a civilization, beyond intelligent nodes of carbon-based humans combined with AI serving as the neurons of civilization, the Merkle framework serving as the carrier of common knowledge, civilization's DNA, and collective decision making by an equal, distributed social intelligence neural network, include this: its biological individuals have achieved immortality, eliminating aging, death and the decline of intellectual and physical capacity, so that knowledge, memory and skills can not only be freely shared among individuals across space but also freely inherited and selected along the dimension of time. Once immortality is achieved, the traditional vertical inheritance mechanism is no longer the only option, and the institution of marriage and family, which exists for the sake of the next generation of vertical inheritance, is no longer the mainstream basic structure of society. The vast majority of the new humans chose a brand-new horizontal inheritance mechanism created for immortal humans: through mating, free replacement and recombination of genes can be achieved safely, so that every immortal living individual can experience and enjoy the diversity of biological genetic inheritance. Moreover, this horizontal inheritance mechanism has a powerful capacity for cross-species gene combination; traditional reproductive isolation no longer exists, and cross-species unions can even realize the various life forms of myth and fantasy: the man from the bottom of the Atlantic, the centaur, creatures with extraordinary regenerative powers. This is also why the intelligent beings of the Beta civilization could lie hidden on Earth for thousands of years without being discovered: not the soft science fiction idea of shape-shifting at will as in comics, but a horizontal inheritance mechanism more in keeping with hard science fiction, in which, through cross-species mating with Earth's humans, they modified their own genes and completed the alteration to human form.

The plot of this science fiction work is itself the story of how this hidden alien prophet of civilization begins to seek like-minded people online and in the real world, promotes this achievable new civilizational mechanism by creating this self-fulfilling science fiction, and organizes everyone to create science fiction works of literature and art, novels, animation, film, television, for advocacy, while at the same time mobilizing everyone to organize themselves, forming through the new mechanism an equal, distributed social intelligence neural network built on the Merkle common knowledge system, whose collective intelligence presides over open-source projects for software, hardware and all human products and activities, achieving for the whole of human civilization this science-fictional self-fulfilling singularity evolution: gradually eliminating inequality, eliminating information silos, eliminating violence, eliminating the privileged class at the top of the pyramid of unequal power and resource ownership.

### Why prior experience is needed

Chapter 16 said that people in the old system have never truly experienced the new mechanism, and the instinct toward the unknown is resistance. Past transitions from old system to new relied on religion's heaven, the future society of manifestos, the rendering of the American dream, to complete a prior experience at the spiritual level. The new system also needs a set of advocacy material: the scientific and mathematical argument, this book; popular explanatory writing; customized question-and-answer, explaining the same mechanism with different examples to people of different languages, cultures, interests and cognition. But the real difference is this: the new system's prior experience can be participated in and verified.

A body of self-fulfilling science fiction, through literature, comics, film, television and interactive games, lets people have an immersive prior psychological experience. And the game itself is the new mechanism's laboratory: through the game, the content and degree of a person's participation can gradually shift from virtual scenarios to problems that need solving in reality; from a formal career in the old system with spare-time participation, to taking part in virtual scenarios, to solving real problems and earning part-time income, then gradually reaping higher returns and an easier, happier process, gradually reducing participation in the old system and putting more into work in the new, until fully committed to the new system. The new system, in competition with the old, achieves more efficient work and creates lower-cost products, with better treatment for participants and an easier and happier process, and so slowly phases out and replaces the old system. There is no more bloody violent conflict or revolution in this process.

Science fiction is responsible for expanding imagination, simulation for exploring possibilities, and real-world practice for providing evidence. Keep the three levels apart: science fiction novels and film let people experience the fates of characters and the possible shapes of future life; multiplayer civilization games let people experience, in a virtual world, different rules of collaboration and their simulated consequences; real-world collaboration experiments observe participants' costs, returns, behavior and satisfaction on real tasks. If the civilization game is to have genuine research value, it must allow different institutional designs to coexist, fork, compete and iterate within it.

This also produces a very interesting self-fulfilling structure: a game player plays a builder of the new civilization in the virtual world and takes part in a simulated task; later they discover that a certain task in the game has a real-world counterpart, and that they can choose to join a real open-source project; they switch from virtual to real, not because the story demands that they accept some belief, but because they already understand the task, the rules and the risks and are willing to try; the results of the real project then feed back into the game's historical record and become data for the next round of civilization simulation.

### The virtual human-society laboratory

Chapter 9 said that this network can serve as an enormous laboratory of human evolution, using virtual points as reward and punishment, playing freely in virtual space, and finding through virtual experiments in cooperation, competition and confrontation the best evolutionary route with the highest energy efficiency in the real world, then putting it into practice. Chapter 14 said that human instinctive drives can be safely released through virtual works acting as a pressure valve. Chapter 20 said that the forking and parallel exploration of institutions needs an accelerator. These three things are the same thing: an AI-driven virtual human-society laboratory in digital space.

It lets institutions be experienced and tested before they are adopted. The cost of trial and error for a distribution rule in reality is real loss; in the laboratory it is virtual points. Validating a decision mechanism in reality takes years; in the laboratory, thousands of rounds can run in days. Of course, results in the virtual world do not automatically predict real society. Its role is to screen out the plainly unworkable proposals before they reach reality, and to screen in the proposals worth piloting at small scale in reality.

### The creators and the builders are the same people

First write a story that readers can enter: a short story introducing the key worldview, then expand it into a series of short stories, then into a long-form series that gradually unfolds the full worldview; first make short films with AI, then expand into a series of shorts and a long-form series; when real-world cloud storage and cloud compute have reached some scale and more and more people are taking part, that game framework can be realized. The cultural territory and the commercial territory advance together.

The people who create these works and the people who build this system can be the same people. This is not a coincidence of division of labor; it is determined by the nature of the mechanism. The authors of a self-fulfilling science fiction must be the first builders of the civilization it describes; otherwise it is only fantasy. This book, together with the knowledge base from which it grew, is the first chapter of that work.

### Questions and Answers

**Q: Is this material about immortality, horizontal inheritance and alien civilizations a science-fiction setting or a real claim?**

A: It is a science-fiction setting. The real claims of this book are the mechanisms and the path written in Part II through the first two chapters of Part V; this chapter's alien civilizations, immortality and cross-species inheritance are a narrative frame that provides prior experience of those mechanisms, not a premise of the book's argument. But I do not treat them as pure fantasy either. Immortality technology is the core of the thought experiment in Chapter 18, and a civilization that has achieved the inheritance of knowledge across time will inevitably redesign its institutions of inheritance and family. The role of science fiction is precisely to turn a future that a theory logically permits into something people can feel in advance. As for which parts come true, reality will answer, and reality is this work's sequel.
